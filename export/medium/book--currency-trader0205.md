# Currency Trader February 2005: the trader's summary

*A 2005 FX magazine issue covering volatility breakouts, pivot-candlestick confluence, current-account seasonality, and a moving-average system.*

**Currency Trader staff and contributors** · 2005 · Forex Mechanics & Macro Drivers · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-52. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This issue covers volatility mean reversion, pivot points combined with candlesticks, true range, and a moving-average breakout system. A study of 43 U.S. current-account releases finds the dollar typically rose before the announcement and fell afterward: the Fed dollar index lost 0.46% on average from the day before release to quarter end, while the NYBOT futures index lost 0.62% (pp. 32–35).

## Key points

- Two consecutive inside bars represent contracting volatility; the issue argues a breakout becomes more likely as inside bars accumulate (pp. 24–26).
- The inside-bar example uses an entry beyond the latest bar, a stop-and-reverse order 5–10 pips beyond its opposite extreme, half profit taken after one risk unit, and a trailing stop on the remainder (p. 25).
- Pivot formulas are PP=(H+L+C)/3, R1=2PP−L, R2=PP+(H−L), S1=2PP−H, and S2=PP−(H−L), using the 5 p.m. ET forex close (p. 28).
- Multiple time-frame pivots matter more when levels coincide; doji and morning-star structures are used as reversal confirmation (pp. 28–30).
- The trend system uses a 5-day SMA of highs or lows against a 28-day EMA of closes, with next-day entry after a crossover and confirmation by the latest close and SMA slope (pp. 44–45).
- Its portfolio test produced 292.83% net return but a 39.73% maximum drawdown, 38.89% winning trades, and 396 trades; the authors note many small losers and poor sideways-market performance (pp. 44–46).

## Actionable rules

1. For the inside-bar setup, place a stop entry beyond the latest inside bar, use a 5–10 pip stop-and-reverse buffer, take half off after one initial risk, then trail the balance (p. 25).
2. For the moving-average system, go long on the next open when the 5-day high SMA crosses above the 28-day close EMA, the latest close is above the high SMA, and that SMA is rising; reverse the conditions for shorts (p. 44).
3. The system test risks 2% of capital per trade, assumes 4 pips round-turn commission and 1 pip slippage per 100,000 units, and exits on a trend change or a 20% loss (p. 44).

## Caveats

The current-account study uses only 43 releases from 1994–2004 (pp. 32–36). The trend system’s 39.73% drawdown and 484-day longest flat period are material; its 20% stop was never reached in the test (p. 46). The parameters require contemporary data and out-of-sample testing.

## Who it is for

FX traders who want a compact survey of several techniques and the discipline to test them separately. It is less suitable as a single ready-made trading plan.
