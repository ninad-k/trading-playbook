# Performance of ASX Resource Stocks: the trader's summary

*Short ASX sector study arguing average resource-sector returns mislead, and that resource investors correctly lead commodity prices by 1-2 years.*

**Noll Moriarty** · 2003 · Investing, Value & Market History · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-4. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This is a short investment-magazine article (Investment Preview, August 2003) analyzing the performance of the Resources sector (Energy + Materials) on the Australian Stock Exchange versus the broader All Ordinaries and Industrials indices. Using monthly price data from 1983-2003 (ASSIRT Library) and Reserve Bank of Australia commodity price data, the author compares cumulative sector returns, tests the correlation and cross-correlation between resource stock returns and a base-metal commodity price index, and examines concentration of market capitalization within the sector. The main finding: resource stock returns show no contemporaneous correlation with commodity prices (-0.1) but a strong cross-correlation when resource returns are advanced by 1-2 years, implying resource investors anticipate commodity price moves rather than react to them.

## Key points

- Resources fell from over 60% of the ASX All Ords in the 1980s to about 14% by 2003, displaced mainly by the Financials sector's growth during 1990s privatisations.
- Since 1983, Industrials returned a compound 10.5% p.a. versus 6.4% p.a. for Resources (price only, excluding dividends).
- Contemporaneous correlation between resource returns and a base-metal commodity price index is essentially zero (-0.1).
- Cross-correlation analysis shows resource stock returns lead the commodity price index by 1-2 years, a stronger and more useful relationship than the raw correlation suggests.
- Sector averages are misleading: in Energy, the top 3 companies (Woodside, Santos, Origin) made up 85% of sector cap; in Materials, the top 3 (BHP Billiton, Rio Tinto, WMC) made up 67%.
- Over the last 5 years of the sample, Resources and Industrials had similar average returns (~5% p.a.), but individual resource-focused managed funds ranged from underperforming to +10% p.a., showing wide dispersion beneath the average.
- Real commodity prices have declined about 1% per year over the past 140 years, a long-term headwind for the sector.
- China's import demand was cited as the dominant swing factor for near-term commodity prices (China: ~5% of world GDP but ~20% of world basic materials consumption in 2002).
- Microcap resource stocks (outside the ASX100) were argued to be an overlooked source of future sector leaders.

## Actionable rules

1. None given. This is a descriptive/analytical article, not a trading system; it offers no entry, exit, stop, or sizing rules.
2. Implied research heuristic (not a formal rule): when evaluating a concentrated sector, check cross-correlation at various lags against a relevant macro driver rather than relying on contemporaneous correlation or sector averages alone.

## Caveats

Data and conclusions are specific to the 1983-2003 period and the Australian market; the commodity-price and China-demand context is dated (2002-03). The cross-correlation "1-2 year lead" finding is presented without confidence intervals, out-of-sample validation, or a specified statistical test beyond a chart-based cross-correlation, so it should be treated as illustrative rather than a validated forecasting signal.

## Who it is for

Equity analysts and investors interested in sector-level return behavior, commodity-equity linkages, and the pitfalls of using sector-average returns to judge concentrated, capitalization-skewed sectors like resources/mining.
