# Various Investment Vehicles: the trader's summary

*A historical Australian equity comparison showing why investment wrappers, taxes and benchmarks affect performance comparisons.*

**Noll Moriarty** · Investing, Value & Market History · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1; OCR plus visual inspection of chart and footnotes. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This one-page adviser handout compares Australian share exposure through insurance bonds, superannuation funds, other managed funds and direct ownership. Its chart uses five years ending June 2004. Moriarty argues that investors should benchmark their results instead of assuming that personal control produces superior returns. The useful lesson concerns comparable measurement; the document does not establish which wrapper is best today (PDF p. 1).

## Key points

- The underlying asset class is held broadly constant while the investment vehicle changes.
- The chart separates diversified-share and industrial-share funds, so differences can reflect portfolio composition as well as vehicle structure.
- Both before-tax and estimated after-tax outcomes appear; they are not interchangeable comparisons.
- Risk is represented by the standard deviation of annual returns, which does not capture every possible loss or liquidity problem.
- The S&P/ASX 300 is used as a proxy for direct investment, rather than an observed sample of individual traders' accounts.
- The after-tax illustration assumes a $70,000 annual income, a 30% average tax rate, 4% dividend return, 60% fully franked dividends and 40% annual turnover. These are the handout's historical modelling assumptions (p. 1, footnotes).

## Actionable rules

1. The author's explicit practical instruction is to compare personal performance with an objective accumulation benchmark, which includes reinvested income (p. 1).
2. When interpreting this comparison, retain its tax, fee, turnover, time-period and portfolio-composition assumptions; changing them changes the meaning of the results. This is an interpretation of the chart, not a separate trading method.

## Caveats

This is an old, single-period comparison, not a controlled study. Index performance cannot establish the performance of the average direct investor. The handout's claim that direct investors generally failed to outperform therefore goes beyond the account-level evidence supplied. A printed industrial-fund outperformance figure is ambiguous and is not repeated here. Publication year is not stated; the underlying data ends in June 2004. No entry, exit or position-sizing system is given.

## Who it is for

Investors learning to compare portfolio results on consistent terms, while recognizing the limitations of adviser presentation charts.
