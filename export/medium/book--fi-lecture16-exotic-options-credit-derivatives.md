# Exotic Options and Credit Derivatives: the trader's summary

*Lecture notes classify path-dependent rate options and introduce CDS, credit-linked notes, spread options, and total-return swaps.*

**Unknown** · Options, Futures & Derivatives · advanced

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-5. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

These fixed-income lecture notes classify interest-rate exotic options by path dependence, correlation dependence, time dependence, and exercise flexibility (p. 1). Barrier caps and floors are described as activated or deactivated when a reference rate crosses a barrier; bounded versions cap the payout in exchange for lower protection (p. 2). Other structures include captions and floortions, chooser or flexi caps, moving-average caps, contingent-premium caps, extendible or cancellable swaps, and range-accrual swaps whose fixed-leg interest accrues only while a floating rate remains in a range (pp. 2–3). N-caps replace an extinguished cap with one at a different strike, while ratchet caps adjust strike from the last fixing and rolling caps increase notional when earlier caplets are not exercised (p. 3). Because payoffs are path dependent and rarely closed form, the notes list trees, Monte Carlo under a risk-neutral measure, and finite-difference methods for pricing (p. 4). Credit derivatives are grouped into default-risk hedges, credit-spread products, and synthetic total-return products. A CDS exchanges a premium for compensation after a specified credit event; a credit-linked note embeds default exposure in a bond; a credit-spread option transfers spread risk; and a total-return swap exchanges a credit asset’s coupons and price changes for a fixed or reference-rate return (pp. 4–5).

## Key points

- Lower premium structures generally deliver less complete protection (p. 2).
- Credit derivatives transfer a specified risk component; a CDS does not remove interest-rate risk in the reference asset (p. 4).
- Payoff definitions and exercise rules determine both valuation method and hedging needs.

## Actionable rules

1. None given. The lecture classifies structures and names valuation methods but does not provide a trade, hedge, or sizing procedure.
2. For instrument analysis, preserve each payoff condition stated in the source: barrier direction and monitoring style, exercise dates, changing notionals, and defined credit events (pp. 1–5).

## Caveats

The notes are a taxonomy and formula-free overview with no calibration, numerical example, or model-risk discussion beyond naming methods. Contract language and market conventions must be verified for a specific instrument.

## Who it is for

Students and practitioners needing a compact map of rate exotics and credit-derivative structures.
