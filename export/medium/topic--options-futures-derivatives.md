# Options, Futures & Derivatives: a trading-library study guide

*Pricing by replication, trading by volatility percentile: the one book here that tested option writing found no inherent edge, and manufactured one through defence and selectivity.*

## What it is

Derivatives are contracts whose value derives from something else — a stock, an index, a commodity, an interest rate. This category covers three overlapping bodies of knowledge. The first is pricing: how a forward, future or option is valued by constructing a portfolio that replicates its payoff, and why no-arbitrage plus risk-neutral valuation produce a fair price without anyone forecasting direction. The second is the Greeks — the sensitivities that turn a position into a set of measurable exposures to price, to the rate of change of that exposure, to time, and to volatility. The third is strategy: how the four building blocks (long and short calls and puts) recombine into spreads, straddles, condors and collars, each with a defined payoff shape matched to a market view. The recurring theme, stated most bluntly by Ward and Gallacher, is that under fair pricing no option strategy is inherently profitable, so an edge has to come from somewhere specific — mispriced volatility, order flow, or disciplined defence — rather than from the structure itself.

## Core principles

- Derivatives are priced by replication: if two portfolios pay the same in every future state, they must cost the same today, or an arbitrage exists [Rubinstein on Derivatives](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/rubinstein-mark-rubinstein-on-derivatives.html) [Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives (5th Edition)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hull-options-futures-and-other-derivative-securities-5th-ed.html) [Introduction to Arbitrage Pricing of Financial Derivatives](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/introduction-to-arbitrage-pricing-of-financial-derivatives.html).
- Risk-neutral valuation gives the same answer as the real risk-averse world because risk preferences cancel out of the pricing equation [Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives (5th Edition)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hull-options-futures-and-other-derivative-securities-5th-ed.html) [Rubinstein on Derivatives](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/rubinstein-mark-rubinstein-on-derivatives.html).
- Put-call parity ties the four instruments together: c + Ke^-rT = p + S₀, which is what makes synthetic positions and conversions possible [Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives (5th Edition)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hull-options-futures-and-other-derivative-securities-5th-ed.html) [Options and Options Trading: A Simplified Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/options-and-options-trading-a-simplified-course.html).
- Direction is the low-edge variable; volatility is the tradable one. Fontanills builds every position to start at zero delta so profit comes from movement or time rather than a correct direction call [The Options Course: High Profit & Low Stress Trading Methods](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed.html) [Profit With Options](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options.html).
- "Cheap" and "expensive" are only meaningful against an underlying's own volatility history, not against a universal level or a raw implied-versus-historical gap [McMillan's Volatility-Based Option Strategy Selection](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options--volatility-based-strategy-selection.html) [Using Volatility Information for Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/volatility-part-2.html).
- Delta neutrality protects only against small moves; a delta-neutral book with large negative gamma is badly exposed to a big move either way [Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives (5th Edition)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hull-options-futures-and-other-derivative-securities-5th-ed.html) [Options: Essential Concepts and Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/options-essential-concepts-and-trading-strategies-2nd-edition.html).
- Black-Scholes assumes normally distributed returns, which real prices violate — so it works near the money and systematically misprices the tails [The Options Edge: Winning the Volatility Game with Options on Futures](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-the-options-edge-winning-the-volatility-game-with-options-on-futures.html) [Back to Basics: Historical Option Pricing Revisited](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/back-to-basics-historical-option-pricing-revisited.html) [Black-Scholes Option Pricing Model](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/black-scholes-option-pricing-model.html).
- Volatility skew is not an arbitrary quirk: it tracks the measurable fat-tailedness of the underlying's actual distribution [Back to Basics: Historical Option Pricing Revisited](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/back-to-basics-historical-option-pricing-revisited.html).
- There is no inherent writer's edge. Gallacher's 1996 study across fifteen commodities found an aggregate payout ratio of almost exactly 1.0 — the option market prices itself fairly on average [The Options Edge: Winning the Volatility Game with Options on Futures](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-the-options-edge-winning-the-volatility-game-with-options-on-futures.html).
- Under fair pricing every strategy breaks even before costs, so professional profits come from order flow, spreads and information rather than clever structures [Options and Options Trading: A Simplified Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/options-and-options-trading-a-simplified-course.html).
- Naked writing carries genuinely unbounded risk, and the literature returns repeatedly to Barings and to eight-standard-deviation days as the reason [The Option Trader Handbook: Strategies and Trade Adjustments](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/george-m-jabbour-the-option-trader-handbook-strategies-and-trade-adjustments.html) [Profit With Options](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options.html).
- Compute maximum risk, maximum reward and breakeven before committing capital — every strategy in the category has closed-form values for all three [The Bible of Options Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/guy-cohen-the-bible-of-options-strategies.html) [The Option Trader Handbook: Strategies and Trade Adjustments](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/george-m-jabbour-the-option-trader-handbook-strategies-and-trade-adjustments.html).
- Managing the position afterwards is half the job: almost any stalled position can be reshaped by rolling, collaring or ratio-spreading into a better risk profile [Trade Adjustment Playbook: Rolling, Collars, Ratio Spreads, and Repairs](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/george-m-jabbour-the-option-trader-handbook-strategies-and-trade-adjustments--trade-adjustment-playbook.html).
- Options carry sentiment information as well as price: put-call ratios and dollar-weighted option activity are read contrarily [Profit With Options](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options.html) [DeMark on Day-Trading Options](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/demark-tom-demark-on-day-trading-options.html).
- Futures spreads are structurally hedged, which is why they carry lower exchange margin and lower volatility than the equivalent outright [Trading Spreads and Seasonals](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joe-ross-trading-spreads-and-seasonals.html) [Understanding Spreads](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/edward-dobson-understanding-spreads.html).
- Multi-leg strategies concentrate cost in commissions and bid-ask spread, and almost every worked example in this category excludes both [Range-Bound Premium Spread System (Butterfly, Condor, Calendar, Collar)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed--range-bound-premium-spreads.html) [The Equity Options Strategy Guide](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/the-equity-options-strategy-guide.html).

## Concrete rules and setups

### Volatility-based selection

1. Compute a composite implied volatility daily, weighted by volume and moneyness, and rank it against at least 200 and ideally 600+ prior trading days — separately ranking the 10-, 20-, 50- and 100-day historical volatility [McMillan's Volatility-Based Option Strategy Selection](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options--volatility-based-strategy-selection.html).
2. At roughly the 10th percentile or lower, buy volatility: straddles, or backspreads where a favourable skew is present. At the 90th percentile or higher, sell it: strangles or ratio spreads, confined mainly to index options [McMillan's Volatility-Based Option Strategy Selection](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options--volatility-based-strategy-selection.html).
3. Judge volatility relative to the same instrument's own range: 25% is near the low end for soybeans and high for Treasury bonds, so the same number implies opposite strategies [Using Volatility Information for Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/volatility-part-2.html) [Volatility](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/volatility.html).
4. Gallacher's measurement: market volatility = 22 × the 30-day mean absolute deviation of daily percentage changes; implied volatility for an ATM option ≈ 40 × (ATM straddle premium ÷ 2) ÷ (futures price × √trading days to expiry). Write only when implied exceeds market volatility [Volatility-Selective Defensive Straddle Writing](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-the-options-edge-winning-the-volatility-game-with-options-on-futures--defensive-straddle-writing.html).
5. Gallacher's defensive hedge: once the future closes beyond the strike plus or minus the premium collected, offset the losing side with a futures position, with a second trigger protecting the hedge if price reverses back through the strike. This defence, not the volatility filter, supplies most of the edge — 1.00 unfiltered, 0.902 defended, 0.884 defended and filtered [Volatility-Selective Defensive Straddle Writing](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-the-options-edge-winning-the-volatility-game-with-options-on-futures--defensive-straddle-writing.html).

### Delta-neutral and spread construction

6. Build the entry to net zero delta — long 200 shares (+200) offset by four long ATM puts or four short ATM calls — and rebalance when combined delta drifts to ±100 [Delta-Neutral Straddle & Adjustment System](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed--delta-neutral-straddles-and-adjustments.html).
7. Straddles need 30-90+ days to expiration (Fontanills prefers around 90); short-dated straddles are eroded fastest by non-linear time decay [Delta-Neutral Straddle & Adjustment System](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed--delta-neutral-straddles-and-adjustments.html).
8. Ratio backspread: sell fewer near-the-money options and buy more further-out ones in a 1:2 or 2:3 ratio for a net credit, on $25-75 underlyings with at least 90 days to expiration. Call backspreads need a reverse skew, put backspreads a forward skew [Call & Put Ratio Backspread System](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed--ratio-backspreads.html).
9. Long butterfly: buy one lower strike, sell two middle, buy one higher, all same type and expiration with 45 days or less. Max risk is the net debit; max reward is (highest strike − short strike) − debit, realised only at the short strike [Range-Bound Premium Spread System (Butterfly, Condor, Calendar, Collar)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed--range-bound-premium-spreads.html).
10. Cohen's family rules: vertical spreads use strikes $2.50-5.00 apart, a month or less for credit verticals and longer for debit; calendars pair a 6+ month long leg with a roughly one-month short leg resold each cycle [The Bible of Options Strategies — Strategy Family Construction Playbook](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/guy-cohen-the-bible-of-options-strategies--strategy-family-playbook.html).
11. Cohen's liquidity filters: at least 500,000 average daily volume in the underlying and option open interest of 100, preferably 500 [The Bible of Options Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/guy-cohen-the-bible-of-options-strategies.html).
12. Trester's index credit spread: sell a far OTM broad-index option and buy one 5, 10 or 15 strikes further out, entered under three weeks to expiration, requiring a minimum $45 net credit and an 85% simulated probability that the index will not touch the stop level [The Index Credit Spread (Trester)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/101-option-trading-secrets-kenneth-r-trester--index-credit-spread.html).
13. Trester's cheap-option buying: cap cost at $200 per stock option and $400 per futures option, buy close-to-the-money with maximum time, require at least a 20% probability of profit, and hold each position to under 5% of the option portfolio [101 Option Trading Secrets](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/101-option-trading-secrets-kenneth-r-trester.html).

### Income and acquisition

14. Wolfinger's covered-call cycle: hold optionable broad-market ETFs in exact 100-share lots, write one front-month call per lot, default to at-the-money for maximum time premium, and require at least 0.5% a month net of commissions with 1.5% as the comfortable target [Covered Call / Uncovered Put ETF Income Method](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/m-wolfinger-create-your-own-hedge-fund-increase-profits-and-reduce-risk-with-etfs-and-opti--covered-call-etf-income-method.html).
15. Covered calls and cash-backed short puts at the same strike carry the same risk profile; the worst case for both is the underlying going to zero, cushioned by the premium [Create Your Own Hedge Fund](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/m-wolfinger-create-your-own-hedge-fund-increase-profits-and-reduce-risk-with-etfs-and-opti.html).
16. Eisen's LEAP put writing: sell 1-2.5 year puts only on stocks you would own at strike minus premium, screen to S&P B or better, avoid volatility above roughly 0.75, target at least $500 premium per contract, and spread sales across the calendar [Using Options to Buy Stocks: Build Wealth with Little Risk and No Capital](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/using-options-to-buy-stocks-build-wealth-with-little-risk-and-no-capital.html).
17. Estimate the probability of the strike being hit before selling any option, and compare it against the premium collected; move the strike further out or shorten the window when the trade-off is unfavourable [Selling Options: Examining Risk](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/prob-tables-article.html).

### Adjustment and management

18. Roll a profitable long option to a further strike when you expect continuation, banking part of the gain; roll a losing directional position into a vertical spread to lower the cost basis [Trade Adjustment Playbook: Rolling, Collars, Ratio Spreads, and Repairs](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/george-m-jabbour-the-option-trader-handbook-strategies-and-trade-adjustments--trade-adjustment-playbook.html).
19. Any repair or defensive adjustment should be entered for a net credit or at most a very small debit — a repair that costs meaningful capital is not a repair [The Option Trader Handbook: Strategies and Trade Adjustments](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/george-m-jabbour-the-option-trader-handbook-strategies-and-trade-adjustments.html).
20. Set the exit trigger before entry — a price, a percentage (the worked example uses 15%), or a technical level — and follow it [The Option Trader Handbook: Strategies and Trade Adjustments](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/george-m-jabbour-the-option-trader-handbook-strategies-and-trade-adjustments.html) [Options Primer](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/marketwise-trading-school-options-trading-primer.html).
21. Butterflies and condors are awkward to adjust; treat them as hold-to-expiration positions rather than actively managed ones [The Bible of Options Strategies — Strategy Family Construction Playbook](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/guy-cohen-the-bible-of-options-strategies--strategy-family-playbook.html).
22. Deep in-the-money puts are frequently exercised early, and in-the-money short calls face elevated assignment risk ahead of an ex-dividend date [The Equity Options Strategy Guide](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/the-equity-options-strategy-guide.html).

### Timing and event structures

23. TD Sequential Setup: a price flip initiates the count, then nine consecutive closes below (buy) or above (sell) the close four bars earlier, cancelled by any bar breaking the condition. Applied to buying short-dated in-the-money options rather than the underlying, so risk caps at the premium [TD Sequential for Options](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/demark-tom-demark-on-day-trading-options--td-sequential-options.html).
24. Merger arbitrage: parity = Σ(exchange ratio × current price of each offered security); spread = parity − target price; annualised return = (spread ÷ price) × (12 ÷ months to close). Confirm the proxy and tax-ruling filings actually exist before committing [Merger Arbitrage Spread Calculation and Deal-Risk Checklist](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wyser-pratte-guy-risk-arbitrage--merger-arbitrage-spread-and-deal-risk-checklist.html).
25. Futures spreads: build the spread line by subtracting the short leg's close from the long leg's, adjust contract counts when the legs differ in dollar value, and enter as a single spread order rather than legging in on volatile pairs [Trading Spreads and Seasonals](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joe-ross-trading-spreads-and-seasonals.html).
26. Trade a spread only where a genuine economic relationship exists between the legs, not a statistical correlation [Understanding Spreads](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/edward-dobson-understanding-spreads.html).
27. Single stock futures carry a 20% minimum initial margin versus 50% for cash stock margin — the capital-efficiency case for using them [Single Stock Futures: A Trader's Guide](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/singlestockfutures.html) [How to Trade the New Single Stock Futures](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/jake-bernstein-how-to-trade-the-new-single-stock-futures.html).

### Risk caps

28. Fontanills: risk no more than 5% of trading capital on a single trade and no more than 50% of the account across all open positions, exiting when the underlying breaks 20% off a new high or low [The Options Course: High Profit & Low Stress Trading Methods](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed.html).
29. Trester: under 5% of the option-buying portfolio per position, and no more than 5-10% of total capital in speculative option buying at all [101 Option Trading Secrets](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/101-option-trading-secrets-kenneth-r-trester.html).
30. Hull's naked-write margin formula: the greater of (100% of proceeds + 20% of the stock price − any out-of-the-money amount) or (100% of proceeds + 10% of the stock price), with 15% replacing 20% for broad-based index options [Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives (5th Edition)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hull-options-futures-and-other-derivative-securities-5th-ed.html).
31. Margin naked index sales above the exchange minimum, sized to what would be required if the underlying moved sharply against the position [Profit With Options](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options.html).

## Common mistakes

- Selling naked options on individual stocks, where takeover, earnings and litigation gaps make the tail risk uninsurable [Profit With Options](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options.html) [The Option Trader Handbook: Strategies and Trade Adjustments](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/george-m-jabbour-the-option-trader-handbook-strategies-and-trade-adjustments.html).
- Concluding options are expensive because implied volatility exceeds historical, when some underlyings structurally trade that way [McMillan's Volatility-Based Option Strategy Selection](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options--volatility-based-strategy-selection.html) [Using Volatility in Options Trading, Part 1](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/using-volatility-in-option-tradingpart-1.html).
- Treating delta as a probability and stopping there, without the distinction between finishing in the money and ever touching the strike [Profit With Options](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options.html) [Selling Options: Examining Risk](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/prob-tables-article.html).
- Assuming a delta-neutral position is safe when gamma or vega exposure is large [Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives (5th Edition)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hull-options-futures-and-other-derivative-securities-5th-ed.html) [Options and Options Trading: A Simplified Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/options-and-options-trading-a-simplified-course.html).
- Trusting Black-Scholes for far out-of-the-money strikes, where the normality assumption fails hardest [The Options Edge: Winning the Volatility Game with Options on Futures](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-the-options-edge-winning-the-volatility-game-with-options-on-futures.html) [Back to Basics: Historical Option Pricing Revisited](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/back-to-basics-historical-option-pricing-revisited.html).
- Ignoring commissions and bid-ask cost on three- and four-leg spreads, which can consume a meaningful share of a capped maximum profit [Range-Bound Premium Spread System (Butterfly, Condor, Calendar, Collar)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed--range-bound-premium-spreads.html) [Volatility-Selective Defensive Straddle Writing](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-the-options-edge-winning-the-volatility-game-with-options-on-futures--defensive-straddle-writing.html).
- Freezing rather than acting on a stop or target — Trester's "inertia," and fatal for options because an expiring contract offers no second chance [101 Option Trading Secrets](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/101-option-trading-secrets-kenneth-r-trester.html).
- Adjusting a losing position as a way of avoiding realising the loss, which the adjustment literature itself flags as its own worst failure mode [Trade Adjustment Playbook: Rolling, Collars, Ratio Spreads, and Repairs](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/george-m-jabbour-the-option-trader-handbook-strategies-and-trade-adjustments--trade-adjustment-playbook.html).
- Selling more cash-backed puts than the account can actually cover, because per-contract premium framing hides the exercise obligation [Create Your Own Hedge Fund](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/m-wolfinger-create-your-own-hedge-fund-increase-profits-and-reduce-risk-with-etfs-and-opti.html).
- Legging into a spread in a volatile or thin pair rather than entering it as one order [Trading Spreads and Seasonals](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joe-ross-trading-spreads-and-seasonals.html).
- Reading a seasonal or spread relationship as durable without a fundamental reason behind it [Trading Spreads and Seasonals](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joe-ross-trading-spreads-and-seasonals.html) [Understanding Spreads](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/edward-dobson-understanding-spreads.html).
- Assuming a hedge is complete: basis risk, gamma risk between rebalances and dividend risk all survive an apparently balanced book [Options and Options Trading: A Simplified Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/options-and-options-trading-a-simplified-course.html) [Rubinstein on Derivatives](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/rubinstein-mark-rubinstein-on-derivatives.html).

## Best books for this topic

1. [Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives (5th Edition)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hull-options-futures-and-other-derivative-securities-5th-ed.html) — the standard reference for pricing, hedging and the Greeks. Graduate level, no trading signals, and the pre-2008 edition predates central clearing and benchmark reform.
2. [Profit With Options](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options.html) — the most rigorous practical treatment of when to buy and when to sell premium, built on percentile ranking rather than intuition.
3. [The Options Edge: Winning the Volatility Game with Options on Futures](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-the-options-edge-winning-the-volatility-game-with-options-on-futures.html) — the only genuinely empirical book here, and honest enough to reject its own promising-looking but hindsight-biased results.
4. [The Options Course: High Profit & Low Stress Trading Methods](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed.html) — the best full curriculum on delta-neutral trading, with numbered road maps and formulas for max risk, reward and breakeven on every strategy.

6. [The Option Trader Handbook: Strategies and Trade Adjustments](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/george-m-jabbour-the-option-trader-handbook-strategies-and-trade-adjustments.html) — the only book devoted to what to do after entry, with exact strikes and breakeven math on a consistent example.
7. [Options: Essential Concepts and Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/options-essential-concepts-and-trading-strategies-2nd-edition.html) — the CBOE's own text, and the clearest statement of the opinion-plus-volatility-view selection framework.
8. [Options and Options Trading: A Simplified Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/options-and-options-trading-a-simplified-course.html) — builds pricing intuition from coin tosses, then argues bluntly that no strategy is inherently profitable. Worth reading for the second half alone.
9. [Create Your Own Hedge Fund](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/m-wolfinger-create-your-own-hedge-fund-increase-profits-and-reduce-risk-with-etfs-and-opti.html) — the most mechanical income method in the category, with a fully worked trading year and honest about the period that flattered it.
10. [Risk Arbitrage](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wyser-pratte-guy-risk-arbitrage.html) — event-driven rather than volatility-driven, and the best account of how to price a deal's closing probability instead of the underlying.
11. [Trading Spreads and Seasonals](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joe-ross-trading-spreads-and-seasonals.html) — the practical entry point to futures spreads, with at least one real year-by-year decision table rather than asserted seasonality.
12. [The Options Course Workbook, 2nd Edition](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/george-a-fontanills-the-option-course-with-exercise.html) — the companion workbook to Fontanills's course: nineteen chapters of scored questions and calculation drills, including the ratio-spread and backspread max-profit, max-risk and breakeven formulas worked step by step. Useful precisely because it forces the arithmetic rather than describing it.

## Open debates

- **Whether option writing has an edge at all.** Trester, Wolfinger and Eisen all build strategies on collecting premium [101 Option Trading Secrets](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/101-option-trading-secrets-kenneth-r-trester.html) [Create Your Own Hedge Fund](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/m-wolfinger-create-your-own-hedge-fund-increase-profits-and-reduce-risk-with-etfs-and-opti.html) [Using Options to Buy Stocks: Build Wealth with Little Risk and No Capital](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/using-options-to-buy-stocks-build-wealth-with-little-risk-and-no-capital.html); Gallacher measured it across fifteen commodities and found a payout ratio of 1.00, concluding any edge must be manufactured through defence and selection [The Options Edge: Winning the Volatility Game with Options on Futures](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-the-options-edge-winning-the-volatility-game-with-options-on-futures.html); Ward argues the same point theoretically — under fair pricing all strategies break even before costs [Options and Options Trading: A Simplified Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/options-and-options-trading-a-simplified-course.html).
- **Where the writer's edge comes from, if it exists.** Eisen argues Black-Scholes ignores expected earnings growth and therefore systematically overprices long-dated puts on growing companies [Using Options to Buy Stocks: Build Wealth with Little Risk and No Capital](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/using-options-to-buy-stocks-build-wealth-with-little-risk-and-no-capital.html); McMillan locates the edge in percentile-ranked volatility [McMillan's Volatility-Based Option Strategy Selection](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options--volatility-based-strategy-selection.html); Gallacher's own filter added only 0.902 → 0.884, with most of the improvement coming from the hedge rather than the selection [Volatility-Selective Defensive Straddle Writing](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-the-options-edge-winning-the-volatility-game-with-options-on-futures--defensive-straddle-writing.html).
- **Whether to defend a position or accept the defined loss.** Jabbour's entire book is a menu of adjustments [Trade Adjustment Playbook: Rolling, Collars, Ratio Spreads, and Repairs](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/george-m-jabbour-the-option-trader-handbook-strategies-and-trade-adjustments--trade-adjustment-playbook.html); Cohen says butterflies and condors should simply be held to expiration [The Bible of Options Strategies — Strategy Family Construction Playbook](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/guy-cohen-the-bible-of-options-strategies--strategy-family-playbook.html); and Jabbour's own caveat concedes the framework gives no quantitative trigger for which adjustment to apply, leaving the same forecasting problem the adjustment was meant to sidestep.
- **Whether stops belong on option positions.** DeMark uses none on inexpensive options, treating worthless expiration as the built-in stop [DeMark on Day-Trading Options](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/demark-tom-demark-on-day-trading-options.html); Trester makes a hard stop non-negotiable for writers [101 Option Trading Secrets](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/101-option-trading-secrets-kenneth-r-trester.html); Cohen leaves uncapped-risk positions needing a stop on the underlying because he supplies no other exit [The Bible of Options Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/guy-cohen-the-bible-of-options-strategies.html).
- **Whether delta hedging is riskless.** The textbook derivation says continuous rebalancing eliminates risk and earns the risk-free rate [Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives (5th Edition)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hull-options-futures-and-other-derivative-securities-5th-ed.html); Ward disputes this directly, and both Hull's own portfolio-insurance discussion of October 1987 and the gamma-between-rebalances problem support the objection [Options and Options Trading: A Simplified Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/options-and-options-trading-a-simplified-course.html).
- **How much capital an options position justifies.** Fontanills allows 5% per trade and 50% of the account deployed [The Options Course: High Profit & Low Stress Trading Methods](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed.html); Trester caps speculative buying at 5-10% of total capital [101 Option Trading Secrets](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/101-option-trading-secrets-kenneth-r-trester.html); Cohen and the CBOE text give no account-level rule at all, deferring to a trading plan they never specify [The Bible of Options Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/guy-cohen-the-bible-of-options-strategies.html) [Options: Essential Concepts and Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/options-essential-concepts-and-trading-strategies-2nd-edition.html).
- **What a win rate means for a short-premium strategy.** Fontanills offers a ~75% win rate as a benchmark [The Options Course: High Profit & Low Stress Trading Methods](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed.html) and Trester's simulator produces 85-96% probabilities [The Index Credit Spread (Trester)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/101-option-trading-secrets-kenneth-r-trester--index-credit-spread.html); both describe exactly the negative-skew profile that looks safe by every visible statistic until the rare loss arrives.
