# Day Trading & Scalping: a trading-library study guide

*Opening ranges, gap fades and failed breakouts, all flat by the close — the library's most rule-specific category and the one most damaged by dead market structure.*

## What it is

Day trading closes every position before the session ends, trading intraday structure rather than a multi-day thesis. Its recurring shapes are few: the opening range and its breakout, the gap and whether it fills, the failed breakout that traps a crowd, the pullback after an impulse, and support and resistance drawn from the prior session's high, low and close. Scalping compresses the same logic into minutes or seconds, accepting a smaller edge many more times. What makes this category distinctive is that costs dominate — commissions, spread and slippage are a much larger fraction of a five-tick trade than of a five-week one — and that the trader has no overnight exposure but also no time for a thesis to work out. What makes it hardest to read is that much of the best practitioner writing describes a market structure that no longer exists: SOES, market-maker tiers, pre-decimal eighths, and floor-era order handling.

## Core principles

- Be flat by the close. Overnight gaps are the risk day trading exists to avoid, and the cautionary examples in this category are dramatic [Day Trading Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/kevin-hagerty-day-trading-course.html) [Stock Patterns for Day Trading and Swing Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading.html).
- Define the exit price before entry; knowing exactly where you are wrong is what makes the discipline mechanical rather than reactive [The Logical Trader: Applying a Method to the Madness](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mark-b-fisher-the-logical-trader-applying-a-method-to-the-madness.html) [The Stock Trader: How I Make a Living Trading Stocks](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tony-oz-how-i-make-a-living-trading-stocks.html).
- Loss size is the only variable fully under the trader's control — win rate and average win depend on stock selection and regime [Day Trading Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/kevin-hagerty-day-trading-course.html).
- Classify the regime before choosing a strategy: trending or range-bound decides which family of setups can work at all [Day Trading the Currency Market](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/day-trading-the-currency-market.html) [The Compleat Day Trader, Volume I](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-i.html).
- Instrument selection is part of the method, not a preliminary. Minimum volume, spread and average range filters appear in nearly every workable system here [Wiggle Breakout System](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading--wiggle-breakout-system.html) [Trading on Momentum](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ken-wolff-2002-trading-on-momentum-advanced-techniques-for-high-percentage-day-trading.html) [Day Trading Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/kevin-hagerty-day-trading-course.html).
- A stop must be sized to the instrument's actual average range, not to a comfortable-looking number — Bernstein's central risk lesson [The Compleat Day Trader II](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-ii.html).
- Give the trade the room its own noise requires: Rudd derives a stock-specific "standard wiggle" from five days of its own 5-minute bars rather than using a fixed stop [Stock Patterns for Day Trading and Swing Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading.html).
- Transaction costs are the binding constraint. Wyckoff's "invisible eighth" plus commissions must be recovered before a trade is even breakeven, and Bernstein devotes a chapter to why sold S&P systems fail once fills and commissions are counted [The Day Trader's Bible (My Secrets of Day Trading in Stocks)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wyckoff-richard-d-the-day-trader-s-bible-or-my-secret-in-day-trading-of-stocks-2.html) [The Compleat Day Trader II](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-ii.html).
- Layer independent weak biases rather than relying on one: Williams calls this "stacking the deck" — a pattern plus a calendar bias plus an intermarket relationship [Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/larry-williams-long-term-secrets-to-short-term-trading.html).
- Trade alongside institutional flow rather than trying to out-analyse it; find what is already being accumulated and enter on structure [Day Trading Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/kevin-hagerty-day-trading-course.html) [The Day Trader's Bible (My Secrets of Day Trading in Stocks)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wyckoff-richard-d-the-day-trader-s-bible-or-my-secret-in-day-trading-of-stocks-2.html).
- Round numbers and prior-session extremes matter because stop and take-profit orders cluster there, which is also why they get run [Day Trading the Currency Market](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/day-trading-the-currency-market.html) [The Camarilla Equation](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/camarilla-levels.html).
- Never mix entry rules from one system with exit rules from another; treat a chosen system as a closed contract [The Compleat Day Trader II](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-ii.html).
- Keep a written record of every trade and of what each pattern group is currently doing — Wolff's tracking diary, Oz's full trade log, Lien's journal [Trading on Momentum](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ken-wolff-2002-trading-on-momentum-advanced-techniques-for-high-percentage-day-trading.html) [The Stock Trader: How I Make a Living Trading Stocks](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tony-oz-how-i-make-a-living-trading-stocks.html) [Day Trading the Currency Market](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/day-trading-the-currency-market.html).
- Volatility contraction precedes expansion intraday as it does daily; NR7 and tight consolidation are the standard preconditions [Intra-Day Trading Strategies: Proven Steps to Short-Term Trading Profits](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/jeff-cooper-intra-day-trading-strategies-proven-steps.html) [Stock Patterns for Day Trading and Swing Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading.html).
- Trade larger when the market is active and smaller when it is quiet — the opposite of what fear suggests [The Logical Trader: Applying a Method to the Madness](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mark-b-fisher-the-logical-trader-applying-a-method-to-the-madness.html).
- Undercapitalisation and overtrading, not bad signals, are the named causes of failure [The Day Trader's Bible (My Secrets of Day Trading in Stocks)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wyckoff-richard-d-the-day-trader-s-bible-or-my-secret-in-day-trading-of-stocks-2.html) [The Compleat Day Trader, Volume I](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-i.html).

## Concrete rules and setups

### Opening range

1. CTOD: build a range from the first two hours' 5-minute *closing* prices, then buy when a 5-minute close prints above the highest of them or sell below the lowest, with a 5-minute closing stop at the opposite level that ratchets in each hour. Bernstein invites testing 1- and 1.5-hour variants [Critical Time of Day (CTOD)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-i--ctod-critical-time-of-day.html).
2. 30-Minute Breakout: mark the first half-hour's high and low, then trade any subsequent half-hour close exceeding that range by a market-specific tick count, with a fail-safe stop or the opposite signal as the exit and a market-on-close finish [The 30-Minute Breakout (30MBO)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-ii--30-minute-breakout.html).
3. ACD: define the opening range (5 minutes for crude, 20 for natural gas and the S&P in Fisher's examples), then set Point A at an instrument-specific tick distance beyond it — 7-8 ticks crude, 15 natural gas. Price must hold at or beyond A for half the opening-range duration to confirm, and only one A confirms per day. Point B, the opposite end of the range, is the stop; an A that also clears the daily pivot range earns a tighter stop at the pivot's far edge — 10 ticks of risk instead of 40 in the book's example [The ACD Method](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mark-b-fisher-the-logical-trader-applying-a-method-to-the-madness--acd-method.html).
4. Opening Reversal: the stock opens and sells off no more than 25% (33% maximum) of its typical daily range, reverses back through the opening price, and is bought an eighth above that open — best in high-relative-strength names as the broad market opens lower [Day Trading Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/kevin-hagerty-day-trading-course.html).
5. Volatility open-range breakout: measure the highest high minus lowest low of the past three days. If today's close is below yesterday's, tomorrow is "buy easier" — buy stop at open + 50% of that measure, sell stop at open − 100%; reverse the percentages on a "sell easier" day [Short-Term Volatility Based Open Range Breakout System](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/j-r-hill-g-pruitt-and-l-hill-the-ultimate-trading-guide--volatility-breakout-system.html).

### Gaps and traps

6. Oops!: today's open is below the prior day's low (rare, since price usually opens inside the prior range); buy when price rallies back to that low. Mirror for sells. Backtested with a $2,000 S&P stop exiting on the next open, and $1,800 in bonds [Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading: The Oops! Pattern](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/larry-williams-long-term-secrets-to-short-term-trading--oops-pattern.html).
7. Bernstein's gap trade: after a gap-down open, a buy stop two ticks above the previous day's low, with a delayed variant that waits through the first trading hour to avoid chasing a gap that never fills [Gap Trading System (GO / Delayed GDO)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-i--gap-trading-system.html).
8. 80-20's: yesterday opened in the top 20% of its range and closed in the bottom 20%; today trades 5-15 ticks below yesterday's low; place the entry buy stop at yesterday's low with the protective stop near today's extreme, closed same day [80-20's](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/street-smarts-laurence-connors--80-20s.html).
9. Gap-fade evidence: on 1998-2008 E-mini data, fading gaps larger than one point with no stop and closing at day's end produced a 72.6% win rate but a profit factor of only 1.08. Smaller gaps fill far more often — 84.5% for gaps under 0.2% of the index, falling to roughly 49-59% for gaps over 1% [Understanding Gaps](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/andrews-scott-understanding-gaps.html).
10. Trap Door: in a high-relative-strength S&P 500 name with at least a 1½-2 point average daily range trading above its 50- and 200-day EMAs, the stock opens below the prior close and trades down at least a point, the second bar makes a lower high and lower low, and the entry comes on the reversal [The Trap Door Setup](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/kevin-hagerty-day-trading-course--trap-door.html).

### Consolidation and pullback

11. Wiggle breakout: screen for at least 100,000 average daily shares and adequate quote depth, estimate the stock's standard wiggle from five days of 5-minute bars, wait for several bars consolidating tightly against a level, buy the break, use a ¼-point initial stop, and convert to a wiggle trailing stop once open profit reaches roughly ⅜ point [Wiggle Breakout System](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading--wiggle-breakout-system.html).
12. Plus One Minus Two: after a new three-day high, wait for a two-period pullback before entering with the original impulse [Intra-Day Trading Strategies: Proven Steps to Short-Term Trading Profits](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/jeff-cooper-intra-day-trading-strategies-proven-steps.html).
13. Oz's system: support and resistance only, with a 3:1 reward-to-risk filter applied before every entry, a maintained watch list of 24-40 liquid names above roughly $50, and scan formulas requiring volume at 1.5-1.7× the 20-day average with a net change of at least ⅝ point [Pullback Swing Trade Scan and Support/Resistance Entries (Full System)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tony-oz-how-i-make-a-living-trading-stocks--pullback-swing-trade-scan.html).
14. Greatest Swing Value: compute the 4-day average open-to-high distance, then enter at the open plus 180% of it, with a $1,600 bond stop and a bailout exit on the first profitable opening after two days [Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading: Greatest Swing Value](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/larry-williams-long-term-secrets-to-short-term-trading--greatest-swing-value.html).

### Levels and cycles

15. Camarilla: eight levels from the prior day's OHLC, four above and four below the close. L3 levels are the primary reversal levels and L4 serves as both the stop for a reversal trade and the breakout level; how the market opens relative to the L3 band decides whether to fade or to trade the break [The Camarilla Equation](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/camarilla-levels.html).
16. LSS three-day cycle: Buy Day (low first, then rally), Sell Day (trades near the prior high), Short Sell Day (high first, then decline), with envelopes rather than single prices, faded when the prior day's oscillator read above 70% and bought below 30% [LSS - An Introduction to the 3-Day Cycle Method](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lss-3-day-cycle-method.html).
17. Pivot levels: computed from the prior session's high, low and close, with an open-based variation preferred on gap days [Pivot Points](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/pivots.html) [One More Zero: Forex Intraday Pivots Trading System](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/forex-intraday-pivots-trading-system-complete-system.html).
18. TDW and TDM filters: weekday and trading-day-of-month biases used as trade filters rather than signals — S&P Mondays closing up 57% of the time at an average of +$109, bond Tuesdays 55% at +$53 [Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading: TDW/TDM Calendar Filters](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/larry-williams-long-term-secrets-to-short-term-trading--tdw-tdm-filters.html).

### Forex intraday

19. Fading the double zeros: find a pair well below its intraday 20-period SMA on a 10- or 15-minute chart, enter long no more than 10 pips below the round number, stop no more than 20 pips below entry, close half at 2R and move the remainder to breakeven [Fading the Double Zeros](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/day-trading-the-currency-market--fading-the-double-zeros.html).
20. Waiting for the real deal: fade the initial GBP/USD move at the London open as a stop-hunt, entering only once the early range breaks in the opposite direction [Waiting for the Real Deal](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/day-trading-the-currency-market--waiting-for-the-real-deal.html).
21. Inside day breakout: enter 10 pips beyond the high or low of the most recent of two or more consecutive inside days, with a stop-and-reverse order on the other side [Inside Day Breakout Play](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/day-trading-the-currency-market--inside-day-breakout.html).
22. Regime classification for FX: ADX above 25 for trending and below 20 for ranging, cross-checked against Bollinger width, moving-average posture and options risk reversals [Day Trading the Currency Market](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/day-trading-the-currency-market.html).

### Momentum and tape

23. Wolff's momentum filters: the stock must be up or down 20%+ from the prior close on news (predictability degrades below 20%), with at least 50,000 pre-market shares across multiple prints, roughly five participants near the inside price, and a spread no wider than 0.15 point [Momentum Day-Trading Setups (Gainers, Dumpers, Gap Plays, Breakouts)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ken-wolff-2002-trading-on-momentum-advanced-techniques-for-high-percentage-day-trading--momentum-day-trading-setups.html).
24. Wolff's sizing: pick the stop distance first (0.15-0.40 point), then size the position so the dollar loss at that stop is an acceptable fraction of equity — his example risks $250 on a $50,000 account, or 0.5% [Trading on Momentum](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ken-wolff-2002-trading-on-momentum-advanced-techniques-for-high-percentage-day-trading.html).
25. Wyckoff's tape rules: start with small lots and increase only after demonstrated success; take roughly a two-point maximum loss as the fallback stop, or place it an eighth to a quarter beyond the nearest point of resistance where one exists; never widen a stop once placed [The Tape Reading Method](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wyckoff-richard-d-the-day-trader-s-bible-or-my-secret-in-day-trading-of-stocks-2--tape-reading-method.html).
26. Scalping discipline: exit if the setup has not resolved within roughly 30 seconds to 3 minutes, on the premise that a scalp that has not worked immediately has already failed [Scalp Trading Methods: Six High-Probability Setups from the Pit](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lbr-scalp-setups.html).

### Exits and risk

27. Williams's three universal exits: always carry a hard dollar stop; take the first profitable opening (the bailout exit) even for a tick; and reverse immediately on an opposite signal rather than waiting [Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/larry-williams-long-term-secrets-to-short-term-trading.html).
28. Lien's caps: at least 2:1 reward to risk on every trade, and no more than 2% of account equity at risk on any one [Day Trading the Currency Market](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/day-trading-the-currency-market.html).
29. Oz's account rules: fixed risk capital, no single non-index position above roughly $25,000-30,000, profits swept weekly rather than compounded, and index products exempt from the concentration cap [The Stock Trader: How I Make a Living Trading Stocks](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tony-oz-how-i-make-a-living-trading-stocks.html).
30. Fisher's adaptive sizing: increase size on a hot streak, cut it by a third to two-thirds during a slump, and reset limits to baseline at the start of each month [The Logical Trader: Applying a Method to the Madness](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mark-b-fisher-the-logical-trader-applying-a-method-to-the-madness.html).

## Common mistakes

- Sizing the stop to what feels affordable rather than to the instrument's real range, which guarantees being stopped by ordinary noise [The Compleat Day Trader II](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-ii.html) [Stock Patterns for Day Trading and Swing Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading.html).
- Assembling a hybrid from one system's entries and another's exits [The Compleat Day Trader II](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-ii.html).
- Pyramiding the wrong way round — adding larger rather than smaller size as the trade moves favourably [The Compleat Day Trader II](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-ii.html).
- Holding a day trade overnight to avoid taking the loss, which reintroduces exactly the gap risk the style exists to avoid [Day Trading Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/kevin-hagerty-day-trading-course.html) [Stock Patterns for Day Trading and Swing Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading.html).
- Carrying a buy-and-hold mindset into an intraday position — Wolff names "stocks always come back" as the primary cause of blown accounts [Trading on Momentum](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ken-wolff-2002-trading-on-momentum-advanced-techniques-for-high-percentage-day-trading.html).
- Chasing a gap in the first minutes rather than waiting for the delayed confirmation [Gap Trading System (GO / Delayed GDO)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-i--gap-trading-system.html).
- Reading a high gap-fade win rate as an edge when the profit factor is 1.08 — the losses are simply larger than the wins [Understanding Gaps](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/andrews-scott-understanding-gaps.html).
- Trading illiquid or wide-spread instruments, where the spread alone exceeds the edge [Trading on Momentum](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ken-wolff-2002-trading-on-momentum-advanced-techniques-for-high-percentage-day-trading.html) [Wiggle Breakout System](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading--wiggle-breakout-system.html).
- Widening a stop after it is placed [The Day Trader's Bible (My Secrets of Day Trading in Stocks)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wyckoff-richard-d-the-day-trader-s-bible-or-my-secret-in-day-trading-of-stocks-2.html).
- Trading larger when quiet and smaller when active, out of fear rather than opportunity [The Logical Trader: Applying a Method to the Madness](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mark-b-fisher-the-logical-trader-applying-a-method-to-the-madness.html).
- Overtrading and undercapitalisation, the two failure modes named directly rather than inferred [The Day Trader's Bible (My Secrets of Day Trading in Stocks)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wyckoff-richard-d-the-day-trader-s-bible-or-my-secret-in-day-trading-of-stocks-2.html).
- Applying pre-decimal, market-maker-era parameters — eighths, tier sizes, SOES mechanics — to modern electronic order books without re-deriving them [Stock Patterns for Day Trading and Swing Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading.html) [Electronic Trading Guide for NASDAQ Level 2](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/online-trading-academy-electronic-trading-guide-for-nasdaq-l2-9908-2.html).

1. [Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/larry-williams-long-term-secrets-to-short-term-trading.html) — every pattern backed by dated, falsifiable backtest statistics, and the only book here that publishes its own sample periods and trade counts.

[The Logical Trader: Applying a Method to the Madness](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mark-b-fisher-the-logical-trader-applying-a-method-to-the-madness.html) — ACD is the most completely specified opening-range framework in the library, with every entry paired to a defined stop.

6. [Day Trading Course](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/kevin-hagerty-day-trading-course.html) — the Traders Equation is the clearest statement of why loss size is the trader's only real lever, and the setups carry explicit filters.
7. [Stock Patterns for Day Trading and Swing Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading.html) — the wiggle concept is a genuinely useful idea: derive the stop from the instrument's own pullback behaviour rather than from a constant.
8. [The Day Trader's Bible (My Secrets of Day Trading in Stocks)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wyckoff-richard-d-the-day-trader-s-bible-or-my-secret-in-day-trading-of-stocks-2.html) — a century old and still the clearest account of reading order flow and placing stops at structure rather than at a distance.
9. [Trading on Momentum](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ken-wolff-2002-trading-on-momentum-advanced-techniques-for-high-percentage-day-trading.html) — the tracking-diary methodology and the stop-loss chapters survive their dated microstructure entirely.
10. [Understanding Gaps](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/andrews-scott-understanding-gaps.html) — the rare source that measures a popular intraday setup across a decade and reports an unflattering profit factor.
11. [Scalp Trading Methods: Six High-Probability Setups from the Pit](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lbr-scalp-setups.html) — six concrete scalping setups with time-based exits, useful as a template even where the reported win rates are one week of data.

## Open debates

- **Fade the gap or trade it.** Williams's Oops! and Connors and Raschke's 80-20's both fade the extreme [Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading: The Oops! Pattern](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/larry-williams-long-term-secrets-to-short-term-trading--oops-pattern.html) [80-20's](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/street-smarts-laurence-connors--80-20s.html); Andrews's decade of E-mini data shows the fade wins often but barely profits, with the edge concentrated in the smallest gaps [Understanding Gaps](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/andrews-scott-understanding-gaps.html); Bernstein's delayed variant tries to have both by waiting an hour [Gap Trading System (GO / Delayed GDO)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-i--gap-trading-system.html).
- **Whether the opening range should be measured on closes or on ticks.** Bernstein's CTOD deliberately uses 5-minute closing prices to avoid one-tick false breaks [Critical Time of Day (CTOD)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-i--ctod-critical-time-of-day.html); Fisher's ACD uses the intraday high and low but requires price to hold beyond the A value for half the range duration to achieve the same filtering [The ACD Method](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mark-b-fisher-the-logical-trader-applying-a-method-to-the-madness--acd-method.html).
- **Fixed stop or instrument-derived stop.** Rudd derives the wiggle from the stock's own history [Stock Patterns for Day Trading and Swing Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading.html) and Wyckoff places stops at points of resistance [The Day Trader's Bible (My Secrets of Day Trading in Stocks)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wyckoff-richard-d-the-day-trader-s-bible-or-my-secret-in-day-trading-of-stocks-2.html); Williams uses hard dollar stops per market and Lien fixed pip stops [Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/larry-williams-long-term-secrets-to-short-term-trading.html) [Fading the Double Zeros](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/day-trading-the-currency-market--fading-the-double-zeros.html).
- **Whether size should vary with recent performance.** Fisher cuts a slumping trader's size by up to two-thirds and expands a hot one's [The Logical Trader: Applying a Method to the Madness](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mark-b-fisher-the-logical-trader-applying-a-method-to-the-madness.html); the fixed-fraction tradition treats varying size by recent results as precisely the bias to eliminate [Trading on Momentum](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ken-wolff-2002-trading-on-momentum-advanced-techniques-for-high-percentage-day-trading.html) [Day Trading the Currency Market](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/day-trading-the-currency-market.html).
- **Whether these setups can be specified at all.** Cooper's book is named setups on annotated charts with no stated entry trigger, stop or target anywhere [Intra-Day Trading Strategies: Proven Steps to Short-Term Trading Profits](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/jeff-cooper-intra-day-trading-strategies-proven-steps.html); Oz, Lien, Fisher and Williams state every parameter [The Stock Trader: How I Make a Living Trading Stocks](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tony-oz-how-i-make-a-living-trading-stocks.html) [Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/larry-williams-long-term-secrets-to-short-term-trading.html). Several widely circulated systems in this category defer their actual rules to a paid product [Spread Trade to Win](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/spread-trade-to-win.html).
- **Whether the evidence survives its own market.** Williams's results are in-sample on a handful of liquid futures from 1977-1998 with no out-of-sample test, and he flags the curve-fitting risk himself [Long-Term Secrets to Short-Term Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/larry-williams-long-term-secrets-to-short-term-trading.html); Oz's 116 trades cover four weeks of one extraordinary episode [The Stock Trader: How I Make a Living Trading Stocks](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tony-oz-how-i-make-a-living-trading-stocks.html); the scalping win rates come from a single week [Scalp Trading Methods: Six High-Probability Setups from the Pit](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lbr-scalp-setups.html). The claimed 4-in-5 reversal rate off Camarilla L3 carries no sample at all [The Camarilla Equation](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/camarilla-levels.html).
- **Whether the microstructure edges still exist.** Lien's stop-hunting and round-number mechanics describe a quote-driven interbank market since largely replaced [Day Trading the Currency Market](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/day-trading-the-currency-market.html); Rudd's and Wolff's market-maker reading assumes a Nasdaq structure eliminated by decimalisation and later reforms [Stock Patterns for Day Trading and Swing Trading](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barry-rudd-stock-patterns-for-day-trading-and-swing-trading.html) [Trading on Momentum](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ken-wolff-2002-trading-on-momentum-advanced-techniques-for-high-percentage-day-trading.html); nothing in this category re-tests those claims against modern order books.
