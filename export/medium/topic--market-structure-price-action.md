# Market Structure & Price Action: a trading-library study guide

*Reading the market from price alone — auction theory, point-and-figure, swings and pivots — plus the exchange plumbing that determines what a fill actually costs.*

## What it is

This category covers two things that usually get separated but belong together. The first is price action: methods that derive structure from price alone — trends as sequences of peaks and troughs, support and resistance, swing pivots, point-and-figure columns, Market Profile distributions — without an indicator layer between the trader and the chart. The second is market structure in the literal sense: how exchanges, market makers, ECNs and order types work, and therefore why a market order fills where it does. The connection is that most price-action rules are claims about where other participants have orders. A level matters because it is watched; a stop gets hit because it sits where stops cluster. Knowing how orders reach the book is part of knowing what the chart is showing.

## Core principles

- A trend is a sequence of rising peaks and rising troughs (or falling ones), and a reversal is not confirmed until both sequences change — a break in only one is a half-signal [Peaks and Troughs](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/peaks-and-troughs-pring.html) [Charting Made Easy](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/john-j-murphy-charting-made-easy.html).
- Broken resistance becomes support and vice versa, because traders caught on the wrong side cover near their old entry while new entrants add on the retest [An Introduction to Charting](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/introduction-to-charting.html) [Charting Made Easy](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/john-j-murphy-charting-made-easy.html).
- A trendline drawn through two points is only a candidate; a third touch that holds is what validates it [Charting Made Easy](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/john-j-murphy-charting-made-easy.html) [Common $ense Commodities: A Common Sense Approach to Trading Commodities](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/common-sense-commodities-a-common-sense-approach-to-trading-commodities.html).
- Establish trend on the higher timeframe first and use the lower one only to time the entry [An Introduction to Charting](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/introduction-to-charting.html) [Multiple Patterns, Multiple Time Frames](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mulitple-patterns-and-time-periods.html).
- Markets spend most of their time not trending — estimates in this category range from roughly 70% to 80% of the time in congestion — so regime classification comes before tool selection [The Triangle Trading Method](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/triangletradingmethod.html) [Congestion Markets: Estimating a Breakout Target](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/congestion-markets-estimating-a-breakout-target.html) [Trend versus No Trend](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/trend-vs-no-trend.html).
- Filtering noise requires an explicit threshold. Point-and-figure uses box size and a three-box reversal; kagi and renko use a turnaround amount or brick height; three-line break lets the market itself set the reversal by requiring a break of three prior lines [Point-and-Figure Charting and Bullish Percent Timing System](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tom-dorsey-s-trading-tips-a-playbook-for-stock-market-success--point-and-figure-and-bullish-percent-system.html) [Kagi Charts](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/beyond-candlesticks-steve-nison--kagi-chart.html) [Three-Line Break Charts](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/beyond-candlesticks-steve-nison--three-line-break-chart.html).
- Discarding time makes structure legible. Point-and-figure, kagi, renko and three-line break all drop the time axis or plot only on new extremes, which is what makes support and resistance visually obvious [The Forex Chartist Companion](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/the-forex-chartist-companion.html) [Renko Charts](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/beyond-candlesticks-steve-nison--renko-chart.html).
- Price is the byproduct of a two-sided auction between a short-term participant who needs a fair price now and a longer-term one who can wait for an advantageous price; volume distributed across price reveals which is active [A Six-Part Study Guide to Market Profile](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/cbot-a-six-part-study-guide-to-market-profile.html).
- Value has a measurable definition: the price range containing about 70% of a session's volume, roughly one standard deviation of the distribution [Market Profile — Construction and Reading Rules](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/cbot-a-six-part-study-guide-to-market-profile--market-profile-construction-and-reading.html).
- Pattern rules can be made explicit enough to remove most discretion — bar counts, tick tolerances, minimum durations — which is what separates Ross's Law of Charts from ordinary chart reading [The Law of Charts](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joe-ross-how-to-spot-a-trend.html) [The Triangle Trading Method](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/triangletradingmethod.html).
- Confirmation across independent factors matters more than the precision of any one: Murphy's closing principle is that a signal is worth what the number of agreeing factors makes it worth [Charting Made Easy](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/john-j-murphy-charting-made-easy.html).
- Order type determines which risk you accept: a market order guarantees a fill but not a price, a limit order the reverse, and a triggered stop becomes a market order [Market Mechanics: A Guide to U.S. Stock Markets](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/nasdaq-education-foundation-a-guide-to-u-s-stock-markets-eng.html) [How the Stock Market Works](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/how-the-stock-market-works.html).
- Clearing and settlement, not the trade itself, account for most of the value chain in an exchange group — the part traders never see [From Trading Floor to Virtual Marketplace](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/deutsche-borse-group-from-trading-floor-to-virtual-marketplace.html).
- Structure changes what the old rules mean: wider intraday ranges, commodity-like institutional turnover and derivatives-driven pricing make previously stable patterns less reliable [The New Laws of the Stock Market Jungle](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/michael-panzner-the-new-laws-of-the-stock-market-jungle.html).
- Almost none of the pattern claims here are tested; where they are, results are sobering — double and triple tops in currencies come out close to a coin flip [The Forex Chartist Companion](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/the-forex-chartist-companion.html).

## Concrete rules and setups

### Trend and swing structure

1. Label an uptrend while peaks and troughs both rise; a failed new high is an alert, but the reversal is confirmed only when price also breaks the previous trough [Peaks and Troughs](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/peaks-and-troughs-pring.html).
2. Treat a reaction as meaningful when it retraces roughly one-third to two-thirds of the prior move, or when a shallower range lasts one-third to two-thirds of the prior move's duration [Peaks and Troughs](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/peaks-and-troughs-pring.html). Murphy gives the same bands: a third minimum, 50% most common, two-thirds maximum consistent with the trend resuming [Charting Made Easy](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/john-j-murphy-charting-made-easy.html).
3. Ross's 1-2-3: point 1 is the prior swing extreme, point 2 requires a full correction, point 3 requires another. The formation is void the moment any bar reaches or passes point 1 [The Law of Charts](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joe-ross-how-to-spot-a-trend.html).
4. Ross's ledge: four to ten bars containing two matching highs and two matching lows, each pair separated by at least one bar, matched within three minimum ticks, forming inside an existing trend [The Law of Charts](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joe-ross-how-to-spot-a-trend.html).
5. Ross's sideways taxonomy: ledges up to 10 bars, congestion of 11-20 bars, trading ranges of 21 or more, with range breakouts usually falling around bars 21-29 [Trading Manual: Appendices 269-320](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joerosstrading-manual-appendices-269-320.html).
6. Ross Hook: the first correction after a breakout of a 1-2-3, ledge, congestion or trading range. Enter on the Hook's breakout; the Trader's Trick enters earlier, on a violation of the high of the first correction bar, but only if the distance to the Hook covers costs and leaves profit — abandon the attempt after three correction bars [Trading Manual: Appendices 269-320](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joerosstrading-manual-appendices-269-320.html).
7. The 1-2-3 as a standalone signal: enter on a break of the horizontal line at the point-2 level, placed slightly inside it rather than exactly on it, to avoid the clustered breakout orders [1-2-3 Trading Signal](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/e123system.html).
8. Wolfe Wave: require points 1-4 to exist, with point 3 below point 1 and point 4 above it for a buy. Project point 5 with the 1-3 line, enter on a reversal back across it, stop beyond the point-5 extreme, and use the 1-4 line as the estimated price at arrival [Wolfe Waves](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wolfe-waves.html).

### Point-and-figure

9. Box size scale (Dorsey Wright): ¼ point from $0-5, ½ point from $5-20, 1 point from $20-100, 2 points from $100-200, 4 points above $200, with a three-box reversal to start a new column [Point-and-Figure Charting and Bullish Percent Timing System](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tom-dorsey-s-trading-tips-a-playbook-for-stock-market-success--point-and-figure-and-bullish-percent-system.html).
10. Forex parameters: one pip per box for major pairs, two to five for wide-spread crosses, with a three-box reversal as the default [Point-and-Figure Breakout Trading (Full System)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/the-forex-chartist-companion--point-and-figure-breakout-trading.html).
11. Trendline signal: buy when a new column of Xs crosses above the declining line drawn along the downtrend's highs, entering at the first X above the prior X-column high; mirror for sells [Point-and-Figure Breakout Trading (Full System)](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/the-forex-chartist-companion--point-and-figure-breakout-trading.html).
12. Bullish percent: the share of a universe on a P&F buy signal, plotted 0-100%. Above 70% is high risk, below 30% low risk; the best buys come from a reversal up out of below 30%, the best sells from a reversal down through 70% [Point-and-Figure Charting and Bullish Percent Timing System](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tom-dorsey-s-trading-tips-a-playbook-for-stock-market-success--point-and-figure-and-bullish-percent-system.html).
13. Dorsey's four-step sequence: market bullish percent, then sector bullish percent and relative strength, then fundamentals, then the individual chart — buying only above the bullish support line and requiring at least 2:1 reward to risk [Tom Dorsey's Trading Tips: A Playbook for Stock Market Success](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tom-dorsey-s-trading-tips-a-playbook-for-stock-market-success.html).
14. Dorsey's sizing and profit-taking: risk exactly 1% of equity per position, with share count derived from the stop distance (a $1,000,000 account, $49 entry, $45 stop gives 2,500 shares), then sell a third at +30%, a third at +50%, and trail the rest [Tom Dorsey's Trading Tips: A Playbook for Stock Market Success](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tom-dorsey-s-trading-tips-a-playbook-for-stock-market-success.html).

### Market Profile

15. Build the profile by assigning each time bracket a letter and recording it at every price traded in that bracket; stacked letters form the distribution [Market Profile — Construction and Reading Rules](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/cbot-a-six-part-study-guide-to-market-profile--market-profile-construction-and-reading.html).
16. The initial balance is the range while only the short-term participant is active, conventionally the first hour, and needs re-tuning for near-24-hour electronic sessions [Market Profile — Construction and Reading Rules](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/cbot-a-six-part-study-guide-to-market-profile--market-profile-construction-and-reading.html).
17. Classify the day by range extension against that balance: 85% of range inside it is a Normal day; extension up to about 2× in one direction is a Normal Variation; well beyond 2× with a close on the extreme is a Trend day; extension both ways is a Neutral day and carries the highest reversal risk [Market Profile — Construction and Reading Rules](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/cbot-a-six-part-study-guide-to-market-profile--market-profile-construction-and-reading.html).
18. Compute the value area by starting at the highest-volume price and repeatedly adding whichever adjacent price carries more volume until 70% of session volume is captured [A Six-Part Study Guide to Market Profile](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/cbot-a-six-part-study-guide-to-market-profile.html).

### Breakouts, congestion and targets

19. Congestion targets: the measured move adds or subtracts the range's height from its boundary; the time-based method subtracts the number of days the congestion took to form; the Andrews method divides the range into eighths and multiplies the range by the count of zone re-entries [Congestion Markets: Estimating a Breakout Target](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/congestion-markets-estimating-a-breakout-target.html).
20. Symmetrical triangle validity: at least 28 days, two reference points on each trendline, and an apex falling in the centre third of the base [The Triangle Trading Method](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/triangletradingmethod.html).
21. Channel projection: draw the trendline, duplicate it to the opposite extreme, then measure the perpendicular width and project it from a retracement point to estimate where a pullback halts, or from a breakout as a target [Master the Channel Projection](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/channeltrading.html).
22. Andrews' Pitchfork: take three reversal points A, B and C, draw A-B, run the median line from C through its midpoint, and project parallels from A and B [Andrews’ Pitchfork](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/andrews-pitchfork.html).
23. Gap screen: require today's low above yesterday's high, plus abnormal volume, prior low volatility and dollar volume of at least $250,000; treat the gap's base as support and reassess if it is penetrated [Using Upward Gaps to Predict Upward Trends](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/using-up-gaps-to-anticipate-upward-price-moves.html).
24. Gimmee bar: with 20-bar, 2σ Bollinger Bands in a range, sell one tick below the low of a bearish reversal bar after an upper-band touch (or buy one tick above a bullish one at the lower band); stand aside if the extreme is near the middle average, the bar is unusually large, or the next bar gaps beyond it [Gimmee Bars](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/gimmibar.html).
25. Regime filter: mark daily and weekly trendlines first, then treat ADX above 25 as trending and below 20 as not, confirming a DI crossover only when price exceeds that period's high or low [Trend versus No Trend](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/trend-vs-no-trend.html).
26. Stops belong at odd price points rather than round numbers, which price tends to touch before turning [Common $ense Commodities: A Common Sense Approach to Trading Commodities](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/common-sense-commodities-a-common-sense-approach-to-trading-commodities.html).

## Common mistakes

- Calling a reversal on a failed high alone, before the opposite swing has actually broken [Peaks and Troughs](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/peaks-and-troughs-pring.html).
- Reading the daily chart in isolation without establishing the higher-timeframe trend first [An Introduction to Charting](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/introduction-to-charting.html) [Multiple Patterns, Multiple Time Frames](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mulitple-patterns-and-time-periods.html).
- Applying trend-following tools in a range or oscillators in a trend, instead of classifying the regime first [Trend versus No Trend](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/trend-vs-no-trend.html) [The Triangle Trading Method](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/triangletradingmethod.html).
- Placing stops at round numbers where everyone else's sit [Common $ense Commodities: A Common Sense Approach to Trading Commodities](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/common-sense-commodities-a-common-sense-approach-to-trading-commodities.html) [1-2-3 Trading Signal](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/e123system.html).
- Adding to winners in a way that raises average risk — pyramiding rather than keeping weight on the original entry [Common $ense Commodities: A Common Sense Approach to Trading Commodities](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/common-sense-commodities-a-common-sense-approach-to-trading-commodities.html).
- Chasing a breakout that was missed instead of waiting for the next setup [The Triangle Trading Method](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/triangletradingmethod.html).
- Treating a P&F double or triple top as an edge when the measured continuation rate is near a coin flip [The Forex Chartist Companion](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/the-forex-chartist-companion.html).
- Trusting a pattern-recognition claim with no target, invalidation level or failure discussion attached [Chart Patterns: Technical Trend Analysis & Chart Pattern Recognition](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/metastock-chart-patterns-tutorial.html) [The Fractal's Edge Basic User's Guide](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fractal.html).
- Assuming a seasonal or technical regularity still holds after it became widely known [The New Laws of the Stock Market Jungle](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/michael-panzner-the-new-laws-of-the-stock-market-jungle.html).
- Using market orders in fast or thin conditions, where a triggered stop can fill far past its level [How the Stock Market Works](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/how-the-stock-market-works.html) [Market Mechanics: A Guide to U.S. Stock Markets](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/nasdaq-education-foundation-a-guide-to-u-s-stock-markets-eng.html).
- Mistaking a pattern's mathematical vocabulary — fractals, chaos, wavelets — for evidence that it has been tested [The Fractal's Edge Basic User's Guide](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fractal.html).

## Best books for this topic

1. [A Six-Part Study Guide to Market Profile](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/cbot-a-six-part-study-guide-to-market-profile.html) — the exchange's own account of auction theory, day types and the value area; the most conceptually complete framework here, though it deliberately gives no trade signals.
2. [Tom Dorsey's Trading Tips: A Playbook for Stock Market Success](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tom-dorsey-s-trading-tips-a-playbook-for-stock-market-success.html) — point-and-figure with actual numbers: box scales, bullish percent thresholds, worked position sizing at 1% risk and a specific profit-taking schedule.
3. [Charting Made Easy](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/john-j-murphy-charting-made-easy.html) — the clearest short primer on trends, patterns, targets and retracement bands. Conventional practice stated well, with no testing.
4. [The Forex Chartist Companion](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/the-forex-chartist-companion.html) — unusual for actually measuring its claims against a large tick database, and candid when the numbers are unimpressive. A charting reference, not a system.
5. [The Law of Charts](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joe-ross-how-to-spot-a-trend.html) and [Trading Manual: Appendices 269-320](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joerosstrading-manual-appendices-269-320.html) — the most rigorously specified price-action rules in the library, with bar counts and tick tolerances that remove most of the usual ambiguity.
6. [Peaks and Troughs](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/peaks-and-troughs-pring.html) — a few pages, and the cleanest statement of what actually constitutes a confirmed trend change.
7. [Market Mechanics: A Guide to U.S. Stock Markets](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/nasdaq-education-foundation-a-guide-to-u-s-stock-markets-eng.html) — the best explanation of how an order really gets filled, written by an academic rather than a vendor.
8. [Profit From Prices](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/profitfromprices.html) — rule-based signals built from raw OHLC and volume, with explicit conditions and stops rather than named patterns.
9. [The New Laws of the Stock Market Jungle](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/michael-panzner-the-new-laws-of-the-stock-market-jungle.html) — useful counterweight: a practitioner's argument for why the older structural assumptions behind many of these methods no longer hold.
10. [Wolfe Waves](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wolfe-waves.html) — a compact, well-specified geometric pattern with entry, stop and projected target defined together, which most pattern sources omit.

## Open debates

- **Whether technical structure works because it forecasts or because it is watched.** The reflexive argument is that enough participants act on the same levels to make the collective reaction the tradable event, with "winning" traders fading the moves the crowd's stops create [Why Does Technical Analysis Work?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/doestawork.html). The auction-theory view instead grounds levels in where volume actually transacted, independent of who is watching [A Six-Part Study Guide to Market Profile](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/cbot-a-six-part-study-guide-to-market-profile.html).
- **How much discretion a pattern method should retain.** Ross reduces pattern recognition to bar counts and tick tolerances [The Law of Charts](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/joe-ross-how-to-spot-a-trend.html); Market Profile deliberately declines to mechanise day-type recognition while the day is forming [Market Profile — Construction and Reading Rules](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/cbot-a-six-part-study-guide-to-market-profile--market-profile-construction-and-reading.html); the Andrews and Wolfe methods leave pivot selection to judgement, which is where most of their variance lives [Andrews’ Pitchfork](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/andrews-pitchfork.html) [Wolfe Waves](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wolfe-waves.html).
- **Which timeframe is tradable at all.** One primer calls day trading categorically the worst way to trade because shorter frames are more random, asserting it without data [An Introduction to Charting](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/introduction-to-charting.html); the multi-timeframe and Market Profile traditions treat intraday structure as readable given the right framework [Multiple Patterns, Multiple Time Frames](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mulitple-patterns-and-time-periods.html) [A Six-Part Study Guide to Market Profile](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/cbot-a-six-part-study-guide-to-market-profile.html).
- **Whether breakout targets can be projected at all.** Three incompatible methods are offered for the same congestion range — measured move, time-based, and an eighths-and-re-entries count — with no comparison between them [Congestion Markets: Estimating a Breakout Target](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/congestion-markets-estimating-a-breakout-target.html), and the channel-projection technique adds a fourth [Master the Channel Projection](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/channeltrading.html).
- **How much risk a price-action setup justifies.** Dorsey fixes 1% per position [Tom Dorsey's Trading Tips: A Playbook for Stock Market Success](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/tom-dorsey-s-trading-tips-a-playbook-for-stock-market-success.html); the 1-2-3 report allows 3% with a 10% stop [1-2-3 Trading Signal](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/e123system.html); most of the category gives no figure at all [Andrews’ Pitchfork](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/andrews-pitchfork.html) [Master the Channel Projection](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/channeltrading.html) [Wolfe Waves](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wolfe-waves.html).
- **Whether structural change has invalidated the older reading.** Panzner argues wider ranges, derivative-driven pricing and institutional turnover have degraded traditional indicators and seasonals [The New Laws of the Stock Market Jungle](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/michael-panzner-the-new-laws-of-the-stock-market-jungle.html); the pit-era frameworks in this category — half-hour brackets, floor specialists, CTI-classified volume — assume a market structure that has since been replaced [A Six-Part Study Guide to Market Profile](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/cbot-a-six-part-study-guide-to-market-profile.html) [Market Mechanics: A Guide to U.S. Stock Markets](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/nasdaq-education-foundation-a-guide-to-u-s-stock-markets-eng.html).

**Category note:** [How the Stock Market Works](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/economics-how-the-stock-market-works.html) and [How the Stock Market Works](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/how-the-stock-market-works.html) are two copies of the same Bradley tutorial and should be reconciled to one page.
