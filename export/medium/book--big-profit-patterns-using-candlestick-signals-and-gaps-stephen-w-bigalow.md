# Big Profit Patterns Using Candlestick Signals and Gaps: the trader's summary

*A candlestick guide that interprets gaps as evidence of changing sentiment and develops bottom, continuation, island, and kicker setups.*

**Stephen W. Bigalow** · 2002 · Candlesticks & Chart Patterns · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-51. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

Bigalow combines candlesticks with gaps to read sentiment. A gap after a bottoming signal can confirm a new advance, while one after an extended rise may mark exhaustion (pp. 3–5, 39–50). He covers bottom reversals, J-Hooks, island reversals, bad-news gaps, and kickers through chart examples rather than a formal test.

## Key points

- A gap up after a bullish reversal candle and oversold stochastic reading is treated as evidence of forceful demand at a bottom (pp. 5–9).
- The source cites an unofficial 80% or better success rate for oversold stochastics plus a bullish candlestick and gap up, while acknowledging that the observations were not formally collected (p. 7).
- A J-Hook is an uptrend, pullback, and renewed gap through or above the prior high; aggressive traders enter as the pullback levels, while conservative traders wait for the gap (pp. 34–38).
- After a major bad-news gap down of 20% or more, the first 30 minutes of churning are read for whether buyers or sellers dominate (pp. 41–44).
- A short-term trader should liquidate when the original bullish reason disappears after a severe adverse news gap; a longer-term investor may wait for a doji and use its low as a stop reference (pp. 41–44).
- A kicker is a two-candle reversal in which the second session opens at the first session’s open and drives in the opposite direction (pp. 45–49).

## Actionable rules

1. In an extended decline, treat a bullish reversal candle, oversold stochastic, and gap up as confirmation and enter near the open (pp. 5–9).
2. For a J-Hook, enter aggressively as the pullback stabilizes or conservatively on the renewed gap, especially near the prior high. The source specifies no stop (pp. 34–38).
3. When a surprise bad-news gap interrupts an uptrend, short-term traders liquidate. The longer-term example waits for a doji and renewed buying, then stops at the post-gap lows (pp. 41–44).
4. On a gap down of 20% or more, observe the first 30 minutes: continued weakness indicates sellers remain active, while a rising price indicates buyers are absorbing supply (p. 44).
5. Treat a kicker that opens at the prior session’s open and drives in the opposite direction as a reversal. The source supplies no numeric stop or sizing rule (pp. 45–49).

## Caveats

The success-rate claim is explicitly unofficial (p. 7). Most examples are individual stocks from 2001–02, with no standardized test. Execution can be worse than the chart open, and the interpretation of a severe news gap depends on company quality and market context (pp. 41–44).

## Who it is for

Pattern traders who understand candlesticks and want a contextual gap framework. It requires independent testing before systematic use.
