# The Colour of Money (Issue No. 221206): the trader's summary

*A short year-end newsletter arguing yield-curve inversion does not reliably predict recessions, and reworking risk/reward with win probability instead of equal odds.*

**Kshitij Consultancy Services** · 2006 · Forex Mechanics & Macro Drivers · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-4. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This is a four-page year-end newsletter (December 2006) from Kshitij Consultancy covering two unrelated topics. The first half examines whether an inverted US yield curve reliably predicts recessions, using data on the DJIA against the spread between the 10-year Treasury yield and the Fed Funds rate since 1962; the second half is a short essay reworking the classic risk/reward (stop-loss vs. take-profit) framework to account for the probability of each outcome being hit, rather than assuming a 50/50 chance.

## Key points

- Six major periods of US yield-curve inversion occurred between 1972 and 2006; the DJIA fell in three of them and rose in three of them.
- The two inversions that preceded large Dow declines (34% and 6%) both happened during 1972–1981, a period of secular rising interest rates.
- Since 1981 (secular declining rates), the Dow fell in only one of four inversion periods, and only by 9%.
- The newsletter concludes recessions correlate more with the direction of rate changes (prolonged increases) than with yield-curve shape itself.
- Traders commonly place take-profit (TP) orders too far away (greed) and stop-loss (SL) orders too close (fear), causing SLs to trigger and TPs to be missed roughly 60% of the time each.
- The standard risk/reward ratio (SL distance ÷ TP distance ≤ 1) ignores the probability of each being reached; the newsletter proposes weighting each side by its probability of execution.
- Example: a 60-pip SL / 40-pip TP with 40%/60% odds of loss/profit gives the same risk-adjusted ratio (1.0) as an equidistant 50/50 setup, while a 65-pip SL / 35-pip TP with 30%/70% odds is favorable (ratio < 1).

## Actionable rules

1. Do not assume yield-curve inversion alone signals an imminent bear market; check whether the broader interest-rate trend is rising or falling.
2. When setting TP/SL, consider narrowing the TP and widening the SL slightly (about 10 pips each in the article's example) to raise the probability that each order is actually hit.
3. Evaluate risk/reward using probability-adjusted terms: Risk/Reward = (SL × P(SL)) / (TP × P(TP)), not simply SL/TP.
4. Favor trade setups where the probability-adjusted ratio is below 1, even if the raw SL is nominally wider than the raw TP.

## Caveats

This is a short opinion newsletter, not a rigorous study — the yield-curve analysis uses only six historical episodes and no statistical testing, and the probability figures used in the risk/reward rework (e.g. 40%, 60%, 70%) are illustrative assumptions rather than measured win rates from real trading data. No source or method is given for estimating a trader's true probability of hitting a given TP or SL in practice.

## Who it is for

Retail forex traders looking for a quick, intuitive reframing of stop-loss/take-profit placement, and readers wanting a brief counterpoint to the conventional wisdom that yield-curve inversion always predicts recession.
