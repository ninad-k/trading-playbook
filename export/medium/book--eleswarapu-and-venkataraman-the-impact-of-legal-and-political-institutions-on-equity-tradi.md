# The Impact of Legal and Political Institutions on Equity Trading Costs: the trader's summary

*A cross-country ADR study links stronger legal and political institutions to lower NYSE trading costs.*

**Venkat R. Eleswarapu and Kumar Venkataraman** · 2002 · Quant, Microstructure & Academic Research · advanced

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-26. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

Eleswarapu and Venkataraman ask whether a company’s country of origin affects its equity trading costs through legal and political institutions. They study 412 NYSE-listed American Depository Receipts from 44 countries, using intraday NYSE Trade and Quote data from January–March 2002 and controlling for price, return volatility, market capitalization, and trading volume (pp. 2–10, 14). Effective spread and 30-minute price impact are their liquidity measures. The authors report higher costs for French-origin civil-law stocks than for common-law or German-origin groups: effective spreads average 0.96% versus 0.63% for English-origin and 0.41% for German-origin stocks (p. 11). Across accounting-quality quartiles, effective spreads fall from 1.07% in the lowest group to 0.64% in the highest (p. 12). Political-risk quartiles show 1.00% versus 0.65% effective spreads and 0.73% versus 0.37% price impact for the highest- versus lowest-risk groups (p. 13).

## Key points

- The common NYSE venue helps reduce cross-exchange market-structure differences, while firm controls address observable liquidity differences (pp. 4–5, 14).
- In regressions, accounting standards, judicial-system efficiency, and political stability remain associated with lower costs; insider-trading-law enforcement loses significance after firm controls (pp. 14–16).
- Holding firm variables at sample averages, the fitted effective spread is 0.95% for an India-level political-risk score versus 0.63% for Switzerland (pp. 15, 24–26).

## Actionable rules

1. The paper gives no entry, exit, or sizing rule; it is an empirical execution-cost study.
2. As a research implication, include country-level accounting quality, judicial efficiency, and political stability alongside price, volatility, firm size, and volume when comparing ADR execution costs (pp. 7–15).
3. Treat the fitted country values as model estimates rather than live spreads or executable forecasts (pp. 15–16).

## Caveats

The sample is a three-month 2002 snapshot of ADRs already selected for NYSE listing, so it is not a broad test of domestic stocks or current markets. Country risk measures are older indices, and correlations in this observational design do not prove that institutions cause the measured spreads. The paper’s fitted country examples are model estimates, not live trading costs.

## Who it is for

Useful for researchers, international investors, and execution analysts comparing liquidity across countries or venues.
