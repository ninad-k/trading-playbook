# Advanced Channeling Patterns: Wolfe Waves and Gartleys: the trader's summary

*Investopedia article compilation defining the five-wave Wolfe Wave and the Fibonacci-based Gartley (XABCD) pattern for predicting channel breakouts, plus bonus pieces on Elliott Wave and Fibonacci extensions.*

**Justin Kuepper** · 2005 · Fibonacci, Gann & Elliott Wave · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-16. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A short compilation of Investopedia-style articles by Justin Kuepper, plus a companion piece by Matt Blackman and Mike Green. The lead article covers two chart patterns that predict channel breakouts and their targets: the Wolfe Wave (a naturally occurring five-wave supply/demand pattern) and the Gartley pattern (an XABCD structure built on Fibonacci ratios, from H.M. Gartley's 1935 book). Bundled pieces cover a computerized approach to Elliott Wave and Fibonacci extensions/clusters.

## Key points

- Wolfe Wave: waves 3-4 must stay inside the channel formed by waves 1-2, with 1-2 roughly symmetric to 3-4; wave 5 pushes slightly outside that channel (often a false breakout) — that is the entry. Target (point 6) is a line extended from point 1 through point 4.
- Waves 3 and 5 commonly measure as 127% or 162% Fibonacci extensions of the prior leg.
- Gartley (XABCD) ratios: AB ≈ 61.8% of XA; BC between 61.8–78.6% of AB; CD between 127–161.8% of BC; XD ≈ 78.6% of XA. Point D is the entry, with an initial target at 61.8% of the CD leg.
- The article claims Gartley setups "follow this model accurately 60% or more of the time," with no disclosed source or sample size.
- Elliott Wave piece cites a proprietary, screensaver-driven backtest (Rich Swannell's team) finding ~65% of instruments too unreliable for Elliott Wave trading, with the most liquid ~35% viable; patterns scoring 80+ (of 100) on their rating were deemed reliable.
- Fibonacci extensions (161.8%, 261.8%) and clusters (overlapping retracements from multiple swings) are covered as support/resistance and target tools.

## Actionable rules

1. Confirm a Wolfe Wave only if waves 3-4 stay within the 1-2 channel and are roughly equal in size/timing to 1-2; enter at wave 5's breakout; target the 1-4 trendline extension.
2. Confirm a Gartley using the AB/BC/CD/XD ratio chain above; enter at point D with an initial target at 61.8% retracement of CD.
3. Paper trade first and always use a stop — both patterns are explicitly subjective, and wave 5's "false breakout" can fail to reverse.
4. Trust an Elliott Wave count only when confirmed across two or more timeframes; treat single-timeframe counts as low-confidence.
5. Restrict Elliott Wave trading to liquid, heavily-traded instruments — the theory is reported to break down on thin or closely-held names.

## Caveats

A compilation of short articles, not one coherent book — rigor varies between pieces, and the Gartley/Wolfe "success rate" claims carry no disclosed methodology. The Elliott Wave statistics come from one vendor's proprietary research and read as a marketing claim rather than independent verification. All three pattern families are acknowledged in the text itself to be highly subjective in real time.

## Who it is for

Traders already comfortable with Fibonacci retracement/extension concepts who want harmonic pattern recognition (Gartley) and Elliott Wave-adjacent tools (Wolfe Wave) added to a channel-trading approach.
