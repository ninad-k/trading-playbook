# Oh, Crap! I Just Lost All of My Money.: the trader's summary

*A five-page promotional excerpt encourages a losing forex trader to continue and gives one concrete instruction: use a stop loss.*

**Rob Booker** · 2003 · Trading Psychology & Discipline · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-5. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

Booker’s five-page free chapter addresses a reader who has lost money in foreign exchange. It identifies three likely reactions: panic about the future, a desire to give up, and a temptation to seek revenge (p. 2). The next page says “Don’t panic,” “Don’t give up,” and, unexpectedly, “Do get revenge,” then promotes the paid remainder of the ebook without explaining what “revenge” means or giving a recovery method (p. 3). The only concrete trading instruction in the available extract is to use some form of stop loss. Booker says he sometimes uses a stop 100 pips away while seeking 10 pips because an earlier, omitted principle kept the amount at risk small enough (p. 4). The conclusion says starting capital matters less than how well it is protected (p. 5).

## Key points

- The source frames losing money as a common but painful route to learning discipline (p. 3).
- It urges the reader not to panic or quit after the loss (p. 3).
- It also literally says “Do get revenge,” but the free extract does not define or justify that advice (p. 3).
- A stop is presented as protection against a sharp market move (p. 4).
- The source says account care matters more than initial capital and suggests a trader could start with a few hundred dollars, without supporting that claim with results (p. 5).

## Actionable rules

1. Place some type of stop loss so a violent market move cannot leave the position wholly unprotected (p. 4).
2. Treat the 100-pip stop and 10-pip objective only as Booker’s personal example; the extract gives no entry, size, expectancy, or basis for generalizing it (p. 4).
3. Do not convert the phrase “Do get revenge” into a trading rule because the available pages never explain it (p. 3).

## Caveats

The excerpt starts at “Reason #2” on p. 4 and refers to “principle #1,” neither of which is included. It therefore cannot support the original note’s claims about pausing, reviewing the trade, avoiding emotional recovery trades, or limiting one-trade account damage. The 100-pip/10-pip example is not presented as a universal ratio and has no cost or probability analysis.

## Who it is for

New forex traders coping with a loss and learning why stops and emotional control matter.
