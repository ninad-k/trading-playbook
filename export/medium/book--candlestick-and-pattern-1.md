# Forex Trading with Candlesticks and Patterns, Part 1: the trader's summary

*A forex pattern primer covering candle reversals, trend structure, breakouts, and a pullback-entry framework.*

**Forex Systems Research Company** · 2003 · Candlesticks & Chart Patterns · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-49. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

Part 1 of this Forex Systems Research course introduces spot-FX mechanics, Japanese candlesticks, and Western chart patterns. Its practical theme is context: a candle matters more when it appears after a clear move and near support, resistance, a trendline, or a prior pivot. The final retracement framework converts that context into a trade sequence—identify the trend, wait for a pullback and confirmed pivot, enter with the trend, place the stop beyond the pivot, and target the next structural or Fibonacci level (PDF pp. 10-18, 22-49).

## Key points

- Intraday forex rarely gaps, so the course permits gapless morning/evening stars while retaining their three-candle reversal logic (PDF pp. 12, 16).
- A doji signals indecision and needs a confirming close; after an uptrend, the stated bearish confirmation is a close below the doji's close (PDF p. 13).
- Engulfing patterns require a definable preceding trend, an opposite-color second body that covers the first body, and ignore shadows (PDF p. 14).
- A hammer's lower shadow should be at least twice its body and needs a confirming close above it (PDF p. 16).
- Double tops and bottoms are tests of previous extremes; wider spacing and alignment with a pivot level can strengthen the setup (PDF p. 27).
- Trendlines gain weight with time and repeated touches; the course highlights the third touch as a candidate entry area (PDF pp. 29-30).
- Rectangles, triangles, flags, and pennants can fail, so a breakout direction remains provisional until price follows through (PDF pp. 33-45).

## Actionable rules

1. Trade in the prevailing direction and wait for a retracement toward a marked Fibonacci level, pivot, trendline, support/resistance zone, or prior high/low (PDF p. 46).
2. For a long, enter one pip above the high of the pullback's pivot-low bar after the following bar confirms it. Place the stop below that pivot if the distance fits the risk limit; reverse these conditions for a short (PDF pp. 46-47).
3. Use a trendline, pivot line, Fibonacci projection, support/resistance level, or reversal pattern as the exit area (PDF pp. 46-47).
4. When point 3 retraces about 61.8% of the move from points 1 to 2, monitor 161.8% and 261.8% projections for point 4 while respecting intervening levels (PDF pp. 47-49).

## Caveats

All 49 recovered OCR pages were reviewed, though chart text has minor recognition errors. Market-size, leverage, broker, platform, and no-slippage claims are dated. Pattern reliability is asserted through selected charts without a controlled sample, costs, or audited expectancy.

Beginning forex chart readers who want a pattern vocabulary and a simple trend-pullback workflow.
