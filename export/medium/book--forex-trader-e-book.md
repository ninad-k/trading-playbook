## Summary

A broad Forex reference manual organized in seven parts: (I) Forex basics, technical vs. fundamental analysis, trading psychology, and 8 core trading recommendations; (II) basic technical-analysis concepts (support, resistance, trend, channel, double top/bottom, triangle, head and shoulders, Fibonacci); (III) chart types (line, bar, candlestick); (IV) an extensive candlestick-pattern glossary; (V) 14 technical indicators with formulas; (VI) spot/forward FX trading mechanics (bid/offer, forward outright, swaps, premium/discount); and (VII) definitions of 33 economic indicators (CPI, GDP, ISM, nonfarm payroll, etc.). It functions as a study reference rather than a narrative book.

## Key points

- Defines the Forex market structure: over-the-counter, no central exchange, ~$1 trillion+ daily turnover (as of writing), currencies always quoted in pairs (base/counter), bid-ask spread.
- Candlestick glossary covers reversal and continuation patterns: Doji variants (dragonfly, gravestone, long-legged), engulfing lines, Hammer/Hanging Man/Shooting Star, Harami (including Harami cross/doji), long white/black lines, Morning/Evening Star (plain and Doji versions), Three Black Crows, Three White Soldiers.
- 14 technical indicators defined with construction logic: ATR, Bollinger Bands, CCI, Linear Regression, MACD, Momentum, Moving Average, Parabolic SAR, ROC, RSI, Slow Stochastic, Standard Deviation, Stochastic, Williams %R.
- Spot/forward section defines bid/offer, forward outright, FX swap, and how premium/discount is calculated between spot and forward rates.
- Economic indicator glossary spans 33 releases (CPI, PPI, GDP, ISM, nonfarm payroll, jobless claims, housing starts, consumer confidence/sentiment, trade balance, money supply, etc.) relevant to fundamental FX analysis.
- Supports (a specific price a currency has failed to cross below) become resistance once broken, and vice versa, per the standard technical-analysis definition given.

## Actionable rules

The document's own "8 most important trading recommendations":
1. The trend is your friend — trade in the direction of the prevailing trend.
2. In up-trends buy the dips; in downtrends sell bounces.
3. Let profits run, cut losses short; always use protective stops, moving them only to reduce risk or lock in profit.
4. Set up a trading plan before entering the market; don't trade impulsively.
5. Employ at least a 3:1 reward-to-risk ratio.
6. When pyramiding: each added layer should be smaller than the last, add only to winning positions, never add to a losing position, and move the stop to breakeven or better as size is added.
7. Be comfortable being in the minority — if you're right, most people will disagree with you.
8. Keep it simple; more complexity isn't always better.

## Caveats

This is a compiled reference/glossary rather than an original analytical work — author and publication year are not identified in the text, and definitions are largely standard textbook material (candlestick patterns, indicator formulas, economic-release definitions) without proprietary insight or backtested rules. No specific parameter settings (e.g., moving-average lengths, RSI period) are given for the indicators beyond their general formulas.
