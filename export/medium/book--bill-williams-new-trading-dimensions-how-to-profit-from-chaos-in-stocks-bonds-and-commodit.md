# New Trading Dimensions: How to Profit from Chaos in Stocks, Bonds, and Commodities: the trader's summary

*Bill Williams' Profitunity method: five 'dimensions' (Fractal, AO, AC, Zone, Balance Line) filtered through the Alligator, plus a psychology chapter on trading 'in the zone.'*

**Bill Williams** · 1998 · Trend Following & Mechanical Systems · intermediate

*Source coverage: partial. PDF pages inspected: 35, 41, 44 (Alligator construction, the Awesome Oscillator definition and the fractal/AO signal rules). These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Overview

This is Bill Williams' follow-up to *Trading Chaos*, repackaging his "Profitunity" method around Chaos theory (nonlinear dynamics, fractal geometry) rather than classical technical analysis. The book has two halves: a mechanical trading system built from five proprietary "dimensions" of market analysis, gated by an indicator called the Alligator, and a closing chapter on trading psychology built around getting into and staying in a Michael-Jordan-style performance "zone." Williams argues price, fundamentals, and standard oscillators are lagging or meaningless ("oversold/overbought" doesn't exist); his indicators are built instead from momentum and its rate of change, which he claims turn before price does.

## Core thesis

Markets are chaotic systems, not random or classically predictable ones — chaotic behavior has an underlying order that can be traded once recognized. Most market analysis (fundamental, oversold/overbought, bullish-consensus surveys, forecasts) produces "ungrounded assessments" — statements that cannot be verified from the market itself. Williams restricts every rule in the book to "grounded" signals derived only from price/volume behavior. His second pillar: traders lose because of their own belief systems, not because of the market, so the mechanical rules must be paired with a psychological practice (staying in "the zone," process over content, letting go of outcome) to be usable.

## Key concepts

- **Alligator** — three offset smoothed moving averages (Jaw/Teeth/Lips) that act as a compass; when intertwined the "Alligator is sleeping" (range-bound market); when spread apart it is "eating" (trending).
- **Fractal** — a 5-bar reversal pattern (a high/low with two lower/higher bars on each side) marking the market's smallest unit of "new information" (Chaos); the trigger for the first entry.
- **Awesome Oscillator (AO)** — a 5-period vs. 34-period simple moving average of bar midpoints; the book's core momentum ("second dimension") indicator.
- **Accelerator/Decelerator (AC)** — the rate of change of AO; a leading indicator of AO itself ("the day after tomorrow's Wall Street Journal").
- **Zone (Green/Red/Gray)** — the combination of AO and AC direction; Green = both rising (aggressive buy), Red = both falling (aggressive sell), Gray = disagreement (stand down).
- **Balance Line** — the fifth dimension; the price level the market would occupy absent new information; price moves away from it more easily than toward it ("path of least resistance").
- **Market Facilitation Index (MFI)** — range divided by volume; introduced in the prior book *Trading Chaos* and only referenced here as closely approximated by the AO histogram.
- **Grounded vs. ungrounded assessment** — a verifiable, market-derived observation vs. an opinion or forecast; the filter Williams applies to reject conventional indicators.
- **Squat bar / blue light special** — a narrow-range/low-volume bar signalling exhaustion; a "blue light special" is a pullback that lets a trader take a signal at a better price than originally triggered.
- **Five magic bullets** — five conditions (Elliott-Wave target zone, AO divergence, a fractal at the extreme, a squat bar, a momentum-direction change) that together mark trend exhaustion.
- **MAP (Momentum, Acceleration, Price)** — shorthand for having AO, AC, and price all aligned; the condition for adding aggressively in the Zone.
- **The zone / flow** — a psychological state of absorbed, non-anxious performance; the book's closing chapter gives an "Anchor / Visualize / Turn up the volume" exercise for entering and re-entering it.

## Rules and setups

1. **Alligator construction**: Jaw (blue) = 13-bar smoothed moving average (SMMA) of the bar midpoint, offset 8 bars into the future; Teeth (red) = 8-bar SMMA offset 5 bars forward; Lips (green) = 5-bar SMMA offset 3 bars forward.
2. **Entry filter**: take no signal from any dimension until price produces the first fractal breakout outside the Alligator's mouth (Jaw). Above the mouth, only buy signals are taken; below the mouth, only sell signals.
3. **Fractal signal**: a minimum 5-bar pattern with the middle bar's high (low) exceeding the two bars on each side. Buy stop = one tick above the fractal high (sell stop = one tick below the fractal low), valid only if price is on the correct side of the Alligator's Teeth (red line).
4. **AO signals** (only after the first fractal breakout): Saucer (3-bar colour change while AO stays on one side of zero), zero-line Cross, and Twin Peaks (a shallower second peak on the same side of zero). Stop placed one tick beyond the signal bar's high/low.
5. **AC signals**: 2 same-colour bars off a peak/trough when AC is already in the trade's favour (crossed the zero line in that direction); 3 bars required when AC is still against the trade. Crossing the AC zero line is not itself a signal, only a bar-count adjustment.
6. **Zone add-ons**: while AO and AC both point the same way (Green/Red Zone), add on the close of every same-colour bar; stop adding after 5 consecutive same-colour bars (though other-dimension signals can still be taken).
7. **Balance Line signal**: from a base bar, one new high/low if the move is away from the Balance Line, two new highs/lows if moving toward it; doubled again (to 2/4) if the current bar is in the "wrong" zone (buying in Red, selling in Green).
8. **Exit hierarchy** (in order of preference): (a) stop-close-only just past the Teeth (red line) while trend intact; (b) trail one tick beyond the extreme of the fifth consecutive same-colour bar, then ratchet with each new extreme; (c) stop-close-only at the Lips (green line) once the market becomes a "runaway" (price angle exceeds the Lips' angle); (d) exit (not reverse) on an opposite-direction signal — least preferred.
9. **Trend-exhaustion check**: treat a top/bottom as more reliable once 2 or more of the five magic bullets align (62–100% Elliott retracement zone, AO divergence, a fractal at the extreme, a squat bar, a momentum-direction flip).
10. **Target**: an "achievable" goal of roughly 10% average monthly return, and a rule-of-thumb aim to capture 3–5x the size of the underlying trend move by adding on aggressively through the Zone and Balance Line signals.

## Risk and money management

The book gives almost no explicit position-sizing rules; it deliberately singles out fixed-percentage stop rules ("never risk over 2 percent of your total equity on one trade") as an example of an "ungrounded assessment" that should be discarded in favor of market-generated stops. Risk control instead comes entirely from the exit hierarchy above: the first protective stop goes in immediately after the fractal-breakout entry (just inside the Alligator's Teeth), and losing trades from the first, low-conviction breakout are accepted as the cost of being positioned early — Williams calls these "low-rent district" trades. No fixed dollar or percentage stop-loss, no formula for contract/share sizing, and no stated maximum drawdown or account-risk figure are given anywhere in the text.

## Psychology and discipline

The final chapter argues that once the mechanical signals are learned, "everything else is psychological," and frames consistent losing as self-inflicted (traders "trade their own belief systems," not the market). Williams borrows Csikszentmihalyi's concept of flow and gives a repeatable technique — Anchor (a physical/mental trigger), Visualize (rehearse the trade in specific sensory detail), and "turn up the volume" (act without deliberation) — for entering and, crucially, re-entering the zone after a loss. He personifies self-sabotage as "Joe Gremlin," urges traders to stay in "grounded," present-tense observation rather than forecasting, and to treat losses as data ("Experience + Reflection = Wisdom") rather than as evidence about the market.

## Chapter map

- Ch 1 — Blasting Off — content vs. process, grounded vs. ungrounded assessments, framing trading as self-discovery.
- Ch 2 — From Chaos to Cosmos to Cash — a non-mathematical primer on relativity, quantum mechanics, and Chaos theory as applied to markets.
- Ch 3 — The Alligator — construction, interpretation, and the "sleeping/eating" trading strategy.
- Ch 4 — The Fractal — the breakout-signal pattern and its interaction with the Alligator's mouth.
- Ch 5 — The Awesome Oscillator — AO construction and its three buy/three sell signal types (Saucer, Cross, Twin Peaks).
- Ch 6 — The Market Accelerator — AC construction, its buy/sell rules above, below, and crossing zero.
- Ch 7 — Trading in the Zones — combining AO and AC into Green/Red/Gray zones for aggressive add-ons and profit-taking.
- Ch 8 — The Balance Line Trades — the fifth dimension; buy/sell rules relative to the Balance Line and zone filters.
- Ch 9 — Techniques for Taking Profits — the four-tier exit hierarchy, divergence, squat bars, and the five magic bullets.
- Ch 10 — Tying It All Together — a six-step trading procedure combining all five dimensions, with worked chart examples.
- Ch 11 — How to Be a Master Trader (Now and Zen: Staying in the Zone) — the flow/psychology chapter and the Anchor-Visualize-Volume exercise.

## Strengths and caveats

The entry/exit and indicator-construction rules are unusually precise and codeable — exact moving-average lengths, offsets, and bar-count conditions are all specified, which is rare for this genre. The system is also explicitly designed to work identically "on all markets, all time frames," which is a testable, falsifiable claim. On the other hand: the book leans heavily on unproven physics analogies (quantum mechanics, chaos theory, holography) as justification rather than statistical evidence; no backtest, win rate, or drawdown statistics are given beyond a handful of cherry-picked three-month chart examples; the explicit rejection of any fixed-percentage risk rule is a significant gap for a mechanical system; and the Market Facilitation Index, despite being one of Williams' better-known indicators, is not actually defined or given trading rules in this book — those live in the earlier *Trading Chaos*, which this text repeatedly cites but does not reproduce.

## Who should read it

Traders who already have a systematic bent and want a fully specified, indicator-based mechanical system (rather than discretionary chart reading) will find this the most implementable of Williams' books, provided they are willing to bolt on their own risk-sizing rules. It also suits readers interested in the psychology of sustaining a systematic edge — the flow/zone material is one of the more concrete "how to re-enter a performance state after a loss" treatments in the genre. Less useful for anyone looking for statistical validation, fundamental context, or a fully self-contained explanation of Williams' entire indicator set (MFI in particular requires the companion book).
