# Market-Neutral Investing: Long-Short Hedge Fund Strategies: the trader's summary

*A 2001 book abstract surveys seven long-short strategies that seek returns from relationships between securities while hedging selected risks.*

**Joseph G. Nicholas** · 2000 · Investing, Value & Market History · advanced

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-5. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This five-page getAbstract summary of Joseph G. Nicholas’s 2000 book defines market-neutral investing as offsetting long and short positions in related instruments to hedge selected market risks (pp. 1-2). It says returns come from the relationship between the long and short elements rather than simply from market direction. The abstract surveys seven categories: convertible arbitrage, fixed-income arbitrage, mortgage-backed-securities arbitrage, merger arbitrage, equity hedge, equity market neutral/statistical arbitrage, and relative-value arbitrage (pp. 2-5). Equity hedge is explicitly described as similar to, but not strictly, market neutral (p. 4). The text makes favorable general comparisons with long-only investing, but it does not provide the underlying data needed to verify them.

## Key points

- Convertible arbitrage combines a long convertible security with a short position in its underlying stock; bullish and bearish variants change the number of shares sold short (p. 3).
- Fixed-income arbitrage targets temporarily distorted relationships, usually uses leverage because spreads are small, and faces interest-rate, market, model, liquidity, and financing considerations (p. 3).
- Mortgage-backed-securities managers seek a spread between an undervalued MBS and a Treasury-based hedge and may use Treasuries, options, futures, caps, floors, swaps, and forwards to target zero duration (pp. 3-4).
- Merger arbitrage in the abstract concerns announced deals: the manager buys the target and shorts the acquirer while bearing the event risk that the transaction fails (p. 4).
- Equity market-neutral and statistical-arbitrage portfolios hold many long positions and an equal or nearly equal dollar amount of shorts; their three stated steps are screening, selection, and portfolio construction (pp. 4-5).

## Actionable rules

1. Identify the long exposure, the short exposure, and the intended hedge relationship before classifying a strategy as market neutral (p. 2).
2. For convertible positions, calculate the hedge ratio rather than assuming one short share amount fits neutral, bullish, and bearish books (p. 3).
3. For equity market-neutral construction, screen for stocks that are difficult to trade in blocks or sell short and account for expected price impact before ranking and sizing the book (pp. 4-5).

## Caveats

This is a publisher-approved abstract, not the complete 336-page book, and it provides no datasets, return tables, hedge formulas, universal stop, or rebalancing schedule. Its claim of long-only-like return with lower volatility is asserted rather than demonstrated in the available pages. The source itself lists market, event, credit, liquidity, interest-rate, model, leverage, and strategy-mix risks across the approaches (pp. 1, 3-5).

## Who it is for

Investors learning the purpose and risk decomposition of long-short market-neutral strategies.
