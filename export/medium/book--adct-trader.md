# The Affluent Desktop Currency Trader: the trader's summary

*Retail forex day-trading manual covering pip/lot mechanics, order types, and four short-term systems (Bollinger/MA/PSAR/MACD/RSI/Stochastic confluence, Inner Bar breakout, MA crossover, candlestick+stochastic) targeting 10-25 pips per trade.*

**Amin Sadak** · 2006 · Day Trading & Scalping · beginner

*Source coverage: partial. PDF pages inspected: 15-16, 40, 48-51 (the risk-per-trade and reward/risk rules and the moving-average chapters). These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This is a retail forex education manual (2006, ShiftTrading.com) promising $500-$1000/week from part-time currency trading. After covering forex mechanics (pips, pip value, lot sizes, order types, support/resistance, Fibonacci retracement), it presents four discretionary short-term systems built from stacked indicators (moving averages, Bollinger Bands, Parabolic SAR, MACD, RSI, Stochastic, candlesticks), each targeting roughly 10-25 pips per trade with defined trailing-stop and risk/reward rules.

## Key points

- Pip mechanics: for most pairs 1 pip = 0.0001, worth ~$10 per standard lot (100,000 units) or ~$1 per mini lot; JPY/USD-base pairs vary slightly (~$8-9.55 cited).
- A stop-loss order is called "the most important order after placing a trade" and should never be omitted; entry stop/limit orders wait for price to reach a level.
- Recommended risk: 2-5% of equity per trade maximum; up to ~15% only when trading mini lots with a stop far enough away to keep the trade viable.
- Minimum risk/reward ratio 1:1.5 (some prefer 1:2); reward should run rather than be capped, since some cited systems win only 40% of trades but stay profitable via reward asymmetry.
- Four systems: A (10-min chart, 10/50 EMA + Bollinger Bands + Parabolic SAR + MACD + RSI + Stochastic confluence breakout of a BB squeeze, 10-25 pips); B ("Inner Bar"/Harami breakout, only at key S/R, trendline, or Fibonacci levels); C (1H chart, 10/40 EMA crossover confirmed by RSI>50 and rising MACD histogram, 20-pip stop); D (candlestick pattern + Stochastic on a 15-min chart, confirmed by a 5/13 EMA cross on the 5-min chart, 15-pip stop).
- Trailing-stop discipline: move to breakeven at 10-15 pips profit, then lock in gains progressively (e.g., +10 at 30 pips, +30 at 50 pips), scaling out partial lots at each milestone.
- Explicit warning against moving a stop further away to avoid a loss — called "a classic beginner mistake."

## Actionable rules

1. Risk 2-5% of account equity per trade maximum.
2. Maintain a minimum 1:1.5 risk/reward ratio before entering; prefer 1:2 where possible.
3. Move the stop-loss to breakeven once a trade is 10-15 pips in profit; continue trailing in stages (never move a stop further away from price).
4. System C entry: 10/40 EMA crossover (1H chart) confirmed by RSI above 50 and a rising MACD histogram, entered on the next candle's breakout; 20-pip stop.
5. System D entry: 15-min candlestick reversal after a significant move, confirmed by a Stochastic %K/%D cross (or a 5/13 EMA cross on the 5-min chart); 15-pip stop.
6. Trade System B (Inner Bar/Harami breakouts) only at recognized key levels — not in isolation.

## Caveats

This is a promotional retail course with unverified income claims ("$500-$1000/week") and no backtest statistics, drawdown data, or broker-cost analysis behind any system. Pip-value and lot-size figures assume a 2006-era broker fee structure. The systems stack many lagging indicators with no stated rationale for the specific parameter choices, a hallmark of curve-fit retail system design rather than researched edge.

## Who it is for

Beginner retail forex traders who want a concrete, mechanically defined starting point (specific indicator settings, stop-loss and trailing-stop rules) for short-term discretionary trading, understood as a starting template to test and validate rather than a proven system.
