# Patterns: Math in Nature: the trader's summary

*A children’s worksheet explains the Fibonacci sequence and constructs a spiral from squares sized 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, and 13.*

**Maggie's Earth Adventures** · 2001 · Fibonacci, Gann & Elliott Wave · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-5. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This educational worksheet introduces Fibonacci numbers through natural patterns such as pinecones, pineapples, shells, and sunflowers. It defines the sequence as 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, where each term is the sum of the previous two (p. 1). The exercise constructs a Fibonacci rectangle by adjoining squares with those side lengths, then drawing a spiral through the geometry (pp. 1-2). The source teaches a mathematical sequence and visual construction; it does not claim that Fibonacci ratios forecast prices.

## Key points

- The sequence is recursive addition of the prior two terms (p. 1).
- The worksheet’s square sizes progress through 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, and 13 (pp. 1-2).
- A spiral is drawn from the resulting rectangle.
- Natural examples motivate pattern recognition but do not establish a market relationship.
- The document contains blank worksheet pages for drawing and listing observations (pp. 3-5).

## Actionable rules

1. Generate the sequence by adding the two prior values; start with 1 and 1 (p. 1).
2. Build adjoining squares in the listed sizes to reproduce the worksheet’s rectangle (p. 2).
3. Use Fibonacci price tools only with separate market evidence; this source gives no trading rule.

## Caveats

The source is a children’s math worksheet, not a trading study. It provides no financial data, price levels, or evidence that natural spirals forecast markets.

## Who it is for

Beginners who need the mathematical meaning of Fibonacci before encountering technical-analysis applications.
