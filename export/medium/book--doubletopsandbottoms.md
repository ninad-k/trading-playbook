# Double Tops & Double Bottoms: the trader's summary

*Short lesson on trading double top ('M') and double bottom ('W') reversal patterns, with a neckline-break entry rule and a measured-move price target formula.*

**Mark McRae** · Candlesticks & Chart Patterns · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-3. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A short "Traders Secret Library" lesson on double top ("M") and double bottom ("W") reversal patterns: how to identify the setup, when to enter on the neckline break, where to place a stop, and how to calculate a price target using the measured-move method.

## Key points

- Double tops consist of two peaks of roughly equal height; double bottoms consist of two troughs of roughly equal depth; both are classic trend-reversal patterns.
- The stronger the preceding trend, the more significant the reversal when the pattern completes.
- Per the author's research, double tops tend to be shorter in duration with a more pronounced breakdown; double bottoms tend to be longer in duration with price action in a smaller range.
- Pattern anatomy: price meets resistance at point A, declines to point B (the neckline), rallies back to fail near the same resistance at point C, then must close below the neckline (point B level) to confirm the pattern.
- The neckline break must occur on a closing basis — until then, the price action could just be consolidation rather than a completed reversal.
- Price target ("measured move") is calculated as: target = neckline − (C − B) for a double top, or neckline + (B − C) for a double bottom (the distance between the neckline and the intervening peak/trough is projected from the neckline in the breakout direction).

## Actionable rules

1. Confirm the pattern only after price closes below the neckline (double top) or above the neckline (double bottom) — do not act on an intraday touch alone.
2. Entry: enter on the neckline break itself, optionally adding to the position if price returns to retest the broken neckline as new resistance/support.
3. Stop-loss: place the stop between the neckline (B) and the secondary peak/trough (C), or above/below C itself if that distance is not too costly.
4. Price target formula (double top example given): if B = 85 and C = 115, target = 85 − (115 − 85) = 55.
5. Price target formula (double bottom example given, EUR/USD daily chart): B = 0.9197, C = 0.8843; target = 0.9197 + (0.9197 − 0.8843) = 0.9551 — reached in the cited example with no pullback.
