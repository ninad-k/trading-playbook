# ADX (Average Directional Index): the trader's summary

*Short lesson on reading ADX as a trend-strength gauge and a simple 30-plus, pullback-to-21-MA entry technique.*

**Mark McRae** · Indicators · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-3. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This is a short trading-lesson handout explaining J. Welles Wilder's Average Directional Index (ADX). It covers what the 0-100 scale means, clears up a common beginner confusion (ADX rises for both strong up-trends and strong down-trends, since it measures trend strength, not direction), and gives the author's own simple technique for timing entries once a trend is confirmed.

## Key points

- **ADX** — a 0-100 oscillator measuring the strength of a directional move, not its direction.
- Readings above 25 indicate the security is trending; readings below 25 indicate a non-trending (range-bound) market.
- The same ADX reading (e.g., 45) can occur in either a strong uptrend or a strong downtrend, which confuses many beginners who expect the line to fall during down-moves.
- Wilder himself called directional movement "the most fascinating concept" he had studied.
- Readings above 60 are rare and, once reached, can signal the trend is approaching exhaustion and due for a change in direction.
- The author pairs ADX with a 21-period moving average on whatever timeframe is being traded.
- Signals are infrequent but described as high-probability when they occur.

## Actionable rules

1. Require ADX to be at or above 30 before considering any trade (no action below this threshold).
2. Wait for price to retrace back to the 21-period moving average while ADX remains elevated.
3. Enter long when price pulls back to the 21-MA during an ADX-confirmed uptrend; enter short on the equivalent pullback during a confirmed downtrend.
4. Place a tight stop on entry — above the most recent high (for shorts) or below the most recent low (for longs), or a fixed dollar amount.
5. Set the first profit target at the most recent swing high (longs) or swing low (shorts) that preceded the pullback.

## Caveats

The material is a brief promotional trading lesson rather than a rigorously tested system; no backtest statistics, win rate, or sample period are given, so the "high probability" claim is anecdotal. It does not specify how ADX itself is calculated (Wilder's original formula using +DI/-DI is assumed but not shown). ## Who it is for

Beginner to intermediate traders who already have a charting platform and want a quick, concrete rule set for combining a trend-strength filter (ADX) with a moving-average pullback entry.
