# How to Trade Better: the trader's summary

*Short Larry Williams article on managing fear and greed, plus a simple 18-day moving-average trend-change signal requiring two consecutive closes on each side.*

**Larry Williams** · 2000 · Trading Psychology & Discipline · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-4. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A brief promotional newsletter article from veteran futures trader Larry Williams, split into two parts. The first half is a psychology essay distinguishing greed (the "active force" that causes overtrading, holding too long, and buying too much) from fear (the "prohibitive" force that causes traders to skip good trades, avoid placing stops, and lie about results). The second half introduces a simple trend-change tool: an 18-day moving average of closing price, with buy/sell setups triggered when price closes on one side of the average for two consecutive days, framed as a symptom of an underlying fundamental condition rather than a standalone signal.

## Key points

- Greed is described as the stronger emotional force for most traders, causing jumping the gun, overholding, and oversizing.
- Fear is described as preventive, rooted in "unknowing" — traders who haven't prepared for contingencies (no stops, no profit plan) fear the unknown future of a trade.
- Fear also causes traders to lie about their wins and losses, including to their own spouses, reinforcing a cycle of denial.
- Trend-change signal: an 18-day simple moving average of closing prices; a buy setup requires two full days closing above the average, a sell setup requires two full days closing below it.
- A single day crossing or merely touching the average without further extension is explicitly said to be insufficient to signal trend change.
- Moving averages are framed as acting like support and resistance; a genuine trend change needs a "powerhouse move" once price reaches the average area, not just a touch.
- The signal is explicitly framed as confirmatory, not causal — Williams states these are "just a sign, a symptom" of the real underlying bullish/bearish fundamental condition, and warns against using them in isolation.

## Actionable rules

1. Trend-change setup: plot an 18-day simple moving average of closing prices.
2. Buy signal: two consecutive full days with the close entirely above the 18-day average.
3. Sell signal: two consecutive full days with the close entirely below the 18-day average.
4. Do not act on a single-day cross or a mere touch of the average with no further follow-through.
5. Confirm the signal against an underlying fundamental/condition-based bias (e.g., commercial positioning) rather than trading the moving-average signal alone.

## Caveats

Very short (4 pages) and functions partly as promotional material for the author's seminars and books; the moving-average rule is illustrated only with a few example charts (Sugar, Lumber, Wheat) rather than backtested statistics. The psychology section is anecdotal and motivational rather than empirically grounded.

## Who it is for

Traders looking for a short, practical reminder on managing fear/greed and a simple, easy-to-plot trend-change filter to combine with a fundamental or seasonal view, rather than a full trading system on its own.
