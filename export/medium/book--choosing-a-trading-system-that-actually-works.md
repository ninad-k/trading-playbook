# Choosing a Trading System That Actually Works: the trader's summary

*Bill Poulos offers a strict, heuristic checklist for mechanical futures systems covering history, liquidity, drawdown, returns, and trade profile.*

**Bill Poulos** · 2004 · Trend Following & Mechanical Systems · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-5. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

Poulos gives his criteria for considering a mechanical futures trading system. The system must be fully mechanical, permit no human overrides or ongoing adjustment, avoid curve fitting, and show consistently good results over at least five years (p. 2). It should trade liquid markets, work across bull, bear, and sideways conditions, and include realistic commissions and slippage in hypothetical performance (p. 2). The remaining tests cover drawdown, minimum account size, annual return, and trade profile (pp. 3-4). These are the author’s screening heuristics, not empirical laws.

## Key points

- Maximum drawdown as a percentage of account value should not exceed half the average annual return, and drawdown duration should not exceed six months (p. 3).
- The stated absolute minimum starting account is historical maximum drawdown plus margin for one contract; Poulos advises an additional buffer because a future drawdown may be worse (p. 3).
- Average annual net profit should be at least twice maximum drawdown over five or more years, and the author says ideally there should be no losing years (p. 3).
- The trade profile calls for 40%-60% profitable trades, a 1.3-2.0 average-win/average-loss ratio, and average net profit per trade at least three times assumed per-trade costs (p. 4).
- The repeated disclaimer states that futures losses occur and no system can guarantee profits or freedom from losses (pp. 1, 3-5).

## Actionable rules

1. First require a mechanical rule set and at least five years of consistently good historical results that include commission and slippage assumptions (p. 2).
2. Apply both drawdown tests: maximum drawdown no greater than half average annual return and duration no longer than six months (p. 3).
3. Then apply the 40%-60% winner, 1.3-2.0 payoff-ratio, and three-times-cost average-trade filters together (p. 4).

## Caveats

The source is a promotional report from Profits Run and gives no dataset or independent validation for its cutoffs. Requiring no losing years, market-direction independence, and fixed metric ranges may exclude valid systems or reward backtest selection. The document does not discuss correlation between systems, changing market structure, leverage scaling, or statistical uncertainty.

## Who it is for

New system traders learning to read performance statistics skeptically.
