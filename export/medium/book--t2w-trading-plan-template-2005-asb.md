# Trading Plan Template: the trader's summary

*A fill-in template covering purpose, markets, tools, risk, exits, setups, journals, discipline, and ten golden trading rules.*

**Tim Wilcox** · 2005 · Trading Psychology & Discipline · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-29. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

Wilcox’s template turns a trading plan into a complete set of rules for the trader’s work, covering motivation, instruments, time frames, tools, market preparation, risk, exits, setups, journaling, and discipline (pp. 3–6, 9–27). It asks roughly 50 questions and stresses that a plan is a response map for outcomes the trader cannot control. The template includes examples of a 1%–3% opening-gap setup with a 50-day moving average and a breakout setup through the prior day’s high or low, but these are illustrative answers rather than a tested system (pp. 23–24).

## Key points

- A plan separates a complete trading life framework from an individual entry/exit strategy (p. 4).
- Risk and money management are treated as the “goalie” of the eleven-unit plan (p. 6).
- Every setup should define its characteristics and be tested before live use (pp. 23–24).
- Example strategies are a gap retracement for non-trending markets and a breakout for trending markets (p. 23).
- The template asks traders to specify whether losing trades exit before the stop, and what exact signals close winners or partial positions (pp. 21–23).
- Post-close review records entry, exit, stop, target, support/resistance, duration, and lessons (pp. 24–25).
- Discipline sanctions escalate from one day off after one rule breach, to two days after two breaches, and an indefinite pause after three (pp. 25–26).
- The ten golden rules include capital preservation, always setting a stop, cutting losses, avoiding revenge and averaging down, keeping records, simplicity, and trading the plan (pp. 27–28).

## Actionable rules

1. Write a plan covering purpose, market, time frame, tools, risk, exit, setup, entry, review, and discipline before live trading (pp. 3–6, 9–27).
2. Test every strategy by backtest, paper trade, or small forward test before committing capital (p. 25).
3. Always set a stop before opening a position where practicable; never average down or chase losses (pp. 27–28).
4. After one, two, or three rule breaches, pause for one day, two days, or indefinitely until the cause is addressed (pp. 25–26).

## Caveats

The document supplies a planning framework, not a validated strategy or universal risk percentage. The illustrative setup numbers are placeholders and must not be mistaken for recommendations (pp. 23–24). Its examples and market assumptions date from 2005.

## Who it is for

Any trader who needs to convert vague intentions into reviewable rules and a repeatable journal process.
