# Secrets of the Millionaire Traders, Volume I: the trader's summary

*Twenty-five interview-derived rules emphasize small starts, adequate margin, breakout confirmation, no averaging down, and disciplined exits.*

**Netfutures and Paul Handforth** · Trading Psychology & Discipline · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-27. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This report distills 25 rules attributed to successful-trader interviews. It emphasizes capital preservation: risk disposable funds, start small, avoid overcommitment, prepare before market hours, and stand aside when uncertain (pp. 4–13). Its breakout rules use opening, daily, weekly, and monthly references; position rules favor staged entries, decreasing-size pyramids, no additions to losers, and short loss duration (pp. 17–24). These are advice and anecdotes, not a performance study.

## Key points

- Start with paper trades and small exposure; a futures example suggests one e-mini or 1,000–3,000 bushels of grain (p. 6).
- Keep roughly three times the margin required for a position to avoid forced liquidation (p. 7).
- If 85% of advisory services are bullish, the report treats that as overbought; below 25% bullish is an oversold sentiment clue (p. 11).
- Trade liquid contract months and avoid markets where low volume makes exits difficult (p. 15).
- Breakouts of opening, weekly, and monthly ranges are progressively longer-horizon direction clues. The prior-day section inconsistently refers to both the previous close and the previous trading range (pp. 17–19).
- Pyramiding should add no more contracts than already open; an example scales 4 contracts to 3, then 2, then 1 (p. 19).
- Never add to a losing position; one interview rule limits a losing trade to two or three days and never across a weekend (pp. 21, 24).

## Actionable rules

1. Paper trade first, then start with the smallest practical contract or lot and maintain about three times required margin (pp. 6–7).
2. For the opening range, buy an upside break and sell a downside break. Apply the same direction logic to weekly and monthly highs or lows; test the daily version separately because its text alternates between the prior close and prior range (pp. 17–19).
3. The report suggests building the intended position in four or more installments after price confirms the initial entry. Add no more contracts at once than are already open; its example scales 4-3-2-1 (pp. 19–20).
4. Do not add to losers; cut losing positions short, and avoid carrying them more than two or three days or over a weekend (pp. 21–24).

## Caveats

The report does not identify interview sample size, selection criteria, or audited results. Thresholds such as 85%/25% sentiment and three-times margin are heuristics. Its prior-day breakout rule is internally inconsistent about whether the reference is the previous close or the full range (p. 18). Breakout rules omit exact stops, targets, and sizing formulas, and futures liquidity varies by contract and era.

## Who it is for

Beginning futures and swing traders who need a concise behavioral checklist before building a tested system.
