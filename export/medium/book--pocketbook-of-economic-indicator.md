# The Pocketbook Of Economic Indicators: the trader's summary

*Quick-reference glossary of 20 major U.S. economic indicators (release schedule, source, and market importance) used to interpret Fed policy and macro releases.*

**Manuel Jesus-Backus** · 2002 · Forex Mechanics & Macro Drivers · beginner

*Source coverage: partial. PDF pages inspected: 7, 14, 17, 23 and the indicator entries checked against the note's thresholds. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A plain-language reference defining the 20 U.S. economic indicators most followed by investors — from the Beige Book and ISM Manufacturing Index to CPI, PPI, GDP, and the Employment Situation. Each entry gives a definition, why it matters for rate/market expectations, source agency, release timing/frequency, and typical revision size. The framing: the Fed watches these for inflation/deflation signals and adjusts rates accordingly, which moves bond, stock, and currency markets.

## Key points

- **Beige Book** — Fed's anecdotal regional summary, 8x/year, 2 weeks before each FOMC meeting; not revised.
- **ISM Manufacturing / Services, Chicago PMI, Philadelphia Fed** — diffusion indices: above 50 = expansion, below 50 = contraction; ISM Manufacturing called "the king" of manufacturing indices.
- **CPI and PPI** — the "core" reading (ex food/energy) is watched for underlying inflation trend; core PPI is framed as a precursor to CPI inflation.
- **Employment Situation** — released first Friday of the month, 8:30am ET; called the single most closely watched release; "full employment" framed as 5.5-6.0% unemployment.
- **GDP** — quarterly, 3rd/4th week at 8:30am ET, with two subsequent revisions; "healthy" growth framed as 2.0-2.5%.
- **Initial Claims** — weekly (Thursday 8:30am ET); a 30,000+ move in the 4-week average signals a substantial labor-market shift.
- **Industrial Production/Capacity Utilization** — utilization above 85% flagged as inflationary.
- Housing Starts, Home Sales, Durable Goods, Retail Sales, Personal Income, Trade Balance, Consumer Confidence, and ECI are each covered with source, timing, and revision notes.

## Actionable rules

None given — this is a reference glossary of what each indicator measures, its release schedule, and typical interpretation, not a trading rule set. A trader can use the release calendar and the "above/below 50" and "core vs. headline" framing as inputs into a news-aware process, but no specific entries, exits, or sizing are prescribed.

## Caveats

Data is dated to the early 2000s: some agency names have since changed (e.g., NAPM renamed ISM), and numeric thresholds ("full employment" at 5.5-6.0%, "healthy" GDP at 2.0-2.5%) reflect that era's Fed reaction function. Release days/times should be re-verified against current BLS/BEA/Fed schedules.

## Who it is for

Traders and analysts who need a fast, plain-English primer on what each major U.S. macro release measures and why it moves markets, as groundwork before building a news/macro-driven approach.
