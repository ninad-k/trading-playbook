# Study Guide for Come Into My Trading Room: the trader's summary

*Exercises and case studies that test Elder's three-part discipline of mind, method, and money management.*

**Alexander Elder** · 2002 · Trading Psychology & Discipline · intermediate

*Source coverage: partial. PDF pages inspected: 1-18, 63-70, 130-154 (the contents and organisation, the Advanced Concepts question block and the answer/rating-scale section). These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

Alexander Elder's companion workbook turns *Come Into My Trading Room* into scored questions, answer explanations, and chart case studies. The organizing framework is “Mind, Method, Money”: emotional discipline, a defined analytical process, and survival-oriented risk control. The reviewed answers focus on channels, stops, account risk, records, and multiple-timeframe analysis. They are most useful as an audit of whether a trader can apply rules consistently rather than merely recognize terminology (PDF pp. 12-18, 130-154).

## Key points

- A trade should have an estimated target and stop before entry so expected reward can be compared with risk (PDF p. 130).
- Channels frame price relative to value; the guide says a well-drawn channel should contain roughly 95% of prices, while acknowledging exceptions (PDF pp. 130-131).
- Stops require both technical placement and money-management sizing and should be entered promptly; mental stops are reserved for demonstrably disciplined traders (PDF p. 131).
- The 2% Rule caps total intended loss on one trade, including commissions and slippage, at 2% of account equity (PDF pp. 137-139).
- The 6% Rule halts new trading when monthly realized losses plus open risk reach 6% of starting equity (PDF pp. 139-141).
- A spreadsheet, equity curve, chart diary, and next-day action plan turn experience into reviewable evidence (PDF pp. 142-149).

## Actionable rules

1. Calculate per-unit risk from planned entry to stop, add estimated costs, then reduce size until total trade risk is below 2% of equity.
2. At the start of each month, record equity. Reject any new trade that would put realized monthly losses plus risk on open positions above the 6% ceiling. If equity actually falls 6% below its starting level, the source says to stop trading for the rest of the month (PDF pp. 139-141).
3. Prepare decisions when markets are closed and document the signals, order, stop, target, and invalidation point.
4. Review weekly, daily, and intraday charts for strategy and tactics, combining trend-following and oscillator evidence rather than relying on one indicator (PDF pp. 152-154).

## Structure of the workbook

The guide is a 100-question scored quiz in two parts — Part One poses the questions, Part Two gives the answers with per-chapter rating scales and a points table that can be re-scored across five trials. The questions map one-for-one onto the parent book's chapters: Financial Trading for Babes in the Woods (Q1-8), Mind - The Disciplined Trader (Q9-18), Basic Charting (Q19-23), Indicators - Five Bullets to a Clip (Q24-39), Trading (Q40-47), Day-Trading (Q48-53), Advanced Concepts (Q54-67), Money Management (Q68-82) and The Organized Trader (Q83-100).

The Advanced Concepts block is built around Elder's Impulse System: matching exercises pair a rising EMA with bullish inertia, a rising MACD-Histogram with growing bullish momentum, a falling EMA with bearish inertia and a falling MACD-Histogram with growing bearish momentum, while flagging that the hardest part of momentum trading is knowing when to exit and that waiting for confirmation reduces a trade's profitability (PDF pp. 63-64). Later questions in the same block present a weekly uptrend alongside a daily chart and ask the reader to label the signals on it.

## Caveats

This is a focused review of the introduction, advanced answers, money-management answers, and case-study instructions. It assumes access to the main book, and the numerical risk limits are Elder's framework rather than guarantees of suitability.

## Who it is for

Traders who want structured drills for position sizing, record keeping, and rule compliance.
