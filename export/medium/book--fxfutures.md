# E-Forex: Currency Trading for the Rest of Us: the trader's summary

*2001 Futures magazine feature explaining the then-new retail e-forex market: how spot forex differs from currency futures, its leverage, and basic risk-management habits.*

**Abe Cofnas** · 2001 · Forex Mechanics & Macro Drivers · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-6. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A December 2001 Futures magazine article by Abe Cofnas introducing retail "e-forex" (direct electronic spot currency trading) as it looked at the industry's birth: how it differs mechanically from currency futures, why leverage and 24-hour access made it attractive, and practical guidance on position sizing, stops, and using demo accounts to keep learning.

## Key points

- **No official spot price** — unlike futures, spot forex quotes are negotiated broker-by-broker; there is no single consolidated tape and no public volume data.
- **Leverage** — e-forex platforms commonly offered 50:1 to 100:1 leverage, letting a trader control a $100,000 lot with about $2,000 (2%) in the account.
- **Retail options access is absent** — OTC spot FX options were limited to large accounts; most retail e-forex firms could not offer exchange-listed currency options.
- **Currencies trend more persistently than stocks or commodities** because they reflect slow-moving macro/interest-rate differentials rather than fast-changing factors like weather.
- **Interest-rate differentials** between central banks (Fed, ECB, BoJ) are called the most important external driver of currency direction.
- **Gold as a cross-check** — a rising gold price in a given currency is described as a signal of weakness in that currency and of disinflation/deflation trends generally.
- **News access asymmetry** — retail traders lacked real-time institutional newsfeeds (Reuters/Bloomberg), putting them at an information disadvantage around news events.
- **Demo trading pitfalls** — traders often do well in demo accounts but lose money live because paper trading removes real fear/greed.

## Actionable rules

1. Position size: trading a single lot is the most common beginner mistake because it forces an all-or-nothing exit decision under emotional pressure; consider multiple lots so one lot can be closed on a rule/technical basis and another on discretion.
2. Use wider stops than instinct suggests — the article cites a 50-pip stop (~$500 value on a standard lot) as a starting reference to avoid being stopped out by normal volatility.
3. If scalping/momentum trading currencies, exit quickly when wrong — volume data (used elsewhere to confirm scalps) is not available in spot forex.
4. Keep a demo account running continuously (not just pre-launch) to test new ideas such as bigger size or wider stops, since forex trading is described as an ongoing learning process.
5. No entry signal, indicator parameter, or profit-target rule is specified; this is a market-structure and habits article, not a trading system.

## Caveats

This is a 2001 trade-magazine piece describing the retail e-forex industry at its launch; leverage levels (50-100:1), the "no volume data" claim, and the broker landscape it describes (dial-up-era platforms, named vendors/brokers, per-firm quote spreads) are all dated relative to post-2010 regulatory leverage caps (e.g., US retail caps near 50:1 or lower) and modern ECN/aggregated pricing. It also contains vendor advertising content (a boxed ad for a named broker) that should be read as promotional, not evaluative.

## Who it is for

Readers who want historical context on how retail forex trading began and why it differs structurally from futures trading; general risk-management habits (position sizing, wider stops, demo practice) still apply, but the market-structure specifics are outdated.
