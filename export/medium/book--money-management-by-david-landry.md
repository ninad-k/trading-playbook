# Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits: the trader's summary

*Four-part TradingMarkets series on drawdown math, 17 practical money-management guidelines, and interviews with professional traders on risk control.*

**Dave Landry** · 1999 · Money Management & Position Sizing · intermediate

*Source coverage: partial. PDF pages inspected: 2, 10 (the drawdown arithmetic and the interviewees' risk-per-trade ranges). These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This four-part series opens with drawdown mechanics (why recovering from a loss needs a disproportionately larger gain), lays out 17 general money-management guidelines, then closes with interviews of professional traders (Manuel Ochoa, Dr. Paul Ruggieri, Jeff Cooper, Kevin Haggerty) on sizing positions and placing stops. The through-line: controlling risk matters more than the entry method or system itself.

## Key points

- **Drawdown recovery is nonlinear** — a 10% loss needs an 11.11% gain to recover; 50% needs 100%; 90% needs 900% (full table given).
- **Risk per trade** — interviewees cite ranges from ~0.25-0.5% (Jeff Cooper, sized to a fixed dollar amount) up to 2.5-5% (Manuel Ochoa: "no professional risks more than 5%").
- **Stop placement** — several interviewees stress that stops too tight relative to actual volatility "guarantee" a loss; size the stop to 3-4 days' average range, then size the position to fit.
- **Portfolio risk** — cap total open-position risk at 20% of equity; treat correlated positions (e.g., crude oil, heating oil, unleaded gas) as one larger position.
- **Capitalization** — prepare for real drawdowns of 2-5x a system's historical backtested drawdown; expect real profits near half of backtested profits.
- **2-for-1 money management** — once profit exceeds initial risk, exit half the position and move the stop on the remainder to breakeven.
- **Pyramiding** — add only to profitable positions, put on the largest tranche first, and keep total position risk within the same caps.

## Actionable rules

1. Risk no more than ~2% of equity per trade as a general guideline (professional interviewees range 0.25-5%).
2. Cap total open-position risk at 20% of equity.
3. Target a minimum 2:1 reward-to-risk ratio, preferably 3:1+.
4. Use real stop orders, never mental stops.
5. Never add to a losing position ("averaging down").
6. Size stops to recent volatility (e.g., 3-4 day average range), not an arbitrary tight number.
7. Keep maximum acceptable drawdown to 20-25%.
8. Capitalize a systematic strategy at 2-5x its historical worst drawdown before trading it live.
9. More active traders should risk a smaller percentage per trade than infrequent position traders.

## Caveats

This is a magazine-style series, not a formal study — guidelines are qualitative "rules of thumb" from experience and interviews, and numeric recommendations from different traders conflict (0.25% vs. 5% risk per trade), reflecting different trading styles (day trading vs. swing/position) rather than one prescribed number.

## Who it is for

Traders who already have an entry method and want grounding in drawdown/risk-of-ruin math, per-trade and portfolio risk caps, and stop-placement logic, illustrated with how several named professionals actually manage risk.
