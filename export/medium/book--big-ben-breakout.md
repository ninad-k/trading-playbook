# Day Trading the FX Market: The Big Ben Breakout: the trader's summary

*Currency Trader magazine strategy article: a GBP/USD opening-range-breakout system that trades the stop-hunt reversal around the Frankfurt/London open.*

**Kristian Kerr** · 2004 · Day Trading & Scalping · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-4. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A September 2004 Currency Trader magazine article describing "Big Ben," a day-trading strategy for GBP/USD (cable) built around the low-liquidity-to-high-liquidity transition when the Frankfurt/London forex session opens around 1 a.m. ET. The setup exploits stop-running on both sides of an early, thin opening range before the market makes its first real directional move of the day, similar in logic to S&P opening-range-breakout strategies.

## Key points

- **Why GBP/USD** — pound/dollar trades lightly in Asian hours (near zero share of volume) and then jumps to a large share of turnover once London opens, producing a "real" volatile market open worth trading.
- **Stop-hunt logic** — interbank dealers use overnight order-flow intelligence to trigger stops on both sides of the market around the Frankfurt/London open, clearing the book before the day's genuine directional move begins.
- **Setup sequence** — the pair must first make a new range low, then reverse to a new range high, then reverse again below the original low, before the short trade triggers.
- **Wide-range variant** — the same setup criteria remain valid even when the opening range is unusually wide, as shown in a case where price traded up 26 pips then reversed 65 pips before the short trigger.
- **Trade management** — scale out half the position once price has moved by the same distance as the initial stop, then trail the remainder.
- **Direction-agnostic** — rules are given for the short side but the strategy is stated to work symmetrically reversed for longs.

## Actionable rules

1. Setup: after the Frankfurt/London open (~1 a.m. ET), GBP/USD makes a new range low at least 25 pips below the opening price.
2. The pair then reverses and trades at least 25 pips above the opening price.
3. The pair reverses again, trading back below the intraday low from step 1.
4. Entry: sell a breakout of at least 7 pips below the established London low (reverse all rules for a long setup).
5. Stop: initial protective stop no more than 40 pips above the entry price.
6. Management: once price has moved in your favor by a distance equal to the entry-to-stop distance, cover half the position and trail a stop on the remainder.

## Caveats

Article is from 2004 and references FX Trek charting software and specific 2004 trade examples; broker spreads, liquidity patterns, and session volumes may have shifted since (notably with the rise of algorithmic/HFT liquidity provision). No formal backtest statistics, win rate, or sample size are given — only two illustrative trade examples. The 24-hour currency-quoting conventions and base-currency exceptions described are basic background, not part of the trading edge.
