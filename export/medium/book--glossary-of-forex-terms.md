# Glossary of Forex Terms: the trader's summary

*A reference glossary covering FX quotes, orders, margin, derivatives, macro terms, and technical-analysis vocabulary.*

**Forex-trader.com compilation** · Forex Mechanics & Macro Drivers · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-11. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This glossary defines the vocabulary needed to read and discuss foreign-exchange transactions. The early entries cover bid, ask, spread, base and quote currencies, dealer versus broker, currency risk, cross rates, and the distinction between spot, forward, and futures contracts (pp. 1–5). It explains that a margin deposit is a performance bond enabling leverage, and that a margin call or automatic liquidation can follow a shortfall (pp. 6–7). Order terms include limit, stop-loss, OCO, open, and good-til-cancelled orders (pp. 6–9). The glossary also defines rollover and rollover rates, settlement and value dates, pips, swaps, forwards, and the relationship between spot and forward pricing (pp. 4–10). Macro and institutional terms include central bank, economic indicator, balance of trade, inflation, political risk, LIBOR, and fixed versus floating exchange rates (pp. 1–6). Technical terms include candlestick and bar charts, trendlines, support, resistance, stochastics, volatility, volume, and whipsaw (pp. 1, 8–11). One useful mechanics example says a USD/JPY quote of 131.40/131.45 has a five-pip spread, which must be recovered by favorable movement before profit (p. 9).

## Key points

- Pair direction always represents buying one currency and selling another (p. 2).
- Margin and leverage change required collateral, while spread, commission, rollover, and slippage determine trading cost (pp. 4, 6–9).
- Definitions are educational and sometimes repeat or differ in wording across entries.

## Actionable rules

1. Read every pair as a simultaneous purchase of one currency and sale of the other, and record the bid, ask, spread, and value date (pp. 1–3, 9–10).
2. Treat margin as a performance bond for leveraged exposure; the glossary says a shortfall may trigger a margin call or liquidation (pp. 6–7).
3. Use the entries as terminology only and verify broker-specific order, margin, and rollover behavior; the glossary provides no trading strategy.

## Caveats

The compilation has no formal citation audit and contains dated or simplified statements, including currency membership, market conventions, and regulatory terminology. It is a vocabulary aid rather than a trading method.

## Who it is for

Beginners learning FX mechanics and reading platform or research documentation.
