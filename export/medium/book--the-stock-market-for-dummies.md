## Key points

- A stock represents partial ownership in a company; investors buy stocks for potential dividends and long-term appreciation versus bank deposits or bonds.
- Technicians trade frequently based on price patterns; fundamentalists hold for long-term growth based on earnings, dividends, and book value.
- Penny stocks: small, low-value companies with little chance of major growth.
- Growth stocks: higher potential but higher risk, from less-established companies.
- Blue chip stocks: well-established, high-priced, low-volatility names (examples given: McDonald's, GM, Coca-Cola).
- A "two-for-one" stock split doubles share count and halves per-share price; a reverse split does the opposite — total holder value is unchanged either way.
- Common stock holders get paid dividends after preferred stock holders but have higher long-term gain potential; preferred stock pays a fixed dividend and is paid out first if a company fails.
- Stockbrokers offer research and advice; discount brokers simply execute orders without advice.
