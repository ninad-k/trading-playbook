## Overview

A 12-chapter, self-described "masterguide" that builds order-flow trading from the matching engine upward: how the limit order book and priority rules work (Ch1), Auction Market Theory and TPO Market Profile (Ch2), Volume Profile (Ch3), footprint and delta mechanics (Ch4), key reference levels and VWAP (Ch5), DOM and liquidity events (Ch6), a strategy-playbook layer (Ch7), pseudocode for computing every indicator in the book (Ch8), tape-reading and instrument specifics (Ch9), market context and internals (Ch10), risk/sizing/psychology (Ch11), and three integrated case studies plus a glossary, formula sheet and master checklist (Ch12). Every sub-topic uses the same four-part scaffold — Core Definition & Objective, Market Mechanics, Visual Blueprint (an annotated ASCII diagram), and Actionable Execution Rules — and almost every worked example uses the E-mini S&P 500 (ES, 0.25-point tick, $12.50/tick). This is the only book in the library that treats market microstructure, auction theory and order flow as a single, deep, mechanically-specified discipline rather than a side note to chart patterns, and it is reviewed here at Tier A accordingly, with three companion system pages (volume profile, footprint & delta, VWAP & session levels) breaking out its most load-bearing methods.

## Core thesis

A market is a continuous double auction searching, trade by trade, for the price that clears the most volume; the last traded price is a stale receipt, and the real, live state of the market is the order book plus the developing record of where the auction has actually transacted (TPO/volume profile) and who has been aggressive (footprint/delta). No single tool is sufficient alone: price advertises, time regulates, and volume validates (the book's restatement of Auction Market Theory), and a tape-reading signal — an imbalance, an absorption print, a delta divergence — only becomes an executable edge once it is (1) classified against the day's regime (trend vs. balance, set within the first 60 minutes via Initial Balance width, open type and overnight inventory), (2) located against value (VAH/VAL/POC, VWAP and its bands, structural levels), and (3) confirmed by an independent second measurement (CVD, delta flip, volume expansion) before an order is placed. This "Context → Location → Trigger → Confirmation → Execution" pipeline (Ch7.1) is the book's organizing idea and the thread that ties the mechanics chapters to the strategy and risk chapters.

## Key concepts

- **Double auction & limit order book (LOB)** — many buyers and sellers quoting simultaneously; price discovery happens when a bid and offer cross; the aggressor pays the spread, the passive side absorbs it.
- **TPO (Time Price Opportunity) profile & Auction Market Theory** — Steidlmayer/Dalton's framework classifying rotations as balance or imbalance, initiative or responsive, using Initial Balance, open type, single prints, poor highs/lows and excess ("tails").
- **Volume Profile: POC, Value Area, HVN/LVN** — a volume-at-price histogram; POC is the mode (fairest price, a magnet); Value Area is the 70%-of-volume band built outward from POC two rows at a time; High-Volume Nodes slow price (agreement), Low-Volume Nodes accelerate it (rejection).
- **Naked/Virgin POC (NPOC)** — a completed session's POC that price has not since retraded, treated as an unfinished, statistically likely-to-be-revisited magnet.
- **Footprint & Delta** — a candle exploded into bid-vs-ask volume per price; Delta = Ask volume − Bid volume; Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD) is delta's running sum, whose slope (not any one bar) is the signal.
- **Imbalance & Absorption** — a diagonal bid/ask comparison flags one-sided aggression (default 300% threshold, min-volume filter, 3+ stacked = institutional zone); absorption is the mechanical opposite — a passive order soaking up aggression with no price progress.
- **Trapped traders / finished vs. unfinished auctions** — late breakout entries whose own stops fuel the reversal; a literal zero print on one side at a bar's extreme marks a resolved ("finished") auction, both sides present marks an unresolved one likely to be revisited.
- **Structural levels & confluence** — PDH/PDL/PDC, PWH/PWL, PMH/PML, ONH/ONL, settlement, prior VAH/VAL/POC and IB high/low, ranked by priority and scored by how many independently-derived levels cluster within a tick tolerance.
- **VWAP & standard-deviation bands** — the volume-weighted average price; slope shows net order-flow direction, price-vs-VWAP shows who is "winning" the session; ±1/2/3σ bands are read as mean-reversion pivots in balance regimes and as ride-the-band continuation cues in trend regimes.
- **Premium / Discount** — price above the prior Value Area High is "premium" (sell-side edge), below Value Area Low is "discount" (buy-side edge); crossed with VWAP position this produces a 3×3 context grid.
- **R-multiple, expectancy, Kelly, risk of ruin** — the book's entire risk chapter is denominated in R (dollar risk defined by the structural stop), with expectancy, fractional-Kelly sizing, drawdown/recovery convexity and risk-of-ruin tables built on top of it.

## Rules and setups

1. **Value Area algorithm** (Ch3): start at the POC row; repeatedly add whichever side's next two rows carry more volume; stop once ≥70% of session volume is enclosed. The "80% Rule": if price opens inside the prior session's Value Area and holds inside it through two 30-minute periods, historical odds favor a full trade through to the opposite Value Area edge (the book cites this figure without showing the underlying sample).
2. **Footprint imbalance** (Ch4): flag a cell when Ask(P) ÷ Bid(P−1 tick) ≥ 3.00 (or the mirror for sells) AND the larger side clears a minimum volume filter; three or more consecutive same-side flags are a "stacked" institutional-grade zone, materially stronger than one isolated print.
3. **Absorption vs. initiative test** (Ch4 §5): both start as a large, lopsided-volume row; they're told apart only by the next 1-2 bars — initiative continues with the same-sign delta and expanding volume, absorption stalls or reverses with delta flipping sign and volume dropping.
4. **Day-classification pipeline** (Ch7.1): by 10:30 AM ET, combine Initial Balance range (vs. its 20-day average), Steidlmayer open type (Drive / Test-Drive / Rejection-Reverse / Auction-in-Range) and overnight inventory to label the day trend-prone or balance-prone before taking any directional trade.
5. **VWAP-band regime rule** (Ch5.6): classify balance (flat VWAP slope, stable σ) vs. trend (rising/falling slope, expanding σ) before acting on a band touch — fade ±2σ only in a confirmed balance regime; in a trend regime, use a ±1σ pullback as a with-trend entry and never fade ±2σ/±3σ.
6. **Position sizing** (Ch11.2): contracts = (Equity × risk%) ÷ (stop_ticks × tick_value), stop distance always taken from market structure (an absorption extreme, an LVN, a single print) rather than chosen first; the book caps practical risk at roughly a quarter to a half of the computed Kelly fraction.
7. **Daily circuit breaker** (Ch11.4): a hard daily loss limit of 2-3R (about 2-3 full stop-outs at current size) and weekly limit of 6-8R; a softer "two-strikes" rule forces a 15-30 minute cooling-off period after two consecutive losers before a third attempt is allowed.

## Risk and money management

This is the one book in the library that treats risk as a full quantitative subsystem rather than a chapter of maxims. It defines 1R directly from the structural stop (stop_ticks × tick_value × contracts), then builds expectancy, E = (Win% × AvgWin_R) − (Loss% × AvgLoss_R), as the actual object being managed — a worked example (42% win rate, +1.8R average winner, −1.0R average loser) nets E = +0.176R/trade, illustrating why order-flow edges are designed to be small, frequent and mechanically repeated rather than large and rare. Four sizing models are given side by side — fixed-fractional, fixed-ratio (Ryan Jones), Kelly/fractional-Kelly, and ATR-based volatility-adjusted sizing — with a full worked comparison across ES, MES, NQ and GC and an explicit warning to round contract counts down, not up. Drawdown mathematics is treated seriously: the recovery-percentage table (a 20% drawdown needs +25% to recover, a 50% drawdown needs +100%) and a risk-of-ruin table driven by win rate and risk-per-trade are both given in full, alongside the blunt observation that at exactly 50% win rate and a 1:1 payoff, ruin is certain regardless of position sizing — sizing cannot manufacture an edge that doesn't exist. Daily/weekly circuit breakers (2-3R / 6-8R) and a "two-strikes" cooling-off rule are specified as mechanical, non-negotiable halts, with position size stepped down immediately on a breach and restored only gradually after a validated recovery. A separate "demo to live to scale" ladder (Ch11.9) gates every increase in size behind a minimum trade count and a statistical quality bar (e.g., ≥100 sim trades with expectancy distinguishable from zero before micro-contract live trading), with automatic, symmetric demotion triggers rather than discretionary overrides.

## Psychology and discipline

The psychology chapter (Ch11.8) is order-flow-specific rather than generic trading psychology: because a footprint, DOM, delta bar, volume profile and VWAP band can each flash something "actionable" within the same 30 seconds, its central fix is a pre-committed signal hierarchy (one primary trigger per setup, everything else a confirming/disqualifying filter only) rather than weighing five live streams by whichever is most visually salient. It names confirmation bias directly (reading a neutral footprint print as "more absorption" because a position is already on), the specific tilt trigger of being stopped by a brief liquidity sweep just before price reverses in the original direction (the "I was right, only unlucky" trap, answered procedurally with the same two-strikes cooldown regardless of how justified the next entry feels), and the twin failure modes of chasing every setup for fear of missing the trend day versus hesitating on setups that already meet the written criteria. Its sharpest distinction is process versus outcome: a "good decision" is one correctly derived from the pre-committed process regardless of result, and the book insists journaling grade the decision against the process (a scorecard of checklist items completed), not the P&L against the decision — because grading outcomes trains a trader to repeat lucky bad decisions and abandon unlucky good ones.

## Strengths and caveats

The book's real strength is structural completeness and internal consistency: every one of dozens of sub-topics is worked through the same four-part scaffold with a fully-computed numeric example (the Value Area two-row algorithm, the VWAP and volume-weighted σ worked by hand across six bars, the position-sizing table across four instruments, the recovery-percentage and risk-of-ruin tables), and the risk chapter in particular is more rigorous and more completely specified than the risk sections of most Tier A titles in this library. It is also, as far as this library's coverage goes, the only source that treats order flow, Auction Market Theory and market microstructure as one connected discipline rather than an appendix to chart patterns. The caveats are real, though. First, it is an instructional synthesis, not a track record: every "worked example" uses clean, round, invented numbers (a 42% win rate assumed to produce exactly +0.176R, a six-bar VWAP built from tidy hypothetical prices) — there is no live or backtested trade log anywhere in the book, and specific empirically-flavored claims ("NPOCs get filled a large majority of the time within 1-10 sessions," "the 80% rule," "roughly 6-7 consecutive losses is normal at a 42% win rate") are stated as settled facts with no cited sample, study, or data source behind them; treat every probability figure in this book as a plausible heuristic from the literature it is drawing on (classical Market Profile writing, standard order-flow-desk lore), not as a validated statistic. Second, the four-block template is applied so uniformly — even to purely definitional material — that some "Actionable Execution Rules" boxes are explicitly filler ("not applicable at this stage"), which padds length without adding information. Third, because the whole book is keyed to a single reference instrument (ES futures) with a literal exchange-supplied aggressor flag, its confident claims about delta reliability do not carry over cleanly to spot forex, CFDs or thinly-aggregated retail feeds — to its credit, Chapter 9 says this explicitly and in detail, rather than glossing over it. Finally, no author, publisher, or date is identified anywhere in the text; it reads as a purpose-built, AI- or ghost-written educational product rather than a text with an identifiable trading track record behind it, which is why it carries no personal-name byline in this note.

## Who should read it

Best suited to an intermediate-to-advanced trader who already reads a standard candlestick/indicator chart and wants a single, mechanically complete reference for footprint, volume profile, TPO and VWAP-based order-flow trading, ideally on a centralized-matching-engine instrument (CME futures) where the underlying aggressor-classification assumptions actually hold. It is a poor first book on markets — it assumes comfort with limit order books, moving averages and basic chart reading before Chapter 1 even starts — and its statistics should be treated as a structured hypothesis set to validate on your own data, not settled findings.
