# Managing Your Money: the trader's summary

*Active Trader magazine feature (July 2000) on trade-risk sizing: the 2%-of-equity rule, stop-based position sizing, Ed Seykota's 'core equity' concept, and a survey of money-management software of the era.*

**Gibbons Burke** · 2000 · Money Management & Position Sizing · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-6. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A feature article by Gibbons Burke from the July 2000 issue of Active Trader magazine, framing money management as the answer to "how much do I trade?" It contrasts buy-and-hold investors (who bet on an always-rising market) with active traders (who must actively manage per-trade risk), lays out the mechanics of sizing a position from a chosen stop-loss price and a maximum percentage of equity at risk, introduces Ed Seykota's "core equity" refinement, and closes with a table of money-management software/spreadsheet tools available at the time.

## Key points

- Money management is the "thermostat" that controls risk exposure; too little risk per trade (undertrading) fails to overcome costs, too much (overtrading) creates unsustainable drawdowns — there is a "sweet spot" between the two.
- Most top traders profiled in Schwager's "Market Wizards" books reportedly capped risk at under 2% of account equity per trade.
- Two ways to set a stop/exit price: (1) a technical/fundamental method that generates a signal price, or (2) a pure money-management stop when no technical opinion exists.
- "Core equity" (Ed Seykota's term) = total equity minus dollar risk currently open in all positions; sizing new trades off core equity automatically shrinks new position size as existing risk grows.
- Warns against the "house money" fallacy — treating unrealized gains as less painful to lose than original capital; a stop left unmoved as a winning position runs can let a 2%-of-equity risk balloon to 9% or more.
- Stops should only ever be moved to reduce risk (tightened in the trader's favor), never loosened.
- If a system's stops are chronically hit, the fix is to re-examine the system's rules — not to second-guess or skip individual stops.
- Lists contemporary risk-sizing software (Money Maximizer's "Size-It" tool, kNOW Software, Athena, Excel with live quotes) and reading including Ralph Vince's "The Mathematics of Money Management."

## Actionable rules

1. Cap risk per trade at roughly 2% of account equity (1% if lower confidence in the setup).
2. Set the stop price before entering, based on either a technical signal or a fixed money-management level.
3. Position size = (max dollar risk) / (entry price − stop price).
4. Size new trades against "core equity" (equity minus dollar risk on all open positions), not total account value, so risk-taking capacity shrinks automatically as exposure grows.
5. Once in profit, raise (never lower) the stop to lock in gains and free up core equity for new trades.
6. If a stop is hit, take the loss immediately — do not override or widen it.

## Caveats

Written in July 2000 for retail traders using early-2000s software and dial-up-era web tools; most of the named software vendors (Money Maximizer, kNOW Software/MoneySoft, Athena, Medved QuoteTracker) and their pricing are now defunct or obsolete, and the software table itself (page 4) is illegible in the source extraction. The 2% figure is presented as a commonly cited industry norm rather than something proven in the article itself.

## Who it is for

Traders who understand they need a risk framework but have never formalized position sizing, stop placement, or the "core equity" concept.
