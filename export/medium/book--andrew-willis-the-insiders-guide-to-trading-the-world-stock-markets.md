## Summary

A beginner-oriented online day-trading manual that first explains how the NYSE, NASDAQ (including Level II quotes), and London Stock Exchange actually work, then walks through the psychology of treating trading as a business rather than gambling, and finally lays out three trading styles — scalp, swing, and core — with specific entry, stop, and exit guidance for each. It closes with sections on fundamental vs. technical analysis, online broker/resource directories, and a market-indicator/terminology glossary. The tone is introductory and promotional in places (era-appropriate references to Level II and early online trading), but the trading-style rules are concrete enough to be directly usable.

## Key points

- Three basic trading styles: scalp (seconds to minutes), swing (days), and core (weeks to months), each requiring progressively more fundamental analysis and patience.
- Scalp trades typically target only 0.125–0.5 points; risk is capped at about 0.125 of the spread or less per trade.
- Scalp entries: buy 0.125 above a resistance breakout, or split the intended position — half before breakout, half at breakout — to balance timing risk.
- Swing trading works off daily/intraday chart patterns (15–30 minute intervals) and typical multi-day up/down cycles (e.g., three days up, two days down).
- Swing stop-loss: set the initial stop ¼ point below the day's entry low, and trail it up each day to ¼ point below the new day's low as the trade progresses.
- Core trading requires separate accounts from scalp/swing activity, wider stops sized to realistic reward-to-risk ratios, and heavier reliance on fundamentals and dominant index trends (S&P 500, NASDAQ Composite, Dow).
- Shorting is only permitted on an uptick (specialists/market makers exempted); avoid shorting a stock purely because "it looks too high" — wait for a confirmed 30-minute low, and avoid scalping shorts as a beginner.
- Roughly 80% of people who attempt day trading fail or quit, largely from an inability to manage the emotional stress of risk, not from lack of a system.
- Never enter a trade with a poor risk-to-reward ratio (the book's example: risking two points to gain half a point is not worth it).
- Avoid trading illiquid names (example threshold cited: around 300,000 shares of daily volume or less).

## Actionable rules

1. Scalp trading: risk no more than ~0.125 of the spread per trade; take profits and flatten all positions before the close — never hold a scalp trade overnight.
2. Swing trading: initial stop = ¼ point below the entry day's low; trail the stop to ¼ point below each subsequent day's low as the position moves favorably.
3. Core trading: use wide, realistic stops set from technical resistance/support levels identified via daily and weekly charts; keep core positions in a separate account from short-term trades.
4. Before a scalp entry, check today's and yesterday's highs/lows, the opening gap, and yesterday's pivot levels; abandon a target if resistance is only ¼ to 1 point away.
5. Only short on an uptick, avoid pyramiding a short into strength, and require a confirmed 30-minute low before treating a stock as weakening.
6. Cut losses at the predetermined stop without hesitation ("small win is better than any loss"); do not widen a stop to avoid taking a loss.

## Caveats

Written for an earlier era of retail trading (NASDAQ Level II terminology, references circa the early-to-mid 2000s online-broker landscape) — some market-structure specifics (decimalized vs. fractional pricing conventions implied by "0.125" increments) and the online resource/web-site directory are dated. The book is a promotional-style manual rather than an academic or research-backed text; risk figures and win-rate claims are asserted, not backtested. No author biography or original publication year is given in the text itself.

## Who it is for

Beginner-to-intermediate retail day traders who want a plain-language walkthrough of market mechanics (NYSE/NASDAQ/LSE) plus a simple, rule-based starting framework for choosing between scalp, swing, and core trading styles.
