# Crossing Over to the Forex Market: the trader's summary

*A worked AUD/JPY case combines Fibonacci retracements and fan lines across the cross and its U.S.-dollar components.*

**Gary Tilkin** · 2002 · Forex Mechanics & Macro Drivers · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-3. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

Gary Tilkin explains cross-rate analysis through a 2001–02 AUD/JPY case. Instead of charting only the cross, he examines AUD/USD and USD/JPY, then uses Fibonacci retracements and fan lines to find levels where several analyses agree. The article argues that long-term extremes reduce market noise and that interest-rate evidence can validate, but should not replace, the technical case (PDF pp. 1–3). It is a single retrospective example rather than a tested system.

## Key points

- A cross-rate trade exchanges two currencies without requiring the U.S. dollar as either side; crosses also serve commercial hedging needs (PDF p. 1).
- For the monthly AUD/JPY move from ¥121.70 in 1990 to ¥56.13 in 2000, the article calculates 23.6%, 38.2%, and 50% resistance at ¥71.88, ¥81.62, and ¥89.64 (PDF pp. 1–2).
- Fibonacci fan lines add a time dimension by passing from the first extreme through 38.2%, 50%, and 61.8% divisions of a vertical line at the second extreme (PDF p. 2).
- A weekly fan-line break in November 2001 produced a worked long at ¥64.27. The 50% fan line and weekly/monthly retracements formed a ¥70.72–¥72.24 target zone, midpoint ¥71.48 (PDF p. 2).
- Component analysis supported the cross: AUD/USD had retracement targets of $0.5514 and $0.5745, while strengthening USD/JPY implied a weaker yen (PDF pp. 2–3).
- Japanese rates were below 2%, while rising Australian ten-year yields reinforced the bullish AUD/JPY interpretation; the author warns that yield preference alone is dangerous (PDF p. 3).

## Actionable rules

1. For a cross, calculate long-term Fibonacci retracements and fan lines on the cross and on both U.S.-dollar component pairs (PDF pp. 1–3).
2. After price breaks one fan or retracement level, use the next levels as candidate targets and favor zones where weekly and monthly levels cluster (PDF p. 2).
3. Define the target zone before entry so the trade can be judged on reward versus risk; the article does not supply the stop calculation (PDF pp. 2–3).
4. Use interest-rate direction as confirmation only when it agrees with the component and cross-rate charts (PDF p. 3).

## Caveats

The article reports one selected historical trade and no sample statistics, transaction costs, stop, or sizing rule. Its rate and price levels are historical. The repository’s `fx-crossing-over-to-the-forex-market-2` source is an exact SHA-1 duplicate, so this is the sole canonical note.

## Who it is for

Intermediate FX traders studying cross-rate confluence and multi-pair analysis.
