# Scalp Trading Methods: Six High-Probability Setups from the Pit: the trader's summary

*Six named S&P 500 futures scalp setups with exact entry, profit-target, and stop-loss numbers, including Linda Bradford Raschke's 'ANTI' pattern.*

**Kevin Ho** · 2003 · Day Trading & Scalping · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-4. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A short magazine article by floor trader Kevin Ho presenting six S&P 500 futures scalp setups, each with an explicit entry trigger, profit target, and stop-loss, grouped into time-sensitive, countertrend, and trend-continuation trades. The final pattern, "The ANTI," is credited to Linda Bradford Raschke. All six are extremely short-duration trades (often under a minute) requiring immediate stop discipline.

## Key points

- Philosophy: "a million trades to make a million dollars" — small, consistent profits with tight predefined risk, not home runs.
- Three categories: time-sensitive (opening range, 10am/3pm reversal windows), countertrend (fading trendless periods), and trend-continuation/retracement.
- Setups are informally back-tested on one week of August 2003 S&P 500 futures data, with reported win rates like 9/9, 14/15, and 10/14 trades.
- Overall accuracy across the six setups is reported near 75%, said to net roughly $200/day per single e-mini contract, scalable with more lots.
- Strong emphasis on cutting losses immediately; most setups auto-exit if unresolved within 30 seconds to 3 minutes.

## Actionable rules

1. Opening Range Scalp: after the first 15-min range forms, buy stop 2 ticks above its high or sell stop 2 ticks below its low; profit target 1 point; stop 1 point or 1 minute elapsed; on stop-out, fade the opposite breakout at double size.
2. 10 O'Clock Jiggle: after two 15-min closes from the open, if price sits near the intraday high, sell 1 tick below the last bar's low (or buy 1 tick above the low near an intraday low); target 1.5 points; stop 1 point or 1 minute; cancel unfilled orders after 10:30am.
3. 3 O'Clock Jiggle: same rules as the 10 O'Clock Jiggle, triggered by the 30 minutes before the 3pm bond-market close; cancel unfilled orders after 3:30pm.
4. Penny Pincher: on 10-min candles, trade bullish/bearish engulfing patterns only in the first and last trading hour; enter at market on the engulfment; target 1 point; stop 1 point or 30 seconds elapsed.
5. Standard Deviation Scalp: on a 5-min chart with a 10-bar moving average and ±1 standard-deviation bands, buy dips to the lower band in a rising-band uptrend or sell rallies to the upper band in a falling-band downtrend; target 2 points or the opposite band; stop 1.25 points or a band-slope reversal.
6. The ANTI: on 5-min bars, use a 7-period slow stochastic with its own 10-period moving average as a trend filter; buy when the stochastic crosses above its rising average, sell when it crosses below its falling average; target 2 points; stop 1.5 points or no progress within 3 minutes.

## Caveats

Sample size is tiny (one week of data per setup), so the 75% accuracy and dollar figures are illustrative, not a rigorous backtest, and predate the shift to decimal/finer tick sizing in equity index futures. All setups assume very fast execution unavailable to every retail trader.

## Who it is for

Active intraday futures scalpers, especially in S&P 500 / e-mini products, who want concrete, mechanical entry/exit/stop rules rather than discretionary setups.
