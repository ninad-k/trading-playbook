# Foreign Exchange as the Trader's Alternative: the trader's summary

*Stocks & Commodities magazine reprint (GAIN Capital) pitching forex as a portfolio diversifier and illustrating trendline breaks and MA-crossover entries on hourly charts.*

**Mark Galant** · 2002 · Forex Mechanics & Macro Drivers · beginner

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-4. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A short magazine article (reprinted from Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities, credited to GAIN Capital's Mark Galant) making the case for retail forex as a diversification vehicle and short-term trading vehicle, then illustrating basic technical setups — trendline breaks, a moving-average crossover system, and MACD/RSI divergence confirmation — on hourly FX charts.

## Key points

- Argues forex offers a favorable risk/return profile versus equities: cites 2001 annualized volatility of ~21.5% for the DJIA vs. ~10.5% for USD/JPY, with USD/JPY volatility comparable to 30-year bond futures (~10%).
- States roughly 80% of currency transactions last 7 days or less, and over 40% last fewer than 2 days — framing forex as inherently short-term and technically driven.
- States roughly 85% of daily forex volume involves the seven "major" currencies (USD, JPY, EUR, GBP, CHF, CAD, AUD).
- Illustrates a trendline break as an entry trigger: a broken uptrend support line becomes resistance, and a close below the trendline for at least two consecutive days signals reversal.
- Illustrates a 10-period/40-period moving-average crossover system on an hourly EUR/USD chart: enter long when the 10-period MA crosses above the 40-period MA, exit when it crosses back below.
- Shows an RSI/MACD bearish divergence (price rising while RSI and MACD fall) as a confirming signal ahead of a trendline break.
- Notes central bank intervention (e.g., Bank of Japan) can move FX short-term but historically has not permanently overridden the underlying trend.
- Lists forex's structural selling points: 24-hour trading, deep liquidity (over $1 trillion/day), and narrow (commonly 5 pips or less) dealing spreads.

## Actionable rules

1. Enter short when price closes below an established uptrend support trendline for at least two consecutive days (former support becomes new resistance).
2. Moving-average crossover system: enter long when the 10-period MA crosses above the 40-period MA; exit (or reverse) when the 10-period MA crosses back below the 40-period MA.
3. Treat RSI/MACD divergence from price as a confirming filter before acting on a trendline break, not a standalone signal.

## Caveats

This is a promotional article from a retail forex broker (GAIN Capital) illustrating generic technical-analysis concepts already common to equities and futures; there is no backtested performance data, and the risk/return comparison uses a single year (2001) of volatility data, which is not a robust basis for a general claim about forex's risk profile.

## Who it is for

Beginners considering forex as a new market who want a short, broker-authored illustration of how standard trendline, moving-average, and divergence techniques transfer to FX charts.
