# Your Trading Plan: the trader's summary

*Short advisory-firm guide on building a personal trading plan, matching method to personality, and ten types of market action to avoid.*

**Windsor Advisory Services** · Trading Psychology & Discipline · beginner

*Source coverage: partial. PDF pages inspected: 3-4 (the expectation and money-management sections). These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A concise guide from Windsor Advisory Services on constructing a personal trading plan around one's objectives, personality, and starting capital. It covers preparation (learning the basics, matching a method to your temperament, keeping the method simple), capital and position sizing, setting realistic profit targets, and closes with ten types of market behavior to avoid.

## Key points

- Match trading style to personality: an impatient trader who dislikes giving back profits is better suited to shorter-term swing trading than long-term trend following.
- Simple systems outperform complex ones for most traders and are easier to stay disciplined with through losing streaks.
- Start with enough capital to survive a string of losses; undercapitalized accounts are statistically more likely to be wiped out before a big winning trade arrives.
- Diversify across a few uncorrelated positions rather than concentrating risk in one trade.
- A realistic long-run target is 30-50% compounded annual growth, which the author places in the top 10% of profitable traders.
- Ten market actions to avoid: trading off news stories, being discouraged by individual losses, chopping and changing methods after a losing streak, treating the market as an enemy, entering without a stop, averaging down a loss, pyramiding top-heavy (adding equal or larger size into a winning move), sharing trades with friends, trading with money you cannot afford to lose, and trading on emotion.
- If pyramiding at all, put the largest position on first and add successively smaller size as the market moves in your favor (bottom-heavy pyramid), not the reverse.
- Most profitable traders win on 50% or fewer of their trades; long-run profitability comes from money management (cutting losses, running winners), not from a high win rate.

## Actionable rules

1. Set a stop-loss at trade entry, every time; never enter without one.
2. Never average into (add to) a losing position.
3. If pyramiding, add only to winning positions, with each added layer smaller than the last, and move the stop to breakeven or better as size is added.
4. Target 30-50% compounded annual return as a realistic (not aspirational) benchmark.
5. Trade only with genuine risk capital.
6. Keep the trading plan and method simple; avoid unnecessary complexity.

## Caveats

General motivational/psychology guidance rather than a specific trading system; no entry/exit signals, indicators, or backtested figures are given. The "30-50% per annum, top 10%" benchmark is asserted without data or citation. Written from a discretionary-trading, largely currency-market perspective.

## Who it is for

Beginner-to-intermediate traders who need a structured framework and discipline checklist for writing their first personal trading plan.
