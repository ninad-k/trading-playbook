# Peaks and Troughs: the trader's summary

*A Dow-style method defines trends and reversals through successive peaks, troughs, and confirmed breaks.*

**Martin J. Pring** · 2000 · Market Structure & Price Action · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-5. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

Pring presents peak-and-trough analysis as a simple way to describe a market’s zigzag structure. An uptrend remains intact while rallies form rising peaks and corrections form rising troughs; a sequence of declining peaks and troughs signals a downtrend (p. 1). A failed new high is an alert, but the bearish signal is confirmed only when price breaks the previous bottom. Conversely, a failed new low followed by a break above the previous high confirms a bullish reversal (pp. 1–2). When only one side of the structure breaks, Pring calls it a half-signal and recommends waiting for full concordance, accepting later entry in exchange for greater confirmation (pp. 2–3). A legitimate reaction generally retraces one-third to two-thirds of the prior move; a shallower correction can qualify if it lasts one-third to two-thirds as long as the preceding advance or decline (p. 3). The article says longer trends produce more significant reversals and illustrates the method with a 4% zigzag overlay on Chrysler, where a later lower peak and new low confirmed a bear trend (pp. 4–5). Pring recommends using peaks and troughs with volume, support/resistance, and other indicators rather than alone (p. 2).

## Key points

- The method separates an early alert from a confirmed trend change (pp. 1–2).
- Time and magnitude both matter when deciding whether a reaction is a meaningful trough or peak (p. 3).
- Half-signals are less reliable but can reduce lag if accepted with explicit risk.

## Actionable rules

1. Keep an uptrend label while peaks and troughs both rise and a downtrend label while both fall; treat a change in only one sequence as an unconfirmed half-signal (pp. 1–2, 5).
2. Require both a contrary peak/trough and a break of the preceding swing before calling the reversal confirmed (pp. 1–2).
3. Treat a reaction as meaningful when it retraces roughly one-third to two-thirds of the prior move, or when a shallower range lasts roughly one-third to two-thirds of the prior move’s duration (pp. 3–5).

## Caveats

Retracement fractions and “significant” swings are rough guidelines, and the article supplies no out-of-sample test or cost model. A zigzag parameter can introduce hindsight because pivots are known only after reversal.

## Who it is for

Traders formalizing trend state and confirmation rules from price structure.
