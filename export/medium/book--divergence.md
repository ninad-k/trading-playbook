# Divergence Decisions: the trader's summary

*Joe Ross treats MACD-Histogram divergence as a cautious confirmation tool combined with seasonal context, risk-reward, and multiple timeframes.*

**Joe Ross** · Indicators · advanced

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-13. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

Ross approaches divergence cautiously and rejects indiscriminate mechanical use of oscillators. The chapter uses MACD Histogram divergence with a seasonal entry signal and Bollinger Bands as a decision aid. A bearish divergence occurs when prices make higher highs while MACD-Histogram makes lower highs, but the author notes that prices may instead enter congestion (pp. 1-5). The example uses 9- and 19-bar moving averages for MACD-Histogram and analyzes short, intermediate, and long-term charts in a 5:1 ratio: each longer frame is five times the prior frame (pp. 2, 4). The decision must still be based on risk, reward, probability, and a protective exit.

## Key points

- Divergence can persist longer than its proponents expect (p. 1).
- MACD-Histogram is the difference between two moving averages (p. 1).
- A bearish divergence is confirmation of possible trend change, not proof of a decline (pp. 3-4).
- Bollinger Bands and higher-timeframe structure are used as filters (pp. 4-5).
- The source emphasizes decision making and protective stops over automatic signals.

## Actionable rules

1. For the illustrated study, use 9 and 19 bars for MACD-Histogram, recognizing the author says these are not magic values (p. 2).
2. Compare short, intermediate, and long-term charts at a 1:5:25 scale (p. 4).
3. Before acting on divergence, define the protective stop and assess the sideways alternative as well as the directional move (pp. 3-5).

## Caveats

The chapter presents examples, not a tested strategy. Indicator settings and the 5:1 ratio are author choices. Seasonal filters, data frequency, and stops are not fully specified in the extract.

## Who it is for

Experienced discretionary traders who use divergence as one contextual confirmation.
