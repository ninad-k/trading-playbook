# Quant, Microstructure & Academic Research: a trading-library study guide

*What survives testing: most popular technical methods do not, liquidity vanishes exactly when needed, and reported risk is systematically understated.*

## What it is

This category is the library's evidence base — the material that tests what the rest of it asserts. It has three strands. Market microstructure studies how prices actually form: who supplies liquidity, how order flow moves price, why spreads and depth follow the patterns they do, and what an order really costs to execute. Quantitative finance supplies the pricing and portfolio mathematics — no-arbitrage, stochastic calculus, factor models, mean-variance optimisation. And the empirical testing literature applies statistical discipline to trading rules themselves, asking whether an entry method beats random entry once costs, out-of-sample validation and multiple-comparison corrections are applied. The results are uncomfortable for much of the rest of this library: the most rigorously tested survey here found that moving-average crossovers, oscillator signals and cycle-based entries performed at or below chance out of sample.

## Core principles

- Test on a fixed diversified portfolio with costs included and a strict in-sample/out-of-sample split, or the result means nothing [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies — Standard Test Methodology](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies--test-methodology.html) [How to Test and Interpret Trading System Performance](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wayne-a-thorp-testing-trading-success.html).
- Compare entries against a random-entry baseline, not against zero — the relevant question is whether a rule beats chance, not whether it makes money on one chart [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies.html).
- Equalise dollar volatility across markets so each contributes comparable risk; equal contract counts silently weight the test toward whichever market moves most [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies — Standard Test Methodology](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies--test-methodology.html).
- Hold the exit constant while comparing entries, or the comparison measures exit variation rather than entry quality [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies — Standard Test Methodology](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies--test-methodology.html).
- Backtests that assume instant execution at closing prices overstate real returns — the small-cap turn-of-year effect largely disappears once order-book depth and liquidation time are modelled [Re-examining the Small-Cap Myth: Problems in Portfolio Formation and Liquidation](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/griffiths-turnbullb-and-white-re-examining-the-small-cap-myth-problems-in-portfolio-format.html).
- Reported risk is systematically understated where pricing is stale: removing return autocorrelation raises estimated standard deviation by 60-100% for illiquid fixed-income hedge fund styles [Hedge Fund Risk Factors and Value at Risk of Credit Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hedge-fund-risk-factors-and-value-at-risk-of-credit-trading-strategies.html).
- Value at Risk is not a coherent risk measure outside elliptical distributions, is biased low, carries large estimation error, and can be deliberately gamed [Exploring the Limitations of Value at Risk: How Good Is It in Practice?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/exploring-value-at-risk.html) [Calculating Value-at-Risk](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fallon-w-calculating-value-at-risk.html).
- Liquidity is a state, not a constant, and it disappears precisely when it is needed [Liquidity Black Holes](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/persaud-liquidity-black-holes.html) [Liquidity Black Holes](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/morris-and-song-shin-liquidity-black-holes.html).
- Liquidity is common across stocks: spreads and depth co-move with market and industry liquidity even after controlling for the individual stock [Commonality in Liquidity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/chordia-roll-and-subrahmanyam-commonality-in-liquidity.html) [Commonality in Liquidity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fernando-commonality-in-liquidity-transmission-of-liquidity-shocks-across-investors-and-se.html).
- Down markets hurt liquidity asymmetrically: spreads widen sharply on down days and narrow only slightly on up days — the single strongest effect in an eleven-year NYSE study [Market Liquidity and Trading Activity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/chordia-roll-and-subrahmanyam-market-liquidity-and-trading-activity.html).
- Price impact is concave in size, roughly proportional to the square root of volume, not linear [Are Supply and Demand Driving Stock Prices?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/supply-demand.html) [A Theory of Large Fluctuations in Stock Market Activity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/xavier-gabaix-a-theory-of-large-fluctuations-in-stock-market.html).
- Order-flow imbalance explains roughly half of return variance on the venue studied, and the predictable component of that imbalance carries almost no incremental impact — consistent with efficient arbitrage of the predictable part [Are Supply and Demand Driving Stock Prices?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/supply-demand.html).
- The U-shaped intraday volume and volatility pattern arises from traders clustering to minimise their own price impact, and it appears even in markets with no market makers at all [A Theory of Intraday Patterns: Volume and Price Variability](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/admati-and-pfleiderer-a-theory-of-intraday-patterns-volume-and-price-variability.html) [The Intraday Patterns of the Spread and Depth in a Market Without Market Makers: The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ahn-and-cheung-the-intraday-patterns-of-the-spread-and-depth-in-a-market-without-market-ma.html).
- Market structure is a variable, not a constant: spreads, depth and price discovery differ measurably across venues, and results do not transfer between them without care [Market Microstructure: A Survey](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/madhavan-market-microstructure-a-survey.html) [Competition Between Exchanges: Euronext versus Xetra](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/competition-between-exchanges-euronext-versus-xetra.html).
- Returns have far fatter tails than a normal distribution, and the tail behaviour connects to the size distribution of trading activity through nonlinear price impact [A Theory of Large Fluctuations in Stock Market Activity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/xavier-gabaix-a-theory-of-large-fluctuations-in-stock-market.html).
- Statistical structure existing in a series does not mean it is exploitable: tests reject randomness in FTSE and S&P returns, yet neither autoregressive nor genetically optimised neural models beat naive benchmarks [Using Neural Networks and Genetic Algorithms to Predict Stock Market Returns](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/using-neural-networks-and-genetic-algorithms-to-predict-stock-market-returns.html).
- Qualitative risk scoring — red/amber/green matrices — produces inconsistent rankings and can be measurably worse than chance [The Failure of Risk Management: Why It's Broken and How to Fix It](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/douglas-hubbard-the-failure-of-risk-management.html).

## Concrete rules and setups

### Testing methodology

1. Katz and McCormick's protocol: a fixed ~30-market futures portfolio, dollar-volatility equalisation from a 200-day average of daily change times point value, a standardised ATR-based exit applied identically to every entry, parameters optimised only on 1985-1994 and verified on 1995-1999, with 3-tick slippage and $15 per round turn included by default and t-tests on every headline figure [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies — Standard Test Methodology](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies--test-methodology.html).
2. Correct for multiple comparisons — a parameter sweep of N runs inflates the apparent significance of the best result, and the correction should be applied explicitly rather than assumed [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies — Standard Test Methodology](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies--test-methodology.html).
3. Avoid compounding inside the test: hold risk exposure constant rather than contract count, so significance tests on trade profitability stay valid [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies.html).
4. Thorp's checklist: re-run any system with commissions, realistic next-bar-open fills, margin cost and dividend or tax treatment; validate on a hold-out period the optimiser never saw; and benchmark against buy-and-hold [How to Test and Interpret Trading System Performance](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wayne-a-thorp-testing-trading-success.html).
5. Always benchmark a predictive model against naive predictors, especially "no change" — the paper that failed to beat them tested both autoregressive and neural approaches [Using Neural Networks and Genetic Algorithms to Predict Stock Market Returns](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/using-neural-networks-and-genetic-algorithms-to-predict-stock-market-returns.html).
6. Judge a strategy by its risk-to-return ratio (standard deviation of per-trade return divided by average return): below 3 is favourable, above 5 should be rejected or downsized. Use the geometric growth coefficient rather than the arithmetic average when trades compound [How to Win the Stock Market Game: Developing Short-Term Stock Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/vladimir-daragan-how-to-win-the-stock-market-game.html).

### What the testing found

7. Breakouts: an 80-day lookback was consistently the best in-sample and returned 76% annualised before costs, but flipped to a loss once 3-tick slippage and $15 commissions were applied. Out of sample it beat chance without being profitable, except a currencies-only volatility-breakout variant [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies.html).
8. Moving-average crossovers, oscillator overbought/oversold signals and cycle-based entries performed at or below random-entry chance out of sample [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies.html).
9. Neural networks and genetically evolved rules — the families with the worst reputation for curve-fitting — were the only ones holding a statistically significant out-of-sample edge, when trained on a large pooled whole-portfolio sample rather than one market [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies.html).
10. Ten sentiment, informed-opinion, liquidity and monetary indicators tested against future S&P changes over 1960-1974 form an early example of the same exercise applied to market indicators [The Predictive Power of Stock Market Indicators](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ben-branch-the-predictive-power-of-stock-market-indicator.html).

### Execution and microstructure

11. Execute large discretionary orders when volume is naturally concentrated — near the open and close — because price impact is lowest there; but expect faster price discovery and shorter-lived information edges in the same windows [A Theory of Intraday Patterns: Volume and Price Variability](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/admati-and-pfleiderer-a-theory-of-intraday-patterns-volume-and-price-variability.html).
12. Expect spreads to widen disproportionately on down days; use limit orders more and market orders less in falling markets [Market Liquidity and Trading Activity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/chordia-roll-and-subrahmanyam-market-liquidity-and-trading-activity.html).
13. Expect thinner liquidity into Friday's close and ahead of holidays, and better liquidity on Tuesdays; volume and depth build ahead of scheduled macro releases and normalise on release day [Market Liquidity and Trading Activity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/chordia-roll-and-subrahmanyam-market-liquidity-and-trading-activity.html).
14. When estimating impact, fit a nonlinear (square-root-like) relation between signed size and price change rather than imposing a linear coefficient [A Theory of Large Fluctuations in Stock Market Activity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/xavier-gabaix-a-theory-of-large-fluctuations-in-stock-market.html) [Are Supply and Demand Driving Stock Prices?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/supply-demand.html).
15. Separate quoted spread, effective spread, realised spread, depth and price impact when evaluating an execution — they measure different things [Market Microstructure: A Survey](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/madhavan-market-microstructure-a-survey.html).
16. Most after-hours price discovery in large Nasdaq names happens in the preopen via ECNs; postclose trades are noisy and frequently reversed [Price Discovery and Trading After Hours](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/barclay-and-hendershott-price-discovery-and-trading-after-hours.html).
17. The limit-versus-market order choice trades immediacy against execution probability and picking-off risk; the resulting spread and resiliency depend on the mix of patient and impatient traders, the tick size and the order arrival rate [Liquidity Supply and Demand in Limit Order Markets](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hollifield-miller-sandas-and-slive-liquidity-supply-and-demand-in-limit-order-markets.html) [Limit Order Book as a Market for Liquidity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/foucault-kadan-and-kandel-limit-order-book-as-a-market-for-liquidity.html).
18. Broker-negotiated upstairs markets lower execution costs on large blocks by tapping unexpressed liquidity and certifying the trade as uninformed [Does an Electronic Stock Exchange Need an Upstairs Market?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bessembinder-and-venkataraman-does-an-electronic-stock-exchange-need-an-upstairs-market.html).
19. Model realistic liquidation time and available depth before believing any small-cap or illiquid-universe backtest — one study needed 12-13 days to build a $10m small-cap portfolio and never fully liquidated it within the holding period [Re-examining the Small-Cap Myth: Problems in Portfolio Formation and Liquidation](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/griffiths-turnbullb-and-white-re-examining-the-small-cap-myth-problems-in-portfolio-format.html).

### Risk measurement

20. Never treat a single VaR number as a complete risk picture, especially for options or other skewed payoffs; pair it with stress testing for scenarios outside the estimation window [Exploring the Limitations of Value at Risk: How Good Is It in Practice?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/exploring-value-at-risk.html) [Guidelines on Market Risk, Volume 3: Evaluation of Value-at-Risk Models](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/evaluation-of-value-at-risk-models.html).
21. Second-order (gamma) approximations paired with multivariate GARCH outperformed the first-order delta models most practitioners used [Calculating Value-at-Risk](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/fallon-w-calculating-value-at-risk.html).
22. Liquidity-adjusted VaR adds a spread-based cost term and a kurtosis correction; standard parametric VaR materially understates risk without them [Modeling Liquidity Risk, With Implications for Traditional Market Risk Measurement and Management](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/bangia-diebold-schuermann-and-stroughair-modeling-liquidity-risk-with-implications-for-tra.html) [How Large Is Liquidity Risk in an Automated Auction Market?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/how-large-is-liquidity-risk-in-an-automated.html).
23. Discount reported hedge fund volatility for illiquid strategies, and treat a short put on high-yield debt — the dominant factor in 17 of 21 indices tested — as a better risk proxy for credit and merger strategies than equity beta [Hedge Fund Risk Factors and Value at Risk of Credit Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hedge-fund-risk-factors-and-value-at-risk-of-credit-trading-strategies.html).
24. Stress-test against correlated stop-outs, since in the liquidity-black-hole model the trigger is endogenous to other traders' exits rather than exogenous [Liquidity Black Holes](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/morris-and-song-shin-liquidity-black-holes.html).
25. Replace qualitative risk grids with calibrated probability estimates, explicit correlation modelling, and acknowledged fat tails [The Failure of Risk Management: Why It's Broken and How to Fix It](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/douglas-hubbard-the-failure-of-risk-management.html).

### Portfolio construction

26. Equity market neutral deployment: from $10 of capital, roughly $9 long and $9 short with about $1 held as a liquidity buffer — comfortably inside Reg T's 50% initial margin, illustrated at 55.6% [Market Neutral Equity Long-Short Portfolio Construction](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/marketneutralstrategies--equity-long-short-construction.html).
27. Select longs and shorts jointly in one optimisation weighing each candidate's expected excess return, volatility and correlation with every other candidate — not as a long book plus a separately built short book [Market Neutral Equity Long-Short Portfolio Construction](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/marketneutralstrategies--equity-long-short-construction.html).
28. Convertible hedge: buy the convertible and short delta-weighted stock, optionally adding a duration-sized short in Treasury futures, collecting the standstill spread of coupon plus short rebate less dividends owed [Convertible Bond Delta-Duration Hedge](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/marketneutralstrategies--convertible-bond-hedging.html).
29. Diversify across positions rather than concentrating: risk falls roughly as 1/√N while returns fall much more slowly, net of added commissions [How to Win the Stock Market Game: Developing Short-Term Stock Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/vladimir-daragan-how-to-win-the-stock-market-game.html).
30. Size stops against the instrument's average daily amplitude: a stop at exactly 1× that amplitude has roughly a 45% chance of being touched on day one alone, rising toward 80% by day eight [How to Win the Stock Market Game: Developing Short-Term Stock Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/vladimir-daragan-how-to-win-the-stock-market-game.html).

## Common mistakes

- Testing a rule on one market and one window, where almost anything can be made to look profitable [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies.html).
- Reporting backtest results without costs — the breakout family's 76% annualised return became a loss once slippage and commissions were added [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies.html) [How to Test and Interpret Trading System Performance](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/wayne-a-thorp-testing-trading-success.html).
- Sweeping parameters and reporting the best result without correcting for the number of comparisons made [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies — Standard Test Methodology](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies--test-methodology.html).
- Assuming fills at closing prices, which quietly assumes infinite depth [Re-examining the Small-Cap Myth: Problems in Portfolio Formation and Liquidation](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/griffiths-turnbullb-and-white-re-examining-the-small-cap-myth-problems-in-portfolio-format.html).
- Taking smoothed, autocorrelated reported returns at face value as evidence of low volatility [Hedge Fund Risk Factors and Value at Risk of Credit Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hedge-fund-risk-factors-and-value-at-risk-of-credit-trading-strategies.html).
- Applying VaR to skewed or fat-tailed payoffs where it stops being coherent, or reading a low VaR as low risk when the estimate is biased downward [Exploring the Limitations of Value at Risk: How Good Is It in Practice?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/exploring-value-at-risk.html).
- Treating liquidity as a fixed property of an instrument rather than a state that can vanish [Liquidity Black Holes](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/persaud-liquidity-black-holes.html) [Liquidity Black Holes](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/morris-and-song-shin-liquidity-black-holes.html).
- Assuming diversification across positions when liquidity is common across them, so they become correlated exactly when it matters [Commonality in Liquidity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/chordia-roll-and-subrahmanyam-commonality-in-liquidity.html).
- Imposing a linear price-impact model when the relation is concave [Are Supply and Demand Driving Stock Prices?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/supply-demand.html) [A Theory of Large Fluctuations in Stock Market Activity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/xavier-gabaix-a-theory-of-large-fluctuations-in-stock-market.html).
- Concluding a series is predictable because it fails a randomness test — structure and exploitability are different questions [Using Neural Networks and Genetic Algorithms to Predict Stock Market Returns](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/using-neural-networks-and-genetic-algorithms-to-predict-stock-market-returns.html).
- Judging a forecasting model by mean absolute error alone rather than by directional accuracy against a naive benchmark [Using Neural Networks and Genetic Algorithms to Predict Stock Market Returns](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/using-neural-networks-and-genetic-algorithms-to-predict-stock-market-returns.html).
- Transferring a microstructure result across venues without checking whether the market institution matches [Market Microstructure: A Survey](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/madhavan-market-microstructure-a-survey.html) [Competition Between Exchanges: Euronext versus Xetra](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/competition-between-exchanges-euronext-versus-xetra.html).
- Using arithmetic average return to describe a compounding strategy [How to Win the Stock Market Game: Developing Short-Term Stock Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/vladimir-daragan-how-to-win-the-stock-market-game.html).
- Ranking risks on a scoring grid, which the evidence suggests is worse than useless [The Failure of Risk Management: Why It's Broken and How to Fix It](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/douglas-hubbard-the-failure-of-risk-management.html).

## Best books for this topic

1. [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies.html) — the single most valuable book in this library for anyone who wants to know whether the other categories' methods work. The methodology matters more than any individual result.
2. [Market Microstructure: A Survey](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/madhavan-market-microstructure-a-survey.html) — the best single entry point to how prices actually form and what the microstructure literature has established.
3. [Market Neutral Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/marketneutralstrategies.html) — practitioner-written, with real hedge ratios and cash-flow tables, and an unusually candid post-mortem of the Askin and LTCM failures.
4. [Paul Wilmott Introduces Quantitative Finance](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/paul-wilmott-quantitative-finance.html) — a readable route into no-arbitrage and probabilistic pricing, and a good model of how to discuss popular-but-unproven methods without either dismissing or endorsing them.
5. [The Failure of Risk Management: Why It's Broken and How to Fix It](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/douglas-hubbard-the-failure-of-risk-management.html) — the argument that the standard risk-management toolkit is unvalidated, with calibration training as the constructive alternative.
6. [Exploring the Limitations of Value at Risk: How Good Is It in Practice?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/exploring-value-at-risk.html) — the clearest short statement of what VaR does not measure and how it can be gamed.
7. [Market Liquidity and Trading Activity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/chordia-roll-and-subrahmanyam-market-liquidity-and-trading-activity.html) — eleven years of NYSE data on when liquidity is actually there, directly useful for execution timing.
8. [A Theory of Intraday Patterns: Volume and Price Variability](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/admati-and-pfleiderer-a-theory-of-intraday-patterns-volume-and-price-variability.html) — the model that explains the intraday volume pattern every day trader takes for granted.
9. [Re-examining the Small-Cap Myth: Problems in Portfolio Formation and Liquidation](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/griffiths-turnbullb-and-white-re-examining-the-small-cap-myth-problems-in-portfolio-format.html) — the clearest demonstration that an anomaly can be real in the data and unavailable in practice.
10. [How to Win the Stock Market Game: Developing Short-Term Stock Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/vladimir-daragan-how-to-win-the-stock-market-game.html) — an accessible bridge between the statistics and actual trading decisions, with the stop-probability arithmetic most trading books omit.
11. [The Mathematics of Financial Modeling and Investment Management](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/the-mathematics-of-financial-modeling-and-investment-management.html) — the reference to keep when the mathematics in the papers above outruns you.

## Open debates

- **Whether technical trading rules work at all.** Katz and McCormick's cost-inclusive out-of-sample tests place moving averages, oscillators and cycles at or below chance, and find only machine-learning families surviving [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies.html); the Trend Following and Indicators categories in this library rest almost entirely on methods that failed those tests. Wilmott's stance is closer to agnostic — descriptive about the tools while questioning their statistical grounding [Paul Wilmott Introduces Quantitative Finance](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/paul-wilmott-quantitative-finance.html).
- **Whether machine learning adds anything.** Katz and McCormick found neural and genetic methods were the only families with a surviving edge [The Encyclopedia of Trading Strategies](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies.html); a Minority-Game multi-agent model beat chance on FX direction [Application of Multi-Agent Games to the Prediction of Financial Time-Series](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/application-of-multi-agent-games-to-the-prediction-of-financial-time-series.html) and a neural regression beat ARMA, logit and MACD benchmarks on EUR/USD [Applications of Advanced Regression Analysis for Trading and Investment](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/trading-and-investment-applied-quantitative-methods-for.html); but a genetically optimised network failed to beat a "no change" benchmark on FTSE and S&P returns [Using Neural Networks and Genetic Algorithms to Predict Stock Market Returns](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/using-neural-networks-and-genetic-algorithms-to-predict-stock-market-returns.html), and a separate network's results rest on two historical episodes [Application of Neural Networks to Stock Market Prediction](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/neural-prediction-of-weekly-stock-market-index-1.html).
- **Whether observed nonlinearity is exploitable.** STAR models find short-horizon nonlinearities in gilt futures that fade for larger moves and vanish after friction costs [Nonlinear Dynamics in High Frequency Intra-Day Financial Data](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mcmillan-and-speight-nonlinear-dynamics-in-high-frequency-intra-day-financial-data.html); five-minute mean reversion in Indian equities varies with liquidity [Serial Correlation in High-Frequency Data and the Link with Liquidity](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/thomas-and-patnaik-serial-correlation-in-high-frequency-data-and-the-link-with-liquidity.html); and non-normality and volatility clustering in FX may partly be artifacts of time aggregation rather than properties of the process [Modeling High-Frequency FX Data Dynamics](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/jorda-and-marcellino-modeling-high-frequency-fx-data-dynamics.html).
- **Whether market structure needs intermediaries.** The Tokyo pure limit-order book supplies immediacy nearly as well as a dealer market [Securities Trading in the Absence of Dealers: Trades and Quotes on the Tokyo Stock Exchange](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/hamao-and-hasbrouck-securities-trading-in-the-absence-of-dealers-trades-and-quotes-on-the.html) and Hong Kong produces the classic intraday patterns with no market makers at all [The Intraday Patterns of the Spread and Depth in a Market Without Market Makers: The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ahn-and-cheung-the-intraday-patterns-of-the-spread-and-depth-in-a-market-without-market-ma.html); yet a designated liquidity provider adds measurable value for illiquid Paris names while doing nothing for liquid ones [Stock Liquidity and the Value of a Designated Liquidity Provider: Evidence from Euronext Paris](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/mann-venkataraman-and-waisburd-stock-liquidity-and-the-value-of-a-designated-liquidity-pro.html), and neither pure nor hybrid structures are competition-proof for order flow [Liquidity-Based Competition for Order Flow](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/parlour-and-seppi-liquidity-based-competition-for-order-flow.html).
- **Whether the intraday volume pattern is U-shaped at all.** The canonical model and the Hong Kong evidence give a U [A Theory of Intraday Patterns: Volume and Price Variability](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/admati-and-pfleiderer-a-theory-of-intraday-patterns-volume-and-price-variability.html) [The Intraday Patterns of the Spread and Depth in a Market Without Market Makers: The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/ahn-and-cheung-the-intraday-patterns-of-the-spread-and-depth-in-a-market-without-market-ma.html); Taiwan order-book data gives a J, driven more by liquidity flow than by informed trading [Explaining Intraday Pattern of Trading Volume from the Order Flow Data](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/lee-fok-and-liu-explaining-intraday-pattern-of-trading-volume-from-the-order-flow-data.html).
- **Whether order flow drives price or merely follows it.** Order imbalance explains roughly half of return variance on the Paris Bourse, pointing at a mechanical price-pressure channel [Are Supply and Demand Driving Stock Prices?](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/supply-demand.html); NYSE-affiliated research finds program trades follow rather than lead S&P moves with little reversal, contradicting the destabilisation claim [Program Trading and Intraday Volatility](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/harris-sofianos-and-shapiro-program-trading-and-intraday-volatility.html).
- **Whether the underlying models hold.** The Markov property is rejected for the bid-ask spread in three of five NYSE stocks tested [Testing the Markov Property with Ultra-High Frequency Financial Data](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/de-matos-and-fernandes-testing-the-markov-property-with-ultra-high-frequency-financial-dat.html), the uncovered-interest-parity and purchasing-power-parity puzzles remain unresolved in the macro literature [International Macroeconomics and Finance: Theory and Empirical Methods](https://ninad-k.github.io/trading-playbook/books/international-macro-economics-and-finance.html), and the pricing frameworks built on those assumptions are used anyway.
