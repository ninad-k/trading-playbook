# Swing Trading Tactics: the trader's summary

*Pristine.com's slide-deck seminar teaching the 4-stage stock cycle and the Pristine Buy/Sell Setup (three consecutive lower-highs/higher-lows bars) for 1-to-5-bar swing trades.*

**Oliver L. Velez** · 2001 · Swing Trading · beginner

*Source coverage: partial. PDF pages inspected: 1-13, 23, 72, 79 (trading styles, stage analysis and the Pristine Buy/Short Setup trigger, stop and management rules). These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Overview

This is the slide deck from a Pristine.com online seminar by Oliver Velez, founder of Pristine.com and co-author of *Tools and Tactics for the Master Day Trader*. Delivered as annotated PowerPoint-style slides with annotated stock charts (INTC, ALTR, NTRS, SVRN, ADTN, FON, CMB and others), it teaches the "Pristine" framework for swing trading: a market-structure model (the 4-stage cycle), a candlestick philosophy borrowed from Japanese charting convention, and a single named, rules-based setup — the Pristine Buy Setup (PBS) and its mirror-image Pristine Short/Sell Setup (PSS) — as the core swing-trading trigger. Because it is a seminar deck rather than a prose book, content is delivered as terse bullet points and annotated chart examples rather than extended exposition; several named sub-concepts (Change of Guard bars, Novice Gaps, narrow-range bars) are labeled on charts but not always defined in full in the text.

## Core thesis

A stock (or the market) can only ever be in one of four repeating stages — bottoming (Stage 1), markup/uptrend (Stage 2), topping (Stage 3), and markdown/downtrend (Stage 4) — driven respectively by uncertainty, greed, renewed uncertainty, and fear; over 65% of trading losses, in Velez's framing, come from being on the wrong side of this cycle. Because every trend (up, down, or sideways) is built from smaller-degree repetitions of the same four-stage cycle, a trader who can identify which "minor" stage (within the current bar-by-bar price action) matches the prevailing "major" stage (the larger trend) has a repeatable, mechanical answer to "when," not just "whether," to buy or short. The Pristine Buy/Sell Setup operationalizes this "stage match" concept into a simple, chart-readable pattern usable largely on its own.

## Key concepts

- **The Atom (basic unit of price)** — the single repeating 4-stage cycle (1→2→3→4→1...) that Velez claims is the only motion a stock's price can make; forms the basis for reading where a stock currently sits in its cycle.
- **4 stages** — Stage 1 (bottoming, uncertainty/ambivalence, low interest), Stage 2 (uptrend, driven by greed, where most participants make money), Stage 3 (topping, renewed uncertainty as sentiment splits), Stage 4 (downtrend, driven by fear, culminating in a climactic sell-off).
- **3 primary trends** — uptrend (higher highs and higher lows; buy dips and breakouts), downtrend (lower highs and lower lows; sell/short rallies and breakdowns), sideways (relatively equal highs and lows; trade both sides).
- **Major/minor stage match** — the key timing rule: buy when a minor (smaller-degree) Stage 2 develops within a major Stage 2 uptrend; short when a minor Stage 4 develops within a major Stage 4 downtrend. A minor Stage 1 or 3 within a major trend means "wait" or "get ready," not yet "act."
- **6 charting time frames** — yearly, monthly (academic/long-term), weekly and daily (the swing trader's core domain — daily charts provide ~95% of entry/exit signals), hourly and 5-/15-minute (day trading/scalping domain).
- **4 trading styles** — Core Trading (weeks to months, weekly charts, "wealth building"), Swing Trading (2–10 days, daily charts, "wealth building"), Guerilla Trading (1–2 days, daily/hourly charts, "income producing"), Micro-Trading (seconds to hours, 5-/15-minute charts, "income producing," i.e., day trading/scalping).
- **Candlestick philosophy** — the book explicitly favors the Japanese convention (relationship of a bar's open to its close) over the Western convention (close vs. prior close) for judging whether a bar was bullish or bearish, arguing the Japanese method is more accurate for traders.
- **3-to-5 bar rule** — bulls or bears rarely win more than 5 consecutive "battles" (bars); a run of 3, 4, or 5 same-direction bars is read as an extended, exhaustion-prone move ("think sell" after 3–5 up bars, "think buy" after 3–5 down bars).
- **CCI(5)** — a 5-period Commodity Channel Index used as a secondary confirming signal, read only in the direction of the prevailing trend (buy signals sought in uptrends, sell signals in downtrends), with standard overbought (+100) / oversold (–100) levels plus an "anticipatory" (earlier) version of each signal.
- **20/40 moving averages** — 20- and 40-period simple moving averages on daily and weekly charts used to gauge the major trend (steadily rising 20/40-MAs with the 20 above the 40 mark a Stage 2 uptrend; the reverse marks Stage 4).
- **Minor Stage 1/3 sub-patterns** — bottoming/topping tails, "COGs" (Change of Guard bars), narrow-range bars (NRBs), and "Novice Gaps" are named as the chart signatures of minor Stage 1 (within an uptrend) or minor Stage 3 (within a downtrend), though the deck does not spell out each pattern's exact bar-level definition in the extracted text.

## Rules and setups

1. **Pristine Buy Setup (PBS) — pattern.** Three or more consecutive lower highs (primary emphasis), three or more consecutive lower lows, and three or more consecutive dark/red (down) bars; the close on the final bar may be above its open as an exception.
2. **Pristine Buy Setup — trigger and stop.** Buy $0.05–$0.10 above the prior day's high (or above the first 30-minute high, only if the prior day's high is too far away). Place a protective stop $0.05–$0.10 below the entry day's low or the prior day's low, whichever is lower.
3. **Pristine Buy Setup — trade management.** Trail the stop under each prior bar's low once two full bars have elapsed, until (a) a price objective is hit, (b) a reversal bar forms, or (c) a gap up occurs.
4. **Pristine Short/Sell Setup (PSS) — pattern.** The mirror image: three or more consecutive higher lows (primary emphasis), three or more consecutive higher highs, and three or more consecutive light/green (up) bars; the close on the final bar may be below its open as an exception.
5. **Pristine Short Setup — trigger and stop.** Short $0.05–$0.10 below the prior day's low (or below the first 30-minute low, only if the prior day's low is too far away). Place a protective stop $0.05–$0.10 above the entry day's high or the prior day's high, whichever is higher (traders with low pain tolerance may use the entry day's high specifically).
6. **Pristine Short Setup — trade management.** Trail the stop above each prior bar's high once two full bars have elapsed, until (a) a price objective is hit, (b) a reversal bar forms, or (c) a gap down occurs.
7. **Directional filter.** Only take PBS-pattern buy signals within a major Stage 2 (uptrend) context and only take PSS-pattern short signals within a major Stage 4 (downtrend) context — a stage-to-stage "match" is what confirms the entry, not the bar pattern in isolation.
8. **CCI(5) confirmation (optional secondary filter).** Look only for CCI(5) buy crossovers above oversold in uptrends and sell crossovers below overbought in downtrends to add confirmation to a PBS/PSS trigger.

## Risk and money management

Risk is defined entirely at the setup level: every PBS/PSS entry carries a hard-dollar stop ($0.05–$0.10 beyond the relevant prior extreme) fixed at the moment of entry, not a percentage-of-equity rule — the book gives no explicit position-sizing formula (e.g., no stated max % of account per trade), so sizing discipline is left to the reader. The trailing-stop rule (move the stop to the prior bar's low/high after two full bars) is the primary mechanism for locking in gains and cutting a trade short of its stated 1–5 bar (buy) or 2–5 day (short) target once a reversal bar or a gap against the position appears — implicitly, the setups are meant to be closed quickly rather than held through an adverse move. The "3-to-5 bar rule" doubles as an informal risk-timing heuristic: after 3–5 consecutive same-direction bars, the deck's guidance shifts from "enter" to "think sell/think buy" (i.e., anticipate exhaustion), which argues against chasing an already-extended PBS or PSS signal.

## Psychology and discipline

The 4-stage-cycle framework is explicitly psychological: Stage 1 (uncertainty/ambivalence), Stage 2 (greed), Stage 3 (renewed uncertainty/doubt), and Stage 4 (fear, culminating in a climactic capitulation) are each tied to a dominant crowd emotion, and Velez frames successful trading as learning to act in sync with — rather than against — the stage the crowd is currently in. The "Only Way to Win or Lose" framing (buy only in the correct buy area of the cycle, sell only in the correct sell area; any other action is definitionally the "faulty" way that consistently loses) is presented as the book's central discipline lesson: traders who lose are, in Velez's telling, unconsciously buying tops and shorting bottoms relative to this cycle rather than trading with it. The seminar repeatedly stresses using more than one time frame together (e.g., weekly context plus daily trigger) as "the most important key" to why a nearly perfect setup on one time frame can still fail — an argument for context and patience over reflexively acting on a single chart.

## Strengths and caveats

The PBS/PSS setup is stated with genuinely codeable, numeric rules (bar counts, dollar offsets for entry/stop) rare in a marketing-oriented seminar deck, and the 4-stage/3-trend framework is a clear, teachable mental model for sequencing entries with trend context. Caveats: this is a promotional seminar excerpt (it explicitly advertises Velez's full book and Pristine's paid video/seminar library at the end) rather than a complete, standalone manual, and as a slide deck rather than continuous prose, several named chart patterns (Change of Guard bars, Novice Gaps, narrow-range bars, the "20/20 bar") are labeled on example charts but not defined with explicit numeric criteria in the extracted text — a reader would need Pristine's fuller materials (e.g., *Tools and Tactics for the Master Day Trader*, referenced throughout) to fill those gaps. No formal backtest, win rate, or drawdown statistics are given for the PBS/PSS setup; all supporting evidence is illustrative annotated charts of past trades rather than a systematic performance study, and the book includes standard hypothetical-performance and risk disclaimers to that effect. The framework and terminology (Stages 1–4, "the Atom") are Pristine's own branding rather than standard technical-analysis vocabulary, so cross-referencing against other technical-analysis texts requires translating the terms (Stage 2 ≈ Dow Theory markup phase / Wyckoff accumulation-to-markup transition, etc.).

## Who should read it

Beginner-to-intermediate swing traders who want a simple, rules-based entry trigger (PBS/PSS) combined with a trend-context framework, and who are comfortable working from a terse, chart-heavy seminar format rather than expository prose. Best paired with Pristine's companion materials (its Guerilla Trading and Cardinal Rules titles) or a standard candlestick reference for the sub-patterns this deck names but does not fully define. Not a source of formal backtested statistics or money-management formulas — readers wanting those should pair it with a dedicated risk-management or systems-testing text.
