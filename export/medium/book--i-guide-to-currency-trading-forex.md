# Guide to Currency Trading: the trader's summary

*Broker-produced primer on spot forex mechanics and history, followed by country-by-country currency profiles covering central bank mandates, monetary policy tools, and key economic indicators.*

**Global Forex Trading (GFT)** · Forex Mechanics & Macro Drivers · beginner

*Source coverage: partial. PDF pages inspected: 160, 172 and the central-bank profile sections; the scan's OCR is heavily degraded across most of its 737 pages. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A reference guide produced by the retail forex broker Global Forex Trading (GFT), split into two parts. The first covers forex market basics: what forex is, its Bretton Woods and free-float history, the 24-hour interbank/market-maker structure, bid/ask quoting, and the majors. The second, larger part is a country-by-country reference (developed markets: Australia, Canada, EU, UK, Japan, New Zealand, Switzerland, US; emerging markets: China, Czech Republic, Hong Kong, Hungary, India, Korea, Mexico, Poland, Singapore, South Africa, Thailand, Turkey) covering each currency's economic overview, central bank mandate and monetary/fiscal policy tools, distinguishing characteristics, and the key economic indicators traders should watch.

## Key points

- Explains forex as an over-the-counter (OTC), 24-hour market with no central exchange, structured around an interbank market, central bank interventions, and third-party market-makers who quote retail-accessible prices.
- Frames currency pairs via bid/ask (buy/sell) quoting conventions and notes that market-maker firms earn the bid-ask spread rather than charging commission.
- For each developed-market currency, documents the responsible central bank, its explicit policy mandate (e.g., Australia's RBA targets currency stability, full employment, and economic prosperity, with an informal 2–3% CPI inflation target), and its rate-setting mechanism (e.g., RBA meets monthly except January, adjusting the cash rate via repo operations).
- Notes commodity/carry-trade linkages: the Australian dollar is cited as having roughly an 80% correlation with commodity prices (especially gold, given Australia is the world's third-largest gold producer) and is described as a popular carry-trade currency due to relatively high interest rates.
- Highlights economic sensitivities specific to each country, e.g., Australia's GDP exposure to drought given agriculture's share of GDP and farm-sector linkages.
- For Canada, documents the Bank of Canada's inflation-targeting framework (1–3% target band) and notes policy can be adjusted at any time rather than on a fixed meeting schedule.
- Lists the standard set of economic indicators to watch per country (e.g., for Australia: GDP, CPI, balance of goods and services, private consumption, PPI).
- Frames interest-rate differentials between countries as a lead indicator of currency-flow direction, since capital moves toward higher relative yields.

## Actionable rules

None given as trading entry/exit rules — this is a fundamentals/macro reference, not a trading system. The closest actionable takeaways: (1) track each currency's central bank meeting calendar and published policy statements/bulletins for signals of rate changes; (2) watch interest-rate differentials between currency pairs as an indicator of potential capital flows; (3) for commodity-linked currencies (e.g., AUD), monitor relevant commodity prices (gold) and weather/harvest conditions as secondary drivers.

## Caveats

No publication date is stated; some GDP and volume figures suggest late-2000s data, but this cannot be confirmed from the text. The PDF's text extraction has font-encoding artifacts throughout that substitute unusual symbols for certain letters, so exact figures and some passages required manual reconstruction; a handful of low-confidence numeric details may be imprecise as a result. As a broker-published marketing document, company history and platform-promotion sections (which are not covered in these notes) should be read with that origin in mind, and the economic-indicator and policy-framework details will drift out of date as central bank mandates and targets change over time.

## Who it is for

Beginner-to-intermediate forex traders who want a compact fundamentals/macro reference — which central bank moves which currency, what mandate it follows, and which indicators to watch — rather than a technical trading system.
