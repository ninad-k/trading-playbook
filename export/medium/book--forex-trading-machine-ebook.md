# Forex Trading Machine Course: the trader's summary

*A retail-FX course built around a two-day GBP/USD continuation setup and a short-horizon EUR/USD momentum system.*

**Unknown** · Forex Mechanics & Macro Drivers · intermediate

*Source coverage: partial. PDF pages inspected: 1-15, 28-32, 91-126, 127-136, 149-162, 163-184 (the three strategy chapters in full, the money-management and stop-loss sections and the hypothetical-results appendix). These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This course combines introductory FX mechanics, support and resistance (including Fibonacci and visual levels), psychology, and money management with three rule-based systems: Forex Cash Cow (swing), Forex Runner (day) and Flip & Go (day). The clearest is “Forex Cash Cow,” a GBP/USD continuation trade after an unusually large daily move. “Forex Runner” is a five-minute EUR/USD momentum and reversal system. The author emphasizes preplanned orders, infrequent high-quality signals, and executing the written plan without discretionary changes during a trade (PDF pp. 6-9).

## Key points

- The Cash Cow setup seeks continuation after a 140-pip directional day and reportedly appears three or four times per month (PDF pp. 92-94).
- Its next-day trigger combines a 70-pip same-direction move with a break at least 30 pips beyond the prior day's extreme (PDF pp. 94, 103-109).
- The prescribed Cash Cow stop is 60 pips; the profit target is 100 pips; the time exit is 11:30 EST on the day after entry (PDF p. 95).
- Forex Runner trades 30-pip intraday swings on a five-minute EUR/USD chart (PDF pp. 127-133).
- Runner uses a 20-pip initial stop and 40-pip target, tightening risk after favorable movement (PDF pp. 134-135).
- The course repeatedly warns that leverage and overtrading magnify errors.
- **Flip & Go** is the course's third system (PDF pp. 149-162): a EUR/USD fade of the "fake 15 pip move" that often opens the European session.
- Reported performance is hypothetical and does not establish robustness (PDF p. 176).

## Actionable rules

1. For Cash Cow, identify a GBP/USD day with a directional move of at least 140 pips; the example uses open and close to establish direction. On the next day, require both a 70-pip move from the day's opposite extreme and a 30-pip break beyond the prior day's high or low. Use a 60-pip stop and 100-pip target; otherwise exit at 11:30 EST the following day (PDF pp. 94-109). Page 109 also limits the continuation move to 100 pips; the examples leave the interaction of these thresholds insufficiently precise for automatic implementation.
2. For Runner, use the broker's five-minute EUR/USD chart. Enter with a 30-pip move, set a 20-pip stop and 40-pip target, and reduce the loss allowance to 10 pips after a 10-pip favorable move. The text then moves the stop to breakeven after six hours without reaching the target, conditional on first reaching that initial 10-pip profit (PDF pp. 128-135).
3. For **Flip & Go**, work the EUR/USD 5-minute chart from exactly 05:00 UK time, including the high and low of the 05:00 bar in the measurement. Wait for the first move of at least 15 pips (measured precisely, to the pip) and fade it: sell at a 15-pip up-move, buy at a 15-pip down-move. Place the stop 15 pips from entry and the profit objective 40 pips from entry. Once price moves 15 pips in the trade's favour, move the stop to breakeven. If the objective is not reached within eight hours of entry, exit at that time (PDF pp. 149-162).
4. Runner also reverses on a 30-pip opposing move. After a stop-out, count the next 30-pip signal from the stop price. After a target, initially wait for a 30-pip move opposite the previous trade; after four hours without one, either direction qualifies (PDF pp. 135-136). These reset rules matter as much as the first entry.

## Caveats

This is a partial review of a dated commercial course. Spread assumptions, broker technology, exact session labels, and claimed hit rates may no longer apply. The strategy examples are selected and backtested rather than independently audited.

## Who it is for

Intermediate retail-FX traders who want explicit historical systems to code and test, with strong skepticism about costs, data conventions, and sample selection.
