# The Forex Quick Guide for Beginners and Private Traders: the trader's summary

*Broker-produced beginner's guide covering forex mechanics, a wide survey of technical and fundamental analysis tools, and stop-loss/take-profit rules of thumb, wrapped around Easy-Forex platform marketing.*

**Easy-Forex** · Forex Mechanics & Macro Drivers · beginner

*Source coverage: partial. PDF pages inspected: 4, 7-8, 10, 30 (the leverage arithmetic, quoting mechanics and the stop/target guidance). These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

A 110-page introductory ebook published by the Easy-Forex™ trading platform as free educational material and lead generation. It covers what forex trading is, how a forex deal and leverage work, a broad-strokes tour of technical analysis (chart patterns, indicators, Elliott waves, gaps, trends) and fundamental analysis (economic indicators, central banks), a short day-trading section, a "20 issues to ask your broker" chapter, general trading tips, and a glossary — with Easy-Forex's own platform features cited throughout as the answer to each issue raised.

## Key points

- **Mechanics** — forex deals are non-delivery contracts between trader and market-maker on a currency pair, principal amount, and rate; over 95% of volume is speculative rather than for actual currency delivery.
- **Leverage** — described as the core forex advantage; a $1,000 margin at 1:100 leverage controls a $100,000 position, so ordinary ~1% daily currency moves become materially larger percentage moves on the margin deposit.
- **Technical analysis toolkit** — the guide catalogs Bollinger Bands, support/resistance and breakouts, CCI, the Hikkake pattern, seven types of moving averages, MACD, momentum/ROC, RSI, the stochastic oscillator, and trendlines, each with a one-paragraph definition and (for RSI, stochastics, momentum) the underlying formula.
- **Technical analysis is built on three premises** stated by the guide: price discounts everything, prices move in trends, and history repeats because trader psychology is stable over time.
- **Two exit-order types** — Take-Profit (limit) orders close a position at a predetermined profit target; Stop-Loss orders cap losses at a predetermined adverse level; both can be adjusted while a deal is open.
- **Stop/target placement rule of thumb** — set the Stop-Loss closer to the entry than the Take-Profit so a trader can be profitable while being right less than half the time (e.g., a 30-pip stop with a 100-pip target only needs to win about one-third of trades).
- **"20 issues" chapter** — framed as broker-vetting questions (execution guarantees, spreads, minimum deposit, security, customer support), but each answer promotes Easy-Forex's own features rather than giving neutral comparison criteria.
- **General risk warnings** — never risk more than you can afford to lose; a trader can never lose more than the margin deposited (assuming guaranteed stop execution); currency markets can move sharply "in days, hours, or sometimes minutes."

## Actionable rules

1. Set Stop-Loss orders closer to the entry price than Take-Profit orders so that a win rate below 50% can still be profitable (example given: 30-pip stop vs. 100-pip target, breakeven at roughly one-third win rate).
2. Never risk more capital than the amount you can afford to lose; size positions to your margin, not your desired position size.
3. Combine technical and fundamental analysis — the guide states that when both point the same direction, the odds of a profitable trade improve.
4. Avoid overly tight stops that get triggered by normal volatility; avoid overly distant targets unrealistic for the timeframe held.
5. Start with small trade sizes (the guide's own broker allows deposits as low as $25) while learning the platform and market before scaling up size.

## Caveats

This is broker marketing material disguised as an educational guide: the "20 issues" and "tips" chapters spend most of their length promoting Easy-Forex's own account features (credit-card funding, no software download, 24/7 support) rather than giving neutral, actionable guidance, and the technical-analysis chapter is a broad glossary rather than a tested or prioritized set of tools. No author byline beyond the company; no publication year given; leverage ratios cited (up to 1:200) exceed limits now imposed by regulators in the US, EU, and UK.

## Who it is for

Absolute beginners who want a single-source glossary of forex mechanics and common technical/fundamental analysis terms, read with the understanding that the broker-specific chapters are sales material, not independent advice.
