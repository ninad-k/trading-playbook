# Asset Valuation & Allocation Models: the trader's summary

*Yardeni’s Fed stock valuation model compares forward earnings yield with bond yields and maps valuation ranges to stock-bond mixes.*

**Edward Yardeni and Amalia F. Quintana** · 2002 · Investing, Value & Market History · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-28. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

Yardeni’s report presents a simple Fed Stock Valuation Model (FSVM) for judging whether the S&P 500 is expensive or cheap relative to bonds. The base model compares 12-month forward earnings yield with the 10-year Treasury yield; fair value is expressed as expected earnings divided by the Treasury yield (pp. 2-3). An improved version adjusts for corporate credit risk and long-term earnings growth, using Moody’s A-rated corporate yield, a growth coefficient, and five-year consensus growth forecasts (p. 4). The report says the model is useful for valuation and broad stock-bond allocation but is not a timing tool because overvaluation can persist (p. 5).

## Key points

- A 29-basis-point average FEY-TBY spread is cited for the historical sample beginning in 1979 (p. 2).
- The improved model’s growth coefficient averaged 0.1 from 1985 in the report’s data (p. 4).
- The report maps more than 30% overvaluation to 70% bonds/30% stocks and more than 15% undervaluation to 10% bonds/90% stocks (p. 5).
- It says overvaluation can correct through lower rates, higher earnings, or lower prices (p. 3).
- Expected returns are described as below average after overvaluation and above average after undervaluation over the next 12-24 months (p. 5).
- FSVM-2 replaces the Treasury yield with Moody’s A-rated corporate yield less a fraction of five-year consensus earnings growth; the report presents this as a risk- and growth-adjusted fair-value model (p. 12).
- The report’s historical charts show an average 13% market weight for long-term earnings growth and an average S&P 500 PEG ratio of 1.2, both with substantial variation (p. 13).
- A-rated corporate yield and the A-rated-minus-10-year-Treasury spread are explicit risk inputs; the spread chart labels a historical average of 131 basis points (p. 14).
- Country panels apply the same stock-valuation framework to the United States, Canada, United Kingdom, Germany, France, and Japan, pairing forward earnings with industrial production and fair-value P/E (pp. 15-22).
- The final comparison reports close co-movement between US and G5 forward-earnings cycles, while the graphs remain historical descriptive evidence (p. 23).

## Actionable rules

1. Calculate forward earnings yield and compare it with the chosen Treasury or corporate yield (pp. 2-4).
2. Label valuation as a range rather than a binary timing signal; the report explicitly warns that extremes can continue (p. 5).
3. Treat the stock-bond percentages as the author’s subjective allocation table, not a universal mandate (p. 5).

## Caveats

The report is a 2002 market commentary using consensus forecasts and historical relationships. Rates, earnings definitions, risk premia, and allocation suitability change. Analyst forecasts can be systematically optimistic; the country charts are descriptive and do not establish a tradable timing rule. The model also omits valuation drivers outside earnings, rates, credit, and the selected growth inputs (pp. 12-23).

## Who it is for

Long-horizon investors comparing equity valuation with interest-rate and credit conditions.
