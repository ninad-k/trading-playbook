# Buy and Hold Is Dead: the trader's summary

*Argues a demographic 'Investment Rate' model dooms buy-and-hold through 2023, then hands over six cash-heavy, rule-based support/resistance systems as the alternative.*

**Thomas H. Kee** · 2010 · Trend Following & Mechanical Systems · intermediate

*Source coverage: partial. PDF pages inspected: 6, 9, 15, 22, 25, 33, 60, 63, 111, 134 (the Investment Rate model chapters and the Game Plan trading process). These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Overview

Written by financial advisor and market strategist Thomas H. Kee after the 2008 crash, this book makes two connected arguments. First, a proprietary demographic model called the Investment Rate shows aggregate U.S. investment demand declining for a 16-year secular period from December 2007 through 2023, so static buy-and-hold faces a multi-decade headwind. Second, because passive strategies and delegated money management cannot navigate that environment, investors need proactive, mechanical trading disciplines that can profit in both directions and sit in cash otherwise. Roughly the first half builds the macro case and psychological groundwork; the second half delivers the technical toolkit and six named trading strategies.

## Core thesis

Buy-and-hold works only during the up-slope of a multi-decade demographic cycle (Kee dates the prior one 1981–2007); during down-slopes, conservative allocations and diversified managed accounts still lose money because broad-based selling drags down "safe" sectors too. Big brokerages have a structural conflict of interest — they earn recurring fees for keeping clients fully invested, not for protecting client wealth — so investors must take direct responsibility for risk control ("contemporary Darwinism"). The antidote is discipline, not prediction: a fixed daily technical process (the "Game Plan") that generates support/resistance levels across three time frames and three markets, converted into mechanical buy/short rules with tight, non-negotiable stops and a large standing allocation to cash.

## Key concepts

- **Investment Rate** — Kee's proprietary demographic model of systematic/aggressive investment demand (tied to when people finish paying for homes, college, and start retirement saving); it identifies long secular up- and down-cycles in the market, with 2007–2023 projected as a down-cycle.
- **Contemporary Darwinism** — the idea that investors must actively adapt and control their own risk rather than delegate it, or be selected out by the market.
- **Brackish investor** — Kee's term for a passive investor who accepts whatever the market and their money manager hand them rather than actively managing risk.
- **Golden handcuffs** — psychological and structural barriers (fees, inertia, brokerage incentives) that keep investors in buy-and-hold long after it stops working.
- **The Game Plan / oscillation cycles** — a repeatable daily technical process: chart the Dow, NASDAQ, and S&P 500 each on near-term (5-min/5-day), midterm (30-min/15-day), and long-term (daily/6-month) windows, draw trendlines to find support and resistance, and combine the levels into a single ordered array.
- **Actionable array** — the current price plus all identified support/resistance levels, sorted low to high; the two levels bracketing price define the immediate trading range.
- **Personal balance sheet / journal** — emotional-conditioning tools: a written list of personal behavioral triggers, and a daily journal for venting opinions so they don't leak into trades.
- **Golden Sequence / Fibonacci calculator** — Fibonacci retracement/extension levels used as a confirmation tool for longer-term reversal levels already identified by technical analysis, not as a standalone signal.
- **Stock filters / sentiment table** — screening tools (from a ~100-stock focus list) that rank stocks by proximity to support/resistance and by near/mid/long-term strength, used as a contrarian sentiment gauge.

## Rules and setups

The book's actionable core is a support/resistance array combined with two buy rules and two short rules, applied identically to the market and to individual instruments:

1. **Buy** when support is tested, or when resistance breaks higher.
2. **Short** when resistance is tested, or when support breaks lower.
3. **Test definition**: price within a small tolerance of a level — 0.05 points for individual stocks, 3 points for the NASDAQ index, 25 points for the Dow.
4. Six named strategies apply these rules with different instruments, holding periods, and stops (see system sub-page for full parameters): **Featured Stock of the Day** (day trading one stock, $0.21 stop/$0.35 profit stop/$0.10 trailing stop, max 3 re-entries, flat by close); **Stock of the Week** (swing trading one stock Monday–Friday, always flat by Friday close); **Strategic Plan** (position trading the Dow via DDM/DXD ETFs, 0.5% stop, 10% trailing profit stop, $50K position size); **Day Trading Strategy** and **Swing Trading Strategy** (both trade QID/QLD against the NASDAQ using a three-point test rule, five-point re-entry rule, and one-point stop, capping loss near 1.5% per parameter); **Lock and Walk Strategy** (one-hour version of the day trading strategy, locks in gains of 1–2% or exits after 60 minutes).
5. Every strategy ends flat/in cash by design between signals — cash is treated as an active position, not an absence of one.

## Risk and money management

Position size is fixed per strategy (same share count, or a flat dollar amount such as $50,000 per trade) rather than scaled to conviction, so return dispersion comes from win rate and stop discipline, not bet sizing. Stops are numeric and non-discretionary: $0.21 on individual-stock day trades, 0.5% on the Dow-based Strategic Plan, and a "two-stops-per-parameter" rule on the index strategies (roughly 1.5% loss cap) after which that level is retired until retested. Kee stresses that tight stops (versus the 8–10% stops he says many brokers use) are what make frequent small losses survivable. Overnight risk is priced separately per strategy — day trading and Lock and Walk carry none; Swing Trading and Stock of the Week accept it explicitly. No portfolio-level diversification is used; the strategies instead rely on being flat in cash whenever no support/resistance test is active, treated as the primary drawdown control.

## Psychology and discipline

The book frames risk control as fundamentally a discipline problem, not an information problem. Kee's daily routine — build the three-market/three-timeframe array after the close, write and then explicitly discount a one-paragraph opinion, and never revisit the analysis intraday — is presented as a template: "the tortoise versus the hare," favoring small, consistent, unemotional actions over dramatic bets. The personal balance sheet (a written list of one's own bad habits) and personal journal (a daily outlet for market opinions) exist to keep emotion out of the mechanical rules, on the theory that unexpressed opinions otherwise leak into position decisions. A repeated warning: relinquishing a predefined stop "just this once" is the single most common cause of large losses.

## Strengths and caveats

The Investment Rate is a proprietary, non-replicable model presented largely through anecdote and assertion rather than a transparent, testable methodology — readers cannot verify its inputs or backtest it independently, and its forecast (a bottom near 2023) should be treated as an unproven, single-author claim. The book is also a product pitch in places: several tools (stock filters, sentiment table, Fibonacci calculator, trade simulator) are proprietary features of Kee's own StockTradersDaily.com service. That said, the six mechanical strategies are unusually complete and concrete for a Tier-A book — full entry/exit/stop parameters, real trade tables, explicit risk caps. Examples are drawn almost entirely from the 2008–2009 crash and recovery, so realized returns (e.g., "36% YTD while the market fell 7%") should not be extrapolated to calmer regimes without independent testing.

## Who should read it

Best suited to self-directed traders wanting a mechanical, cash-heavy alternative to passive investing who are willing to build their own support/resistance charts daily, or anyone wanting a concrete illustration of converting trendline analysis into hard entry/exit/stop rules. Less useful for buy-and-hold or fundamentals-first investors, since the premise explicitly rejects that approach, or for readers seeking validated backtests rather than a practitioner's live-account narrative.
