# How to Make Money Shorting Stocks in Up and Down Markets: the trader's summary

*Pristine.com report describing a short-selling setup: fade an upper-Bollinger-Band close followed by a gap-down open, on the theory that market makers are net short and want the stock lower.*

**Pristine Capital Holdings** · 1998 · Day Trading & Scalping · intermediate

*Source coverage: full. PDF pages inspected: 1-7. These are study notes, not verified trading results.*

## Summary

This short promotional report argues that specialists and market makers, forced to sell inventory into a strong up move to provide liquidity, often end a strong day "net short." The setup joins that net-short liquidity provider before they cover, using Bollinger Bands and a gap-down open as the trigger, with a defined stop and a scaling profit-protection exit.

## Key points

- **Premise** — a stock closing strongly near its daily high represents heavy buying that market makers absorb by selling inventory, often ending net short.
- **Tool** — 20-period exponential Bollinger Bands (2 SD) on a daily chart covering roughly 3-6 months.
- **Setup 1** — the stock closes outside (above) the upper Bollinger Band, ideally near the day's high, on a wide-range bar (≥$2 high-to-low preferred).
- **Setup 2** — the next day the stock must gap down, opening at least $0.50 below the prior close; without the gap, no trade.
- **Worked example** — Amoco (AN) closed at $47 (above its band) May 1, 1998; opened at $46.25 on May 4 (gap down); shorted near the open with a stop just above the prior high; fell to $40.25.

## Actionable rules

1. Entry filter: close above the upper 20-period/2-SD Bollinger Band, near the day's high, on a wide-range up day.
2. Confirmation: next session must open ≥$0.50 below the prior close; skip if it doesn't gap down.
3. Entry: sell short at market (or via limit) once the gap-down open is confirmed.
4. Stop: 1/8 point above the high of the prior (setup) day.
5. Management: hold 2-3+ days with a trailing stop — e.g., move to breakeven after $1 gain, protect half after $2, two-thirds after $3+.
6. In a broadly bullish market, take profits sooner rather than pressing the short.

## Caveats

This is a promotional piece (it ends pitching paid mentoring) rather than a rigorously tested system: only one detailed worked example is given, with no aggregate win-rate. It predates the 2007 repeal of the uptick rule and assumes late-1990s floor-specialist/market-maker structure that electronic market making has since replaced, so the "forced net short" mechanism no longer operates identically today.

## Who it is for

Swing traders interested in a specific mean-reversion short setup combining Bollinger Band extension with a gap-down confirmation, who understand it needs independent validation on current market structure.
