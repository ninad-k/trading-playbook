Manual Trader

Everything else on this site is a reference. You look something up, you read the note, you leave. That is useful once you already know what you are looking for — and useless when you don’t.

This is the other thing. It is a course, and it runs in one direction.

How it works

Nine stages. Each one names the skill it is going to give you, teaches it, works through an example, hands you one drill to practise, and ends with a short self-check. You are meant to be able to answer those questions before you move on. If you can’t, the next stage will not land, because it is built on the one before it.

There are no reading lists here and no links off to the library mid-lesson. The 582 book notes are one click away and will still be there afterwards. Sending you to them halfway through a lesson would break the thing this course is for, which is building one skill on top of another in order.

Where it goes

It starts with what a price chart actually is — which sounds too basic to bother with, and is where most people’s mistakes are actually rooted. It ends with the matching engine: the auction that produces every price you have been reading, and the order-flow evidence of what large participants are doing inside it.

The middle is the part that decides whether anyone makes money. Stage 6 is where risk enters, and it is deliberately placed after you can read a chart and before you learn a single intraday setup. Almost every course does this the other way round, teaches entries first and money management as an appendix, and produces traders who can spot a pattern and still lose everything.

What it will not do

It will not give you a system to run. There is no set of parameters at the end that you can turn on. What it gives you is the ability to read what is in front of you, size a position so that being wrong is survivable, and judge a method — including the ones sold to you — on evidence rather than on how confident its author sounds.

That is the skill. The rest is practice, and the practice is yours.

The nine stages

  1. What a Chart Actually IsLook at any price chart and state plainly what it records, what it has already thrown away, and why the timeframe you are viewing is a choice you made rather than a fact about the market.
  2. The Candle and the SequenceRead the anatomy of a single candle for what it says about the fight between buyers and sellers, then read three candles in a row as a sequence — without reaching for a memorised catalogue of pattern names.
  3. StructureLook at any chart and say, in one sentence, whether it is trending or ranging, and point at the exact bar where that last changed.
  4. Patterns as Structure in MotionLook at a classical chart pattern and describe it as the swing structure you already know how to read, then state the one thing that would prove it wrong.
  5. Indicators, and What They Actually ComputeState, for any indicator you use, the exact arithmetic it performs on OHLCV data, and explain why loading a chart with several of them at once adds far less than it looks like it does.
  6. The TradeTurn a chart opinion into a position with a stop chosen from structure, a size chosen from your account, and a size calculation you can do by hand before every trade.
  7. The SessionRead the shape of a single trading day and say, at any point in it, what phase it is in and what that implies.
  8. Where Price Comes FromSee the machinery that produces every price you have been reading since stage 1, and know what a retail trader can and cannot actually observe in it.
  9. Your Own PlaybookAssemble everything you have learned into a written, testable process, and know what actually breaks discipline and how to stop it.
