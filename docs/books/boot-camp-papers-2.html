Summary

A 2006 self-published manual by Thomas Yeomans (“CanadianTom”) describing how he abandoned technical analysis after losing money with it and moved to trading the market’s reaction to scheduled economic-report releases (retail sales, NFP, and similar A/B/C-classified reports). The first ~50 pages cover his personal system — preparing for a release, defining a “trigger” (the gap between expected and actual numbers), sizing based on the size of that gap, and exiting at self-defined consistency zones he calls “anchors” — along with a candid, opinionated take on money management. The remaining ~100 pages are a lengthy reference section on U.S. and Canadian economic indicators (leading/lagging/coincident classification, report calendars, GDP/CPI/trade balance definitions), which are general background material rather part of his personal trading rules.

Key points

  • Core method: trade the reaction to scheduled economic releases, not chart patterns; the author states he places market orders immediately after comparing the actual release number to consensus expectations.
  • Trigger: the gap between the expected (consensus) and actual number on a release; a small gap (e.g., 0.1% surprise) is deemed not worth trading, while a large gap (e.g., 10% surprise) is treated as a high-confidence, larger-size trade.
  • Reports are informally classified A/B/C by tradability; the trader recommends using at least two simultaneous news feeds (one audio, one text) to get release numbers as fast as possible, since even a one-minute delay can mean the move is over.
  • Anchors: self-identified price zones (not classic support/resistance or a technical indicator) where price has historically tended to dawdle; used purely as exit/profit-taking areas once trade momentum decelerates, not as entry signals.
  • Money management is described impressionistically rather than as a formal system: the author reports letting his spouse manage withdrawals from the trading account, and explicitly rejects fixed risk/reward ratios or predicted win rates, arguing every trade’s sizing is judged “on the fly.”
  • Broker/platform notes specific to the era: recommends Oanda for its lack of a dealing desk against the trader, flexible lot sizes, and configurable slippage tolerance, with 50:1 leverage described as the maximum offered.
  • General principle stated repeatedly: keep only the capital needed for the week’s planned trades in the live account, to cap worst-case loss exposure to broker failure or a catastrophic move.
  • The lengthy back section catalogs U.S. and Canadian economic indicators (trade balance, CPI, GDP, GNP, ISM/PMI, consumer surveys) and classifies them as leading, lagging, or coincident.

Actionable rules

  1. Trade only around scheduled economic releases where the gap between consensus and actual is large enough to be considered tradable (the author gives no fixed numeric threshold — it is built from experience per report type).
  2. Use at least two simultaneous, low-latency news feeds (one audio, one text) to confirm release numbers before acting.
  3. Size the trade in proportion to the size of the surprise (actual vs. expected) — bigger surprise, bigger position.
  4. Exit at self-identified “anchor” zones once price momentum around them slows, rather than using a fixed profit target.
  5. Keep only the capital needed for the current week’s planned trades in the live trading account.
  6. Do not rely on a fixed money-management or risk/reward formula; size and exit decisions are made per-trade based on current conditions.

Caveats

This is a self-published, first-person, promotional document (it repeatedly recommends the author’s paid live-trade-call service and specific vendor discount codes) rather than a peer-reviewed or independently verified system; claimed win rates (“99% of trades profitable”) are asserted without disclosed statistics or an audited track record. Broker and leverage details (Oanda, 50:1 maximum leverage) reflect the pre-2010 U.S. retail forex regulatory environment and are outdated. The “anchor” exit concept and trigger-sizing approach are explicitly described by the author as discretionary and not systematized, so they cannot be mechanically coded as written. The large economic-indicator reference section (roughly two-thirds of the page count) is general background material, not part of the author’s specific trading method.

Who it is for

Discretionary forex traders interested in news/economic-release trading who want one practitioner’s framework (triggers, anchors, feed selection) plus a general reference on U.S./Canadian economic indicators, with the understanding that the “system” is loosely defined and unverified.
