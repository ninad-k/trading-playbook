The Market as Mass Mind

Phil Dunn · Tier B article beginner

Phil Dunn explains price as the aggregate of participant expectations and presents technical patterns as potentially self-reinforcing beliefs.

Summary

Dunn argues that “the market” is shorthand for the combined decisions of participants reacting to information. Prices move as bids and offers change, and the resulting price level reflects the aggregate expectations of traders rather than an independent market entity (pp. 1-2). He uses this view to explain why technical analysis can appear self-fulfilling: if enough participants identify the same channel support and place buy orders there, the shared behavior can help produce a bounce (p. 3). The article says an unexpected move should prompt a review of either the facts or the trader’s assumptions, while acknowledging that charts record past behavior and do not themselves cause prices to move (p. 2).

Key points

  • Market price aggregates many individual decisions.
  • Shared chart assumptions can create feedback in commonly watched levels (p. 3).
  • Technical analysis is an information-to-behavior mechanism, not secret intrinsic knowledge (p. 2).
  • A failed expectation can indicate wrong facts, wrong assumptions, or both.
  • The source cites a historical BIS FX volume figure of $1.9 trillion daily and over 90% speculative (p. 3).

Actionable rules

  1. Treat chart levels as coordination points whose effect depends on participation, not as physical laws (pp. 2-3).
  2. When price contradicts the plan, audit assumptions and information before blaming the market (p. 2).
  3. Use technical patterns with awareness of external news and the possibility of self-reinforcing or failing expectations.

Caveats

The article is conceptual and uses dated FX statistics. It does not provide a tested pattern, stop, target, or sizing rule.

Who it is for

Beginners learning how market psychology can connect chart patterns with collective behavior.
