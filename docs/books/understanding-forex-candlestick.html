An Introduction to Japanese Candlestick Charting

James de Wet · Tier B — concise note manual beginner

A short candlestick primer defines bodies, shadows, doji, and trend-context reversal formations such as hammers and engulfing patterns.

Summary

De Wet introduces Japanese candlesticks as a way to view the open, high, low, and close while emphasizing the relationship between the open and close. A filled body marks a close below the open; an open body marks a close above it; shadows show the high and low extremes (pp. 2-4). The manual lists terminology such as spinning tops and doji, then explains that reversal patterns only have meaning in the context of the prior trend. A hammer requires a long lower shadow, a small body, little upper shadow, and a downtrend; an engulfing body must cover the prior real body in a definable trend (pp. 5-8). Dark-cloud cover, piercing, morning/evening stars, and shooting stars are also described (pp. 9-13).

Key points

  • Small bodies can signal equilibrium; a doji has little or no real body (pp. 5-6).
  • Identical shapes can imply different things in different prior trends (p. 7).
  • A hammer and hanging man share a shape but differ by location in a downtrend or uptrend (p. 7).
  • A piercing pattern closes above the midpoint of the prior black body (p. 9).
  • A shooting star is presented as a warning rather than a major reversal signal (p. 13).

Actionable rules

  1. Label the body and shadows from OHLC data before interpreting a formation (pp. 2-4).
  2. Require the trend context specified for each pattern; do not trade the shape in a sideways market (pp. 7-9).
  3. Treat a reversal pattern as a warning that the current direction may end; the source says price may move sideways instead of reversing (p. 7).

Caveats

The document is introductory and does not provide expectancy, stop distances, or position sizing. Historical claims about Japanese origins are anecdotal. Pattern names alone do not establish direction or profitability.

Who it is for

Beginners learning candlestick construction and context-dependent reversal vocabulary.
