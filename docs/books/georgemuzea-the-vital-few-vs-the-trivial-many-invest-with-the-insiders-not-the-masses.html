Summary

George Muzea argues that corporate insiders are the “Vital Few” whose aggregate behavior can be more informative than the views of advisers, media personalities, and the investing public. His contrarian “Magic T” combines insider buying or selling with advisory sentiment, media mood, public mood, and market condition. All inputs except insiders are interpreted in reverse: widespread pessimism and an oversold market support buying, while optimism and overbought conditions support staying out (PDF pp. 168-172).

Key points

  • Investment style and market timing should be explicit rather than improvised.
  • Muzea favors top-down market-risk assessment, then uses insider activity to select value or growth stocks.
  • The strongest bottom setup combines heavy insider buying, extreme pessimism, high mutual-fund cash, an oversold market, and panic news (PDF p. 171).
  • Insider signals can lead price by months, so patience is part of the method.
  • Technical confirmation can reduce the lead time of contrarian signals.
  • Relative strength helps identify stocks that resist a broad decline; insider accumulation adds confirmation (PDF p. 156).
  • A 200-day moving average and market internals serve as coincident or confirming evidence, not the initial contrarian trigger.

Actionable rules

  1. Build two columns, Buy and Stay Out. Put aggregate insider activity in the matching column; place advisory, media, public mood, and overbought/oversold readings in the opposite column because they are contrarian inputs (PDF pp. 170-171).
  2. Act most confidently when the inputs cluster on one side; do not interpret one insider purchase as an aggregate signal.
  3. After a contrarian Buy reading, seek price confirmation through improving volume, a trendline break, or a reclaim of the 200-day average (PDF pp. 153-156).

The insider framework in detail

  • Who counts as an insider — officers and directors of listed companies, plus any outside holder of 10% or more; every change in holdings must be reported to the SEC within two business days, and the Freedom of Information Act makes the filings public (PDF p. 62).
  • Normal behaviour, and the divergence that matters — insiders normally buy into price weakness and sell into price strength, because they benchmark the quoted price against what they believe the business is worth. The tradable signal is a departure from that pattern: an insider (Muzea singles out the CFO) selling into falling prices is read as a warning that conditions have deteriorated; insiders buying at prices higher than their own earlier purchases is read as confirmation that conditions are at least as strong as before, with better-than-expected news typically surfacing a few months later (PDF pp. 62-64).
  • Two insider populations — Value Insiders, roughly 70% of the total, who watch book value against price and whose aggressive accumulation Muzea says has accompanied every intermediate or major market bottom he has followed in 37 years; and Catalytic Insiders, who ignore book value and buy only when they can see good news within about two quarters, making them the group momentum buyers should track to judge whether a breakout has support (PDF pp. 64-66).
  • Muzea’s own weighting of the problem — about 60% of investing success is judging the market’s risk/reward at a given moment, 20% is stock selection, and the last 20% is checking the sector and industry, which he suggests doing by pulling the insider-trading profiles of the other leading companies in the same industry as a confirmation step (PDF pp. 71-72).
  • The single rule he most wants remembered — avoid or sell any stock showing insider selling into price weakness; he states plainly that if a reader takes nothing else from the book, this one rule is the one that prevents large losses (PDF p. 72).

Caveats

The method relies on discretionary classification and dated market-data channels. Guarantees and claims of annual opportunities are not supported by a formal test. Insider purchases can reflect signaling, compensation, or personal liquidity rather than superior forecasts.

Who it is for

Contrarian equity investors who want to combine public filings, sentiment, and technical confirmation.
