Summary

An eBook by forex trader Rob Booker (with Jacob Fedje) documenting research into trading the monthly US Non-Farm Payroll (NFP) release for multi-day/multi-week gains instead of the seconds-after-release scalp. It presents two tested “box breakout” systems built on the pre-release trading range, backtested by hand on GBP/USD, EUR/USD, and USD/JPY charts back to late 2002, plus untested ideas (surprise-magnitude filter, indicator confirmation).

Key points

  • NFP release — first Friday of the month, 8:30am ET, US Bureau of Labor Statistics; a major mover for currencies, stocks, and bonds because the Fed weighs it in policy decisions.
  • Why not trade the release itself — the author lists broker unreliability, execution speed, slippage, and outsized loss potential (100+ pip moves in seconds) as reasons to avoid the immediate-release scalp; also warns that short-term NFP trading breeds a “greedy, short-term mindset.”
  • System 1: “New York Box” — draw the high/low of price between 12:00am and 7:00am ET; trade a breakout of that range on NFP Friday, hold for up to a month, with a 100-pip stop and a reversal rule if stopped out.
  • System 2: “No-Friday System” — skip NFP Friday itself; enter on a breakout of Friday’s high/low on a later day, again with a 100-pip stop and one reversal trade per month, held for the full month.
  • Untested idea 1 — only trade when the actual NFP number differs from the expected number by 75,000–100,000 jobs, trading in the direction implied by the surprise or by a New York Box breakout.
  • Untested idea 2 — use MACD-Histogram (12,26,9) and Slow Stochastic (9,3,3) for overbought/oversold, divergence, or moving-average-crossover confirmation of the post-NFP direction.
  • Caution — the author explicitly flags the strategy as not backtested in May and states the report never produced a loss across all of 2005, but also that many months had no valid setup at all.

Actionable rules

  1. New York Box: mark the high and low of price from 12:00am to 7:00am ET on NFP Friday; go long on a close above the box or short on a close below it (can trigger on the first post-news candle or later Friday); hold for the rest of the month.
  2. New York Box stop: place the stop 100 pips beyond the opposite side of the box; if hit, reverse the position with a new stop 1 pip beyond the opposite side of the original box.
  3. No-Friday System: skip Friday; enter on a break of Friday’s high/low on a subsequent day; use the same 100-pip stop and one-reversal-per-month rule; hold for the rest of the month.
  4. Consolidation exit: if 4-5 consecutive bars open and close within the same tight range after entry, exit near breakeven rather than waiting out the full-month hold.
  5. If using the untested surprise-magnitude filter, only trade when the actual-vs-expected NFP gap is 75,000-100,000 jobs; skip smaller or larger surprises.

Caveats

The author is explicit that this is research, not a finished trading system: results are hand-tallied from chart screenshots rather than a rigorous backtest report, sample size is limited (about 4 years, GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/JPY only), and the rule “never worked in May” is asserted without an explanation. Both systems risk 100 pips to capture an uncertain, sometimes-zero-setup monthly move, and neither includes a defined profit target (positions are held for “the entire month” to see the maximum favorable move, which is not the same as a rule for taking profit in real time).

Who it is for

Forex swing traders interested in trading around scheduled US macro releases who want a structured, wider-stop alternative to scalping the NFP print, and are willing to do their own further backtesting before risking capital.
