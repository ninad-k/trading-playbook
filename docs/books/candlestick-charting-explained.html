Candlestick Charting Explained

An introductory primer explains candle bodies and shadows and uses changing open-close relationships to read sentiment.

Summary

This primer introduces Japanese candlesticks as a visual way to compare the open and close within a session while retaining the high and low. The real body spans open to close; upper and lower shadows show the session extremes (pp. 3-4). The text presents candlesticks as a way to infer crowd sentiment and emphasizes that the same closing price can communicate different information depending on where the session opened. Its worked example describes a small bearish body after a rally as evidence that bullish momentum may have stalled; a trader might protect a profit at the close or next open, while a downtrend context could support a short below the candle’s low (p. 6).

Key points

  • Green/positive bodies indicate a close above the open; red/negative bodies indicate a close below it (p. 3).
  • Shadows identify intraday rejection and the session’s high and low (pp. 3-4).
  • Candle interpretation is more useful when compared with the prevailing trend and prior bars.
  • A small body represents a session where buyers and sellers were relatively balanced (p. 6).
  • The primer links a long green body to a sequence of price advances driven by demand and a long red body to falling demand, while presenting this as a sentiment interpretation rather than an order-book measurement (pp. 7-10).
  • It describes consolidation as a battle zone that can precede continuation or reversal, and names four variants: bearish continuation, bullish continuation, bearish reversal, and bullish reversal (pp. 12-16).
  • Support and resistance are treated as areas, with prior resistance potentially becoming support and prior support potentially becoming resistance (pp. 17-19).
  • The source defines up, down, and sideways trends by higher highs/higher lows, lower highs/lower lows, and relatively equal highs/lows, respectively (p. 20).
  • The document is an introduction and points to later units for named patterns.

Actionable rules

  1. Record open, high, low, and close; derive body and shadows before labeling a candle (pp. 3-4).
  2. Interpret a candle in its prevailing trend; the worked example treats the same bearish candle differently when it appears within a downtrend (p. 6).
  3. Use the example’s below-low short idea only as a conditional illustration, not as a universal signal (p. 6).

Caveats

The primer contains promotional pages and does not test candlestick expectancy. Its four consolidation patterns and sentiment explanations are instructional claims, not measured probabilities. The supplied copy includes historical promotional links and should not be treated as current course information.

Who it is for

New traders learning the vocabulary and visual construction of candlestick charts.
