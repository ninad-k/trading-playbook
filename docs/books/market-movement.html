Summary

A 10-page promotional essay from Windsor Advisory Services making the case for contrarian trading at trend turning points. The core argument: price is not set by a rational tug-of-war between supply and demand fundamentals but by the emotional reactions of market participants, a constant of human nature across centuries. The essay walks through historical manias (Tulip Mania, and references to the South Sea Bubble and Mississippi Madness) and crowd-psychology research (Gustav Le Bon, the Milgram-style obedience experiment) to argue that markets are driven by herd behavior, and that traders who can think independently and act against the crowd hold a structural edge, especially at turning points.

Key points

  • The ratio of winning to losing traders has stayed roughly constant historically despite huge advances in information and forecasting technology, implying information access is not the deciding factor.
  • Price is set by the collective, largely emotional judgment of market participants (hope, fear, greed, euphoria), not by objective calculation of fundamentals.
  • Financial “experts” in the media are repeatedly shown to make their worst predictions near major tops and bottoms; a Wall Street Journal dart-throwing contest reportedly beat expert stock pickers.
  • Crowds exhibit collective behavior distinct from individuals — Gustav Le Bon’s “The Crowd” (1890) is cited: a crowd forms a “single being,” is highly suggestible, and reflects the lowest common denominator of its members.
  • Humans are wired to seek group belonging (a survival trait), making it psychologically difficult to trade against the herd even when it is profitable to do so.
  • Historical mania case: Dutch Tulip Mania (1630s) — a single bulb reportedly rose to the equivalent of $20,000+ before collapsing over 90%.
  • The essay’s practical thesis, echoing J. Paul Getty: “buy when everyone else is selling and hold until everyone else is buying.”

Actionable rules

None given as concrete entry/exit rules, position sizing, or stops. The piece is conceptual, ending with a pointer to the firm’s separate “Shooting Star Trading Method” for specific tools to identify and trade emotional extremes — that method’s mechanics are not included in this document.

Caveats

This is a marketing piece for an advisory service rather than a standalone trading manual; it contains no testable rules, backtest data, or risk management framework. Its historical anecdotes (Tulip Mania, the WSJ dart contest, the obedience experiment) are used illustratively and should not be treated as rigorously sourced. The promised toolset for spotting turning points is referenced but not provided in this text.

Who it is for

Traders new to contrarian and crowd-psychology concepts who want a readable, anecdote-driven introduction to why markets overshoot; not for readers seeking a concrete, rules-based system.
