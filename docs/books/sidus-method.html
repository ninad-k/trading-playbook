The Sidus Method

Sidus · Tier B manual beginner

A moving-average tunnel system uses 18/28 EMAs and 5/8 WMAs to signal trend entries and exits.

Summary

The Sidus method uses a one-hour chart, an 18- and 28-period EMA tunnel, and 5- and 8-period WMAs (p. 1). The red EMA tunnel represents longer-term trend; the WMAs provide shorter-term entry timing. A long signal occurs when the WMAs cross the tunnel upward, with extra strength when 5 WMA also crosses 8 WMA upward. Short signals reverse the conditions (p. 1). The basic rule is to enter when the tunnel boundaries cross and exit when they cross again; the author recommends a 10–15 pip stop (p. 2). The method closes a long when price reaches a top and 5 WMA drops below 8 WMA, and closes a short on the opposite cross. The author says he manually backtested EUR/GBP and EUR/USD in real time, but supplies no sample, dates, trade log, or results (p. 3).

Key points

  • Tunnel width and crossings define trend state; narrow or crossing tunnels warn of reversal (p. 1).
  • The 10–15 pip stop is an author recommendation, not a volatility-adjusted rule.

Actionable rules

  1. Use one-hour candles (the source allows 30-minute bars with more whipsaws), an 18/28 EMA tunnel, and 5/8 WMAs (p. 1).
  2. Enter only while the EMA tunnel is extremely narrow or crossing: long when both WMAs cross upward through it, short on the reverse; a same-direction 5/8 WMA cross strengthens the signal (p. 1).
  3. Exit a long when 5 WMA falls under 8 WMA after a top, exit a short on the reverse after a bottom, and close when the two tunnel boundaries cross or collapse together (p. 1).
  4. Use the author’s 10–15 pip stop as a hypothesis requiring pair-specific testing (p. 2).

Caveats

Evidence is a personal testimonial and limited manual backtest. Whipsaws, execution costs, and untested pairs may materially change results.

Who it is for

Beginners testing a transparent moving-average trend template.
