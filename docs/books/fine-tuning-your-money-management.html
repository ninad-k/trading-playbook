Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System

Bennett A. McDowell · 2004 · Tier B — concise note article beginner

A trade-size formula supports 2% per-trade, 2% per-sector, and 6% total open-risk limits.

Summary

McDowell argues that position size should follow the loss affordable at the planned stop, since entries, stop distances, and account equity change from trade to trade. His formula is risk amount less commission, divided by absolute entry-to-stop distance. In the worked example, $500 risk less $80 commission, divided by $1.50 per share, permits 280 shares (p. 1).

The article caps one-trade risk at 2% of account equity, including commission and estimated slippage. It also limits total risk in one sector to 2% and total open portfolio risk to 6% (pp. 2–3). Scaling out is presented as a way to realize part of a gain while keeping residual trend exposure, but it requires divisible size and adequate liquidity (pp. 3–4).

Key points

  • The 2% rule is conditional on a system with at least a 55% win rate and a 1.6:1 average win/loss ratio; those assumptions are asserted, not demonstrated (p. 2).
  • A profitable position ceases to count against the article’s risk budget after its trailing stop locks a gain (p. 3).
  • The IBM example uses a $50,000 account, $1,000 risk, $91.49 entry, $90.23 stop, $51.22 commission, and 753 shares while using margin (pp. 4–5).
  • The author also advises risk capital only, no borrowed trading funds, and a trading account no larger than 10% of net worth (p. 3).

Actionable rules

  1. Calculate size from risk dollars less commission divided by the absolute entry-to-stop distance (p. 1).
  2. Limit each trade and each sector to 2% of account equity, and keep aggregate open risk at or below 6% (pp. 2–3).
  3. Include commission and estimated slippage in risk, recalculate size for every trade, and use a market-derived stop rather than forcing a fixed position size (pp. 1–3).
  4. Scale out only when initial size and market liquidity make partial exits practical (pp. 3–4).

Caveats

The 2%/2%/6% limits and claims that good systems win about 60% are author prescriptions without a risk-of-ruin derivation or test. Treating a profitable stop as zero risk ignores gaps. The 2004 commission and margin examples are dated.

Who it is for

Traders adding explicit sizing and portfolio-risk limits to an already tested method.
