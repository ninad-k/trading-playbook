Summary

A short e-book (stressfreetrading.com) modeled on the “7 habits” self-help format, applied to trading. Each chapter argues that successful traders share a behavioral trait rather than a specific method: they take full responsibility for every trade, trade a system that fits their own personality and risk tolerance, plan every trade in advance and follow that plan mechanically, keep learning, hold genuine self-belief, measure results in points/process rather than dollar emotion, and keep trading as one balanced part of life rather than an obsession.

Key points

  • Habit 1 — Take complete responsibility: never blame the market, broker, or news for a loss; after every loss ask only “Did I follow my rules?” and adjust the rules, not the excuse.
  • Habit 2 — Find a system that fits your personality (time horizon, stress tolerance, need for action) rather than copying a currently popular style (e.g., day trading) that doesn’t suit you.
  • The author estimates most traders quit within six months, and that surviving two years is roughly when a trader develops rules that actually fit them.
  • Habit 3 — Plan a trade and trade the plan: before entering, predetermine the entry, initial stop, profit-taking/trailing-stop approach, and add-on rules, so decisions become automatic rather than emotional (quotes Richard Dennis: “worry about what you are going to do when [prices] get there”).
  • A worked example shows scaling in (buy 100 at $42, add 100 at $50), and raising a trailing stop as price advances, from an initial $39 stop to an eventual $130 exit.
  • Habit 4 — Keep working at learning to trade properly; treat trading as a profession requiring ongoing study, not a one-time skill acquisition.
  • Habit 5 — Maintain positive self-belief, distinct from overconfidence, built on a track record of following one’s own rules.
  • Habit 6 — View trading performance as a score in points/process quality, not as raw dollar wins/losses, to reduce emotional attachment to outcomes.
  • Habit 7 — Keep trading as part of a balanced life rather than letting it dominate; burnout and over-attachment degrade decision quality.

Actionable rules

  1. After every trade, ask “Did I follow my rules?” — if no, identify why before trading again.
  2. Never enter a trade without predefining the initial stop-loss level before or immediately upon entry.
  3. Define in advance how profits will be taken (trailing stop distance, scale-out levels) and how add-on entries (if any) will be triggered.
  4. If you feel the need to ask a third party’s opinion on an open position, treat that as a signal your plan/discipline has failed: close the position, review the plan, and only re-enter once you no longer need outside opinion.
  5. No specific position-sizing percentage, win-rate target, or timeframe numbers are given — the “rules” are behavioral, not quantitative.

Caveats

This is an informal, self-published motivational e-book rather than a researched or tested framework; it contains no statistics, backtests, or specific risk-per-trade figures. Its worked trade example is illustrative, not evidence of a repeatable edge. Content overlaps heavily with other discipline-focused trading psychology titles in this library.

Who it is for

Newer discretionary traders who already have or are building a trading system and need a psychological/behavioral checklist for sticking to it; not for readers seeking indicators, setups, or quantitative rules.
