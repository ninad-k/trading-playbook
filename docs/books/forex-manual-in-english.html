Summary

A generic broker-produced primer on foreign exchange trading, organized in seven chapters: the history and structure of the FX market, the major currencies and central banks, market types (spot/forward), fundamental analysis, technical analysis, Fibonacci/Elliott Wave basics, and a closing survey of FX risk categories. It reads as introductory reference material rather than a tested trading method — no proprietary system or backtested rules are presented.

Key points

  • Covers FX history from the Bretton Woods Accord through free-floating currencies and the launch of the Euro, plus the role of the IMF and G-7 central banks (Fed and counterparts).
  • Describes market structure: trading with brokers, direct dealing, dealing systems, and matching systems; distinguishes the spot market from the forward market.
  • Fundamental analysis chapter frames currency moves around interest rate differentials, economic data, and central bank action, without a scored or weighted model.
  • Technical analysis chapter is the most detailed: Dow Theory basics, chart types (line, bar, candlestick), trend/support/resistance, percentage retracement, and trendlines.
  • Catalogs classic reversal patterns — head-and-shoulders (and inverse), double top/bottom, triple top/bottom, rounded top/bottom, diamond — each with standard signal descriptions.
  • Catalogs continuation patterns — flags, pennants, triangles, wedges, rectangles — and gap types (common, breakaway, runaway, exhaustion) with the standard interpretation of each.
  • Chapter 6 gives a short Fibonacci-analysis primer and an Elliott Wave overview (impulse wave variations, diagonal triangles, truncated fifth/failure waves).
  • Chapter 7 enumerates FX risk categories: exchange rate risk, interest rate risk, credit risk, and country risk, each described conceptually.
  • Ends with a glossary of foreign exchange terms.

Actionable rules

None given as a numbered trading system. The book instead states the conventional interpretation of each classic pattern (e.g., a head-and-shoulders top or a confirmed breakaway gap signals a trend reversal/continuation), which a trader would still need to translate into concrete entry, stop, and sizing rules.

Caveats

This is a broker educational manual, not a tested strategy book — pattern and indicator descriptions are the standard textbook definitions with no statistics on win rate or edge. No author or publication date is given in the source; content (fixed exchange-rate history, absence of the euro-area expansions after early 2000s) suggests an early-2000s vintage. Runs a well-trodden overlap with other general FX/technical-analysis primers in this library, so treat it as a reference/glossary rather than a differentiated read.

Who it is for

Complete beginners to FX who want one document covering market structure, fundamentals, and classic chart patterns in a single pass, or as a glossary/reference rather than a source of a tradable edge.
