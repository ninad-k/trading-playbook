Runaway Market TBBLBG Breakout Method

Mark Boucher · 1999 · Tier A — deep note system intermediate

Boucher's system for entering already-trending 'runaway markets' on Thrust Breakout, Breakaway Lap, or Breakaway Gap bars, filtered by relative strength and fundamental fuel.

What it is

A momentum/breakout system for joining stocks or futures already in a fast, “runaway” trend, rather than catching reversals or picking tops/bottoms. It combines a market-selection filter (relative strength, fundamentals, pattern frequency) with a three-pattern breakout vocabulary the author calls TBBLBG (Thrust Breakout, Breakaway Lap, Breakaway Gap) for entries and adds, plus a percent-of-capital risk overlay with trailing stops. Designed for daily bar charts on stocks and futures, held for weeks to months.

Rules

  1. Screen for runaway candidates. Require all of: 5+ TBBLBG-family patterns within a trailing 21-day window; price has not closed below its 200-day moving average since the uptrend began; Relative Strength rank of at least 65 on pullbacks or 80 on new highs; EPS growth rank of 80+ (IBD-style); institutional ownership under 16% (start scaling out above 40% sponsorship); long-term debt under 50% of capitalization; a specific fundamental catalyst (“fuel”) for continued growth.
  2. Confirm entry on a TBBLBG breakout bar — any one qualifies: Thrust Breakout (bar range ≥2x the 20-day average range; volume above the prior day; close in the top/bottom third of the range; low below/high above the prior close); Breakaway Lap (low above the prior close but below the prior high); Breakaway Gap (low above the prior high, a full gap). Must occur as a continuation out of a brief pause within an established trend, not as an isolated bar.
  3. Optional confirmation. Favor setups where a classical pattern coincides (e.g., a Double Bottom: first low A > second low C; B-to-C distance within 20% of C-to-D; the rally out of C exceeds C).
  4. Pullback alternative. When fuel/runaway criteria are met but no breakout is available, an entry on a pullback of no more than 38% of the prior move is acceptable, at lower reliability than a TBBLBG breakout.
  5. Position building (Creeping Commitment). Add to the position only once the trailing stop has moved beyond the original entry, so added size carries no risk to initial capital.
  6. Exit. Hold with a trailing stop until stopped out; no fixed profit target (“let profits run”).

Risk

  • Theoretical Risk (entry price to initial Open Protective Stop) capped at 2% of account capital.
  • Initial stop set at the nearest market-determined support/resistance from price action, not a fixed percent or dollar distance.
  • Trail the stop to each new consolidation’s support/resistance level once broken in the trade’s favor; tighten further in more volatile conditions.
  • No stated maximum concurrent positions or portfolio-level heat cap.

Caveats

The pattern-frequency statistics behind the screen (e.g., “85% of 1,300 runaway markets”) are the author’s own research and not shown with a reproducible methodology — treat as descriptive, not verified. RS-rank and EPS-rank depend on proprietary data services (IBD-style rankings, TradingMarkets.com’s RS engine) that must be replicated independently. Optimized for U.S. equities with abundant sponsorship/momentum data in a late-1990s bull market; the ownership and EPS-rank filters do not translate directly to futures, forex, or non-U.S. equities. No rule is given for conflicts between TBBLBG entries and classical-pattern confirmation, or for risk across multiple simultaneous runaway positions.
