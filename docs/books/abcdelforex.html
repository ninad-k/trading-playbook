Summary

An Italian-language introductory ebook (“The ABCs of Forex”) distributed free online, aimed at complete forex beginners. It has two halves: first, market mechanics (currency pairs, pip value, leverage, margin, spread, order types, trading sessions); second, a broad tour of technical analysis tools (support/resistance, trendlines/channels, Fibonacci, moving averages, Bollinger Bands, MACD, oscillators vs. lagging indicators, chart patterns, pivot points, multi-timeframe analysis, and Elliott Wave) plus a short section on choosing a regulated broker.

Key points

  • Pip mechanics — a EUR/USD move from 1.3615 to 1.3630 is worth 15 pips; with 100:1 leverage, a $1 position gains/loses $0.15 (15% of capital) on that move.
  • Leverage range — typical retail forex leverage runs from 50:1 to 500:1 depending on broker.
  • Margin — available margin = total capital − money committed to open positions + unrealized P&L on those positions; some brokers auto-close positions at zero margin, others let equity go negative.
  • Order types — market order (immediate fill), limit order (fills only at a chosen price), stop-loss order (closes an open position at a chosen adverse price).
  • Best trading windows — the Tokyo/London and London/New York session overlaps carry the highest volume; Tuesday-Wednesday are the most active weekdays, while Friday, Sunday’s open, holidays, and the minutes right after news releases are flagged as lower-quality/riskier times to trade.
  • Fibonacci levels used — retracement levels 0.236/0.382/0.500/0.618/0.764; extension levels 0/0.382/0.618/1.000/1.382/1.618.
  • Pivot point formulas — PP=(H+L+C)/3; S1=2×PP−H; R1=2×PP−L; S2=PP−(H−L); R2=PP+(H−L).
  • Indicator classification — oscillators (Stochastic, Parabolic SAR, RSI) are “leading” but prone to false signals; moving-average crossovers and MACD are “lagging” but more reliable, at the cost of a delayed entry; the book recommends using 2-3 timeframes together rather than one.
  • Chart patterns covered — symmetrical/ascending/descending triangles, double top/bottom, head-and-shoulders (and inverse), traded by placing entries on both sides of the pattern’s breakout level.

Actionable rules

  1. Never trade against the prevailing trend, regardless of which tool triggered the signal.
  2. For triangle/head-and-shoulders breakouts, place stop orders on both sides of the pattern so either breakout direction is caught, canceling the unfilled order once one triggers.
  3. Head-and-shoulders profit target = distance from the head to the neckline, projected from the neckline breakout point.
  4. Use no more than 2-3 chart timeframes at once (e.g., 1hr/4hr/daily) to avoid conflicting signals.
  5. Verify a broker’s regulatory registration (e.g., NFA membership in the U.S.) before funding an account.

Caveats

Source document is entirely in Italian; this note is a translation/summary, not a direct quotation. It is a generic, non-proprietary forex-basics compilation (undated, likely mid-2000s given EUR/USD price examples) rather than an original research work — content overlaps heavily with standard retail forex-education material found elsewhere in this library.

Who it is for

Absolute beginners to forex (and Italian-speaking readers specifically) who need pip/margin/leverage mechanics and a first tour of common technical indicators in one short document.
