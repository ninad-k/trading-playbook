Thoughts on Trading

Joe Ross · Tier B article beginner

Joe Ross argues that behavior modification and adaptation matter more to trading success than collecting indicators or searching for a magic system.

Summary

Ross says the most important trading improvement is changing behavior under changing market conditions. He contrasts this with seminar attendees who seek a secret indicator, guru, or quick route to wealth. Market knowledge and technical vocabulary are necessary starting points, but the decisive skill is adapting to market situations outside the trader’s control and changing situations the trader can control (p. 1). The second page promotes a mental-training product to readers whose discipline wavers in volatile markets or who doubt their system after a few losses. The piece is a reflection and advertisement, not a method or performance study.

Key points

  • Indicators, moving averages, and oscillators do not create success by themselves (p. 1).
  • Traders need to examine how they think and react, not only their chart knowledge (p. 2).
  • Volatility and losing streaks can expose weak discipline (p. 2).
  • Behavior modification includes adaptation to market states and control of personal actions.

Actionable rules

  1. Examine how you think, act, and react under different market conditions (pp. 1-2).
  2. Adapt your behavior when market conditions change, while changing the situations that are within your control (p. 1).
  3. Seek help with behavior modification if discipline falters in volatility or after a few losing trades (p. 2); the source supplies no numerical trading setup.

Caveats

The two-page article is anecdotal and promotional. It provides no measurable intervention, stop, target, timeframe, or sizing rule.

Who it is for

Traders who keep acquiring tools but need to improve execution behavior.
