Summary

A Traders’ Mag article (2003) by Chris Terry arguing most new traders fail from trading without a written business plan, and offering a generic template rather than a market-specific method. The plan has two parts: personal growth (managing psychology/attitude) and a seven-item business plan covering mission, goal-setting, financial/time commitment, record-keeping, trading methodology, drawdown rules, and compensation.

Key points

  • Thesis: “without a plan to win, you automatically plan to fail” — likened to a soccer team with no goal to score into.
  • Personal-growth section recommends Positive Mental Attitude reading (Napoleon Hill, Norman Vincent Peale), positive self-talk, peer association, and regular exercise.
  • Seven-part template: mission statement, trade/personal-growth goals, financial/time commitment, record-keeping, trading methodology, drawdown/contingency rules, compensation.
  • Example financial commitment: fund a short-term account with a $100,000 minimum at up to 4:1 margin, plus separate seminar/book/data budgets.
  • Time commitment should be scheduled specifically (e.g., nightly analysis 8–10pm, or by 6:45am next morning if missed).
  • Record-keeping should log trade rationale, timeframe, profit objective, stop, and a post-trade rule-adherence review.
  • Example trend-entry: 38–50% retracement off highs, price at the 20-period EMA, a bull-flag pattern, stochastic oversold (≤20).
  • Example breakout entry: trade the first hour’s range after 5+ days of consolidation; buy a bull flag off a 1-minute chart on the break.

Actionable rules

  1. Write a mission statement defining the kind of trader you intend to become.
  2. Define max trades/day, target points-per-trade, and a reading goal.
  3. Commit a specific dollar amount and specific hours in writing.
  4. Log every trade (rationale, timeframe, target, stop, rule-adherence review) and reconcile daily.
  5. Pre-define exact entry/exit criteria before trading a setup live.
  6. Set a hard drawdown rule (example: halt for the day after 3 straight losers, or stop entirely at 50% drawdown) and a written contingency plan.
  7. Decide, in the plan, what monthly income the business must generate.

Caveats

Generic, motivational business-planning advice, not a tested trading system — sample entry criteria and dollar figures are illustrative only, with no performance data. The PMA recommendations reflect one author’s philosophy, not evidence-based psychology.

Who it is for

New or newly-serious retail traders who already have a method and need a structural framework — goals, capital allocation, drawdown rules, record-keeping — for treating trading as a business.
