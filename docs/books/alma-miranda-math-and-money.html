Summary

A newsletter-style ebook arguing that money management, not a “holy grail” indicator, determines trading survival. It walks through why trading is a “minus-sum game” once commissions and slippage are counted, how required capitalization affects whether a strategy is even profitable after fixed costs, how position sizing changes total return for an identical win/loss sequence, and closes with sections on emotional discipline, margin accounts, and compounding.

Key points

  • After a loss, the percentage gain needed to recover exceeds the percentage lost: a 15% loss needs 17.7% to recover; four consecutive 15% losses require roughly 92% profit just to break even.
  • Fixed per-trade commissions mean the same win/loss percentage sequence produces very different total returns depending on account size: the book’s worked example shows -8% total P&L on a $1,000 position size vs. +28% on a $10,000 position size for identical percentage wins/losses.
  • Position sizing is illustrated with a $100 account: risking $25/trade (25% of equity) survives only 4 straight losses, while risking $2/trade (the “2% rule”) survives 50 straight losses — but the more conservative 2% sizing produced far less total profit than 25% sizing in the book’s specific 100-trade example (60% win rate).
  • A free position-sizing calculator and capitalization spreadsheet are referenced as companion tools (external links, not usable from this text).
  • Emotional control is framed as inseparable from money management: decisions should be made when the market is closed, not while watching intraday price action on open positions.
  • Margin accounts are presented as a way to double trading capital/leverage but “can lose faster or profit faster” — the author frames margin interest as a cost of doing business, not a reason to avoid it.
  • The Magic of Compounding chapter models reinvesting profits monthly, illustrated with a hypothetical $5,000 account swing-trading twice a week compounding at an assumed 24%/month.

Actionable rules

  1. Never risk more than a small, fixed percentage of account equity per trade (book contrasts 2% vs. 25% examples; does not mandate a single number).
  2. Base position size on your own back-tested or paper-traded win rate and average win/loss size, not a guess — feed those numbers into a position-size calculator.
  3. Keep separate performance records per distinct strategy; use conservative (not optimistic) back-tested figures until you have real trade data.
  4. Make buy/sell decisions only when the market is closed to remove intraday emotional interference.
  5. Only trade securities priced above $5 if using margin (a U.S. margin-eligibility rule cited in the text).

Caveats

Self-published marketing-adjacent ebook (readers are required to opt into a newsletter to receive it); the compounding illustration assumes a 24% monthly return sustained for 24 months, which is not a realistic or repeatable trading assumption and should be read as illustrative math, not a projection. The position-sizing comparison (2% vs. 25% risk) is a single hypothetical 100-trade sequence, not a robust simulation, and omits drawdown/ruin-probability analysis. No discussion of stop-loss placement despite promising to cover it.

Who it is for

Beginners who have a working strategy but have never quantified how capitalization, commissions, and position size interact to determine overall profitability.
