The Anti

Laurence A. Connors and Linda Bradford Raschke · 1995 · Tier A — deep note system intermediate

Trades the resumption of a longer-term momentum trend after a short-term stochastic pullback 'hooks' back in the trend's direction.

What it is

A retracement-entry pattern that uses two stochastics of different speeds — a fast %K and a slow %D — to define trend and pullback, entering when a short-term correction resolves back in the direction of the slower, longer-term momentum line. The authors call the underlying mechanism “positive feedback”: when a short-term and longer-term momentum cycle both point the same direction, the resulting move tends to be explosive. It works across all timeframes and markets, from daily charts to 5-minute S&P bars, with an average holding period of two to four bars.

Rules

Indicator setup: a 7-period %K stochastic (default smoothing of 4 if adjustable) as the “fast” line, and a 10-period %D stochastic as the “slow” line.

Buy setup (sells are reversed): 1. The slow line (%D) has established a definite upward trend. 2. The fast line (%K) begins rising with the slow line, then a price consolidation/retracement pulls the fast line back down toward the slow line. 3. Enter when price causes the fast line to turn back up in the direction of the slow line again — a “hook.”

Trigger / entry timing (two options): 4. When %K and %D have shown opposing slopes for at least three days (tension building), place a buy stop one tick above the previous day’s bar, trailing it down to each new day’s high if unfilled — this fires when %D returns to a positive slope. 5. Alternatively, draw a trend line across the tops of the retracement/congestion and enter on its break; entering after the %K hook has already formed is also acceptable but implies a shorter remaining holding window (plan to exit within about two days).

Stop and exit: 6. Place the initial stop just below (above, for sells) the bar of entry — at the small swing low/high formed by the retracement. The market “should not” return to this point. 7. Exit on a buying/selling climax within three to four bars; this is a quick trade, not a longer-term hold — average holding time is two to four days.

Risk

Risk is defined tightly by the retracement’s own swing extreme (the low of a pullback for a buy, the high for a sell), placed at the point where the setup’s premise is invalidated. The authors emphasize that “good” trades under this pattern rarely get stopped out — the stop is meant to sit just beyond a level the setup logic says should hold — which keeps average risk small relative to the reward from a successful two-to-four-bar move. No fixed percent-of-equity sizing is given beyond the book’s general money-management rules (full position at entry, immediate stop, trail as profitable).

Caveats

The pattern requires visually identifying a stochastic “hook” and/or drawing a subjective trend line across a retracement, so despite a mechanical entry trigger (a stop order at a defined price), pattern recognition itself retains a discretionary element — the authors note it “may not always be apparent on the bar charts alone.” A related caution: the setup is not meant to become a longer-term trade — entries taken after the hook has already formed should be exited within about two days rather than held for a larger move. No independent backtested win rate, sample size, or drawdown is cited for the Anti specifically; the evidence is a series of annotated crude oil, cotton, Micron Technology, and 5-minute S&P chart examples from 1995.
