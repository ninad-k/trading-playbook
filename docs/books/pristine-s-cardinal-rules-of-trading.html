Summary

A compiled special report for subscribers of “The Pristine Day Trader” newsletter, consisting of a series of dated educational inserts on gap rules, stop-loss discipline, order types, short-selling setups, price-channel trading, and a simple day-count technique for picking short-term bottoms. Trade types (scalp, day, overnight, swing, core) are defined, and most sections give specific dollar/percentage numbers rather than general advice, though the tone throughout is promotional for Pristine’s paid seminars and services.

Key points

  • Gap rule: do not buy a stock that gaps open more than $0.50 above the prior close or the recommended buy price (whichever is higher); $0.37 allowance for “Pristine Lite” plays; a gap beyond the allowance permanently invalidates the trade even if price later falls back into the buy range.
  • $1/$2 rules: once a position moves $1 in your favor, move the stop to breakeven; never let a $2.00 gain turn into a loss.
  • Stop-loss sizing: do not lose more than 8% (10% max) on any stock priced above $15; consider a “time stop” — exit any short-term pick that goes 10 trading days without hitting its target or stop.
  • Order-entry rules: never place a market order before the open; place buy-stop orders only after the stock has opened for trading, to avoid being filled at an artificial opening gap.
  • Short-selling setup: short a stock that closed outside (above) its upper 20-period, 2-standard-deviation Bollinger Band, then gapped down the next day by at least $0.50 below the prior close; stop placed 1/8 above the prior day’s high; hold 2–3+ days with a trailing stop.
  • 30-Minute Gap Buy/Sell Rule: after a stock gaps up (down) at the open by $0.50 or more, wait 30 minutes, then buy (sell) on a break to a new intraday high (low), with a stop just beyond the intraday low (high) — used to distinguish real follow-through from an artificial opening spike.
  • Channel trading: draw two parallel trendlines containing ~90–95% of price action once a trend is established (two higher highs/higher lows), bisect with a midline; buy near the lower channel line, sell (or lighten up) near the upper line, optionally confirmed with a 5,3 stochastic (buy below 95, sell above 85).
  • 3-5-8 day count: in an uptrend, corrections in a rising stock tend to bottom on the 3rd, 5th, or 8th down day; the author looks for two prior instances of the same cycle length before trading it.
  • Trade-type definitions: scalp (1/4–5/8 point objective, intraday), day trade, overnight, swing (2–5 days, $1–$4 objective), and core (weeks to months).

Actionable rules

  1. Do not buy a stock gapping open more than $0.50 above the prior close or recommended entry (whichever is higher); $0.37 for Pristine Lite plays.
  2. Move stop to breakeven once a position is up $1; never let a $2.00+ gain turn into a loss.
  3. Cap loss at 8% (10% max) on stocks above $15; consider exiting after 10 trading days with no target/stop hit.
  4. Short setup: close outside the upper Bollinger Band (20-period, 2 SD), then a next-day gap down of $0.50+; stop 1/8 above the prior day’s high; trail for 2–3+ days.
  5. 30-Minute Gap Rule: wait 30 minutes after a $0.50+ opening gap, then enter on a break to a new intraday high/low with a stop just beyond that day’s opposite extreme.
  6. Buy near the lower channel trendline, sell/lighten near the upper trendline; confirm with a 5,3 stochastic crossing below 95 (buy) or above 85 (sell).
  7. In an established uptrend, look to buy pullbacks that bottom on the 3rd, 5th, or 8th consecutive down day, after confirming the pattern with two prior occurrences.

Caveats

This is a promotional subscriber document (repeated references to Pristine’s paid seminars, “Executioner” order-entry platform, and toll-free numbers) rather than an independent publication; specific claimed win rates and “near perfect” signal language are not backed by disclosed statistics. Several rules (uptick rule for shorting, market-maker/specialist inventory dynamics) reflect late-1990s U.S. equity market structure that no longer applies in the same form after Reg NMS and the 2007 repeal of the uptick rule. No author or publication date is printed in the extracted text; based on content (references to 1998 trades, pre-decimalization pricing in fractions) it appears to date from the late 1990s.

Who it is for

Short-term U.S. equity traders who want a large set of concrete, numbers-based rules for gaps, stops, order entry, and channel/cycle timing, and who can look past the promotional framing.
