3rd Watch

Alan S. Farley · 2000 · Tier A system intermediate

What it is

3rd Watch is Farley’s adaptation of William O’Neil’s classic cup-and-handle (C&H) pattern, scaled down to work across all time frames rather than only the 52-week-high daily setup O’Neil originally defined. The underlying mechanic is a triple-top failure: price rallies to a high, fails twice more (forming the “cup” and “handle”), then breaks out decisively on the third attempt as accumulated selling pressure from the first two failures is finally absorbed.

Rules

  1. Pattern shape: left cup side (rally + failure at a high), base, right cup side (rally back to the old high, second failure), handle (smaller pullback/base), then a third rally that breaks out to new highs. A “cup and two handles” (C&2H) variant bases twice above/around the old highs before breaking out.
  2. Farley’s compressed scan (vs. O’Neil’s strict 52-week/large-cap version): looks for breakouts within 6-month highs rather than 52-week highs; requires no new high within 4 weeks of the signal (short base/handle allowed); requires breakout-bar volume at least 150% of average (O’Neil’s original standard demands volume 50%+ above the 50-day VMA plus stricter cup-volume rules, which Farley considers too restrictive for many valid real-world patterns).
  3. Handle location as accumulation signal: a handle forming below the cup implies accumulation still needs to build before breakout; a handle congesting right above the old highs signals a stronger stock likely to trend sharply once the platform completes.
  4. General pattern variant: works with ascending triangles and rectangle formations that break resistance on the third test, not just the literal rounded-cup shape; count the highs and watch for a pause at the barrier on the third approach.
  5. Intraday application: applies down to very short time frames — even 1-minute charts — by looking for a break of an intermediate high (e.g., the first-hour range) on the third test.
  6. Entry timing: enter on a narrow-range bar forming near the high before the breakout (lower risk, avoids slippage/Level II demand on the actual break), or wait for the breakout and buy the first pullback; if neither is available, drop to the next-lower time frame and enter on any small pullback with a flat stop.
  7. Corrections filter: 3rd Watch candidates dry up fastest of all 7-Bells during market corrections on daily charts — treat a shortage of daily 3rd Watch setups as itself a market-timing signal, and shift to shorter time frames (5-minute/60-minute) which continue producing candidates through corrections.

Risk

Place a stop just below the tight congestion pattern near the third high (for congestion-entry) or below the pullback low (for breakout-pullback entry). Because 3rd Watch breakouts can move sharply, Farley favors longer holding periods and trailing stops here versus other setups — let profits run once the position is in the black, rather than taking a fixed, quick profit target. Measure overhead supply (the next obvious resistance level above entry) before sizing the trade; a nearby intermediate high limits the reward side of the reward:risk calculation even on an otherwise clean setup.

Caveats

Avoid chasing parabolic breakouts through new highs without a base — these lack a well-defined stop level and are prone to violent reversals since there are no trend mirrors below to offer support. The compressed 6-month/4-week/150%-volume filter is Farley’s own relaxation of O’Neil’s original rules and is not validated against O’Neil’s stricter version in the text — it is presented as a practical scan, not a backtested optimum.
