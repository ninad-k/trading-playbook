Summary

John Allen Paulos uses his own WorldCom losses to examine why mathematical literacy does not immunize investors against narrative, overconfidence, averaging down, and social influence. Rather than prescribe stocks, he surveys behavioral finance, technical and fundamental analysis, efficient markets, options, volatility, diversification, chaos, and game theory. His central lesson is epistemic: market outcomes are uncertain, patterns are easy to manufacture after the fact, and being logically correct differs from correctly anticipating other participants (PDF pp. 9-15).

Key points

  • Keynes’s beauty-contest analogy explains why prices reflect beliefs about other investors’ beliefs, not just business value.
  • Mental accounting made an unexpected windfall feel expendable and helped the author concentrate in WorldCom (PDF pp. 9-10).
  • Confirmation bias and attachment made contrary evidence feel like an opportunity to average down (PDF pp. 21-22).
  • Data mining can generate apparently successful historical rules that fail on new data (PDF pp. 34-38).
  • Expected value must be interpreted through utility; the same dollar risk matters differently at different wealth levels (PDF pp. 153-155).
  • Portfolio return is a weighted average, but portfolio risk also depends on covariance.
  • Assets that are not perfectly positively correlated can reduce combined volatility (PDF pp. 156-160).

Actionable rules

These are editorial applications of Paulos’s examples, not rules stated by the author:

  1. Write down what evidence would falsify an investment thesis before buying; do not let ownership change the standard (PDF pp. 21-22, 34-38).
  2. Treat surprising historical patterns as hypotheses and test them on unseen data (PDF p. 37).
  3. Size risk in light of the investor’s utility for gains and losses, not expected dollars alone (PDF pp. 153-155).
  4. Inspect weights and covariances rather than counting holdings as if every additional name diversifies equally (PDF pp. 156-160).
  5. Separate a company’s value case from expectations about other market participants (PDF pp. 11-15).

Caveats

Paulos explicitly offers no trading system (PDF p. 13). The WorldCom narrative is instructive but retrospective, and the mathematical examples simplify real distributions, unstable correlations, taxes, and transaction costs.

Who it is for

Investors who want a readable bridge between probability, psychology, and portfolio reasoning.
