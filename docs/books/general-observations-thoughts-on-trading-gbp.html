Summary

An informal forum post by a self-described offshore currency fund manager (“Marc,” posting as “MakoForex”/”ICT ML”) sharing his personal, non-systematized approach to trading GBP-USD and GBP-JPY, written up after repeated requests from readers of the Global-View trading community. It is a loose collection of chart-reading habits — EMA pullback entries, stop sizing by pair, Fibonacci retracement targets, moving-average crossovers, and notes on the GBP-JPY carry trade — rather than a formal method.

Key points

  • GBP pairs trade thinner than EUR pairs, producing higher volatility and larger, sometimes “nudge-able,” price swings.
  • Once a strong trend move starts, defers to higher-timeframe (hourly, then 4-hour) overbought/oversold readings rather than 30-minute indicators, which can stay pinned at extremes through a strong trend.
  • GBP-USD trending behavior: price tends to ride its 30-minute 20-period EMA and pull back to the 50-period EMA; a favored entry is after the 50 EMA has contained a pullback and price resumes trend direction, with an exit on a candle close through the 20 EMA.
  • Near the end of a trend move the 50 EMA can fail and price runs to the 200-period EMA, which often produces multi-day sideways chop.
  • Cross-checks entries against 30-minute stochastics; if stochastics disagree with an EMA-based entry, defers to the stochastics.
  • Reviews the weekly chart every Sunday for a directional bias for the coming week.
  • Fibonacci behavior specific to GBP: price tends to retrace to and bounce from the 50% level; a break of the 50% level usually leads to a break of the 61.8% level too, and fibonacci projections from a broken consolidation channel reliably reach at least the 50% projection target (cited example: a ~2,000-pip GBP-USD move from 1.7000 to 1.8525 area anticipated this way).
  • Moving-average cross significance by timeframe: a 30-minute 50/200 EMA cross is worth roughly 150–200 pips after confirmation; an hourly 50/200 cross roughly 100–150 pips; a 4-hour 50/200 cross signals the prior trend is dead and a new one is ~25% underway.
  • Describes a March 2004 loss: heavily long GBP-JPY from 191, adding at 200, targeting 210, wiped out by an unexpected UK trade-deficit release that triggered carry-trade unwinding and an 800-pip single-day-and-a-half decline.
  • Warns that trading straight through scheduled data releases with straddled orders is risky even with guaranteed stop/entry fills; cites a personal 75-pip slippage loss on a stop-and-reverse order during a data release.

Actionable rules

  1. Use a minimum 40–50 pip stop on GBP-USD trades (even from a “perfect” entry); use 100–150 pip stops on GBP-JPY.
  2. Enter trend-continuation trades after the 30-minute 20 EMA pullback has been contained by the 50 EMA and price resumes in the trend direction; exit on a candle close back through the 20 EMA.
  3. Treat a break of the 50% Fibonacci retracement level as a signal the 61.8% level will likely also break.
  4. Review the weekly chart every Sunday before trading the week; during indecisive (“on the fence”) weeks, trade smaller and keep stops tight until a key level breaks and holds.
  5. Use OCO (one-cancels-other) orders around scheduled data releases rather than trying to predict the reaction, or flatten positions ahead of the release entirely.

Caveats

Written by an anonymous forum poster (self-identified as a currency fund manager but unverifiable) with no formal backtest, win-rate, or drawdown statistics — the rules are personal heuristics validated only by the author’s own anecdotes, including one described large loss. Book recommendations mentioned (Market Wizards, Reminiscences of a Stock Operator, works by Murphy and Schwager) are general reading suggestions, not part of the method itself. Timeframes and pip figures reflect mid-2000s GBP-USD/GBP-JPY volatility and spreads.

Who it is for

Intermediate discretionary forex traders already comfortable with EMAs, stochastics, and Fibonacci retracements who want to see how one active GBP trader combines them, rather than readers looking for a complete, rules-based system.
