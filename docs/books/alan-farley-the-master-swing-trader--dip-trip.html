Dip Trip

Alan S. Farley · 2000 · Tier A — deep note system intermediate

Buy sharp, low-volume pullbacks within an established uptrend at Fibonacci/S-R confluence, exiting defensively into whatever bounce follows.

What it is

Dip Trip is the first of Farley’s “7-Bells” setups: buying a sharp countertrend pullback within a strong, established uptrend on the expectation that the primary trend reasserts itself. It formalizes “buying the dip” with precise timing rules, and is related to Raschke and Connors’ Holy Grail setup. It works best in earlier trend phases (avoiding already-overbought markets) and on stocks that decline into the pullback on low volume without violating important support.

Rules

  1. Candidate filter: strong prior uptrend, decline on volume lower than the last few rally bars (natural profit-taking, not a change in character), and no violation of important S/R during the pullback.
  2. Depth: corrections typically retrace 38–50% of the prior rally leg before a tradable bounce; use a Fibonacci grid drawn on the rally to find the pullback zone, favoring confluence with a prior high, a moving average, or a double-bottom-style second low.
  3. Time symmetry: the corrective move should complete in fewer bars than the trend wave that preceded it — count bars in the last rally and compare to the pullback’s duration; a pullback taking as long as or longer than the rally reduces bounce quality.
  4. Entry trigger: shift down to the next-lower time frame at the retracement zone and confirm a reversal (e.g., hammer/doji candle, small double bottom) before entering; on intraday charts, favor bottoms forming within the first 45 minutes of the session.
  5. Continuation gap variant: buying the first test of an unfilled continuation gap (roughly the midpoint of the prior trend) is a favored low-risk entry, since continuation gaps rarely fill on the first attempt.
  6. Win rate: Farley states disciplined dip trip execution can carry a 60–75% %WIN, but requires defensive exit management since some dips fail and roll into new lows.
  7. Exit: use a trailing stop or an expansion-based exit; take whatever the bounce offers rather than waiting for a full retest of the prior high, since dip trip bounces are frequently a “B-wave” correction that ultimately fails.

Risk

Stop loss sits just below the pullback’s low or the nearest broken S/R; if a subsequent decline reaches that far, Farley advises exiting immediately rather than waiting for a retest (“odds favor price violating support if a subsequent decline reaches that far”). Because dip trip is a countertrend entry inside an active trend, position sizing should respect the book’s general 3:1 reward:risk filter and the entry should be avoided during the last hour of the session, especially if the stock sits near its daily low.

Caveats

The setup depends on subjective judgment of “strong prior trend” and “natural profit-taking volume,” with no hard volume or percentage thresholds given beyond the 38–50% retracement band. Farley explicitly warns dip trips carry major “trend relativity” risk — a bounce that looks tradable on a smaller time frame can be erased instantly by a larger-scale corrective move, so the setup should always be filtered against the higher time frame in the 3D charting framework.
