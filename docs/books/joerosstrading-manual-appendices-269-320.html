Summary

This extract contains three appendices from Joe Ross’s trading manual: the Law of Charts, the Trader’s Trick Entry, and rules for identifying congestion. It defines 1-2-3 reversals, ledges, trading ranges, Ross Hooks, and several forms of congestion, then uses a long chart sequence to demonstrate entry and trade-management choices. The Trader’s Trick section gives a specific way to enter before a Ross Hook breakout when there is enough room to cover costs and take a profit. The material is self-contained for these patterns even though some cross-references point to the larger manual.

Key points

  • A 1-2-3 high or low is nullified if price reaches or passes point 1; point 2 and point 3 require the specified full corrections rather than merely visible turns (pp. 1-5).
  • A ledge has at least four and no more than 10 bars, two matching highs and two matching lows separated by at least one bar, with matches within three minimum ticks; it must occur within a trend (p. 6).
  • Sideways action is classified as ledges of no more than 10 bars, congestion of 11-20 bars, and trading ranges of 21 or more bars. Trading-range breakouts are said to occur usually on bars 21-29 (p. 9).
  • A Ross Hook is the first correction after the breakout of a 1-2-3 formation, ledge, trading range, or congestion (pp. 7-10).
  • It says that in sideways action, a 1-2-3 plus a Ross Hook can serve as a tie-breaker, while warning that Hook violations can be false breakouts (pp. 11-13).
  • The Trader’s Trick section describes entering ahead of a Hook breakout to reduce exposure to stop-running; its procedure tests the first, second, or third correction bar and stops attempting entries after more than three correction bars (pp. 27-35).
  • The appendix warns that gaps beyond an intended entry can cause slippage, and that adding contracts increases new risk as a move matures (pp. 18, 23-25, 35-37).
  • Congestion by a measuring bar requires opens or closes on four consecutive bars to remain within the measuring bar’s range; congestion by alternation uses four alternating bars when higher-high/lower-low progression is absent (pp. 39-43, 50).

Actionable rules

  1. For a 1-2-3 reversal without a Trader’s Trick opportunity, sell the break of point 2 in a 1-2-3 high or buy the break of point 2 in a 1-2-3 low. Do not enter if price gaps beyond the entry; require price to trade through it (pp. 12-13).
  2. For a Ross Hook, enter on a breakout of the Hook itself when an earlier Trader’s Trick entry is unavailable. Treat the breakout as vulnerable to a false move rather than guaranteed continuation (p. 13).
  3. For a Trader’s Trick long entry, buy a violation of the high of the first correction bar after the Hook only if the distance to the Hook is enough to cover costs and take profit. If unfilled, repeat on the second and third correction bars; stop seeking the entry after more than three correction bars. Reverse the procedure for shorts (pp. 34-35).
  4. When a position has sufficient profit and no additional risk is desired, trail a stop at 50%; after three correction bars in a downtrend, move a lagging stop to one tick above the third correction bar (pp. 21, 23).

Caveats

The 52-page extract contains complete appendix rules but depends heavily on chart illustrations and occasionally refers to other course volumes. Claims about insiders, stop running, and heightened probability are author explanations rather than independently tested mechanisms. The position-building examples leave allocation to trader preference, and no portfolio-level risk percentage or independently tested expectancy is supplied (pp. 20-25, 27-37).

Who it is for

Readers using Joe Ross’s broader manual who want appendix references and chart-reading context.
