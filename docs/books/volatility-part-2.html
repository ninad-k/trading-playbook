Using Volatility Information for Trading

Guy Bower · Tier B — concise note article intermediate

A soybean example uses volatility level, range, consolidation, and speed of change to frame option strategies.

Summary

Bower argues that a volatility number is meaningful only relative to the instrument’s own history. In a soybean-futures chart spanning a little over four years, 20-day statistical volatility is around 25%, with a range from above 42% to about 14% (pp. 1–2). The article asks four questions: where volatility is now, its high and low, where it consolidates, and how quickly it changes (p. 1). Because 25% is near the lower end of the soybean range but would be high for Treasury bonds, the same absolute percentage should not drive the same strategy in every market (p. 2). Bower says short-volatility strategies such as short straddles, butterflies, and ratio spreads may be unsuitable when current volatility is low and could rise quickly. Conversely, high-volatility periods may support shorter holding periods for short-volatility trades, while low-volatility periods may favor longer-dated long-volatility positions with more patience (pp. 2–3).

Key points

  • Relative percentile and rate of change can matter more than the raw volatility percentage.
  • The article distinguishes long-volatility and short-volatility positioning without making a directional call.

Actionable rules

  1. Compare current volatility with the same underlying’s historical high, low, consolidation zones, and transition speed rather than comparing raw percentages across assets (pp. 1–3).
  2. The author treats low relative volatility as a reason to avoid short-volatility structures and consider longer-dated long-volatility exposure; at high volatility, the author expects short-volatility trades to realize more quickly (pp. 2–3). These are suggestions from one chart, not tested rules.

Caveats

The soybean chart is illustrative, not a strategy backtest; no option prices, Greeks, transaction costs, or realized returns are supplied.

Who it is for

Options traders choosing hypotheses from volatility context.
