Summary

A short biographical profile of John Bollinger (Market Masters Pty Ltd’s “MasterStroke” series, reprinted from the Australian Technical Analysts Association Journal, Sept/Oct 2002) that explains why he developed Bollinger Bands in the early 1980s, how the bands are constructed and parameterized for different timeframes, and how to read price behavior relative to the bands, including the %b and bandwidth derived indicators.

Key points

  • Origin — Bollinger created the indicator to fix a weakness of fixed-width trading envelopes: the appropriate envelope width changes over time, so he combined a moving average with a standard-deviation-based variable width.
  • Standard (intermediate-term) parameters — middle band: 20-day simple moving average; upper band: middle + 2 standard deviations; lower band: middle − 2 standard deviations.
  • Short-term parameters — 10-day moving average with 1.5 standard deviations.
  • Long-term parameters — 50-day moving average with 2.5 standard deviations.
  • Reading rule 1 (trend continuation) — price closing outside a band (above upper or below lower) suggests the current trend will continue.
  • Reading rule 2 (band-to-band) — price moving from one band toward the middle average is likely to continue on to the opposite band.
  • The squeeze — powerful price moves tend to begin after volatility contracts and the bands tighten around price action.
  • %b — position within the bands: 1.00 at the upper band, 0.00 at the lower band, 0.50 at the middle.
  • Bandwidth — (upper band − lower band) / middle band; a direct volatility-contraction/expansion measure.

Actionable rules

  1. Plot a 20-day SMA with bands at ±2 standard deviations for standard/intermediate-term analysis (use 10-day/±1.5 SD for short-term, 50-day/±2.5 SD for long-term).
  2. Treat a close outside either band as a signal the existing trend is likely to persist, not immediately reverse.
  3. Watch for band tightening (low bandwidth) as an alert that a large directional move may be imminent — the article does not specify a numeric bandwidth threshold.
  4. Use %b and bandwidth together with price action and other indicators (not bands alone) to generate trade signals; the article explicitly frames Bollinger Bands as most valuable “in conjunction with” other analysis, particularly for options traders.

Caveats

This is a short profile/promotional article, not Bollinger’s own book: it gives the band construction and two basic reading rules but no specific entry/exit/stop system, no numeric squeeze threshold, and no discussion of false signals or whipsaws in ranging markets.
