Summary

This is a one-page results table, not a book or course. It lists roughly two dozen individually numbered trading accounts (rank, account number, first name, country, equity, total rate of return, daily standard deviation, and a “risk-adjusted rate of return” score used to rank them). The header references “Sienna” and “FX TC” in the underlying file metadata, suggesting this is a snapshot exported from a broker’s or fund’s contest or leaderboard tracking system, most likely for a forex trading competition or a bank of managed accounts. There is no explanatory text, no description of the trading system, entry/exit rules, or methodology behind the numbers — it is raw output only.

Key points

  • Accounts are ranked by a “risk-adjusted rate of return” metric, not by raw return alone.
  • Top-ranked accounts combine a positive total return with a low daily standard deviation (e.g., rank 1 shows 2.04% total return with only 0.02% daily standard deviation).
  • Some accounts show extreme total returns (one exceeds 3,000%) but rank far lower once volatility is factored in, because their daily standard deviation is also very high (over 37%).
  • Equity values cluster near a $250,000 minimum, suggesting a required minimum account size for participation.
  • Traders are identified by first name and country only (e.g., Jordan, Bahamas, United States, Germany, Malaysia), consistent with an international retail or managed-account competition.
  • No trade log, instrument list, position sizing, or strategy description is included.

Actionable rules

None given. The page contains no trading rules, entry/exit logic, or risk parameters — only outcome statistics for unnamed strategies.

Caveats

This document is content-free from a study standpoint: it is a results table with no method disclosed, so nothing about the underlying strategies, whether the “risk-adjusted rate of return” formula is a standard Sharpe-style ratio or a proprietary score, or the time period covered can be verified. High raw returns paired with high daily volatility (in some rows over 10% per day) are consistent with high-leverage, high-risk trading rather than skill, and the ranking metric appears designed to reward consistency over raw gains — but this is inference, not stated fact. Treat any numbers here as unverifiable marketing/competition output, not evidence of a repeatable edge.

Who it is for

Not useful as a study document. It might interest someone specifically researching how FX trading contests or managed-account platforms rank participants by risk-adjusted return, but it teaches no trading method.
