Summary

This short promotional article by Mark McRae argues that trading success is not about finding a perfect system but about accepting that trading is “a game of probabilities” and then executing a chosen system’s rules consistently without emotional interference. It offers a three-part mental routine (before a trading day, during a trade, after a trade) intended to build the discipline of a professional trader over a suggested 30-day practice period.

Key points

  • Trading success reduces to following a positive-expectancy system consistently; the specific system matters less than the trader’s ability to execute it without deviation.
  • A “successful day trader” is defined simply as someone who follows a trading plan with a defined entry and exit (target or stop).
  • Day trading is framed as inherently repetitive and “boring” — consistency, not excitement, is the goal.
  • The main obstacle to success is identified as “the emotional you,” not the market or the system.
  • A before-trading visualization routine is suggested: mentally rehearse following the plan, and use a visual metaphor (e.g., imagining each trade as one of 100 boxes) to reduce the emotional weight of any single trade.
  • A during-trade routine emphasizes physical relaxation, controlled breathing (4-count in/hold/out), constant self-talk narrating the trade mechanically, and avoiding “what if” thinking.
  • An after-trade review step asks the trader to rate how rational versus emotional their execution was, framed as two competing “bar charts” to balance over time.
  • The article repeatedly stresses speed of decision-making is still required in live trading — calm and mechanical does not mean slow.

Actionable rules

  1. Before trading, mentally rehearse the day’s plan and visualize following entries/exits/position sizing exactly as designed.
  2. During a trade, use physical relaxation and slow counted breathing (in 4, hold 4, out 4) to reduce emotional interference.
  3. Narrate each trade’s mechanics out loud or internally (entry time/price, signal, target, stop, any stop adjustments) to stay objective.
  4. Avoid “what if” and speculative thinking while in a position; treat the trade as mechanical execution of a pre-defined plan.
  5. After each session, review and rate the balance between rational and emotional decision-making rather than only reviewing P/L.
  6. Practice this before/during/after routine consistently for 30 days to build the habit.

Caveats

This is a short, informally written promotional piece (distributed as free content linked to a trading course) with no specific market, indicator, or risk-management numbers, and no evidence or citations behind its claims. It should be read as a psychology/discipline primer, not as a tested trading methodology.

Who it is for

Beginner day traders who already have a trading system but struggle with emotional discipline in execution.
