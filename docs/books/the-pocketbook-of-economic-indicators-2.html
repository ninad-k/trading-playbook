Summary

This is a beginner’s reference guide defining 20 US economic indicators that investors and traders commonly watch to anticipate Federal Reserve interest-rate decisions and broad market direction. For each indicator it gives a plain-language definition, why it matters, the releasing agency, exact release timing, frequency, and how/when the data gets revised. The framing throughout is that indicators feed the Fed’s inflation/growth assessment, which drives interest-rate moves, which drive stock and bond prices.

Key points

  • Beige Book — Fed’s anecdotal regional economic summary, released 8x/year, 2 weeks before each FOMC meeting; not revised.
  • Chicago PMI / Philadelphia Fed / ISM Manufacturing — regional and national factory surveys; readings above 50 = expansion, below 50 = contraction; ISM is “the king” of manufacturing indices.
  • CPI / PPI — “core” versions (ex food & energy) are the ones to watch for underlying inflation trend; PPI changes precede CPI moves.
  • Employment Situation (nonfarm payrolls) — first Friday of the month, the single most closely watched release; full employment framed as 5.5-6.0% unemployment.
  • GDP — the broadest activity measure; “healthy” growth framed as 2.0-2.5% at 5.5-6.0% unemployment; quarterly with 2 subsequent revisions.
  • Housing Starts, Existing/New Home Sales — housing is only ~4% of GDP levels but a much larger share of GDP changes due to volatility.
  • Industrial Production & Capacity Utilization — industrial sector ~25% of GDP but a concentrated source of GDP swings; utilization above 85% is flagged as inflationary.
  • Initial Claims — weekly; a 4-week moving average move of 30,000+ signals a substantial labor-market shift.
  • Consumer Confidence, Personal Income & Consumption, Retail Sales, International Trade, ECI — tied to consumer spending being ~two-thirds of the economy; only Confidence moves of 5+ points are significant.

Actionable rules

  1. Watch “core” CPI and “core” PPI (ex food & energy) rather than headline figures for the underlying inflation trend that drives Fed policy.
  2. Treat capacity utilization above 85% as an inflation-pressure warning sign.
  3. Treat a change of 30,000 or more in the 4-week moving average of initial claims as a substantial labor-market shift.
  4. Treat a Consumer Confidence Index move of at least 5 points as significant; smaller moves are noise.
  5. Prioritize the Employment Situation report and GDP release above other indicators for market-moving potential, per the guide’s framing.
  6. Check an indicator’s revision history before trading off an initial release — several (Durable Goods, Housing Starts, Personal Income, GDP) carry “moderate to substantial” revision risk.

Caveats

This is a 2002-era US-only reference; specific release-day conventions, agency URLs, and the ISM/Fed “full employment” and “healthy GDP growth” bands reflect that period’s institutional norms and may have shifted since. It is purely descriptive (definitions and calendar mechanics) — it contains no trading signals, backtests, or position-sizing guidance, and several of the “raw data” web links are now outdated.

Who it is for

Traders and investors, especially in forex and rates/macro markets, who need a quick-reference primer on what each major US economic release measures and when it is likely to move markets.
