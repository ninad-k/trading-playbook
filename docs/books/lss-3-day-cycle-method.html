Summary

A short introductory guide by futures trader George Angell to the “LSS” system, his software implementation of George Douglas Taylor’s 1950s “Book Method.” The core idea: markets are recurrently “engineered” by informed participants — a lower opening creates a buying opportunity (Buy/”L” day), the market rallies to near the prior high (Sell/”S” day), then opens at an extreme for a short-sale opportunity (Short Sell/”SS” day) — a repeating 3-day cycle. Gap moves are explained as stop-loss orders (placed above/below the prior day’s high/low) being run by “smart money,” who then fade the resulting short-lived move. The book gives a practical framework — buy/sell “envelopes,” an overbought/oversold oscillator, and volume clues — for identifying the cycle day and trading it with defined risk.

Key points

  • 3-day cycle — Buy Day (low first, then rally) -> Sell Day (trades near prior high) -> Short Sell Day (high first, then decline), sometimes stretching to 4-5 days.
  • Taylor’s “Book Method” — a running log (“taking the count”) of recent rallies/declines deriving support/resistance “envelopes” rather than a single price.
  • Buy/sell envelope — a price zone, not a single price, allowing averaging in while the zone holds.
  • Non-trending filter — fade rallies if the prior day’s one-day overbought/oversold reading was above 70%; buy dips below 30%.
  • Trending filter — follow trend using a 10-day oscillator: buy above 50%, sell below 50%.
  • Volume signal — tops/bottoms (even intraday) tend to form on markedly higher volume.
  • Suggested markets: S&P 500, US Treasury bonds, Swiss franc, Deutschemark, British pound, silver, NYSE Dow, coffee, copper, soybeans.
  • Day-trading margin cited as roughly half of overnight margin, doubling effective leverage.

Actionable rules

  1. Identify the prior day’s cycle position by whether the high or low printed first, to anticipate the next day’s pattern.
  2. Trade within the buy/sell envelope, not a single price; average in if the zone holds.
  3. Exit immediately on a genuine envelope breakout (price moves away rather than snapping back) — the anticipated pattern failed.
  4. Non-trending days: fade using the one-day oscillator (sell above 70%, buy below 30%).
  5. Trending days: follow the 10-day oscillator (buy above 50%, sell below 50%).
  6. Take small losses quickly when wrong.
  7. Use Market-on-Close orders for day-trade exits.

Caveats

Reflects 1950s pit-trading concepts retrofitted with 1990s/2000s software; several exact envelope-calculation rules are referenced but not reproduced (the author defers to Taylor’s original book). Margin/market specifics are dated.

Who it is for

Short-term futures day traders interested in contrarian, stop-run-fade setups, willing to supplement this with Taylor’s original “The Taylor Trading Technique.”
