DOSR: The Daily Open Support and Resistance Technique

A short forex intraday technique that draws support/resistance from the daily candle's open, high, and low to trade 5-minute breakouts.

Summary

A three-page forex trading note (credited to FXOperator.com, 2007) describing the “Daily Open Support and Resistance” (DOSR) technique. It uses the previous daily candle’s open, high, and low to draw one support and one resistance line, then trades breakouts of those lines on the 5-minute chart with a fixed 1:1 risk-reward.

Key points

  • On a bullish daily candle (close above open), ignore the low and close; draw resistance at the day’s high and support at the day’s open.
  • On a bearish daily candle (close below open), ignore the high and close; draw resistance at the day’s open and support at the day’s low.
  • Enter long when a 5-minute bar closes above the resistance line; enter short when a 5-minute bar closes below the support line.
  • Confirmation is based on a 5-minute bar closing beyond the line, not merely touching it.
  • The author reports testing the method on EUR/USD, GBP/USD, and GBP/JPY with a high proportion of winning trades, though no formal statistics are given.
  • Two execution modes are offered: watch the 5-minute chart and confirm on close, or place resting stop orders at the support/resistance levels with preset stop-loss and take-profit.

Actionable rules

  1. Stop-loss: exit if the 5-minute bar closes beyond the stop-loss level (not on a mere touch).
  2. Take-profit: risk-reward ratio of 1:1 (e.g., 20-pip stop, 20-pip target).
  3. Suggested stop/target sizes by pair: EUR/USD 20 pips, GBP/USD 30 pips, GBP/JPY 50 pips.
  4. Optional scaling: enter with 2+ lots, take partial profit at the first 20-pip target, then move the remaining stop to breakeven to let the rest run.
  5. Only trade the three tested pairs (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, GBP/JPY) unless the trader does further testing.

Caveats

The document is a short, informal trading note rather than a researched system — it gives no sample size, win rate, or drawdown data beyond a single worked example. It predates widespread algorithmic/HFT dominance of short-term FX price action, so the reliability of a simple open-based support/resistance breakout on 5-minute bars is unverified for current markets. Spread and slippage costs at 20-pip targets are not addressed.

Who it is for

Discretionary short-term forex traders looking for a simple, rules-based intraday breakout framework built from the previous day’s candle; not suited to traders wanting statistical validation or multi-asset applicability.
