Summary

An official CBOT educational booklet explaining what a futures contract is and why people trade it (leverage, liquidity, transparency, financial integrity via clearing), the trader types (public, locals, proprietary, market makers) and styles (scalpers, day traders, position traders), core mechanics (ticks, contract months, volume vs. open interest, order types), a short primer on fundamental and technical analysis, and a closing 10-point list of trading guidelines plus a glossary.

Key points

  • Margin is a performance bond, not a down payment: $1,400 margin (~4%) controls a $32,500 soybean contract; $2,400 controls a $100,000 T-bond contract.
  • Distinguishes initial margin, maintenance margin, and margin calls; positions mark to market daily.
  • Four trader/style categories: scalpers (seconds-long, high volume), day traders (no overnight positions), position traders (days to months), market makers (profit from spread).
  • Volume/open interest reading: rising price + rising volume + rising open interest = healthy trend; low volume with high open interest suggests commercial/hedger dominance.
  • Covers market, limit, and stop (including stop-limit) orders, and durations (day, GTC, fill-or-kill, market-on-close).
  • Options on futures: call buyers gain long exposure if exercised, put buyers gain short exposure; sellers take the opposite side for premium with theoretically unlimited risk.
  • Moving averages are trend-confirming, not forward-looking: shorter averages (5-day) generate more signals and whipsaws; longer averages (20-day+) are smoother but slower.
  • Spread trading (e.g., calendar spreads) is presented as a lower-margin, lower-risk alternative to outright positions.

Actionable rules

  1. Trade only with a clear directional thesis — buy low/sell high, or the reverse going short.
  2. Risk only capital you can afford to lose completely; exclude essential savings from trading funds.
  3. Set a definite risk limit (dollar amount or % of margin) before entering, and decide the exit point in advance.
  4. Match contract choice (volatility, liquidity, mini vs. full size) to account size; check margin requirements first.
  5. Diversify across a few contracts without spreading too thin to track.
  6. Write a trading plan before every trade: goal, risk tolerance, exit trigger, order types, monitoring method.
  7. Stick to the plan — cutting a loss at the pre-set exit point under pressure is called a defining trait of successful traders.
  8. Practice with simulated trading before committing real capital; verify broker credentials via the NFA.

Caveats

Vendor-neutral exchange marketing/education material (CBOT, pre-CME-merger era; some contract and fee details are dated), not a trading strategy — the “trading guidelines” are general discipline principles rather than a testable system, and the technical/fundamental analysis sections are introductory only.

Who it is for

Complete newcomers to futures trading who need to understand contract mechanics, margin, and order types before choosing a specific strategy or broker.
