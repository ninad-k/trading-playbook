The Gann Wheel

Summary

A short excerpt introducing the Gann Wheel, also called the Square of Nine — a spiral of consecutive integers reportedly used by W.D. Gann to derive price support/resistance. It explains the wheel’s construction (numbers spiral outward from 1 in a square grid that can be conceptually wrapped into a 360-degree circle) and walks through a manual exercise: locating a price on the wheel and measuring 90-, 180-, and 270-degree rotations from it to find related numbers. It positions itself as a conceptual stepping stone before the (not included here) mathematical/software application.

Key points

  • The wheel spirals consecutive integers outward from 1 at the center, filling a square grid.
  • Overlaying a circle lets any number be treated as a point on 360 degrees.
  • A vertical line through center marks the 0–180 degree axis; a horizontal line marks 270–90.
  • “Up”/”down” refer to greater/lesser numeric value, not position on the page.
  • The wheel functions as a square-root calculator: rotational relationships between numbers mirror relationships between their square roots.
  • A number off a convenient axis (e.g., 37) requires rotating a physical/acetate overlay to align 0 degrees with it before reading related angles.
  • The text draws a speculative, unverified numerological link between the Great Pyramid’s dimensions and Fibonacci ratios.
  • The paper/manual version is noted as hard to apply to instruments under $150 and to many futures contracts without an unspecified conversion factor.

Actionable rules

  1. Place the price on the Square of Nine grid and identify its angle on the implied circle.
  2. Project 90-, 180-, 270-degree rotations to get candidate support/resistance levels (example: from 15, 90 degrees up is 19, 180 up is 23, 180 down is 8).
  3. For an off-axis price, rotate an overlay so its zero-degree line aligns with that price, then read the other angles.
  4. No entry, stop, or sizing rule is given — only the mechanics of deriving numeric levels.

Caveats

A 4-page fragment of a larger course that itself admits practical limitations (poor fit for low-priced instruments, unspecified conversions) addressed “later” elsewhere, not here. The pyramid/Fibonacci aside is unsupported speculation (“who knows?” per the text) and no backtested evidence of forecasting accuracy is given.

Who it is for

Traders already interested in Gann-style geometric price analysis who want a plain-language intro to how the Square of Nine is constructed and read.
