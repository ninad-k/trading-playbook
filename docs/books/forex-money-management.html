Forex Money Management

FX Master · Tier B — concise note manual intermediate

A forex primer defines core equity, caps per-trade risk, scales size to stop distance, and warns against correlated exposure and Martingale sizing.

Summary

This short primer defines money management as the amount of capital and risk committed to one trade. It defines core equity as starting balance minus amounts in open positions and illustrates the concept with a $10,000 account (p. 3). The source prefers 1% risk per trade and says risk should never exceed 3%; its $100,000 example uses a 50-pip stop, $20 per pip, and $1,000 maximum loss, while a 200-pip stop uses $5 per pip (pp. 3-4). It recommends recalculating later trades from core equity and reducing correlated EUR/USD and GBP/USD exposure (p. 4). It rejects Martingale doubling after losses and describes anti-Martingale resizing with gains and losses (pp. 4-5).

Key points

  • Stop distance and pip value determine position size (pp. 3-4).
  • Correlated currency positions can multiply effective risk (p. 4).
  • Five consecutive losses do not make the sixth outcome more likely to win (p. 4).
  • Size should adjust with account equity under the source’s anti-Martingale example (p. 5).
  • The source prefers preserving capital over aggressive recovery.

Actionable rules

  1. Cap planned loss at 1% of core equity in the source’s preferred model; never exceed its 3% ceiling (p. 3).
  2. Set size from stop distance and pip value so the maximum loss stays within the cap (pp. 3-4).
  3. Halve correlated positions when necessary and do not increase size after losses (p. 4).

Caveats

The examples use historical leverage and simplified pip values. This is an educational primer, not a broker-specific calculator or validated strategy.

Who it is for

Forex traders learning basic risk budgeting and correlation-aware sizing.
