Overview

Robert Miner, a technical analyst and educator who has taught Fibonacci/Elliott Wave methods for over 20 years, wrote this book to do what he says almost no trading book does: specify a full trading plan with exact entry price, exact initial stop, and rule-based trade management through the exit, for any market and any time frame. The book (with an accompanying instructional CD, not covered here) works through four factors — momentum, pattern, price, and time — that together identify “conditions with a high probability outcome,” then teaches two objective entry techniques, a position-sizing formula, and a two-unit exit/trade-management plan illustrated with bar-by-bar trade walkthroughs.

Core thesis

No single indicator or technique reliably calls market turns; but when multiple-time-frame momentum, Elliott Wave pattern position, Fibonacci price projections, and Fibonacci/cycle time projections all point to the same zone, the trader has a real statistical edge. Momentum and price frequently diverge (a price trend can continue while momentum reverses, and vice versa), so no single factor is trusted alone. Once a high-probability zone is identified, execution must still be completely objective — entries only after price confirms the anticipated move, stops placed only where the setup itself is invalidated, and exits governed by a trailing stop that lets the market end the trade rather than a fixed profit target.

Key concepts

  • Dual Time Frame Momentum Strategy — trade only in the direction of a higher time frame’s momentum, executing on the next lower time frame’s momentum reversal in that same direction.
  • Momentum reversal — a fast/slow oscillator line cross (Miner’s own DTosc, or Stochastics/MACD); the trigger event for both entries and stop-trailing decisions.
  • Overbought/oversold (OB/OS) filter — a higher-time-frame momentum reading in the OB or OS zone caps new entries in that direction until a reversal occurs.
  • Overlap guideline — Miner’s simplified Elliott rule: overlapping price sections signal a correction (3+ waves, expect trend resumption); non-overlapping five-wave structure signals a trend (impulse).
  • Alternate price/time projections (APP/ATP) — Fibonacci ratio projections of one price/time swing onto the next, used to forecast where and when a correction or trend segment should end.
  • Time Band — an overlap of historical high-to-high and low-to-high (or low-to-low/high-to-low) bar counts, giving a narrow calendar window for a probable reversal.
  • Trailing One-Bar entry (Tr-1BH/L) — enter one tick beyond the high/low of the last completed bar once conditions are met; a close, low-capital-exposure trigger.
  • Swing Entry (SE) — enter one tick beyond the most recent swing high/low; wider stop, higher win rate than the trailing-bar entry.
  • Capital exposure — Miner’s preferred term for dollar risk per trade (he calls “risk/reward ratio” a largely bogus concept since the reward side is always a guess).
  • Two-unit trading — every position is split into a short-term unit (assume the setup is only a minor correction) and a long-term unit (assume a full trend), each with its own exit plan.

Rules and setups

See the linked system pages for full, code-ready parameters. In summary:

  1. Setup filter: higher time frame momentum bullish (not OB) or bearish (not OS) → only trade in that direction following a lower time frame momentum reversal; confirm with pattern overlap (correction vs. trend) and, where available, Fibonacci price/time projections converging in the same zone.
  2. Entry (Strategy 1 — Tr-1BH/L): buy/sell one tick beyond the last completed bar’s high/low following the qualifying momentum reversal; stop one tick beyond the swing high/low made prior to entry.
  3. Entry (Strategy 2 — Swing Entry): buy/sell one tick beyond the most recent swing high/low; stop one tick beyond the swing low/high made prior to entry. Wider risk, higher reliability than Strategy 1.
  4. Position size: never risk more than 3% of account equity on one trade (Unit size = 3% of equity ÷ per-unit capital exposure), 6% max across all open trades, 10% max monthly drawdown before standing aside.
  5. Exit/trade management: two units per trade; trail the short-term unit’s stop to a Tr-1BL/H after a second momentum reversal against the position or a 61.8% retracement, whichever comes first; hold the long-term unit with a wide stop until the higher time frame momentum reaches OB/OS or a Fibonacci price target zone is reached, then trail it tightly.

Risk and money management

Position size is derived mechanically: divide the maximum dollar risk (3% of account equity per trade, 6% across all open trades) by the per-unit capital exposure implied by the entry and initial stop; round down. If the resulting size is unaffordable or the per-contract risk is too wide for a leveraged futures account, Miner’s stated fix is to drop to a smaller time frame for entry (usually shrinking the stop distance) or trade an unleveraged proxy (an ETF or mutual fund) instead of the leveraged future. A maximum 10% monthly account drawdown triggers a stand-down for the rest of the month. Miner explicitly rejects fixed risk/reward ratio filters (e.g. “only take 3:1 trades”) as a largely fictional discipline, since the reward side of any such ratio is never more than a guess.

Psychology and discipline

Miner is blunt that professional traders typically win only 30–50% of trades and that amateurs fail mainly by refusing to accept this, over-sizing positions and widening stops “for room.” He repeatedly warns against exiting winners at a fixed price target (“you can never go wrong taking a profit” is called nonsense) because doing so caps the return on the rare large trend trades that must offset frequent small losses. Multi-unit trading is framed explicitly as a psychological aid — the short-term unit locks in a small win even when the larger analysis turns out wrong, which makes it easier to hold the long-term unit through the drawdowns a real trend requires.

Strengths and caveats

The book is unusually specific and testable for an Elliott/Fibonacci text — every rule states an exact entry, stop, and adjustment trigger rather than “buy around here.” Its worked examples are all after-the-fact and hand-picked to teach the decision process, not a statistical backtest; no win-rate, drawdown, or equity-curve data is presented for the system as a whole. The pattern-recognition layer still leans on Elliott Wave labeling, which retains real subjectivity despite Miner’s “overlap guideline” simplification. Momentum indicator settings (e.g. a 13-day lookback) are explicitly described as re-optimized periodically rather than fixed, which readers should treat as a curve-fitting risk if applied mechanically.

Who should read it

Traders already familiar with basic Fibonacci retracement and Elliott Wave terminology who want a fully specified, mechanical entry-to-exit trade plan rather than another pattern-recognition primer. Less suited to complete beginners, since pattern and price/time projection concepts are taught quickly and assume some prior exposure (Miner’s own Dynamic Trading covers the underlying Elliott/Fibonacci projection method in far more depth).
