Summary

A slide-deck course teaching a day-trading style built around watching a “basket” of a few correlated large-cap Nasdaq stocks (e.g., SUNW, CSCO, ORCL, INTC, BRCM, JNPR, CIEN, QCOM, PMCS) alongside Nasdaq 100 futures (“noodles”), 1-minute and 3-minute stochastics, and Level 2 order-flow reading (cranks/crosslocks, the “ax” market maker). The core idea is to trade only when multiple timeframes and instruments move in “synergy,” and to separate scalping (large size, tight stops, 1-minute signals) from swing trading (smaller size, wider stops, 3-minute signals) within the same session.

Key points

  • A buy/short signal requires synergy across: 1-minute stochastics, 1-minute NQ futures (“noodles”) stochastics, 3-minute noodles stochastics, sector/Tier-1 stock direction, and Nasdaq Level 2 upticks/downticks.
  • Two market regimes: “oscillation” markets (scalp reversals off the 20/80 stochastic bands) and “trend” markets (play 3-minute moving-average consolidation, PUP, or gap-fill breakouts/breakdowns).
  • 2½-point price levels act as recurring support/resistance (“speed bumps”); a stock breaking two consecutive 2½-point levels is flagged as primed for a reversal back to the 5-period range.
  • The trading day is broken into named windows: premarket (8:00–9:15), crank reversals (9:15–9:30), open scalps (9:30–10:00), post-open trends (10:00–11:30), a “dead zone” (11:30–2:00), post-dead-zone (2:00–3:00), and last-hour scalps (3:00–4:00).
  • Recommends starting with 100-share paper/live trades, targeting 70% consistency (7 of 10 trades), before scaling size.
  • Stops are framed as “an expense” and “a pause to reevaluate,” not a failure — the emphasis is on cutting losers fast and never letting a scalp-sized loser become a swing-sized loser.

Actionable rules

  1. Scalps: larger share size, tight stops tied to 1-minute stochastic/noodle premises; exit immediately when the premise fails.
  2. Swings: smaller share size, wider stops tied to 3-minute trend breaks, using 15-period support/resistance for stop placement.
  3. Trade laggard stocks within a basket/sector, since sectors move together from leading to lagging names.
  4. Money management: use $20,000–$50,000 of dedicated risk capital (no more than $100,000), and withdraw excess profits weekly rather than letting balances compound in the trading account.
  5. Avoid trading during the 11:30–2:00 “dead zone.”
  6. Paper-trade first; scale from 100 shares upward only after repeating 70% trade consistency for a full month.

Caveats

Full slide content was read (36 pages, ~1,560 words, terse bullet-point format with heavy Sun Tzu quoting for motivational framing). No author or publication date is given in the source; jargon like “noodles,” “cranks/crosslocks,” and specific named stocks (SUNW, CSCO, JNPR) date this to the early-2000s dot-com-era Nasdaq market structure (SOES, Island ECN, Selectnet), which no longer reflects current market microstructure or order-routing venues.

Who it is for

Intermediate intraday equity traders interested in a multi-timeframe, basket/sector-correlation approach to scalping liquid large-cap tech names, with an emphasis on strict, mechanical loss-cutting.
