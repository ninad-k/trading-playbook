Using COT Report to Forecast FX Movements

Kathy Lien · 2005 · Tier B — concise note article intermediate

Kathy Lien explains using CFTC futures positioning flips, extremes, and open interest as delayed context for spot-FX trends.

Summary

Lien explains why FX traders use the CFTC Commitments of Traders report when spot currency volume is decentralized. The weekly report covers futures positions and is published Friday for the preceding Tuesday, creating a three-business-day delay (p. 1). The article emphasizes noncommercial positioning for currency analysis and describes three uses: flips across net-zero positioning, extreme speculative positioning, and changes in open interest (pp. 2-5). It gives a historical CHF example in which a Federal Reserve study reportedly guessed USD/CHF direction 73% of the time using open interest, but warns that flip behavior differs by currency pair (p. 2-3).

Key points

  • Commercial futures positions may not represent spot-FX exposure well because hedging often occurs in spot markets (p. 2).
  • Currency-futures direction must be mapped carefully to the quoted FX pair; CHF futures and USD/CHF have opposite sign conventions (p. 2).
  • Extreme positioning can signal crowded trades and possible exhaustion (p. 3).
  • Rising open interest can confirm a trend; falling open interest can warn that participation is weakening (p. 4).
  • COT data are delayed and better suited to broader positioning context than fast entries.

Actionable rules

  1. Record the report date and the underlying Tuesday position date before interpreting a COT change (p. 1).
  2. Map futures direction to the FX pair’s base currency before labeling long or short (p. 2).
  3. Check which COT premise has historically fit the specific currency pair; the article says positioning flips work better for some currencies than others (pp. 3, 5).

Caveats

This is a 2005 educational article using historical examples. The reported 73% figure is not a current or universal success rate. COT categories, reporting delays, and market relationships can change.

Who it is for

Forex traders who want delayed futures positioning as a macro and sentiment filter.
