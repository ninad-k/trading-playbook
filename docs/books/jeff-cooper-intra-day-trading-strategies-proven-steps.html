Overview

This is a short, chart-driven book by veteran short-term trader Jeff Cooper, illustrating his named intraday and swing-trading setups directly on annotated price charts (daily, hourly, and 10-minute timeframes, mostly 2003-dated stock examples such as Coach, Avid Technology, Lexmark, ADTRAN, and Rent-A-Center). Every page pairs a chart with hand-labeled callouts pointing to a named pattern — 1-2-3, Lizard, TNT, NR7, ORB, Expansion Breakout, Bubble Oops, and others — as the price action develops. The sampled pages consist almost entirely of these annotated chart plates (numbered A-1 through A-47) rather than connected explanatory prose.

Core thesis

Cooper’s approach treats short-term price action as a sequence of recognizable, repeating structural patterns — thrusts, pauses, pullbacks, gaps, and volatility contractions — that recur across timeframes (the same “1-2-3” or “TNT” labels are applied to daily, hourly, and 10-minute charts of different stocks). The implicit thesis is that a trader who learns to recognize these named structures in real time can anticipate continuation or reversal points with a defined, favorable risk-to-reward entry, particularly around pullbacks after an initial impulse (“the first 2-period pullback sets up a good risk-to-reward entry if the impulse is legitimate”).

Key concepts

  • Cooper 1-2-3 (and 1-2-3+1) — a three-point swing structure (an initial move, a pullback, and a test/thrust point) used as a setup for both continuation and reversal entries; shown on multiple timeframes swinging “to a test of thrust” or breakout.
  • Plus One, Minus Two — after a new multi-day high (a “3-day high” in the example), a 2-period pullback sets up a risk/reward entry if the preceding impulse move was legitimate; the same logic is applied in reverse (“Thrust minus 2”) to short setups.
  • NR7 (Narrow Range 7) — the narrowest daily range of the last seven bars, used as a volatility-contraction signal that frequently precedes an expansion move (referenced repeatedly as a precondition for breakouts).
  • ORB (Opening Range Breakout) — a trade taken on a break of the first period’s high/low range after the open, shown on 10-minute charts as both a breakout and, in one example, a “reverse play” when the breakout fails.
  • Lizard — a reversal/failed-move pattern referenced as “Lizard Buy,” “Lizard Test Sell,” and “Long Tailed Lizard,” generally appearing at a failed tail or rejection point on the chart; the exact trigger rule was not recoverable from the sampled pages (see Rules and setups).
  • TNT (Thrust and Turndown) — a pattern where an initial directional thrust is followed by a turndown/reversal, used both as a standalone label and combined with a “pivot” (“Pivot & Thrust”).
  • Expansion Breakout — a breakout from a flat-top (or otherwise compressed) consolidation range, confirmed by a volume expansion on the breakout bar.
  • Rule of 4 / Angular Rule of 4 Breakout — a breakout pattern referencing a four-bar (or four-point) structure and/or trendline angle; shown alongside gap breakouts (“Gap ‘n Go”) but without a fully stated numeric definition in the sampled pages.
  • Bubble Oops — a short setup that triggers after a persistent parabolic (“bubble”) advance, when the stock gaps open on the next session but then trades below the prior day’s close.
  • Holy Grail — referenced as a first-pullback-after-breakout buy setup (the well-known pattern associated with a trend/moving-average pullback), used here as a label rather than redefined with Cooper’s own parameters.
  • Spike Volume Bottom (SVB) — a capitulation-style low marked by a volume spike, with follow-on “Undercut of SVB” and “Offset of SVB” patterns describing subsequent tests of that low.
  • Gap ‘n Go — a gap that continues in the direction of the gap rather than filling, treated as a bullish (or bearish) continuation signal, often paired with trendline or Rule-of-4 confirmation.
  • 7 Bar Run / 7 Bar Reaction / 7 Day Count — Cooper’s practice of counting bars (typically in groups of seven) within a move to time an expected pause, pivot, or reversal point.

Rules and setups

The sampled pages are chart-annotation plates rather than a rules chapter: they show where each named pattern occurred on a specific chart, but do not provide a consolidated, numbered rule set with explicit entry triggers, stop-loss placement, or profit targets for any setup. What can be stated with confidence from the sample:

  1. Plus One, Minus Two entry: after a new 3-day high (or, in reverse, a new short-term low), wait for a 2-period pullback before entering in the direction of the original impulse; the book states this sets up “a good risk-to-reward entry if the impulse is legitimate,” but does not give a numeric stop or target in the sampled pages.
  2. Bubble Oops trigger: after a sustained parabolic advance, if the stock gaps open the next session but then trades below the prior session’s close, that is the short-signal trigger (“Bubble Oops Triggered”).
  3. NR7 as a precondition, not a standalone trigger: the narrowest-range-of-7 day is repeatedly shown immediately before breakouts (e.g., paired with “Holy Grail Buy, 1st Pullback After Breakout” and with the Rule of 4 Breakout), suggesting it functions as a volatility filter rather than an independent entry signal.
  4. ORB reverse play: when an opening-range breakout fails to follow through, the book shows this failure itself generating a “reverse play” (a trade in the opposite direction), though the specific failure/confirmation threshold is not stated.
  5. All other named patterns (1-2-3, TNT, Lizard, Expansion Breakout, Rule of 4, Jesse James Pivot, Grenade, Triangle Pendulum, Second Mouse): the sample shows real chart examples of each pattern with a qualitative label, but no explicit, numerically specified entry price, stop-loss level, or profit target could be recovered. Per the source-fidelity constraint on these notes, no such numbers are stated here — a reader wanting codeable rules for these setups will need Cooper’s full explanatory text, which was not present on the OCR-sampled pages of this scan.

No system sub-pages were created for this book: although several of these setups (1-2-3, Expansion Breakout, Lizard) are plausibly Cooper’s named, self-contained methods elsewhere in his writing, the pages sampled from this particular scan consist of chart labels only, without the entry/exit/stop specification needed to write rules “complete enough that a reader could code them.”

Risk and money management

No explicit position-sizing, percent-of-equity risk, or universal stop-loss rule is stated in the sampled pages. Stop placement is implied contextually on individual charts (e.g., using the low of a pullback bar, or a prior swing point, as an informal stop reference) but never stated as a numeric rule applicable across setups. The book’s overall approach — favoring short holding periods, defined pullback entries after a confirmed impulse, and volatility-contraction (NR7) preconditions before breakouts — implies a preference for tight, structure-based stops over fixed-percentage stops, but this is an inference from the chart annotations rather than a stated rule.

Psychology and discipline

The sampled pages contain no dedicated psychology or discipline discussion; this appears to be purely a chart-example/appendix section of a larger work, and any psychological content from the full book was not present in the OCR sample.

Chapter map

The sampled material has no chapter divisions or section headings beyond a single continuous sequence of annotated chart plates, numbered A-1 through A-47 (with a handful of intervening blank pages, likely facing-page gaps in the original scan). Each plate shows one stock, one timeframe, and one or more of Cooper’s named setups annotated directly on the price chart. Strengths and caveats

The chart annotations are concrete and grounded in real, dated examples (all charts appear to be from late 2001 through April 2003), which makes pattern recognition tangible in a way that prose description alone cannot. However, this is a significant limitation for these notes: all 48 pages of OCR text have been read, and they consist almost entirely of chart labels and short captions, with essentially no connected explanatory prose defining any setup’s precise entry trigger, stop-loss rule, or profit target. Terms like “Rule of 4,” “Angular Rule of 4,” “Jesse James Pivot,” “Grenade,” and “Triangle Pendulum” are used as if previously defined elsewhere (likely in the main body of a companion Cooper book, such as Hit and Run Trading, that this appendix may have been excerpted from), but that defining text was not present in this scan. Readers should treat every named pattern in these notes as a labeled chart observation, not a validated, backtested rule; no win rates, sample sizes, or performance statistics are given anywhere in the sampled pages. All examples are single-stock, single-instance illustrations from a roughly 18-month window in 2001-2003 — no discussion of how the patterns perform in aggregate, across market regimes, or after transaction costs is present.

Who should read it

Short-term/intraday traders already familiar with Cooper’s terminology (from his other books) who want additional annotated chart examples of his named setups. Not suitable as a standalone introduction to any of these patterns, since the explanatory rule text needed to trade them mechanically is not present in the sampled pages of this particular scan.
