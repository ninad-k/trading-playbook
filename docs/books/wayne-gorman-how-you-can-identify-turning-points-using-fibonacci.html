Summary

Part 1 of a two-part Elliott Wave International ebook by EWI analyst Wayne Gorman, based on a March 2008 webinar. It reviews the mathematics of the Fibonacci sequence and Golden Ratio, then works through how Fibonacci ratios and multiples apply to the internal structure of Elliott waves: how corrective waves retrace prior waves, how impulse wave 5 relates to waves 1-3, how wave 4 divides a move’s price/time range, and how zigzags, flats, and triangles relate their sub-waves by fixed Fibonacci ratios. It closes with a Q&A section clarifying common misconceptions.

Key points

  • Golden Ratio (phi) — 0.618 (inverse 1.618) derived by dividing adjacent Fibonacci numbers to infinity; appears in nature, DNA structure, and a documented 62/38 split in human binary decision-making under uncertainty.
  • Retracement guideline — second waves commonly retrace 0.618 or 0.786 (deep) of wave 1; fourth waves commonly retrace 0.382 or 0.236 (shallow) of wave 3.
  • Wave 5 projection (no extension) — wave 5 ≈ 0.618 × (net distance of waves 1-3) from the end of wave 4, or alternatively ≈ 0.382 × the same distance.
  • Wave 5 with extensions — third-wave extension: wave 5 ≈ wave 1; fifth-wave extension: wave 5 ≈ 1.618 × net distance of waves 1-3; first-wave extension: waves 2-5 ≈ 0.618 × the extended wave 1’s length.
  • Fibonacci dividers — wave 4 (its start or end) commonly splits the whole impulse move’s price range, and separately its time duration, into the Golden Section (0.382/0.618) or occasionally 50/50.
  • Corrective-wave ratios — zigzags: wave C ≈ wave A (or 0.618×/1.618× wave A); flats: wave C ≈ wave B ≈ wave A; expanded flats: wave C ≈ 1.618 × wave A; triangles: alternating legs relate by 0.618 (1.618 if expanding).
  • Scale choice — use log scale for long-horizon/wide-range charts (Fibonacci relationships reflect percent change), but check log, arithmetic, and multiple representations separately.
  • Fibonacci cluster — the most reliable turning-point signals occur where multiple independent Fibonacci relationships (price and/or time) converge at the same level or date.
  • Hard rule (not a guideline) — wave 2 must retrace less than 100% of wave 1, or it is not a valid wave 2 by Elliott Wave Principle’s definition.

Actionable rules

  1. To estimate a wave 5 top/bottom, multiply the net distance traveled of waves 1-3 by 0.618 and project from the end of wave 4 (use 0.382 as an alternate target).
  2. For a wave 2 retracement look for 0.618 or 0.786 of wave 1; for wave 4 look for 0.382 or 0.236 of wave 3.
  3. For triangles, project the next leg as 0.618 × the leg two positions prior (1.618× if expanding).
  4. If no Fibonacci relationship is visible on log scale, re-check the same points using price multiples, then raw arithmetic values.
  5. Treat a single Fibonacci hit as weak evidence; require at least two independent relationships (price and/or time) clustering at a level/date before treating it as high-confidence, allowing roughly a one-day tolerance for time projections.

Caveats

This is guideline-based pattern-fitting, not a mechanical system: the author repeatedly notes ratios are “common,” not guaranteed, and several worked examples miss the exact Fibonacci level by a few points. Assumes the reader can already count Elliott waves (impulse/corrective structure, degree labeling). Part 2 (trading application) is a separate ebook not included here.

Who it is for

Traders already familiar with basic Elliott Wave Principle who want a structured reference to the standard Fibonacci price and time ratios used to project wave targets and identify high-probability turning points.
