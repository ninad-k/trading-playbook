CAI Discretionary Risk Matrix: Speed vs. Impact

J.C. Coppola III (Choice Alternative Investments) · Tier B — concise note article intermediate

A discretionary grid links stop width and risk or entry-size bands to trend structure and the manager's confidence.

Summary

Choice Alternative Investments frames loss through both speed and impact: a large loss realized quickly is described as more destabilizing than a slower or smaller one. Its matrix connects trend condition, stop distance as a percentage of price, and a separately labeled risk band (p. 1).

The four cells are: strong trend with a 2.5–5% stop and 1–5% risk; early trend with a 5–10% stop and 2.5–5% risk; saw-tooth trend with a 10–15% stop and 2.5–5% risk; and late trend with a 15–20% stop and 1–2.5% risk (p. 1). Page 2 restates similar quadrants using “entry size”: narrow-stop positions use 2.5–5% stops and 1–5% entry size, while defensive wide-stop positions use 15–20% stops and 1–2.5% entry size.

Key points

  • The source distinguishes narrow, fast-loss stops from wide, slow-loss stops (pp. 1–2).
  • “One hump behind” means the 5–10% stop band; “two to three humps behind” describes the 10–20% bands (p. 2).
  • Strong, liquid, tightly coiled trends are associated with larger positions; late, choppy, or confidence-impaired conditions receive smaller ones (pp. 1–2).
  • The document suggests flattening, paper trading, pausing, or debriefing after a setback rather than forcing participation (p. 2).

Actionable rules

  1. Classify the setup as strong, early, saw-tooth, or late trend before selecting the source’s corresponding stop and allocation band (p. 1).
  2. Use one prior swing for the tighter 5–10% case and two or three swings for the 10–20% cases (p. 2).
  3. Reserve the larger allocation band for tight patterns with adequate liquidity and reduce or flatten exposure when confidence or market structure deteriorates (p. 2).

Caveats

The percentages are discretionary bands without a track record or definition of whether every “risk” and “entry size” percentage uses the same capital base. Trend stages, confidence, and “humps” are subjective. Stops as wide as 20% require instrument-specific sizing and gap analysis.

Who it is for

Discretionary traders studying how one fund linked stop geometry, trend stage, and psychological state.
