Channel Strategy

Kathy Lien · 2006 · Tier A system intermediate

Trade breakouts from a narrow price channel (two parallel trendlines) on intraday or daily charts, entering 10 pips beyond either boundary and trailing the stop as the breakout develops.

What it is

A channel is formed by drawing a trendline through recent price action and a second line parallel to it, such that most price activity is contained between the two lines. Because currencies tend to spend relatively little time in tight ranges and develop strong trends readily, narrow channels — especially those forming during the quieter Asian session ahead of a London or U.S. session breakout, or just before a major economic release — offer a repeatable breakout setup. The strategy simply trades in the direction of whichever boundary breaks first.

Rules

  1. Identify a channel (two parallel lines) on either an intraday or daily chart, where price is contained within a visibly narrow range.
  2. Place entry orders 10 pips above and 10 pips below the channel (a long stop order above, a short stop order below); whichever fills first becomes the trade.
  3. Place a protective stop just inside the channel, roughly 10 pips under (for longs) or over (for shorts) the broken channel line.
  4. Trail the stop as price continues moving in the breakout direction.

Worked examples (USD/CAD 15-min, EUR/GBP 30-min, EUR/USD 5-min) use a fixed initial target of double the channel’s total width, though a trailing stop (as covered in the book’s risk-management chapter) is offered as an alternative for capturing a larger move.

Risk

Risk is bounded by the roughly 10-pip buffer between the entry (10 pips beyond the channel) and the stop (10 pips inside the broken boundary), so the initial risk is small and largely a function of the channel’s own width and the fixed 10-pip offsets. Because entry orders are placed on both sides of the channel simultaneously, only one side should fill; if both stop orders trigger in a whipsaw (a false break followed by an immediate reversal through the other side), the trader can be caught in back-to-back small losses — the strategy does not include an explicit rule for canceling the untriggered opposite order after the first fills, so traders need to manage that manually.

Caveats

The examples are short-duration intraday setups (5-minute to 30-minute charts) with narrow total channel ranges (13–30 pips), meaning transaction costs (spread) represent a proportionally larger share of the target than in the book’s daily-chart strategies — the book does not quantify spread costs for these examples. As with Lien’s other technical strategies, the worked examples are a small, hand-picked illustrative sample rather than a systematic backtest, and the “narrow channel” and “breakout” concepts are visually/subjectively identified rather than defined by a precise quantitative threshold (e.g., no specific ADX or volatility cutoff is given for what counts as a valid channel).
