Pristine Buy and Sell Setup

Oliver L. Velez · 2001 · Tier A system beginner

Velez's core swing entry: buy after 3+ consecutive lower-highs/lower-lows/red bars breaking above the prior high; short the mirror pattern breaking below the prior low.

What it is

Pristine.com’s named entry pattern for swing trading (1–5 bar upside targets on the buy side, 2–5 day targets on the short side), built on daily charts. The idea is to buy a short, corrective pullback within an uptrend right as it exhausts, or short a short corrective rally within a downtrend right as it exhausts, using a simple three-bar counting pattern rather than a subjective read of the chart. Velez presents it as usable largely stand-alone, though the book frames it as most reliable when the pattern occurs in the direction of the larger (“major”) trend — a minor Stage 2 pullback pattern within a major Stage 2 uptrend, or a minor Stage 4 rally pattern within a major Stage 4 downtrend.

Rules

Pristine Buy Setup (PBS) — for uptrends: 1. Identify three or more consecutive bars each printing a lower high than the prior bar (primary criterion — emphasis is on the highs). 2. Confirm three or more consecutive bars each printing a lower low than the prior bar. 3. Confirm three or more consecutive dark/red (down) bars; an exception is allowed for the close to be above the open on the final (most recent) bar of the pattern. 4. Entry: buy $0.05–$0.10 above the prior day’s high. If the prior day’s high is too far from the current price, an alternate trigger is a break above the first 30-minute high of the current session. 5. Initial stop: $0.05–$0.10 below the entry day’s low or the prior day’s low, whichever is lower. 6. Trade management: once two full bars have closed after entry, trail the stop up under each subsequent bar’s low. Exit when (a) the price objective is reached, (b) a reversal bar forms, or (c) the stock gaps up (taken as a sign the move has already run its course and should be protected/taken).

Pristine Short/Sell Setup (PSS) — for downtrends (mirror image): 1. Identify three or more consecutive bars each printing a higher low than the prior bar (primary criterion — emphasis is on the lows). 2. Confirm three or more consecutive bars each printing a higher high than the prior bar. 3. Confirm three or more consecutive light/green (up) bars; an exception is allowed for the close to be below the open on the final bar of the pattern. 4. Entry: short $0.05–$0.10 below the prior day’s low. If the prior day’s low is too far from the current price, an alternate trigger is a break below the first 30-minute low of the current session. 5. Initial stop: $0.05–$0.10 above the entry day’s high or the prior day’s high, whichever is higher (a trader with low pain tolerance may specifically use the entry day’s high rather than the prior day’s). 6. Trade management: once two full bars have closed after entry, trail the stop down above each subsequent bar’s high. Exit when (a) the price objective is reached, (b) a reversal bar forms, or (c) the stock gaps down.

Directional context filter (both setups): the book’s stated best use is to take PBS patterns only when they occur as a minor-stage pullback within a larger Stage 2 (major uptrend) context, and PSS patterns only as a minor-stage rally within a larger Stage 4 (major downtrend) context — i.e., trade the setup with the dominant trend rather than as a standalone counter-trend or reversal signal.

Risk

Risk per trade is defined by a fixed dollar offset (a nickel to a dime) beyond the relevant prior extreme rather than a percentage-of-account rule, so position size (not stop distance) is the lever a trader must use to control dollar risk per trade — the book does not supply a position-sizing formula. The trailing-stop mechanic (moving the stop to the prior bar’s low/high after two bars) is the main built-in profit-protection tool, but it also means a fast one- or two-bar move against the position before the trail engages can still hit the wider initial stop. Both setups are explicitly time-limited in intent (1–5 bars for buys, 2–5 days for shorts) — the book gives no guidance for what to do if price stalls sideways without either hitting the stop or the trailing exit, which is the setup’s chief unaddressed risk scenario.

Caveats

No backtested win rate, average gain/loss, or drawdown statistics are given for either setup anywhere in the source material — all supporting evidence is a handful of annotated historical chart examples (Chase Manhattan, ALTR, NTRS) rather than a systematic study, and the source explicitly disclaims that hypothetical/simulated results do not represent actual trading. The setup’s stated edge depends on correctly reading the larger trend context (major Stage 2 vs. Stage 4) using the book’s own stage-cycle framework, which is itself a discretionary, chart-based judgment rather than a formally defined indicator — two traders could reasonably disagree on which “stage” a chart is in. The fixed $0.05–$0.10 entry/stop offsets reflect 2001-era, wider-spread, pre-decimalization-adjacent market microstructure for liquid large/mid-cap stocks; on modern penny-tight-spread markets or on more volatile/illiquid names, a trader would need to rescale these offsets (e.g., to a volatility-based measure like average true range) rather than use the fixed dollar amounts as written.
