Andrews’ Pitchfork

Phil Dunn · Tier B — concise note article intermediate

A two-page introduction plots a median line and parallel tines from three reversal points to frame trend channels.

Summary

This short introduction presents Andrews’ Pitchfork as a channel-identification tool for trending markets. It uses three significant reversal points: point A, point B, and an earlier point C. A line is drawn from A to B; a median line begins at C and bisects the A-B line; parallel lines from A and B create the tines (pp. 1-2). The resulting structure can suggest support and resistance where price may stall or reverse. The document notes that Andrews’ broader Action/Reaction work includes additional pivot-count rules, but those are not developed here.

Key points

  • The pitchfork divides a major channel into two equidistant minor channels (p. 1).
  • Three reversal points define the geometry (pp. 1-2).
  • Median and tine lines are potential reaction areas, not guaranteed barriers.
  • The extract is a quick introduction and omits more complex variants.

Actionable rules

  1. Select significant reversal points A and B, then an earlier trend point C (pp. 1-2).
  2. Draw the A-B line, connect C to its midpoint, and project parallels from A and B (p. 2).
  3. Treat line interaction as support/resistance context; the source gives no mechanical entry, stop, or target.

Caveats

The two-page note is descriptive and untested. Choosing “significant” pivots is discretionary, and line breaks can reflect market volatility rather than a reliable reversal.

Who it is for

Chart readers learning the geometry of Andrews’ Pitchfork.
