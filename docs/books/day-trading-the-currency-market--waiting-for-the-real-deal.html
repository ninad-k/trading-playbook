Waiting for the Real Deal

Kathy Lien · 2006 · Tier A — deep note system intermediate

A GBP/USD-specific fade of the initial London-session move, entered only after the early Frankfurt-to-London range is broken in the opposite direction, on the premise that the first move is often a dealer-driven stop-hunt.

What it is

This strategy targets GBP/USD specifically because U.K. and European market makers dominate its pricing and are described as “notorious stop hunters.” The premise: as interbank dealing desks open their books at the start of European trading, they trigger nearby client stops on both sides of the market to capture the pip differential; only once those stops are cleared does the “real” directional move begin. The strategy waits for this initial move to reverse and break the opposite side of the early range before entering, aiming to trade the genuine move rather than the stop-hunt. It works best following the U.S. open or a major economic release, when there is a clear catalyst for the eventual real move.

Rules

Range definition: the “Frankfurt/London power hour” range is the price action in GBP/USD from 1:00 a.m. to 2:00 a.m. New York time (early European trading).

Long setup: 1. Watch for GBP/USD to make a new range low at least 25 pips above the opening price during the power-hour window. 2. Wait for the pair to reverse and break above the high of that range. 3. Place an entry order to buy 10 pips above the high of the range. 4. Place a protective stop no more than 20 pips from the entry. 5. If the position moves in your favor by double the amount risked, cover half the position and trail a stop on the remainder.

Short setup (mirror image): 1. Watch for GBP/USD to open in Europe and trade more than 25 pips above the high of the power-hour range. 2. Wait for the pair to reverse and break below the low of that range. 3. Place an entry order to sell 10 pips below the low of the range. 4. Place a protective stop no more than 20 pips from the entry. 5. If the position moves in your favor by double the amount risked, cover half and trail a stop on the remainder.

Worked examples in the text consistently show entries taken during the early U.S. session, once the initial London move has reversed against the Frankfurt/London range, with trailing stops managed via a two-bar high/low.

Risk

Risk is fixed at up to 20 pips per trade via the initial stop; the built-in first target is double that (up to ~40 pips). All three worked examples in the source book produced net profits of roughly 90–130+ pips combined across both scaled-out portions, but this is a small, hand-picked sample. The strategy depends on GBP/USD’s specific session/liquidity structure (heavy U.K./European market-maker dominance) and a real catalyst (U.S. open or major release) to produce a genuine follow-through move — without one, the “real deal” may never materialize and the position risks being caught in continued chop.

Caveats

This is explicitly a GBP/USD-specific strategy, not a general FX setup, because it relies on the pair’s particular market-maker concentration; applying it unmodified to other pairs is not supported by the text. The “stop hunting” premise describes mid-2000s interbank/voice-broker market dynamics; the extent to which this behavior persists in later, more electronic and regulated market structures is untested in the book. Examples are limited to three illustrative trades from 2005, not a systematic backtest.
