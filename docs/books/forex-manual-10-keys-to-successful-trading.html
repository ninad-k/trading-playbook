Summary

This is the introductory section of Jared F. Martinez’s “10 Keys to Successful Trading” manual, published by Market Traders Institute / ForexTips.com. The chapters that cover the actual 10 Keys (equity management, buy/sell signals, support/resistance, price swings, Fibonacci ratios, trends, trend reversals, fundamentals, and protective stops) are marked “omitted” in this copy’s table of contents — only the foundational chapters survive: what the FOREX is, a trader’s levels of development, reading candlestick charts, and the mechanics of market, limit, and stop orders.

Key points

  • Defines five trader levels: beginner (paper trade ≥1 month), advanced beginner (trade 1–2 lots real money), competent (in control with equity management), proficient, expert (mechanical, no emotion).
  • Describes FOREX structure: ~4,500 participating institutions, no central exchange, ~$1.5 trillion daily volume at time of writing, three participant types (commercial banks, speculators, central banks).
  • Lists FOREX advantages: liquidity, 24-hour access, two-way (long/short) trading, high leverage (margin as low as 1–2% of position value), fast execution, no commissions (cost embedded in the bid/ask spread, cited at roughly 4 pips), strong historical trendiness, and a recommendation to focus on 1–4 currency pairs.
  • Explains candlestick basics: a white/empty body is bullish (close near the high), a black/filled body is bearish (close near the low).
  • Covers bullish reversal patterns: hammer, piercing line, bullish engulfing, morning star, bullish doji star.
  • Covers bearish reversal patterns: hanging man, dark cloud cover, bearish engulfing, evening star, doji star, shooting star.
  • Covers neutral/indecision patterns: spinning top, doji, double doji, harami, long-legged doji, dragonfly doji, gravestone doji.
  • Distinguishes order types: market orders (immediate but subject to slippage of roughly 1–6 pips), limit orders (fill at a specified price or better, 70–90% fill rate once the market trades through the price), stop orders (become market orders once triggered, used mainly as protective stop-losses).
  • Explains order-management procedures: One-Cancels-the-Other (OCO, pairing a profit target and a stop-loss so one cancels the other) and Cancel/Replace (used to ratchet a stop-loss toward breakeven and beyond as a trade moves favorably).

Actionable rules

  1. Complete at least one month of paper trading before risking real money (Level One).
  2. Trade only 1–4 currency pairs when starting out rather than following the whole market.
  3. Always pair an entry with an OCO bracket (protective stop plus profit target) rather than managing the trade manually.
  4. Use Cancel/Replace to walk a stop-loss to breakeven and then progressively lock in profit as a trade moves in your favor (example given: buy Swiss franc at 1.410, stop at 1.390; after a favorable move, cancel and replace the stop at 1.410 then 1.440, locking in 30 pips).
  5. Prefer limit orders over market orders when avoiding slippage matters more than certainty of a fill.

Caveats

This copy of the manual omits Chapters 5–10, which is where the actual “10 Keys” (equity management specifics, buy/sell signals, Fibonacci ratios, trend trading, trend reversals, fundamentals-based consolidation trading, and protective stop rules) are taught — those chapters are listed in the table of contents as “(Omitted).” What remains is solid but introductory: general FOREX mechanics, candlestick pattern names, and order types, with no proprietary system rules. Some figures (daily FOREX volume ~$1.5 trillion, 4-pip spreads, $1,000–$2,000 margin requirements) reflect early-2000s market conditions and are outdated for current markets.

Who it is for

Absolute beginners who need FOREX vocabulary, candlestick pattern recognition, and order-type mechanics before moving on to an actual trading system; not useful on its own for traders seeking specific entry/exit rules.
