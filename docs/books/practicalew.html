Practical Elliott Wave Trading Strategies

Robert Miner · 2002 · Tier B — concise note course advanced

A six-part tutorial turns Elliott-wave labels into conditional trade decisions using pattern rules, invalidation levels, and alternate counts.

Summary

Miner’s tutorial treats Elliott Wave as a framework for conditional decisions rather than a forecasting contest. Five-wave structures are treated as impulses and three-wave structures as corrections; the trader identifies the probable position, specifies what would confirm it, and specifies what would invalidate it (pp. 1–5). The central practical discipline is to trade the market’s confirmation and keep an alternate count when evidence is ambiguous. Examples across bonds, beans, and the S&P show how wave rules determine the side of the market and the maximum logical stop.

Key points

  • The three daily-close rules are: Wave 2 cannot retrace more than 100% of Wave 1; Wave 3 cannot be the shortest impulse wave; and Wave 4 cannot close inside Wave 1’s closing range (pp. 3, 10).
  • After a completed five-wave trend, the minimum expectation is a three-wave ABC correction, even if a larger five-wave move may follow (p. 5).
  • Every count should answer three questions: the probable position, the confirming market action, and the invalidating action (pp. 4, 10–11).
  • If the wave structure is unclear, the author says to stop forcing labels and classify the move broadly as impulse or correction (pp. 4, 11, 33).
  • After a probable Wave 2 high, the tutorial favors shorts while price remains below that high, with a stop no higher than one tick above it (pp. 35–37).
  • After a probable fifth-wave low, it recommends considering longs on the first correction rather than trying to buy the exact bottom (pp. 39–42).

Actionable rules

  1. Label only obvious swings, test the count against the three Elliott rules, and keep an alternate count where evidence is balanced (pp. 3–4, 29–32).
  2. Trade only after the market performs the specified confirmation; abandon the count when its invalidation level is reached (pp. 4, 10–11).
  3. For a short after a probable Wave 2, keep the stop no higher than one tick over the Wave 2 high while the bearish count remains valid (p. 37).
  4. After a likely fifth-wave low, wait for the first corrective pullback to establish a long rather than assuming the exact low (pp. 39–42).

Caveats

Wave labels are discretionary and can be fit retrospectively. The examples are educational case studies, not a statistical performance test. The rules are stated as most relevant to daily closing data and may not transfer unchanged to intraday charts (p. 3). Elliott Wave narrows scenarios but does not guarantee a structure or outcome (p. 42).

Who it is for

Advanced chart readers who want explicit invalidation logic for Elliott analysis. It is a poor fit for anyone seeking a mechanical, parameter-complete system.
