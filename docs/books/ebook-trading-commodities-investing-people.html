Summary

A free lead-magnet ebook (in Italian) from the Investing People seminar company introducing retail traders to commodity futures and options ahead of a paid two-day “Commodities Trading” seminar. It covers commodity groupings, futures/margin mechanics, worked leverage examples (silver, gold, coffee), hedgers vs. speculators, spread trading, and the Commitment of Traders (COT) report. About a third of the document is seminar marketing (curriculum, guarantees, trainer bios, contact details).

Key points

  • Commodities are grouped into grains, the soybean complex, livestock, financial futures (over 80% of global futures volume per the text), metals, softs, and energy.
  • Futures/options transfer price risk from those who must manage it (hedgers) to those willing to assume it (speculators), with exchanges providing centralized price discovery.
  • Margin is a performance bond, marked-to-market daily, varying by volatility and account type (lower for hedgers, higher for speculators).
  • Worked example: a silver future bought near $11.50 and sold near $20.50 ~18 months later, against ~$6,000 margin, shows an ~800% return; the same bet via options is shown at ~1,300%, with capped downside.
  • Gold futures example: 100 troy oz contract, so each $1 move changes contract value by $100; coffee example: 37,500-lb contract, $375 per cent move.
  • Spread trading (long/short in two related futures of different expirations) is presented as generally lower-risk than outright positions (except intercommodity spreads) with reduced margin.
  • COT report classifies open interest into Non-Commercial (“large speculators” — aggressive, tight stops), Commercial (active hedgers, often correctly positioned counter-trend), and Non-Reportable/small traders (often wrong-footed, slow to reverse).
  • The ebook states it is educational only, and that shown results (real or simulated) do not guarantee future performance.

Actionable rules

None given as a testable system — this teaches definitions (margin, leverage, spreads, COT categories), not entry/exit/stop rules; those are reserved for the paid seminar (listed only by topic: direction, time, volatility, credit spreads, debit spreads, iron condors).

Caveats

Marketing content for a paid course: the 800%/1,300% leverage examples are cherry-picked historical moves, and the disclaimer confirms results are illustrative, not representative. No risk-per-trade guidance or backtested performance is included; the COT interpretation offered is a common retail heuristic, not data-supported. Roughly a third of pages are seminar advertising.

Who it is for

Complete beginners to commodity futures and options wanting a plain-language first exposure to contract mechanics, leverage, hedging vs. speculation, and the COT report.
