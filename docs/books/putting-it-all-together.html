Summary

A forum-shared transcript of the fifth and final session of a currency-trading seminar taught by Ed Ponsi (FXEducator). It first demonstrates how to combine a daily-chart support/resistance level with a lower-timeframe (30-minute) entry trigger using RSI and MACD, using two live EUR/USD and USD/CAD examples, then shifts to psychology: realistic goal-setting, judging trades by process rather than outcome, and keeping the plan simple.

Key points

  • Core multi-timeframe idea: a support/resistance level visible on the daily chart is “the same” level on any lower timeframe (a 30-minute chart is just a daily chart broken into smaller pieces, with 48 thirty-minute candles per day) — use the daily level to define the trade and a lower timeframe to time entry.
  • RSI usage as defined in the transcript: buy signal when RSI rises back above 30 after dipping below it; sell signal when RSI falls back below 70 after rising above it.
  • Worked example 1 (EUR/USD, March 2005): price retests a broken daily resistance level (now support) at the same time RSI gives an oversold buy signal on the 30-minute chart, with MACD confirming shortly after — the trade doesn’t need to be held for days just because the level is a daily one.
  • Worked example 2 (USD/CAD): a major daily support level (1.2000) also functions as the boundary of an intraday trading range on the 4-hour chart, generating repeated buy/sell opportunities inside that range.
  • Trading in short timeframes without reference to the daily chart’s key levels is characterized as a common beginner mistake — the daily context materially improves the odds of intraday setups.
  • Goal-setting rule of thumb: targeting 1.5%/week compounds to roughly a doubled account in one year — offered as an attainable alternative to chasing outsized, account-blowing goals.
  • Core discipline principle: “a good trade isn’t always a winning trade, and a winning trade isn’t always a good trade” — judge trades by whether the plan was followed, not by the P&L outcome of any single trade.
  • Advises never reinforcing a lucky win that broke the trading plan, and treating any deviation-driven win as luck rather than skill.
  • Simplicity is favored explicitly over complexity — the text argues elaborate/obscure techniques are not inherently superior to simple ones executed well.
  • Closes with physical/mental-health advice: regular exercise, scheduled breaks from screens, and awareness that overwork can lead to subconscious self-sabotage (e.g., careless trading errors).

Actionable rules

None given as a numbered trading system — this session is about process and mindset rather than a specific entry/exit method. The closest to concrete numeric guidance: 1. RSI buy trigger: RSI crosses back above 30 after being oversold; RSI sell trigger: RSI crosses back below 70 after being overbought. 2. Use the daily chart to identify the key support/resistance level; use a lower timeframe (e.g., 30-minute) only to time entry around that level. 3. Goal-setting target cited as reasonable: ~1.5% account growth per week.

Caveats

This is a shared forum transcript of a spoken seminar segment, not an edited book — it is explicitly the closing, most conversational part of a five-part series, so it assumes the reader already has the technical-analysis, fundamental-analysis, and money-management grounding from the earlier (unavailable here) sessions. No formal backtest, win-rate, or risk-per-trade figures are given for the RSI/support-resistance approach shown; the two worked examples are illustrative rather than statistically validated. The 1.5%/week compounding goal is presented as aspirational guidance, not a guaranteed or typical outcome.

Who it is for

Beginner-to-intermediate traders who already understand basic technical and fundamental analysis and want guidance on combining timeframes and building the discipline/goal-setting habits to execute a plan consistently.
