Summary

A multi-part educational primer (originally a ChartSchool-style article series) covering candlestick construction and interpretation from first principles: how a candle is built from open/high/low/close, the difference between long and short bodies and shadows, Marubozu, spinning tops, and doji variants, then moving into single-, two-, and three-candle reversal patterns with explicit confirmation rules and worked stock-chart examples (EDS, Broadcom, Medtronic, Veritas, Lucent, Ciena, Sun Microsystems, Nike, Genzyme).

Key points

  • Real body and shadows — a hollow/white candle means close > open (buying pressure); a filled/black candle means close < open (selling pressure); shadows show the session’s high/low beyond the body.
  • Marubozu — no shadows; open equals low and close equals high (white) or open equals high and close equals low (black), signaling one side controlled the entire session.
  • Doji family — open ≈ close; variants include the long-legged doji (large indecision), dragonfly doji (long lower shadow, bullish-leaning), and gravestone doji (long upper shadow, bearish-leaning); significance depends on the preceding trend.
  • Hammer / hanging man and inverted hammer / shooting star — identical shapes (small body, one shadow ≥2x the body) whose bullish/bearish meaning depends entirely on whether they form after a downtrend or uptrend; both require next-session confirmation.
  • Star and Harami positioning — “star position” candles gap away from the prior candle (used in evening/morning stars); “harami position” candles are contained within the prior candle’s body.
  • Bullish reversal patterns — bullish engulfing, piercing pattern, bullish harami, hammer, inverted hammer, morning star, and bullish abandoned baby, each requiring a prior downtrend and (except the abandoned baby) bullish follow-through confirmation.
  • Bearish reversal patterns — the mirror set: bearish engulfing, dark-cloud cover, bearish harami, hanging man, shooting star, evening star, and bearish abandoned baby.
  • Confirmation and context requirements — a genuine reversal needs (1) an existing trend to reverse, defined via a moving average, trendline, or peak/trough analysis, and (2) follow-through within 1–3 sessions (gap, long candle, or high-volume move) in the reversal’s direction.
  • Blending candlesticks — a multi-candle pattern can be reduced to one synthetic candle using the first candle’s open, the last candle’s close, and the pattern’s high/low; a bullish engulfing or piercing pattern blends into a hammer, a bearish engulfing or dark-cloud pattern blends into a shooting star.

Actionable rules

  1. Classic hammer/hanging man/shooting star/inverted hammer: the long shadow should be at least 2x the real body’s length, with little or no opposite shadow.
  2. Treat a candidate downtrend as confirmed using any one of: price below its 20-day EMA, each swing peak/trough lower than the last, or price below its down-trendline (10-day EMA for shorter-term traders).
  3. Require bullish/bearish confirmation within 1–3 sessions after most single/double-candle patterns (gap, long candle in the reversal direction, or high-volume move); morning/evening star and abandoned-baby patterns are strong enough not to require further confirmation.
  4. Increase confidence by combining a candlestick reversal with an existing support/resistance level, a momentum divergence (MACD, RSI, Stochastics), and a volume/money-flow signal (OBV, Chaikin Money Flow) pointing the same direction.
  5. In a trading range, use bullish reversal candlesticks/patterns (bullish engulfing, hammer, piercing pattern, three white soldiers, etc.) to confirm support, and the bearish set (bearish engulfing, hanging man, shooting star, three black crows, etc.) to confirm resistance.

Caveats

This is a glossary-and-rules primer, not a backtested system — no win rates, sample sizes, or systematic performance statistics are given; all examples are individually selected historical stock charts (mostly late-1990s/2000 names) illustrating the pattern rather than a rigorous, out-of-sample test. Candlesticks alone do not reveal the intraday sequence of price moves, only the open/high/low/close relationship, a limitation the article states explicitly.

Who it is for

Traders who already understand basic bar charts and want a structured, rule-based reference for recognizing and confirming the standard candlestick reversal patterns before combining them with trend and momentum tools.
