Summary

A pattern-recognition reference, not a narrative book: each entry defines a classic chart formation, its typical duration, required volume behavior, breakout confirmation, and technical price target, followed by short entries on four common indicators.

Key points

  • Continuation patterns covered: flags, pennants (bull and bear variants), ascending/descending/symmetrical triangles, rectangles, rising/falling wedges, and cup-and-handle.
  • Reversal patterns covered: double top/bottom, triple top/bottom, head-and-shoulders top/bottom (with variants for drooping/flat shoulders and multiple heads), rounding top/bottom, one-day and island reversals, diamond top, and broadening top.
  • Flags and pennants require a sharp, high-volume “flagpole” move first; the pattern itself should resolve in under 20 trading sessions, with price targets projected by adding the flagpole’s height to the breakout point.
  • Triangles are read as neutral (symmetrical), bullish (ascending), or bearish (descending); reliable breakouts occur between roughly two-thirds and three-quarters of the way to the triangle’s apex, with volume contracting during formation and expanding on the break.
  • Head-and-shoulders and double/triple top-bottom patterns are reversal signals; their price target is the pattern’s height projected from the neckline or breakout level.
  • DMI (+DI/−DI crossover) signals trend direction; Wilder’s “extreme point rule” sets the reversal stop at the high or low of the crossover bar.
  • MACD (12/26/9 EMA) is read via signal-line crossovers, zero-line crosses, overbought/oversold extension, and bullish/bearish divergence.
  • RSI uses 70/30 overbought/oversold levels (Wilder’s original thresholds) and divergence/failure-swing signals for reversals.

Actionable rules

  1. Flag/pennant formations: require a sharp prior move on heavy volume; pattern should resolve within 20 trading sessions (ideally 1-4 weeks); target = flagpole height added to the breakout level.
  2. Triangle breakout: enter on a close beyond the trendline occurring between 66% and 75% of the way to the apex; target = triangle’s widest height projected from the breakout.
  3. DMI: buy on +DI crossing above −DI, sell on the reverse; place a stop at the high (short) or low (long) of the crossover bar (Wilder’s extreme-point rule).
  4. MACD: buy when MACD crosses above its 9-day signal line, sell on the reverse cross; corroborate with divergence from price at extremes.
  5. RSI: treat readings above 70 as overbought and below 30 as oversold; a failure swing (RSI fails to make a new high/low with price, then breaks its prior trough/peak) confirms a reversal.
  6. Stochastic Momentum Index: buy when SMI crosses back above −40 from below, sell when it crosses back below +40 from above.

Caveats

Undated and author unattributed (likely a compiled broker/website reference, structurally similar to StockCharts.com-style chart-pattern write-ups); contains no track record, backtest statistics, or discussion of pattern failure rates. Several patterns (e.g., triangles, flags) are explicitly noted in the text as capable of failing or reversing rather than continuing, which the entries flag but do not quantify. Best used as a glossary/reference rather than a standalone trading system.

Who it is for

Beginner-to-intermediate technical traders who want a compact, illustrated-style reference defining the standard chart patterns and the four most common oscillator/trend indicators in one place.
