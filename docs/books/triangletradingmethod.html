The Triangle Trading Method

David N. Windover · 1997 · Tier B — concise note manual advanced

A futures method combines symmetrical triangles, volatility contraction, cyclical timing, and a defined exit process.

Summary

Windover presents a symmetrical-triangle method for actively traded futures. It combines volatility breakout and trend-following ideas with a recurring timing tendency, aiming to reduce directional subjectivity. The trader scans daily charts, validates a triangle with explicit geometric rules, and waits for a breakout. A valid formation requires at least 28 days, two reference points on each trendline, and an apex in the center third of the triangle base (pp. 3, 6). The example places an initial stop near the center of the apex and moves it to breakeven on day 4 (p. 3). The author stresses that exits matter more than entries and warns that stops may fill worse than intended (p. 2).

Key points

  • Decreasing volatility is treated as consolidation before a possible expansion (p. 4).
  • The pattern can be applied across markets and timeframes, but the examples use futures (p. 4).
  • Markets trend only part of the time; the guide cites roughly 30% trending and 70% non-trending conditions (p. 5).
  • Missing a breakout should not lead to chasing; wait for another setup (p. 5).
  • The author explicitly rejects hypothetical performance claims and acknowledges pattern-recognition bias (pp. 2-3).

Actionable rules

  1. Require at least 28 days and four alternating trendline reference points (p. 6).
  2. Accept a triangle as symmetrical only when the apex lies in the center one-third of the base (p. 6).
  3. In the illustrated method, use an initial apex-centered stop and move it to breakeven on day 4; treat this as the author’s example, not a universal rule (p. 3).

Caveats

This is a self-reported method with no independent expectancy study. Stop orders may incur slippage, and the author says not every visible triangle was traded. The source recommends chart familiarity and warns against risking money before understanding the rules (pp. 2-3).

Who it is for

Experienced futures traders studying volatility contraction and geometrically defined breakout setups.
