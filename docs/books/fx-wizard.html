FX Wizard

Rob Walton · 2005 · Tier B — concise note manual beginner

A discretionary trend method using alert swings, pivots, ADX and directional movement, with fixed trading hours and trailing stops.

Summary

Walton presents a discretionary trend method built from a chart alert, pivot levels, ADX, and directional movement. The manual frames successful trading as the combination of a trading system, money management, and discipline (pp. 10–12). Its worked sequence marks an entry at the alert bar’s extreme or a nearby pivot, then waits for rising ADX above 20 and a rising directional indicator above 20 before trading (pp. 26–38). The method is demonstrated on 15-minute and one-hour charts and is claimed to work on other time frames, without a controlled test (pp. 26, 45, 49–52).

Key points

  • The author treats fear, greed, hesitation, and system-hopping as execution problems rather than evidence that a method needs constant replacement (pp. 6–12).
  • The chart template uses 5-, 6-, and 80-period exponential moving averages, Heikin-Ashi candles, pivot levels, an alert indicator, ADX, and the two DI lines (pp. 18–25).
  • Example management uses a 25-pip initial stop, then a stop one pivot behind price or roughly 25 pips, with a 100-pip move used as a profit-taking reference (pp. 37–42).
  • The preferred trading window is European/UK hours, 08:00–16:00 GMT; the author allows extension through the U.S. close at 21:00 GMT (p. 45).
  • The manual’s compounding illustration begins with £1,000 and £2 per pip, targets 125 pips per week, and compounds a claimed 25% weekly return (pp. 46–47).

Actionable rules

  1. Mark the relevant high or low of the alert swing as the candidate entry, using a nearby pivot instead when practical (pp. 31–34, 41, 45).
  2. Enter only when ADX is rising above 20 and the DI line for the intended direction is also rising above 20 (pp. 29, 32–38, 45).
  3. Restrict trades to 08:00–16:00 GMT; the author permits an extension to the U.S. close at 21:00 GMT (p. 45).
  4. Define the stop before entry. Worked examples use a 25-pip stop and then trail approximately one pivot or about 25 pips behind price, but the manual gives no percentage-of-equity sizing rule (pp. 32, 38, 41–42).

Caveats

The manual repeatedly describes trades as nearly risk-free and promotes extreme compounding, but supplies no controlled performance study. Its 25% weekly objective is a promotional illustration rather than a defensible expectation (pp. 46–52). The examples use fixed 25-pip stops without connecting them to account equity, so readers must supply their own position-sizing rule. Alert and pivot choices also leave meaningful discretion.

Who it is for

Beginning FX traders learning to turn a discretionary idea into explicit entry, stop, and exit rules. Experienced traders should treat the parameters as hypotheses to test.
