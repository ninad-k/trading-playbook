Summary

Tom Joseph, developer of the Advanced GET software, argues that only about 35% of classical Elliott Wave rules are unambiguous, but that portion accounts for roughly 80% of the method’s profit potential. He builds two mechanical trade types around that subset, using proprietary indicators (Elliott Oscillator, Profit Taking Index, Wave Four Channels, Displaced Moving Average) to remove subjectivity from wave counting on futures and stocks (60-minute, daily, weekly, monthly charts).

Key points

  • Impulse pattern — a five-wave move: Wave 1 (weak, low participation), Wave 2 (vicious pullback, no new low), Wave 3 (longest/strongest, gaps, stops triggered), Wave 4 (orderly profit-taking), Wave 5 (weaker second attempt, often diverges from Wave 3).
  • Elliott Oscillator — difference between a short and long moving average; large spread = Wave 3 in progress, shrinking spread/divergence at new highs = Wave 5.
  • Profit Taking Index (PTI) — compares buy/sell momentum in Wave 3 vs. Wave 4; PTI below 35 statistically predicts a failed Wave 5 (double top).
  • Wave Four Channels — three time/price bands; holding above the first (blue) channel implies ~80% odds of a strong Wave 5; above the second (green), ~60%; breaking the third (red) channel makes a strong Wave 5 unlikely.
  • Displaced Moving Average (DMA) — a 6-7 period MA shifted forward 5 periods, used only to trigger entries at the end of Wave 4 or Wave 5, not as a standalone tool (documented ~21% accuracy alone).
  • Trend Regression Channels — best-fit linear regression with standard-deviation bands; a channel break signals trend change.

Actionable rules

  1. Type 1 (buy end of Wave 4): wait for the Oscillator to pull back to zero; price retraces at least 38% of Wave 3; PTI above 35; retracement holds above the Wave Four Channels; stop two Fibonacci levels below entry; require profit/stop ratio > 1.5.
  2. Type 2 (sell end of Wave 5): confirm Oscillator divergence against the Wave 3 peak; enter on a DMA or Trend Regression Channel crossover; stop above the previous high; initial target = previous Wave 4 low.
  3. Reverse logic applies for declining five-wave sequences (shorts).

Caveats

Relies on proprietary Advanced GET indicators that cannot be replicated exactly without the software; probability figures (94%, 80%, 60%) are stated without the underlying study data or sample size shown in the excerpted material. Marketing material for the author’s own product; treat performance claims as promotional.

Who it is for

Traders already familiar with basic Elliott Wave who want a semi-mechanical rule set (with concrete stop/target logic) rather than pure subjective wave counting.
