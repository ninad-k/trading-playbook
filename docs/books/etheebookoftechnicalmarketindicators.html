The E-Book of Technical Market Indicators

Wall Street Courier · Tier B — concise note manual beginner

A reference guide explains breadth, sentiment, short-sale, volatility, and positioning indicators with formulas and historical examples.

Summary

This compact reference surveys NYSE breadth, volume, high-low, volatility, options, short-sale, sentiment, and futures indicators. It repeatedly warns that indicators can conflict and that no single measure is right all the time. The guide’s practical approach is to select a consistent group of indicators, compare them with broad indexes, and interpret divergences rather than chase every new signal. For the Advance-Decline Line, advances are added and declines subtracted from a chosen base. Its example starts at 100,000 and uses 1,269 advances versus 1,457 declines to reach 99,812 (pp. 5-6). The weekly Advance-Decline Ratio example uses a 10-week moving average, with readings below 90 described as intermediate bottoms and above 170 as tops (p. 7).

Key points

  • Breadth asks whether index movement is broad or concentrated in a small group of issues.
  • A/D and upside-downside volume measures can be smoothed with moving averages (pp. 5-8).
  • The upside-downside volume line adds advancing volume and subtracts declining volume from a base; the high-low differential subtracts new lows from new highs and the high-low ratio divides the two (pp. 9-16).
  • The author gives source-specific interpretations for the Global Futures Bottom Indicator: zero or negative readings, 1-5, readings up to 25, and readings above 600 are assigned different meanings, while other readings are to be ignored (pp. 16-18).
  • TRIN combines advancing/declining issues with advancing/declining volume (pp. 20-21); the Trend Index uses daily highs divided by highs plus lows, with 80% and 20% shown as bullish and bearish boundaries (p. 22).
  • VIX, option ratios, smart-money flow, futures sentiment, and short-sale ratios are presented as sentiment tools, with the guide repeatedly emphasizing contrary interpretation and smoothing (pp. 23-43).
  • The guide interprets public-heavy shorting as potentially associated with bottoms, but this is a heuristic, not a guarantee (p. 42).
  • Historical examples use NYSE, Dow, S&P 500, and futures data and therefore require current-data checks.

Actionable rules

  1. Calculate the A/D Line recursively as prior value plus advances minus declines; choose and retain a clear base (pp. 5-6).
  2. Smooth noisy ratios before interpreting extremes; the guide specifically demonstrates a 10-week moving average for A/D (p. 7).
  3. For the guide’s sentiment ratios, record the underlying population and denominator before comparing readings; examples distinguish equity options, index options, NYSE volume, and short-sale categories (pp. 23-43).
  4. Treat indicator divergences as caution or confirmation and combine them with price structure rather than using one threshold alone.

Caveats

The guide is educational and its thresholds are historical examples. Data definitions, index composition, and indicator availability change. Several Global Futures indicators are proprietary and accompanied by promotional reliability claims rather than independent tests (pp. 16-18, 27-30, 43). It explicitly rejects a “Holy Grail,” and the extract does not establish out-of-sample expectancy for the thresholds.

Who it is for

Beginners building a small, consistent indicator dashboard and learning how breadth and sentiment measures are calculated.
