Summary

John Tracy teaches financial reports through the links among the income statement, balance sheet, and cash-flow statement. The scanned file is oddly ordered: it opens around printed Chapter 11 and places the title and preface near PDF page 128. The reviewed section covers income taxes, retained earnings, earnings per share, operating cash flow, and investing and financing cash flows. Its key message is that profit, equity accounts, and cash are distinct and must be reconciled rather than treated as synonyms.

Key points

  • Net income increases retained earnings; dividends reduce it. Retained earnings is an equity account, not a cash reserve (PDF p. 7).
  • EPS converts total profit into profit per outstanding share (PDF p. 8).
  • Tax expense can differ from tax paid, creating deferred-tax balances (PDF pp. 3-4).
  • Operating cash flow reconciles accrual earnings to cash through changes in operating assets and liabilities (PDF pp. 11-14).
  • Rising receivables and inventory consume cash; rising operating liabilities temporarily provide cash (PDF pp. 11-13).
  • Depreciation is added back because it is a noncash expense, but net income plus depreciation is an incomplete cash-flow measure (PDF pp. 12-14).
  • A profitable company can still encounter liquidity trouble (PDF p. 18).

Actionable rules

The following checklist is an editorial application of Tracy’s accounting links, not a screening system supplied by the book:

  1. Read the statements together: trace revenue and expenses into receivables, inventory, payables, taxes, retained earnings, and cash.
  2. Reconcile net income to operating cash flow; identify which working-capital accounts explain the gap.
  3. Treat undefined “cash flow” claims skeptically and determine whether they mean operating cash flow, net income plus noncash expenses, or net change in cash (PDF p. 14).
  4. Separate operating, investing, and financing cash movements before judging business quality.

Caveats

The fifth edition predates many accounting changes, and the source file is reordered. Despite the filename, the title page identifies John Wiley & Sons as publisher (PDF pp. 128-129). Tax rates and GAAP details are historical. This note covers only the inspected ranges.

Who it is for

Investors and managers learning to interpret accrual accounting and the three primary statements together.
