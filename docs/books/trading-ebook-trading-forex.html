Summary

Reprinted from Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities (2002), this article by GAIN Capital founder Mark Galant makes the case for retail forex trading as a diversification tool alongside equities and bonds, and walks through basic technical setups (trendline breaks, moving-average crossovers, multi-indicator confirmation) using hourly GBP/USD, EUR/USD, and Canadian dollar charts as examples.

Key points

  • 2001 annualized volatility comparison: DJIA ~21.5%, 30-year bond futures ~10%, USD/JPY ~10.5% — forex volatility is argued to be comparable to fixed income, not equities.
  • About 80% of currency transactions last 7 days or less; over 40% last fewer than 2 days, making technical (not fundamental) analysis dominant for entries/exits.
  • About 85% of daily forex volume involves the “majors” (USD, JPY, EUR, GBP, CHF, CAD, AUD).
  • A trendline break (price closing below/above the line for at least two consecutive days) is used as a trend-reversal/entry signal; a broken support level is expected to flip into resistance.
  • A 10-period/40-period moving-average crossover is shown as a trend-following entry/exit system on an hourly EUR/USD chart.
  • RSI/MACD divergence against price is used as a secondary confirmation signal before acting on a trendline break.
  • Central bank intervention can move currencies short-term, but the article claims markets ultimately readjust and resume their trend.
  • Forex advantages cited: 24-hour trading, deep liquidity (>$1 trillion/day), tight spreads (5 pips or less), no uptick rule for shorting, 2% margin (higher leverage than equities’ 50%), and no commissions.

Actionable rules

  1. Enter short when price closes below an established uptrend trendline for at least two consecutive days (mirror for longs on downtrend breaks).
  2. Use a 10-period/40-period moving-average crossover: buy when the 10-period MA crosses above the 40-period MA, exit/reverse when it crosses back below.
  3. Confirm reversal signals with RSI/MACD divergence from price before entering.
  4. Weigh fundamental drivers (relative interest rates, economic stability, political stability, trade balance) as filters for whether a trend is likely to persist long enough to trade.

Caveats

This is a promotional magazine article from a forex broker’s founder, not an independent research piece — it has an inherent incentive to promote retail forex trading. No stop-loss, position-sizing, or backtested performance figures are given for any of the setups shown; the charts are illustrative examples, not a systematic study. The 2002-era leverage and cost figures (2% margin, near-zero commissions) may not reflect current retail forex conditions.

Who it is for

Complete beginners to forex who want a short, non-technical introduction to why the asset class exists and the simplest chart-based entry ideas, before moving to more rigorous system-design material.
