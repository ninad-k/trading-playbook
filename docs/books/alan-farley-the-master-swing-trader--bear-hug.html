Bear Hug

Alan S. Farley · 2000 · Tier A — deep note system advanced

Two short-sale scans for weak markets: fade a bear-market rally into resistance, or short the breakdown from a low-relative-strength NR7 congestion.

What it is

Bear Hug is Farley’s dedicated short-selling setup, built from two distinct scans rather than one pattern: (1) a countertrend rally in an established downtrend that fails into resistance, and (2) the Coiled Spring narrow-range (NR7) logic applied to low relative-strength stocks instead of strong ones. Because short selling has different mechanics than buying (short squeezes can erupt without warning), Farley frames Bear Hug as requiring more patience and precision than any long-side setup.

Rules

  1. Rally-into-resistance scan: find bear-market stocks completing a countertrend rally into intermediate resistance (commonly the 50-day EMA), typically retracing about 62% of the prior breakdown or hitting a horizontal ceiling. Confirm with RSI turning down from overbought or Stochastics rolling over from a top. Require a visible topping pattern (double top, descending triangle) at the rally high before entry; if the chart is ambiguous, drop to a lower time frame for confirmation.
  2. NR7 scan: apply Coiled Spring’s NR7 (narrowest range of last 7 bars) logic but filter for low relative strength instead of high — price constricts after a decline, and gravity is expected to resolve the range to the downside. Enter short on the breakdown or via a sell stop at the NR7 boundary; this scan produces faster results than the rally-fade scan.
  3. Timing filter: avoid selling during the first and last hour of the session until experienced; favor sideways-to-mildly-up markets over sharply falling ones, since squeezes are more dangerous when a decline is already accelerating.
  4. 50-200 Pinball application (documented sub-case): stock pierces the 50-day MA in a selloff but holds above the 200-day MA; price fails on a bounce back to the 50-day, forming a topping pattern within the 50/200 congestion zone; short the failed 50-day retest, expecting an eventual break of the 200-day.
  5. Channel Short application: short within an established parallel price channel (PPC) rather than waiting for the channel to break — look for smaller embedded channels/harmonics within the larger one as additional entry confluence.
  6. Reward measurement: project the next major low as the target for both scans; retain part of the position if price nears that low in anticipation of a further break, or exit fully if the strategy has a limited holding period.

Risk

Never place a market order into a sharp decline — a short squeeze can erupt without warning and a market order will fill at the worst possible price. For the rally-fade scan, place the stop just above the apex of the topping pattern or the rally high. For the NR7 scan, keep a tight stop on the far side of the congestion, matching the Coiled Spring approach; Farley notes this scan has an especially good reward:risk profile because even without confirming indicators, failure is recognized (and can be cut) quickly.

Caveats

Short selling is explicitly harder than the long-side setups in this book — market professionals are described as deliberately triggering short squeezes at the levels where uninformed shorts cluster. Farley recommends starting with smaller short positions than long positions even for experienced traders, and favoring “singles” (small, frequent short-sale profits) over swinging for large gains. The rally-fade scan depends on subjective identification of a “clear topping formation,” with no fixed retracement percentage beyond the ~62% guideline.
