Summary

A Federal Reserve Bank of New York institutional publication describing the mechanics, structure, participants, and instruments of the U.S. foreign exchange market, plus the Fed and Treasury’s role in intervention. It is a reference document, not a strategy book: market structure and its 24-hour OTC dealer-network nature; the roles of dealers, customers, central banks, and brokers; how spot, forward, FX swap, currency swap, and currency-option instruments are priced and quoted; bank trading-room and back-office operations; and how the Treasury and Fed have historically conducted and legally authorize currency intervention.

Key points

  • The FX market is the world’s largest, operates nearly 24 hours across a dealer network rather than a central exchange, and is overwhelmingly OTC with only a modest exchange-traded futures/options segment.
  • The dollar is the market’s most-traded currency and historically the near-universal vehicle currency, though direct cross-currency trading and the euro’s 1999 launch reduced that dependence.
  • Four participant groups: FX dealers (mostly banks), financial/nonfinancial customers, central banks, and brokers (voice and, increasingly, electronic).
  • Spot rates quote a bid/offer, a base and terms currency, in basis points; cross rates derive from dollar rates.
  • Forward points reflect the interest-rate differential under covered interest rate parity: Points = Spot × [(1 + terms rate × days/year) / (1 + base rate × days/year) − 1]; a parity violation implies a riskless “round-tripping” arbitrage that gets closed quickly in liquid markets.
  • FX swaps (spot + offsetting forward) and currency swaps (longer-dated principal/interest exchange) are distinguished; OTC currency options use equity-option pricing logic (delta hedging, put-call parity).
  • U.S. intervention authority splits between Treasury (Exchange Stabilization Fund, under the Gold Reserve Act of 1934 and Bretton Woods Agreements Act of 1944) and the Fed (Federal Reserve Act), both executed by the NY Fed’s FX Desk under FOMC oversight.
  • The stated post-1978 U.S. goal — “countering disorderly market conditions” — was interpreted very differently across five documented policy periods from 1978–1990, from hands-off (1981–85) to highly active (post-Plaza Accord, 1985–87).

Actionable rules

None — descriptive institutional/market-structure material, not a trading system. Practical value: macro/mechanical literacy on how spot, forward, and swap pricing relate via interest-rate parity, and how central bank intervention is authorized and has historically been deployed.

Caveats

Published in the late 1990s; details on broker mix and intervention-era history are dated relative to today’s electronic FX market, though spot/forward/swap pricing mechanics and covered interest parity remain valid. Descriptive and historical, not prescriptive — no risk-management or trading-strategy content.

Who it is for

Forex traders and analysts wanting an authoritative, non-strategy reference on how the interbank/institutional FX market is structured and priced, and how monetary/fiscal authorities intervene in it.
