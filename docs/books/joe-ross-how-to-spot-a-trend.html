The Law of Charts

Joe Ross · Tier B — concise note manual intermediate

A price-action lesson defines 1-2-3 turns and ledges with bar-count and tick-tolerance rules for trend continuation.

Summary

Ross defines trend pivots through a 1-2-3 high or low. A number 1 point is the last extreme of the prior swing; a number 2 requires a full correction that creates the relevant higher/lower high and low; a number 3 requires another full correction (pp. 2-5). The entire formation is invalidated when a bar reaches or passes point 1 (p. 5). He also defines a ledge as at least four bars with two matching highs and two matching lows, each separated by at least one bar; matches should be within three minimum tick fluctuations, and a ledge may contain no more than ten bars (p. 6). A ledge must form within an existing trend and is expected to break in the trend direction.

Key points

  • A full correction defining point 2 may use one bar or a combination of up to three bars (pp. 2-4).
  • Point 3 requires at least one and no more than two bars forming the correcting high/low sequence (pp. 3, 5).
  • Ledges are shorter than trading ranges; the manual describes ranges as more than ten bars (p. 6).
  • The rules attempt to reduce visual subjectivity with explicit bar and tick conditions.
  • The source describes continuation expectations, not guaranteed outcomes.
  • A Ross Hook is the first correction after a 1-2-3, ledge, or trading-range breakout; in a sideways market it acts as a tie-breaker after opposing 1-2-3 formations (pp. 7-11).
  • Ross classifies sideways action as ledges of up to 10 bars, congestions of 11-20 bars, and trading ranges of 21 or more bars, with many range breakouts expected around bars 21-29 (p. 9).
  • The manual says a 1-2-3 breakout defines a trend and a later Ross Hook breakout establishes it; it warns that Hook violations can still be false breakouts (pp. 10-13).
  • Later examples stress that gaps across an intended entry can create slippage and invalidate a pending order, and that stops should protect an already favorable move rather than be improvised from hindsight (pp. 17-19).

Actionable rules

  1. Mark points 1, 2, and 3 only after the stated full-correction condition is complete (pp. 2-5).
  2. Nullify a 1-2-3 setup if any bar equals or exceeds point 1 (p. 5).
  3. For a ledge, require four to ten bars, two separated matching highs and lows, and no more than three ticks of mismatch (p. 6).

Caveats

The extract is a teaching manual with chart examples rather than a statistical validation. Pattern recognition remains discretionary at the boundaries, and the Ross Hook, Trader’s Trick, gap, and stop examples are source-specific procedures rather than universal expectancy or stop-distance evidence (pp. 11-19).

Who it is for

Price-action traders who want explicit definitions for swing pivots and short consolidations.
