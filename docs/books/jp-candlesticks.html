Summary

A promotional introductory article from The Candlestick Forum (publisher of Stephen Bigalow’s “Profitable Candlestick Trading”) explaining why candlestick charting works and walking through the major reversal formations. Its central argument: candlesticks work because enough traders use them that the resulting crowd behavior becomes self-fulfilling, and combining candlestick signals with stochastics lets a trader anticipate reversals earlier than traders using trendlines or bar charts alone.

Key points

  • Long/short days — a “long” body is relative to the last two to three weeks of price action, not an absolute pip count.
  • Marubozu — no shadows on one or both ends; Black Marubozu is a weaker signal than White Marubozu; a Closing Marubozu (no shadow at the close end) is stronger than an Opening Marubozu.
  • Spinning top — small body relative to shadows; in a trending/oscillating market the next day tends to move in the direction of the opening price.
  • Doji family — Gravestone Doji (open/close at the low) favors market tops; Long-Legged Doji and Rickshaw Man signal loss of directional consensus.
  • Engulfing patterns, dark cloud cover, piercing pattern — standard two-candle reversal formations, described only at a high level (no numeric penetration rule given).
  • Hammer / hanging man / shooting star — long shadow, small body at one end of the range; the article calls the bottom-of-downtrend inverted-hammer version bullish and stresses waiting for confirmation.
  • Morning Star / Evening Star — three-day bottom/top reversal signals.
  • J-Hook pattern — an uptrend pulls back without stochastics reaching oversold, then hooks back up; described as a double profit opportunity (the retest of the prior high, then a possible breakout).
  • Fry-Pan Bottom / Dumpling Top / Tower Top / Tower Bottom — named as patterns visible in candlesticks but invisible to conventional bar-chart technicians (referenced, not detailed).

Actionable rules

  1. Combine candlestick signals with stochastics: near a trendline or support/resistance level, check whether stochastics are turning together with a candlestick signal before trusting the level to hold.
  2. In a J-Hook setup, if stochastics curl upward with no bearish candle signal as price approaches the prior high, expect the high to be broken; if a weak/bearish signal appears with stochastics flattening or curling down, take profits instead.
  3. Always treat a Doji as a high-alert signal regardless of shadow length; the longer the shadows, the more significant it is.
  4. No numeric stop-loss, position-sizing, or target rules are given anywhere in the text.

Caveats

This is marketing content for The Candlestick Forum’s paid services and Bigalow’s book, so it names patterns without giving precise penetration percentages, sample sizes, or win rates — e.g., “well into the body” for dark cloud cover / piercing pattern is never quantified. The March 2000 NASDAQ top example is asserted rather than shown with data. Treat it as a plain-language orientation to candlestick reversal concepts, not a tested system.

Who it is for

Beginners who want a first, jargon-light introduction to why candlestick reversal signals are said to work and how they might be combined with a momentum oscillator like stochastics, before moving to a fuller reference such as Nison’s or Morris’s candlestick books.
