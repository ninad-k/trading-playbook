Hole-in-the-Wall

Alan S. Farley · 2000 · Tier A — deep note system intermediate

Short the rally back into a sudden, high-volume gap-down that ends a rally at a new high, since the first pullback into the gap rarely fills.

What it is

A hole-in-the-wall is a gap-down that abruptly ends an active uptrend at (or near) a new intermediate high, on volume that exceeds the last rally impulse. Farley treats it as a distinct gap category not covered by Edwards and Magee’s classic breakaway/continuation/exhaustion taxonomy, occupying its own niche between an exhaustion gap and a reversal. It generates the most reliable short setups of any of the 7-Bells and works especially well intraday, since gaps most often occur at the session open.

Rules

  1. Signal: a stock at or near a new intermediate high gaps sharply lower; decline volume must exceed the prior rally impulse, confirming a genuine shock rather than routine profit-taking.
  2. First rule of thumb: even weak holes should not have their first pullback fill the gap completely — a rally into the gap that fails to fill it is a consistent, near-universal short opportunity regardless of the eventual trend.
  3. Read the follow-through bar: narrow price action near the bottom of the signal bar’s range implies rapid continued decline; a bounce off the low implies the stock may base sideways until the gap eventually fills.
  4. Intraday application: lay a Fibonacci grid across the pre-gap uptrend; the rally back into the hole often stalls at a retracement level (38%, 50%, or 62%) of the longest applicable trend. Market wisdom: if a gap hasn’t filled within the first hour, treat it as unlikely to fill that session, raising the odds of a profitable short on any late bounce toward the gap.
  5. Confirming damage: the strongest holes violate both the 20-bar and 50-bar EMA; holes that hold above these averages retain more relative strength and call for more defensive (tighter) exit management.
  6. 1-2 Decline application (documented sub-case, “50-200 Pinball”): stock pierces the 50-day MA after a bull run but holds above the 200-day MA; a failed bounce back to the 50-day forms a topping pattern (head and shoulders / double top) inside the 50/200 congestion; short on the failed retest, adding on the eventual break of the 200-day.
  7. Island Reversal application: a second gap in the opposite direction that completes an island reversal is a variant worth separate short entry once confirmed.
  8. Gap echo caution: a further gap in the opposite direction (“gap echo”) can immediately refill the hole and trap shorts — confirm declining volume into any bounce before shorting, and exit fast if a new up-gap appears.

Risk

Entering the signal bar itself is high risk (volatile, no confirmation); Farley recommends most strategies stand aside on the gap bar and wait for the first short-term bottom, then short the rally back toward the gap with a stop above the high of that bounce. For trend-following continuation shorts (price making an immediate low after the gap), place the stop over the high of the shock bar and trail it down as the position gains. The 50-200 Pinball application places stops just above the failed 50-day-MA retest.

Caveats

Not all holes ignite a full downtrend — some are absorbed sideways until the gap eventually fills, so the setup requires judging the “intensity of the shock” (gap width, volume, prior relative strength), which is qualitative. Works best in individual equities with clean daily-open gaps; less reliable in 24-hour or continuously traded markets without a defined open. Requires care to distinguish a genuine hole from a gap echo trap.
