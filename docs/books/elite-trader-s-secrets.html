Summary

Despite its title, this is not general trading advice but a monograph from Elliott Wave Research describing a “Refined” Elliott Wave Principle. The author claims to have run roughly a million computerized forecasts on real chart data versus random-walk data, finding that the closer a pattern matches the software’s “average shape” rating, the more likely the forecast direction was correct — around 80% on real data versus ~45% on random data for the same wave. The bulk of the book catalogs the eleven canonical Elliott Wave patterns, their formation rules, and a worked Microsoft Corp. chart-labeling example.

Key points

  • Eleven pattern types split into impulsive/motive (with the trend: Impulse, Leading/Ending Diagonal, labeled 1-2-3-4-5) and corrective (against the trend: Zigzag, Double/Triple Zigzag, Flat, Double/Triple Sideways, Contracting/Expanding Triangle, labeled with letters).
  • Ten degree labels from Submicro (minutes-hours) to Grand Supercycle (decades+), nesting smaller patterns inside larger ones.
  • Liquidity matters — the author claims Elliott patterns are reliable only in liquid, crowd-driven markets (major indices, currencies), not thinly traded or manipulated ones.
  • Diagonal rules (example) — Wave 2 never retraces beyond Wave 1’s start; Wave 3 always exceeds Wave 2 by price; Waves 2 and 4 must overlap; Wave 5 must be ≥50% of Wave 4; Wave 3 is never shortest of Waves 1, 3, 5.
  • “Preferred count” — when multiple valid labelings exist, the one obeying the most guidelines is treated as highest-probability.
  • Central claim — random-walk forecasts were correct ~45% of the time on average per wave versus ~80% on real data (up to 4x random), per the vendor’s proprietary dataset.

Actionable rules

  1. Apply Elliott forecasting only to liquid, broadly traded instruments — avoid thin or few-participant markets.
  2. Verify all formal rules for a pattern (e.g., the diagonal rules above) before accepting a count; use guidelines only to choose between rule-valid counts.
  3. Prefer the count matching the pattern’s statistically “average shape” as the higher-probability forecast.
  4. Treat any Elliott count as provisional; expect to revise labeling and targets as the pattern develops.

Caveats

A vendor-authored promotional document for the firm’s own software; the headline statistics (a million forecasts, 45% vs. 80% accuracy) are asserted with no disclosed methodology, sample composition, or independent replication, and the supporting appendix is sold separately. Treat the accuracy claims as unverified marketing rather than peer-reviewed research.

Who it is for

Traders already familiar with Elliott Wave basics who want a concise catalog of the eleven pattern types and formation rules, understanding the forecasting-accuracy claims are unverified.

Educational summaries only. Nothing here is financial advice. Test every rule before risking capital.
