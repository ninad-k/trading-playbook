IQ Trader · Tier B — concise note book beginner

Summary

Published under the “IQ Trader” brand (with repeated Telegram/YouTube/Instagram links and an “@iq_trader_01 / @trading_expert_088” byline throughout, alongside an explicit disclaimer that trades made from the book are the reader’s own risk), this is a four-part reference covering candlestick patterns (32 entries), chart patterns (22 entries), a short standalone section on Fibonacci retracement, and 12 indicators (Volume, RSI, Moving Averages, Bollinger Bands, Fibonacci Retracement again, MACD, Stochastic, MFI, Parabolic SAR, ADX, Supertrend, OBV). Every entry, regardless of type, is written to an identical four-field template — Formation, Timing, Entry, Stop Loss, Exit — with no chapter narrative connecting them and no worked numerical examples anywhere in the book.

Key points

  • Candlestick catalogue — the standard set (Tweezer Top/Bottom, Kickers, Marubozu, Harami, Three Outside Up/Down, Shooting Star, Hanging Man, Inside Bars, Spinning Top/Bottom, Doji family, Piercing/Dark Cloud Cover, Morning/Evening Star, Hammer/Inverted Hammer, Engulfing, Pin Bar, Three Inside Up/Down, Three Black Crows/White Soldiers) — 32 in total, each with the four-field box.
  • Chart pattern catalogue — Cup and Handle, Rectangles, Rounding Bottom, Triangles (ascending/descending/symmetrical), Flags, Pennants, Double/Triple Top/Bottom, Diamond Top/Bottom, Head and Shoulders (and inverse), Rising/Falling Wedge, Broadening Triangle — 22 in total, same template.
  • Fibonacci — retracement levels 23.6/38.2/50/61.8/78.6%, extension targets 127.2/161.8/200%, entered on a level-plus-confirmation basis (candlestick, moving average, volume, or oscillator agreement).
  • Indicators — each given the same four fields: Volume (breakout confirmation via a spike), RSI (>70 overbought / <30 oversold, entries on the retreat from an extreme), Moving Averages (Golden/Death Cross), Bollinger Bands (fade the bands on a reversal), MACD (line/signal crossover), Stochastic (>80/<20), MFI (>80/<20), Parabolic SAR (dot flip), ADX (>25 = strong trend, combined with +DI/−DI crossover), Supertrend (line flip), OBV (rising/falling with price, or divergence).

Actionable rules

Every entry follows the same four-step recipe: (1) identify the formation against the stated trend context (e.g., Hammer only after a downtrend, Shooting Star only after an uptrend); (2) enter on a break of the pattern’s high (bullish) or low (bearish); (3) place the stop beyond the pattern’s own opposite extreme; (4) exit at “a significant support/resistance level” — this exact vague phrase recurs for nearly every pattern in the book rather than a measured-move, pattern-height, or fixed R:R target. Indicator rules follow the equivalent pattern: a threshold cross or crossover is the entry, the most recent swing high/low is the stop, and the opposite threshold or a fading of the signal is the exit.

Caveats

The book gives no numeric price levels, no measured-move or pattern-height target formulas (unlike this library’s other candlestick/chart-pattern references, which typically specify a projected target from a neckline or pole height), and no worked examples on any real or hypothetical chart — “a significant support/resistance level” is left entirely undefined, which makes several of its own entry/stop/exit boxes effectively non-actionable as written. No win rates, sample sizes, or backtests are offered for any pattern or indicator, and none are claimed either. The text is thin on trend/level context compared to this library’s stronger beginner references (e.g., this library’s charts-dont-lie, which pairs the same pattern catalogue with concrete pattern-height targets and a confluence checklist) — this book reads as a condensed glossary rather than a taught method. The repeated social-media handles and marketing links embedded in the disclaimer and index pages mark it clearly as a self-published lead-generation product rather than an edited trading text; treat its promotional framing accordingly.

Who it is for

A quick-reference glossary for a reader who already understands trend and support/resistance and just wants a compact, one-line-per-pattern reminder of formation and rough entry logic. Not a substitute for a beginner’s first book (which needs the target-projection and confluence logic this book omits) or for a trader wanting tested, numerically specific setups.
