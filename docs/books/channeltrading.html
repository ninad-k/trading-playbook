Master the Channel Projection

Mark McRae · Tier B — concise note article beginner

Short lesson on drawing price channels and projecting the channel's width forward to estimate retracement and target levels.

Summary

A brief educational article that extends the standard trend-channel technique. After reviewing how a traditional channel is drawn (a trendline duplicated and shifted to touch the opposite extreme), it shows how to measure the channel’s width and project that width forward from a retracement point to estimate where a pullback might halt, and separately how the same channel width can be used as a price target after a breakout.

Key points

  • Standard channel — draw trendline T1 along the trend, duplicate it as T2 and shift it to touch the opposite swing extreme, containing ~95% of price action.
  • Overshoot signal — price piercing the channel line signals an overbought (uptrend) or oversold (downtrend) condition and momentum exhaustion.
  • Channel width (T3) — the perpendicular distance connecting T1 to T2, treated as a fixed measured distance.
  • Retracement projection (T4/T5) — duplicate T3 and place it below T1 (in an uptrend) to project where a retracement may halt; duplicate T1 again from the base of T4 to get T5, the projected support/resistance line.
  • Target projection — in a downtrend continuation, the same channel-width measurement is used as a breakout price target rather than a retracement level.
  • Reported reliability — the author states price “often” stops near the T5 projection, offered as an informal observation, not a tested statistic.

Actionable rules

  1. Draw the primary trendline (T1) along the established trend.
  2. Duplicate T1 and shift it (T2) to touch the most recent high (uptrend) or low (downtrend), forming the channel.
  3. Measure the perpendicular channel width as a line (T3) connecting T1 to T2.
  4. To project a retracement halt: duplicate T3 below T1 (uptrend) as T4, then duplicate T1 from the base of T4 to get T5 — the retracement’s likely support/resistance.
  5. To project a breakout target: apply the same channel-width measurement from the breakout point in the direction of the move.
  6. No stop-loss, entry trigger, or position-sizing rule is given; this is a level-projection technique only.

Caveats

This is a short promotional lesson (from tradingforbeginners.com) with no backtested statistics, no worked numerical example with actual price levels, and no risk-management guidance. The technique is geometric and subjective (channel placement depends on which swing points are chosen) and should be combined with an entry/exit and risk framework from elsewhere.

Who it is for

Traders already familiar with basic trendlines who want a quick technique for projecting retracement and target levels from a channel’s width.
