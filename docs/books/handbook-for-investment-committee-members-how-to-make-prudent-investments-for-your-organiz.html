Summary

Russell Olson explains what an institutional investment committee must govern, what it should delegate, and how it can make prudent decisions without every member becoming an investment expert. The fiduciary acts solely for the organization, hires a competent adviser, establishes policy, chooses asset classes, selects and monitors managers, and maintains custody and reporting controls (PDF pp. 15-17). The core portfolio argument is that expected return, volatility, and correlation must be considered together and stress-tested because every forecast is uncertain.

Key points

  • Committee members need integrity, preparation, sponsor knowledge, judgment, and willingness to learn more than specialist portfolio skills (PDF p. 16).
  • A written investment-policy statement should connect objectives, horizon, risk, liquidity, allocation, delegation, and review.
  • Adding a volatile but weakly correlated asset can reduce total portfolio risk more than adding a conservative asset that moves with existing holdings (PDF p. 76).
  • Correlation estimates are uncertain and can change, which supports diversification across genuinely different return sources.
  • Each incremental asset class contributes less diversification than the prior one.
  • Managers must be evaluated against mandates and style-appropriate benchmarks.
  • Pension liabilities and endowment spending needs require different portfolio structures.

Actionable rules

  1. Define authority, conflicts, meeting duties, adviser responsibilities, and reporting in operating policies before choosing investments.
  2. Approve a policy allocation using explicit expected returns, volatilities, and cross-asset correlations; run sensitivity tests around assumptions.
  3. Avoid cash as a strategic long-term allocation unless liquidity needs justify its return drag; Olson argues for a 0% target while acknowledging operational balances (PDF p. 77).
  4. Evaluate growth and value managers separately because their styles cycle differently (PDF p. 81).
  5. Revisit allocation and manager fit, while resisting performance-chasing after short intervals.

Caveats

Return histories, products, laws, and institutional practice are dated to 2005. The 0% cash preference is an opinion and may be unsuitable where near-term liabilities or capital calls are material.

Who it is for

Trustees and committee members overseeing pensions, endowments, foundations, or nonprofit reserves.
