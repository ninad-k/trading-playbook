80-20's

Laurence A. Connors and Linda Bradford Raschke · 1995 · Tier A — deep note system beginner

A day-trade-only setup that fades a follow-through move the morning after a bar opened in one extreme of its range and closed in the opposite extreme.

What it is

A day-trading pattern built on research by Steve Moore (Moore Research Center) showing that a bar closing in the top or bottom 10% of its daily range has an 80-90% chance of following through the next morning but only about a 50% chance of actually closing higher/lower that day — implying a strong tendency toward a midday reversal. Trader Derek Gipson added a pre-qualifier (the setup bar must also open in the opposite extreme of its range) to strengthen the edge, and the closing-range threshold was loosened from 90% to 80% without hurting profitability, giving the pattern its name. It is explicitly a day trade only, built to exploit George Douglass Taylor’s observed buy-day/sell-day market rhythm.

Rules

For buys (sells are reversed): 1. Yesterday the market opened in the top 20% of its daily range and closed in the bottom 20% of its daily range (day-session data only; night-session data is ignored). 2. Today the market must trade at least 5-15 ticks below yesterday’s low (a guideline — the exact amount is left to trader discretion). 3. An entry buy stop is placed at yesterday’s low. Once filled, place an initial protective stop near today’s low extreme. 4. Trail the stop up to lock in accrued profits. The trade is closed out same-day — this is a day trade only, not held overnight.

Risk

Risk is capped at roughly the distance between the entry (yesterday’s low) and today’s low extreme where the initial stop sits — the source’s bond example shows a fill at 116-08 with the pattern producing a 3/4-point gain, and other examples show gains from a few ticks to roughly 200 points in cotton on a single day. Because it is a day trade only, overnight gap risk is eliminated by design. No fixed percent-of-equity sizing is specified; the authors note explicitly that large profits from 80-20’s are “the exception, not the rule” — it is a scalping strategy meant to exploit a modest, high-frequency statistical edge in daily reversal tendency, not a home-run setup.

Caveats

Not every 80-20 setup triggers or reverses — the pattern is discretionary rather than fully mechanical, and the authors suggest an additional filter (favoring setup bars with a larger-than-normal daily range) to improve selectivity, without making it a hard rule. The 5-15 tick trigger threshold in rule 2 is explicitly left to trader judgment rather than fixed, which limits the setup’s out-of-the-box mechanical repeatability. The book cites Steve Moore’s original research (tabulated in the appendix) as the statistical basis for the underlying reversal tendency, but the 80-20’s execution rules themselves (entry offset, stop placement) are not separately backtested in the source — only illustrated through 15-minute bar chart examples in bonds, cotton, and soybeans from 1995.
