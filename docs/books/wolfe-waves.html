Wolfe Waves

Linda Bradford Raschke · Tier B — concise note book advanced

A five-point wave pattern uses trendline projections to define a reversal entry, stop, and estimated price arrival.

Summary

The Wolfe Wave chapter presents a five-point chart pattern based on alternating action and reaction. For a buy setup, point 2 is a significant swing high, point 3 is a lower bottom than point 1, and point 4 is a top above point 1; the sell setup reverses these relationships (p. 1). The trader draws a line from points 1 to 3 to project the anticipated reversal area at point 5. The estimated price at arrival (EPA) is the line from point 1 to point 4, which becomes the projected objective (p. 1). The chapter says points 1–4 must exist before the pattern is sought, and that point 2 need only be a significant swing rather than a major long-term reversal (pp. 1, 5). Entry follows a reversal back above the 1–3 line for a buy, especially after a small overshoot at point 5; the initial stop is placed just under the new reversal and can be moved to breakeven (pp. 1, 4). Examples in S&P, sugar, Canadian-dollar, Boeing, and other charts show targets met or nearly met, including a cited 12-point S&P move (p. 3). The chapter claims three to six setups per week in one S&P five-minute example, but gives no systematic sample or expectancy (p. 4).

Key points

  • Pattern validity depends on point ordering and relative highs/lows, not merely on a visual five-wave shape (pp. 1, 5).
  • The EPA is a geometric projection, not a guaranteed target.
  • Volatility is said to help the pattern form, while runaway conditions weaken the setup (p. 1).

Actionable rules

  1. Wait until points 1–4 exist; for a buy, point 3 must be below point 1 and the best setups have point 4 above point 1. Reverse the relationships for a sell (p. 1).
  2. Draw the 1–3 line to project point 5 and enter only after price reverses back across it; place the initial stop beyond the new point-5 reversal (pp. 1–4).
  3. Draw the 1–4 line as the EPA objective and move the initial stop quickly to breakeven according to the chapter (p. 1). The examples do not define partial exits.

Caveats

Examples are selected historical charts and the method is discretionary. The claimed setup frequency and target examples do not establish a win rate, risk-adjusted return, or robustness across markets and timeframes.

Who it is for

Experienced chart readers testing geometric reversal patterns with explicit execution rules.
