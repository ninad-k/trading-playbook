Summary

A compilation of short trader-profile interviews originally published by FWN (Futures World News) in 1996. Each of the 18 profiles is a few paragraphs covering how the trader got started, their primary method, and closing advice for beginners. Subjects include George Angell (S&P scalping via “action points” instead of stops), Jake Bernstein (technical/cyclical, MBH Commodity Advisors), Tom Bierovic (discretionary momentum-retracement trader), Walter Bressert (cycles + oscillators), Tom DeMark (Sequential and other proprietary timing indicators), George Fontanills (options for delta-neutral risk reduction), Lee Gettess (risk-control focus, Volpat system), Cynthia Kase (proprietary statistical indicators, PeakOscillator/Dev-Stop), George Lane (inventor of Stochastics), Glenn Neely (NEoWave, an objectivized Elliott Wave variant), Grant Noble (contrarian media-sentiment reading), Linda Bradford Raschke (discretionary technical swing trading), Rick Redmont (Wyckoff volume theory + %D), Angelo Reynolds (Eurodollar pit scalper), Joe Stowell (“cups and caps” pattern), Gary Wagner (candlesticks + Western TA), Ben Warwick (“event trading” on news reactions), and Larry Williams (creator of %R, Commitments of Traders analysis).

Key points

  • Angell: trades S&P futures using “action points” instead of stops, waiting for a bounce before exiting a losing position; attributes most trader failure to lack of discipline, being underfinanced, and not understanding market “paradox.”
  • Bierovic: targets a 40% win rate with a 2:1 reward/risk ratio, favoring liquid markets with ≥5,000 average daily volume and ≥20,000 open interest.
  • Gettess: built the Volpat (“volatility and pattern”) system; treats risk control, not profit target, as the only thing a trader can actually control.
  • Kase: takes partial profits on early danger signals (reversal patterns, momentum divergence); warns beginners against trading without at least two years’ living expenses set aside.
  • Lane: never trades without a stop-loss; trades primarily 3/15/30-minute charts using stochastics, volume, and trendlines.
  • Raschke: limits active trading to 5-6 markets at a time to avoid “sloppy” decision-making; does not use a fixed risk/reward ratio, preferring to let winners run.
  • Reynolds: caps position size at 5% of capital as a Eurodollar pit scalper; average holding time 5-10 minutes.
  • L. Williams: parlayed $10,000 into $1.1M in the 1987 Robbins World Cup Trading Championship; created the %R indicator and uses COT (Commitments of Traders) net-position shifts as a contrarian signal.

Actionable rules

  1. Bierovic: 2:1 minimum reward/risk ratio, targeting >40% win rate, only in markets with ≥5,000 ADV / ≥20,000 open interest.
  2. Reynolds: risk no more than 5% of trading capital per trade.
  3. Lane: always use a protective stop-loss, without exception.
  4. Raschke: cap simultaneous active markets at 5-6 to preserve decision quality.
  5. Kase: keep at least two years of living expenses outside the trading account before trading for a living.

Caveats

These are self-reported, undated (1996) magazine-style profiles, not audited track records — claims of specific returns or win rates are the traders’ own accounts and cannot be verified. Several named systems (Volpat, NEoWave, Sequential, Kase indicators) are only described at a high level; readers wanting to implement any of them need the traders’ own dedicated books/courses.

Who it is for

Readers who want quick exposure to a range of different trading styles (discretionary, mechanical, cyclical, options-based, sentiment-based) and the risk-management habits several well-known traders say mattered most.
