Summary

The inaugural (December 1995, Volume 1 Issue 1) issue of “Pristine Tips,” the newsletter of Pristine Capital Management, written by co-founder Oliver L. Velez. It presents two setups the author calls “no-brainers” — reliable enough, in his telling, to act on essentially every time they appear — illustrated with an AMC Entertainment (AEN) chart, plus a separate four-step trading plan for managing a position once entered, and a short piece using the NYSE Daily New Lows count as a market-timing gauge.

Key points

  • 6-8 Week Breakout — a stock emerging from a 6-8 week sideways consolidation on above-average volume, signaling renewed institutional buying and often a rapid short-term (and sometimes multi-month) advance.
  • 50-day moving average (MA) advance — the 50-day MA’s slope classifies a stock as strong (rising), neutral (flat), or weak (declining); pullbacks to a rising 50-day MA are described as a high-reliability bounce/buy zone due to institutional buying interest there.
  • Combined signal — when a stock triggers both setups simultaneously (a 50-day MA bounce that is also a 6-8 week breakout), the resulting move is described as “almost guaranteed” to be explosive; illustrated by three touches of AEN’s 50-day MA, the third of which coincided with its breakout.
  • NYSE Daily New Lows gauge — when daily new lows stay consistently under 30, the market is considered bullish and safe to play aggressively; when consistently above 30, trade more cautiously.
  • 4-step trade management plan — (1) Entry via buy-stop order near the recommended buy price; (2) Initial stop placed just below the entry day’s low; (3) On day 2 (if not stopped out), raise the stop to the entry day’s low; (4) Exit/sell on the day price closes above the entry day’s high — typically day 2-3, sometimes up to day 4-5; if the entry-day high is never exceeded, the trade is held until stopped out.
  • Result cited — AEN, recommended Nov 10, 1995, rose to $28.75 for a >53% two-week gain, attributed to the combined breakout/MA-bounce signal.
  • Author’s framing — the goal of the four-step method is small, consistent gains (“nickels and dimes”) during difficult market environments rather than large individual wins.

Actionable rules

  1. Buy a stock breaking out of a 6-8 week sideways range on above-average volume.
  2. Buy pullbacks to a rising 50-day moving average.
  3. Entry via buy-stop order at (or near) the recommended buy price.
  4. Initial stop: just below the entry day’s low; raised to the entry day’s low if not stopped out by day 2.
  5. Exit on the day price closes above the entry day’s high (typically within 2-5 trading days).

Caveats

This is promotional newsletter content for a paid advisory service (Pristine Capital Management / “The Pristine Day Trader”), not an independent study — the cited AEN result and the “372 previous recommendations” reference are unverified claims by the publisher. The specific setups and thresholds (6-8 weeks, 50-day MA, <30 new lows) are not backtested here and reflect mid-1990s market conditions.

Who it is for

Beginner swing/position traders looking for two simple, chart-pattern-based breakout setups and a basic mechanical stop-management routine.
