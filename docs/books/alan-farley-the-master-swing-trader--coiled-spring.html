Coiled Spring

Alan S. Farley · 2000 · Tier A — deep note system intermediate

Trade the expansion that follows a narrowest-range-of-seven-bars (NR7) print, entering in whichever direction price breaks out.

What it is

Coiled Spring locates the “empty zone” transition from a rangebound, low-volatility market into a new directional trend, using Toby Crabel’s narrow-range-bar concept (NR7: the narrowest high-low range of the last seven bars). The bar immediately following an NR7 — or one to three bars later — often marks the start of an expansion move. Farley’s version filters for high relative strength (favoring long breakouts), while the related Bear Hug setup filters for low relative strength (favoring short breakdowns).

Rules

  1. Signal: identify an NR7 — the bar with the smallest high-low range of the preceding seven bars. A second consecutive NR7 (“NR7-2”) is a stronger signal.
  2. Context filter: look for declining volume and narrowing price swings building into the NR7 (visual congestion narrowing toward an apex), not an isolated narrow bar in isolation. Favor candidates near intermediate highs or the top of a bull-market congestion pattern, confirmed by 20-bar/50-bar/200-bar moving averages in an uptrend.
  3. Location: best signals form at the apex of triangles, tops of declining bull flags, or against Bollinger Band extremes/trendlines — i.e., where other S/R already suggests an impending breakout.
  4. Entry: place a bilateral entry stop just outside both extremes of the NR7 bar; cancel the untriggered side once one order fills. This “let the market pick the direction” approach keeps risk low because the NR7’s own range defines a tight stop.
  5. Time-frame filter: valid on 5-minute and 60-minute intraday charts (in sufficiently liquid names) but Farley says to avoid 1-minute NR7 signals — false signals increase as holding period shortens; the safest short-term version trades NR7s off the 60-minute chart or longer.
  6. Triangle Coil application (a documented sub-case): triangle should not extend beyond a 50% retracement of the prior trend; look for three touches of top/bottom trendlines before breakout; breakout typically occurs about two-thirds of the way from pattern start to the projected apex; the NR7 often prints at the triangle midline or against a trendline.

Risk

Stop loss sits at the opposite extreme of the triggering NR7 bar — inherently tight because the bar’s range is, by definition, unusually narrow. Exit target: watch for the breakout to stall at the first S/R obstacle (expansion-contraction tendency); take a quick exit on the first expansion bar if time doesn’t permit price to clear intervening levels, or hold with trailing stops through a series of expansion bars when multiple time frames cross-verify the breakout (Farley notes markets trend only 15–20% of the time, so a quick-bar exit is usually the higher-probability choice).

Caveats

Pure NR7 signals have no inherent directional bias — expansion can go either way — so the long-only Triangle Coil / Coiled Spring filter (relative strength, uptrend context) is what gives directional edge; without it, use the bilateral entry-stop technique instead of guessing direction. The setup rarely shows up on standard mathematical/momentum indicators, so it depends on visual identification of narrowing range, which is not objectively quantified beyond the NR7 definition itself. Requires high, liquid volume for reliable intraday application; thin or erratic stocks will generate false narrow-range signals.
