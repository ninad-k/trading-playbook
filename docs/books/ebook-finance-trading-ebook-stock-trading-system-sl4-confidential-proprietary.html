Summary

A “confidential trading memorandum” marketing the SL4 platform (a now-defunct proprietary trading terminal) to brokers and traders, followed by a reprinted 2001 Bear Stearns wire-service piece on day-trader growth. The document is a features brochure, not a trading method: it lists the platform’s order-execution, charting, and market-maker-tracking capabilities without disclosing the logic behind its signals or backtested performance.

Key points

  • SL4 scans stocks/futures/forex in real time and surfaces “high-probability” buy/sell opportunities using an undisclosed proprietary algorithm applied to time & sales, participant, and depth-of-book data.
  • It tracks the current trading day’s top 10 market makers per stock and their inferred positioning (heavily long/long/short/heavily short), updated dynamically.
  • Alerts cover 52-week high/low breakouts, market-maker accumulation/distribution, panic-condition detection, pivot points, and multi-timeframe trend/cycle indicators.
  • A “basket trading” feature consolidates signals across multiple related assets into one weighted opportunity.
  • The appended Bear Stearns note (Amy Butte, Jan. 2001) reports that semi-professional/day-trader accounts grew their trade frequency 55% in 2000 even as total online accounts and other trader segments’ activity fell or flattened.

Actionable rules

None given — the memorandum describes software features and marketing claims, not disclosed entry/exit rules, position sizing, or backtested statistics.

Caveats

This is sales literature for a specific (now-obsolete, early-2000s) trading terminal; all “high-probability” and accuracy claims are asserted without supporting data. The appended Bear Stearns industry note is a separate, unrelated document bundled into the same file and describes broker-industry trends, not a trading method. Treat the whole document as a historical curiosity about circa-2001 day-trading platform marketing rather than a source of tradable rules.

Who it is for

Not useful for learning a trading method; only of interest for historical context on early-2000s Level II/depth-of-book day-trading platforms and day-trader demographics.
