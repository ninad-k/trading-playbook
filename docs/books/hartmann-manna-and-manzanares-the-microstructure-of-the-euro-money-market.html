The Microstructure of the Euro Money Market

Philipp Hartmann, Michele Manna, and Andrés Manzanares · 2001 · Tier B — concise note paper advanced

Five months of intraday data show how ECB events, reserve operations, and settlement infrastructure shape euro overnight liquidity and volatility.

Summary

This paper examines the newly formed euro overnight interbank market, asking how monetary-policy institutions, private trading venues, and payment infrastructure affect intraday prices and liquidity. The authors analyze five months of tick data from November 1999 to March 2000, covering six voice brokers in four euro-area countries plus the UK and the Italian electronic MID market (pp. 1–3). They measure quoting frequency, turnover, mid-rate volatility, and bid-ask spreads around ECB Governing Council meetings, M3 releases, main refinancing operations, reserve-maintenance deadlines, Treasury liquidity shocks, settlement days, and the Y2K changeover.

Key points

  • Quoting frequency and volatility generally have a two-hump or U-shaped intraday pattern; spreads are flatter and sometimes weakly single-humped (pp. 1–3, 50–51).
  • Overnight rate levels differ little between brokers in different euro-area countries, indicating rapid cross-border integration despite different trading channels (pp. 1–3, 50–51).
  • ECB Governing Council days bring higher activity, volatility, and spreads, especially after the 1:45 p.m. policy-release time, but the economic size of the move is small and volume does not dry up before the announcement (pp. 5, 34, 50–51).
  • Monthly M3 releases produce modest short-term volatility changes; quoting nearly stops in the half-hour before release in MID, while volume remains present (pp. 35–37, 50–51).
  • Tuesday main refinancing operations create heavy liquidity reallocation after the 11:15 a.m. allotment announcement; MID turnover is about 6.2 billion euros in the Tuesday lunch interval, without unusual spreads (pp. 38–40).
  • End-of-maintenance-period days roughly double quote frequency, more than double volatility in many intervals, and widen average voice-broker spreads to 7.7 basis points versus 5.6 on normal days (pp. 41–43).
  • Y2K changeover days show sharply reduced quoting, very high volatility, and wider spreads, while MID turnover remains moderately close to normal (pp. 49–51).

Actionable rules

  1. None given. The study provides market-microstructure evidence rather than an entry, exit, or sizing strategy.
  2. As a risk-monitoring implication, expect worse liquidity around reserve-maintenance deadlines and exceptional operational shocks; the paper’s measured spreads and volatility are historical observations, not universal thresholds (pp. 41–44, 49–51).

Caveats

The sample covers only five months shortly after euro launch and relies heavily on broker quotes plus one electronic venue. Some event groups are small, especially M3 releases and Treasury shocks. The authors cannot fully separate Italian tax-day liquidity shocks from reserve-maintenance effects (p. 43). Results describe overnight euro funding and should not be transferred directly to spot FX or other markets.

Who it is for

Macro traders, rates traders, and researchers who need to understand why liquidity and transaction costs change around central-bank operations and settlement deadlines.
