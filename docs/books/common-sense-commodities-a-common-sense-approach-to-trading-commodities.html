Summary

A beginner-level, homework-based mini-course on commodity chart reading and basic risk control. It walks a new trader from reading monthly/weekly/daily charts through drawing and confirming trendlines, using 50% retracement levels, recognizing trading ranges and channels, spotting gaps and spikes, and identifying reversal patterns (123 method, head and shoulders, double/triple tops and bottoms) and continuation patterns (triangles, wedges, flags, pennants). It ends with lessons on entering trending markets, adding to winners correctly, and understanding and managing risk with targets and trailing stops.

Key points

  • A trend needs at least three touches (“hits”) on a trendline before it is considered confirmed.
  • The 50% level (midpoint of a prior major move) is a key support/resistance and profit-target reference, calculated first on monthly charts, then weekly, then daily.
  • Stops belong at “odd” numbers, not round/even numbers, because price often touches even numbers before reversing and triggers unnecessary stop-outs.
  • Pyramiding (adding in a way that increases average risk) is the “wrong way” to build a winner; pillaring (adding while keeping size weighted toward the original entry) is the “correct way.”
  • Recommends broker alerts over resting open orders so the trader can evaluate conditions before acting.
  • Covers slippage, order types (stop, limit, GTC), and reward/risk ratio before teaching any chart patterns.

Actionable rules

  1. Require a minimum reward/risk ratio of 2:1 before taking a trade (the author states he personally likes to see 2:1, occasionally less but not often).
  2. Confirm a trendline only after three price “hits” on it.
  3. Place stop-loss orders at odd-numbered price points, never at round numbers, to avoid being stopped out by noise around psychologically obvious levels.
  4. Use the 50% retracement of the last major move (monthly, then weekly, then daily) as a profit target and as a stop/entry reference point.
  5. Always place a stop-loss order at the same time as every entry order; never trade without one.
  6. Add to winning positions via pillaring, not pyramiding, to avoid disproportionately increasing risk as a trade moves in your favor.

Caveats

Full text was read up to roughly the midpoint (through channels, gaps, and spikes) plus a keyword pass across the whole document for stop, target, and risk terminology; the reversal- and continuation-pattern lessons (head and shoulders, triangles, flags) were not read in full detail. The material is a scanned homework-style course aimed at complete beginners and predates modern electronic/algorithmic commodity markets; some order-type and broker-alert mechanics (e.g., calling a broker to set an alert) are dated.

Who it is for

Complete beginners to commodity/futures charting who want a structured, homework-driven introduction to trendlines, retracements, and basic risk control before moving to more advanced technical-analysis material.
