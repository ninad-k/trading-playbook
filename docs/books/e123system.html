Summary

A promotional 24-page report by Mark Crisp (stressfreetrading.com, 2002) that defines the “1-2-3” chart pattern — a low (point 1), a correction (point 2), and a retest that fails to exceed point 1 (point 3) — as a setup valid across all timeframes and instruments. The report covers pattern recognition, an entry trick for beating the crowd at the breakout, and an exit framework built around trailing stops that lock in progressively more profit as a trade runs. About a third of the document is sales material for the author’s other products.

Key points

  • 1-2-3 pattern: Point 1 = the bottom (or top); Point 2 = the correction; Point 3 = the retest that does not exceed Point 1 — if it does, the pattern is invalid.
  • Buy signal triggers on a breakout above the horizontal line drawn at the Point 2 level (mirror-image sell signal for tops).
  • Pattern is claimed to appear on all timeframes (5-minute to monthly) and all chart types (bar, line, Renko, point & figure, candlestick), with roughly 70% directional accuracy claimed by the author (uncredited/unverified).
  • General rule of thumb: the more bars involved in forming the 1-2-3, the larger and longer-lasting the resulting move (daily 1-2-3 ≈ 2–8 week move; weekly ≈ 4–16 weeks; monthly ≈ 2–12 months, per the author’s informal table).
  • Entry “trick”: instead of buying/selling exactly at the Point-2 breakout level (where crowd orders cluster), place the order slightly inside that level to get filled ahead of the breakout crowd and reduce slippage.
  • Initial stop loss: no more than 10% for stocks; a predetermined dollar amount for futures; risk no more than 3% of total account equity per trade.
  • Tiered profit protection: at +25% gain, move stop to breakeven; beyond +25%, protect half the gain; beyond +100% gain, protect 75% of the gain.
  • Author’s stated order of importance for a trading system: (1) trader psychology, (2) money management, (3) trade entry/exit rules — the reverse of what most new traders assume.

Actionable rules

  1. Identify a valid 1-2-3 pattern: bottom (Point 1) → correction (Point 2) → retest that stays above Point 1 (Point 3), or the mirror-image for a top.
  2. Enter on a break of the horizontal line at the Point 2 level, placed slightly inside that level rather than exactly at it, to front-run breakout-crowd orders.
  3. Set initial stop loss at no more than 10% (stocks) and risk no more than 3% of account equity per trade.
  4. At +25% open gain, move stop to breakeven.
  5. Beyond +25% gain, trail the stop to protect half the accumulated gain; beyond +100% gain, trail to protect 75% of the gain.
  6. Favor patterns that form over more bars (weekly/monthly over daily) when looking for the largest expected moves.

Caveats

The document is largely a marketing piece for the author’s other paid products (Momentum Stock Trading System, commodity trading course); the quoted “70% accuracy” and “twelve valid signals” claims on the General Electric chart example are asserted without any independently verifiable backtest, sample size, or methodology. The pattern itself is a generic higher-low/lower-high retracement setup common to many chart-pattern systems, not something unique to this report.

Who it is for

Discretionary swing traders who want a simple, visually-driven pullback/breakout pattern with an explicit (if informal) stop-and-trail money-management framework layered on top.
