Practical Fibonacci Methods for Forex Trading

Ken Marshall and Rob Moubray · 2005 · Tier B — concise note book intermediate

A chart-based ABCD method that confirms Fibonacci retracements with candles or price patterns and targets projections.

Summary

Ken Marshall and Rob Moubray present a compact forex method built around an A-to-B impulse, a pullback to point C, and a projected target at D. Retracement levels locate candidate entries; candlestick patterns, double tops or bottoms, higher lows or lower highs, support, and trendlines supply confirmation. Projection levels then frame exits. The method is demonstrated across intraday and daily charts, with emphasis on confluence rather than automatic trading at every Fibonacci line (PDF pp. 4-26).

Key points

  • Principal retracements are 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, and 78.6%; the source sometimes uses 76.4% because of its charting platform (PDF pp. 3-5).
  • Candidate extensions include 100%, 127%, 161.8%, 200%, and 261.8% (PDF p. 7).
  • For a long, measure a rise from swing low A to swing high B, then wait for a pullback toward C. Reverse this geometry for a short (PDF pp. 5-7).
  • Do not assume the first retracement will hold; require a reversal signal near C (PDF pp. 5, 8).
  • Confluence occurs when two or more retracements or projections derived from different swings cluster in a narrow price area (PDF p. 9).
  • Higher-timeframe levels can affect shorter trades and should be marked before selecting the lower-timeframe setup (PDF pp. 4, 22).

Actionable rules

  1. Mark significant swing A and B in the direction of the main trend and plot the listed retracement levels.
  2. At C, require confirmation such as a morning/evening star, engulfing candle, tweezer, double top/bottom, higher low/lower high, or inside-bar break (PDF pp. 4-8).
  3. Use 161.8% as a primary projection candidate while monitoring 100%, 127%, 200%, and 261.8% and any confluence with other swings (PDF pp. 7, 10-26).
  4. Before entry, seek a reward of at least 1.5 times the planned risk (risk:reward of 1:1.5). The source suggests stops 15 pips beyond the last swing high for shorts or 10 pips beyond the last swing low for longs, without widening them afterward (PDF p. 27).
  5. Move the stop ten pips for each favorable ten-pip move, then tighten more aggressively after price reaches 75% of the original target (PDF p. 28).

Caveats

All pages were reviewed from OCR, but charts contain minor recognition errors. Examples are selected illustrations rather than a backtest, and exact Fibonacci reactions are not guaranteed; the authors explicitly recommend study, testing, and paper trading (PDF p. 31).

Who it is for

Forex traders who already recognize swing points and candlestick reversals.
