Fibonacci, Gann & Elliott Wave

Ratio and wave methods for projecting where a move should end: precise about geometry, subjective about labelling, and almost never tested at scale.

What it is

These three traditions share a premise: market swings relate to one another in fixed proportions, so where a move should end can be projected before it gets there. Fibonacci analysis measures a swing and marks retracement levels (38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%) and extensions (127%, 161.8%, 261.8%) derived from the ratio 0.618 and its inverse. Elliott Wave adds structure: a five-wave move with the larger trend, a three-wave correction against it, nested at every degree, with three inviolable rules that make any count falsifiable. Gann adds a geometry of price and time — angles, the Square of Nine, swing charts — plus, in his more practical writing, straightforward breakout rules. What unites the category is that it projects rather than reacts. What divides it is how much subjective labelling each method requires, and several authors here build their whole approach around removing that labelling.

Core principles

  • The ratio set comes from consecutive terms of the Fibonacci series converging on 0.618 and 1.618; 50% is not a Fibonacci ratio but is used as the midpoint by convention Candlesticks, Fibonacci, and Chart Pattern Trading Tools Fibonacci Applications and Strategies for Traders.
  • A single ratio hit is weak evidence; a cluster of independent projections landing in the same narrow zone is the actual signal Dynamic Trading — Time, Price and Pattern Projection Method How You Can Identify Turning Points Using Fibonacci, Part 1: Understanding Fibonacci Mathematics and Its Connection to the Wave Principle Practical Fibonacci Methods for Forex Trading.
  • A retracement level traded alone loses money. Fischer’s own test of a bare 61.8% rule on the S&P 500 produced nine trades and a net loss, used deliberately as the baseline his confluence method improves on The Fibonacci-Candlestick-Chart-Pattern Confluence Method The Truth About Fibonacci Trading.
  • Which level will hold is unknowable in advance — across one author’s four worked examples, support appeared at 38.2%, 50%, 76.4%, and in one case nowhere at all The Truth About Fibonacci Trading.
  • Elliott’s three rules are inviolable and make a count falsifiable: wave 2 never retraces beyond the start of wave 1, wave 3 is never the shortest of 1/3/5, and wave 4 never enters wave 1’s territory (diagonals excepted) Elliott Wave Rules and Guidelines Basic Guide to Understanding Elliott Wave.
  • Everything else in Elliott is a guideline, not a rule — alternation, channelling, and the ratio targets are tendencies the analyst must not treat as constraints Elliott Wave Principle How You Can Identify Turning Points Using Fibonacci, Part 1: Understanding Fibonacci Mathematics and Its Connection to the Wave Principle.
  • A clean count exists only about half the time, and forcing one is worse than having no pattern opinion at all Dynamic Trading MTPredictor Trading Course — Part 2 Practical Elliott Wave Trading Strategies.
  • Because several counts are usually valid at once, an alternate count must be maintained; it becomes the working hypothesis the moment the preferred count is invalidated Elliott Wave Principle Practical Elliott Wave Trading Strategies.
  • Every count should state three things in advance: the probable position, what would confirm it, and what would invalidate it Practical Elliott Wave Trading Strategies.
  • Wave structure supplies the stop for free: the level that invalidates the count is the level that invalidates the trade Practical Elliott Wave Trading Strategies How To Identify High-Profit Elliott Wave Trades in Real Time Dynamic Trading.
  • Several authors argue the productive move is to discard the count and keep the ratios — Fischer replaces wave-counting with swing filters, Pesavento with fixed XABCD ratio patterns, Walker with eleven C-wave shapes Fibonacci Applications and Strategies for Traders Fibonacci Ratios with Pattern Recognition How To Identify High-Profit Elliott Wave Trades in Real Time.
  • Time projections are treated as an independent dimension: a price target and a time target converging is stronger than either alone Fibonacci Applications and Strategies for Traders Dynamic Trading — Time, Price and Pattern Projection Method.
  • Construction matters more than the level: anchor to a genuine significant extreme rather than an arbitrary local one, and redraw when a new extreme forms Fibonacci and Gann Applications in Financial Markets.
  • Extensions into price territory with no trading history behind them are the least reliable projections in the kit Fibonacci and Gann Applications in Financial Markets.
  • The methods are reported to work only in liquid, crowd-driven markets and to break down in thin or closely held ones Elite Trader's Secrets: Market Forecasting with the New Refined Elliott Wave Principle Advanced Channeling Patterns: Wolfe Waves and Gartleys.

Concrete rules and setups

Retracement and extension levels

  1. Working retracements: 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6% (sometimes 76.4% depending on the platform); extensions at 100%, 127%, 138.2%, 161.8%, 200%, 261.8%, 423.6% Fibonacci and Gann Applications in Financial Markets Practical Fibonacci Methods for Forex Trading Fibonacci Studies (AIQ TradingExpert Pro Reference Manual excerpt).
  2. Fischer’s default working level is 61.8%: 38.2% enters too early and generates false signals, while waiting past 61.8% risks missing the move The Fibonacci-Candlestick-Chart-Pattern Confluence Method.
  3. Confirm the swing before trading it — no signal until a counter-move of at least the minimum filter size occurs (100 basis points daily on Swiss franc, mark or yen; 200 on sterling; doubled on weekly charts) Fibonacci Applications and Strategies for Traders.
  4. Confluence rule: require two or more retracements or projections derived from different swings to cluster in a narrow band before treating the zone as significant Practical Fibonacci Methods for Forex Trading How You Can Identify Turning Points Using Fibonacci, Part 1: Understanding Fibonacci Mathematics and Its Connection to the Wave Principle.
  5. Fischer’s confluence entry: at the 61.8% retracement, require either a candlestick reversal pattern or a completed 3-point chart pattern before entering; stop at the prior swing extreme, trail on a 3-day high or low, target 0.618× or 1.618× the initiating swing The Fibonacci-Candlestick-Chart-Pattern Confluence Method.
  6. Marshall and Moubray’s ABCD: mark swing A to B with the trend, wait for the pullback to C, require a confirming candle or pattern there, target 161.8% while watching 100/127/200/261.8%. Require reward of at least 1.5× risk, stop 10-15 pips beyond the anchoring swing, then trail ten pips per favourable ten pips and tighten past 75% of target Practical Fibonacci Methods for Forex Trading.

Elliott Wave projections

  1. Miner’s wave-ratio table: wave 2 retraces 50-78.6% of wave 1; wave 3 is at least 100% of wave 1, then 162% or 262%; wave 4 retraces 23.6-50% of wave 3; wave 5 runs 62%, 100% or 162% of wave 1 when wave 3 extended; wave C is 62%, 100% or 162% of wave A Dynamic Trading.
  2. Gorman’s projections: wave 2 commonly retraces 0.618 or 0.786 of wave 1, wave 4 commonly 0.382 or 0.236 of wave 3; wave 5 ≈ 0.618 × the net distance of waves 1-3 projected from the end of wave 4. Triangle legs project as 0.618× the leg two positions prior, or 1.618× if expanding How You Can Identify Turning Points Using Fibonacci, Part 1: Understanding Fibonacci Mathematics and Its Connection to the Wave Principle.
  3. Impulse validation checklist: waves 1, 3 and 5 should each subdivide into five; invalidate if wave 2 breaks the start of wave 1 or wave 4 overlaps wave 1; use 1.618× wave 1 to judge whether wave 3 extended; watch for a wave 5 failure when wave 4 retraces more than 50% of wave 3 Impulse Wave Checklist.
  4. Miner’s three-step projection: establish wave position, cluster the price-ratio projections, then check whether a Fibonacci time projection lands on the same dates — and skip the market entirely if no clean count exists Dynamic Trading — Time, Price and Pattern Projection Method.
  5. Walker’s C-wave method: eleven end-of-correction shapes sharing one entry mechanic — a stop order one tick beyond the bar labelled “d” or “4” — and one stop mechanic, one tick beyond the bar labelled “e” or “5” The C Wave Method.
  6. Joseph’s mechanical setups: buy the end of wave 4 when the Elliott Oscillator pulls back to zero, price retraces at least 38% of wave 3, the Profit Taking Index is above 35 and price holds above the Wave Four Channels; sell the end of wave 5 on oscillator divergence against the wave 3 peak, targeting the prior wave 4 low Practical Applications of a Mechanical Trading System Using Simplified Elliott Wave Analysis.

Harmonic and geometric patterns

  1. Gartley “222”: D completes at 0.618 or 0.786 of X-A about 75% of the time, with an AB=CD leg inside A-D and BC at 0.618 or 0.786 of AB. If X is exceeded, expect continuation to 1.27 or 1.618 of X-A Fibonacci Ratios with Pattern Recognition — Gartley "222" and Butterfly Patterns.
  2. AB=CD: the C-D leg equals A-B about 60% of the time and extends to 1.27 or 1.618 the rest Fibonacci Ratios with Pattern Recognition.
  3. The alternative Gartley ratio chain: AB ≈ 61.8% of XA, BC between 61.8-78.6% of AB, CD between 127-161.8% of BC, XD ≈ 78.6% of XA, entering at D with a first target at 61.8% of CD Advanced Channeling Patterns: Wolfe Waves and Gartleys.
  4. Wolfe Wave: waves 3-4 must stay inside the 1-2 channel and be roughly symmetric with it; wave 5 pushes slightly outside as a false breakout, which is the entry, with the 1-4 line extended as the target Advanced Channeling Patterns: Wolfe Waves and Gartleys.
  5. MTPredictor’s setup: price inside a Wave Price Target zone, a coloured Reversal Bar forming there, and price then exceeding that bar’s extreme — all three required, with the stop just beyond the bar’s opposite extreme MTPredictor — WPT / Reversal Bar Trade Set-Up (TS) MTPredictor Trading Course — Part 1.
  6. ICWR: identify the active wave, draw Fibonacci levels from its extremes, require a full candle to close inside the 0.382-0.618 retracement channel and then beyond the 0.250 or 0.750 confirmation level, filtered by daily RSI(14) against 50. Intraday uses 5-minute bars, a 40-pip minimum wave and a 50-pip stop; long-term uses 4-hour bars, 150 pips and 100 The ICWR Trading Rules.

Gann

  1. Chart hierarchy: monthly for the main trend, weekly for confirmation, daily only for timing — and leave alone stocks trading a narrow range on low volume Gann's Trend Detector Method.
  2. The 3-point rule: buy when price crosses a previous high by 1-3 points with the stop 3 points below the old top; sell short on a break of an important low by 3 points. Old tops become bottoms and old bottoms become tops New Stock Trend Detector.
  3. Gann’s capital rule: divide capital into ten parts and risk one part on the initial trade; after three consecutive losses, reduce the trading unit against remaining capital How to Trade.
  4. Gann’s pyramid: add a second lot only after a five-point favourable move, then move the stop on the combined position to within three points of the second entry; a worked smaller-addition sequence runs 100, 50, 30 and 20 shares at successive five-point intervals. Avoid adding near a double top when long or a double bottom when short How to Trade.
  5. Square of Nine: place the price on the spiral, read 90-, 180- and 270-degree rotations as candidate levels (from 15, 90 degrees up gives 19, 180 up gives 23, 180 down gives 8); rotate an overlay to align zero with an off-axis price The Gann Wheel.
  6. Gann Swing Chart: the stop sits at the price of the trend indicator line’s last change of direction — a structural stop rather than a fixed distance The Gann Swing Chart System.

Entries, stops and sizing

  1. Miner’s reversal-day triggers, valid only at a projected reversal zone: a Reversal Day makes a new high then closes below both its own open and the prior close, stop one tick above its high; a Signal Day opens in the top third, makes a new high and closes in the bottom third Dynamic Trading — Entry Triggers and Stop-Loss Rules.
  2. Miner’s sizing: never risk more than 3% of equity on one trade, 6% across all open trades, and stand aside for the month after a 10% drawdown. Size = maximum dollar risk ÷ per-unit capital exposure, rounded down Miner's Entry, Position Size, and Two-Unit Trade Management Plan.
  3. Competing risk caps elsewhere in the category: 3% of equity as a hard stop ceiling Fibonacci Ratios with Pattern Recognition, 2-3% with any wider setup skipped MTPredictor Trading Course — Part 1, and a strict 2:1 minimum profit-to-risk filter checked before every entry MTPredictor Trading Course — Part 2.
  4. Walker’s profit target is a percentage of exchange margin — roughly 50% on the S&P, 100% on currencies — on the reasoning that margin scales with volatility How To Identify High-Profit Elliott Wave Trades in Real Time.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the instant a retracement level is touched, with no confirming evidence The Fibonacci-Candlestick-Chart-Pattern Confluence Method The Truth About Fibonacci Trading Practical Fibonacci Methods for Forex Trading.
  • Forcing a wave count when the structure is not clean, rather than standing aside Dynamic Trading — Time, Price and Pattern Projection Method Practical Elliott Wave Trading Strategies.
  • Anchoring a retracement or fanline to an arbitrary local extreme rather than a genuine significant one, and failing to redraw when a new extreme forms Fibonacci and Gann Applications in Financial Markets.
  • Targeting extension levels in price territory with no prior trading history behind them Fibonacci and Gann Applications in Financial Markets.
  • Treating Elliott’s guidelines — alternation, channelling, ratio targets — as though they were the three hard rules Elliott Wave Principle.
  • Carrying a single preferred count with no alternate, so an invalidation leaves no plan Elliott Wave Principle Practical Elliott Wave Trading Strategies.
  • Applying the methods to thin or closely held instruments where they are reported to break down Elite Trader's Secrets: Market Forecasting with the New Refined Elliott Wave Principle Advanced Channeling Patterns: Wolfe Waves and Gartleys.
  • Choosing a swing filter or minimum swing size by eye — too small over-generates signals, too large yields none, and no author gives a formula Fibonacci Applications and Strategies for Traders The Fibonacci-Candlestick-Chart-Pattern Confluence Method.
  • Applying 1930s point-based stops or 1990s one-tick placements to decimalised modern markets without rescaling New Stock Trend Detector Dynamic Trading — Entry Triggers and Stop-Loss Rules.
  • Taking vendor hit-rate claims at face value: the 75% Gartley figure, the “more than 80%” Butterfly figure, and the 45%-versus-80% random-walk comparison all come without disclosed methodology or sample Fibonacci Ratios with Pattern Recognition Elite Trader's Secrets: Market Forecasting with the New Refined Elliott Wave Principle.
  • Reading natural-world Fibonacci occurrences as evidence about markets; the arithmetic is real, the inference is not Patterns: Math in Nature.

Best books for this topic

  1. Elliott Wave Principle — the foundational text, and unusually precise about which claims are rules and which are guidelines. Read for the framework, not the forecasting record.
  2. Dynamic Trading — the most complete practical synthesis: explicit ratio tables, a codeable entry-trigger catalogue, and honesty about the roughly 50% of the time no clean count exists.
  3. High Probability Trading Strategies — Miner’s later and most disciplined version, with the clearest position-sizing and trade-management rules in the category.
  4. Fibonacci Applications and Strategies for Traders — the best attempt to keep Fibonacci while discarding wave-counting, with explicit swing filters and time-goal projections.
  5. Fibonacci Ratios with Pattern Recognition — the source of the harmonic-pattern tradition, with the Gartley and Butterfly ratios stated more precisely than anywhere else here.
  6. Candlesticks, Fibonacci, and Chart Pattern Trading Tools — worth reading for the confluence chapter alone, which shows the losing baseline before the improvement rather than only the winners.
  7. Fibonacci and Gann Applications in Financial Markets — the most candid field guide: reports failures as readily as successes, and says plainly which asset classes suit these tools.
  8. New Stock Trend Detector — Gann without the esoterica: chart hierarchy, breakout entries, point stops and a pyramiding rule.
  9. How To Identify High-Profit Elliott Wave Trades in Real Time — narrows Elliott to the one wave worth trading and gives every pattern an entry and stop referencing named bars.
  10. How You Can Identify Turning Points Using Fibonacci, Part 1: Understanding Fibonacci Mathematics and Its Connection to the Wave Principle — the cleanest reference for which ratio applies to which wave, in both price and time.

Open debates

  • Whether the wave count is needed at all. Frost and Prechter treat the structure as the theory Elliott Wave Principle; Fischer discards the count and keeps only the ratios, arguing Elliott’s forecasting claims do not hold Fibonacci Applications and Strategies for Traders; Pesavento replaces it with fixed XABCD geometry Fibonacci Ratios with Pattern Recognition; Joseph keeps roughly the 35% of Elliott’s rules he considers unambiguous and claims that portion carries most of the profit Practical Applications of a Mechanical Trading System Using Simplified Elliott Wave Analysis.
  • Whether wave counting can be objective in real time. Walker’s premise is that narrowing to the C wave makes independent traders reach the same levels How To Identify High-Profit Elliott Wave Trades in Real Time; Frost and Prechter concede two or more valid interpretations are usually acceptable and the right count is often clear only in hindsight Elliott Wave Principle; MTPredictor’s answer is to move the judgement into software, which relocates the subjectivity rather than removing it MTPredictor Trading Course — Part 2.
  • Whether the methods have been tested. The only quantitative claims come from vendors — a million computerised forecasts at 80% versus 45% on random data Elite Trader's Secrets: Market Forecasting with the New Refined Elliott Wave Principle, a screensaver-distributed backtest finding 65% of instruments unsuitable Advanced Channeling Patterns: Wolfe Waves and Gartleys, and Joseph’s 94/80/60% channel odds Practical Applications of a Mechanical Trading System Using Simplified Elliott Wave Analysis — none with disclosed methodology. The one transparent test in the category is Fischer’s, and it shows the bare retracement rule losing money The Fibonacci-Candlestick-Chart-Pattern Confluence Method.
  • Whether fixed reward-to-risk filters are meaningful. Griffiths requires 2:1 before every entry and skips anything below it MTPredictor Trading Course — Part 2; Miner rejects such filters outright as fictional discipline, since the reward side is never more than a guess, and defines risk purely as distance to the invalidation level Dynamic Trading High Probability Trading Strategies.
  • How much capital a projected zone justifies. Miner caps at 3% per trade and 6% overall Miner's Entry, Position Size, and Two-Unit Trade Management Plan; Gann’s ten-part rule implies 10% on the initial trade How to Trade; the ICWR system gives no equity percentage at all, only a fixed pip stop and a warning about leverage ICWR Forex Trading Strategy.
  • Where the ratios come from. Most sources ground them in natural growth and the golden ratio How You Can Identify Turning Points Using Fibonacci, Part 1: Understanding Fibonacci Mathematics and Its Connection to the Wave Principle Candlesticks, Fibonacci, and Chart Pattern Trading Tools; MacLean grounds them instead in the psychology of buyer-seller attrition and is explicit that Gann’s planetary material is outside his scope Fibonacci and Gann Applications in Financial Markets; Pesavento includes planetary-cycle background while stating it is unnecessary for profitable trading Fibonacci Ratios with Pattern Recognition.
  • Whether Gann’s own framework is internally consistent. MacLean concedes Gann’s 360-day-year price/time construction introduces systematic error against a five-day trading week, and offers no correction beyond experience Fibonacci and Gann Applications in Financial Markets; the Square of Nine primer admits it fits poorly to instruments under $150 and to many futures without an unspecified conversion factor The Gann Wheel.
