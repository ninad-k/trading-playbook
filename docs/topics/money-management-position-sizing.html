Money Management & Position Sizing

How much to risk per trade and across a portfolio: the library converges on small fixed-fractional risk, and disagrees sharply on how close to the growth-optimal fraction anyone should trade.

What it is

Money management answers one question that is separate from “what should I buy?”: how much? Given an entry price, a stop, and an account balance, it determines the number of shares or contracts, and it governs how much of the account may be exposed across all open positions at once. Most authors in this category insist on that separation — position sizing is not stop placement, not diversification, and not trade selection (Special Report on Money Management, The Trading Game: Playing by the Numbers to Make Millions). The category also covers what follows from sizing: the arithmetic of drawdown and recovery, the probability of ruin, when to add to a winner, and how correlated positions quietly compound exposure. The recurring claim, stated most forcefully by Van Tharp and Ryan Jones, is that two traders taking identical signals will produce wildly different equity curves, and that the difference is almost entirely sizing.

Core principles

  • Signal generation and money management are separate problems, and a positive-expectancy system can still be driven to ruin by oversized bets Money Management Strategies for Futures Traders Special Report on Money Management Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom.
  • Drawdown recovery is non-linear: the gain needed to recover always exceeds the loss taken, and the gap widens sharply past 50% — a 50% loss requires a 100% gain, a 90% loss a 900% gain Money Management Strategies for Futures Traders Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits Position-Sizing Effects on Trader Performance: An Experimental Analysis.
  • Sizing should be anti-martingale — exposure rises with equity and falls after losses. Martingale progressions redistribute outcomes but cannot manufacture an edge Special Report on Money Management The Trading Game: Playing by the Numbers to Make Millions The Mathematics of Gambling: Section Four — Money Management.
  • No staking scheme rescues a negative expectancy; expectancy must be established before sizing is layered on top The Mathematics of Gambling: Section Four — Money Management Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom The Mathematics of Money Management.
  • Maximising expected value on each bet leads to ruin with probability one under repeated reinvestment; the correct objective is expected log capital, which is what makes growth-optimal sizing a fraction rather than everything A New Interpretation of Information Rate The Mathematics of Money Management.
  • Size must be recomputed for every trade, because entry, stop distance and equity all change; a constant share or contract count is the wrong size on most trades Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System Managing Your Money The Truth About Money Management.
  • Risk must be normalised across instruments before it can be compared. Percent-risk sizing does this through the entry-to-stop distance; volatility sizing does it through ATR or standard deviation Van Tharp's Four Position-Sizing Models The Money Manager Trading Strategy Nine Position-Sizing Models (Equal Units, % Risk, % Volatility, Kelly).
  • Per-trade limits are insufficient on their own: aggregate open risk needs its own ceiling, or several individually acceptable positions combine into an unacceptable one Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits Portfolio-Level Commodity Trading: Maximizing Risk-Adjusted Gains with Trade Management Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System.
  • Correlated positions are effectively one larger position and must be budgeted as such Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits Forex Money Management Money Management Strategies for Futures Traders.
  • Portfolio volatility is driven by covariance, not by the weighted average of individual volatilities, so a lower-return, low-correlation holding can reduce total risk more than its own numbers suggest Portfolio Risk Reduction: Optimising Selection of Resource Projects by Application of Financial Industry Techniques Volatility Matters: The Case for Investment in Resource Stocks Hedge Fund Course.
  • The growth-maximising fraction is also the drawdown-maximising one: at optimal f, the historical drawdown is never smaller than f itself as a percentage of equity, which is why almost every author trades a fraction of it The Mathematics of Money Management Secrets of Today's Top Traders (Interview With C.V.) The Truth About Money Management.
  • Leverage, not underlying volatility, is what makes futures accounts fail, and most traders do not consciously register the multiple they are running The Four Biggest Mistakes in Futures Trading Understanding the Relationship Between Risk, Reward and Margin/Equity Ratio in Managed Futures.
  • Because leverage is a free parameter, a fund’s apparent riskiness is a business decision rather than a property of its strategy — compare managers by risk-adjusted ratio, not raw volatility Understanding the Relationship Between Risk, Reward and Margin/Equity Ratio in Managed Futures Hedge Fund Course.
  • Live results are worse than backtests, so capitalisation should assume drawdowns larger than anything in the test window Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits Secrets of Today's Top Traders (Interview With C.V.) The Truth About Money Management.
  • Sizing is only as good as the trader’s willingness to execute it; a mathematically optimal fraction that produces an unbearable drawdown will be abandoned mid-decline Money Management Strategies for Serious Traders The Four Biggest Mistakes in Futures Trading CAI Discretionary Risk Matrix: Speed vs. Impact.

Concrete rules and setups

Per-trade sizing formulas

  1. Percent-risk sizing: position size = (equity × risk %) ÷ (entry − stop), always rounded down Managing Your Money Money Management and Risk Control for Traders Van Tharp's Four Position-Sizing Models.
  2. The same formula net of costs: (risk amount − commission) ÷ entry-to-stop distance. Worked example: ($500 − $80) ÷ $1.50 = 280 shares Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System.
  3. Percent-volatility sizing: size = (equity × volatility %) ÷ dollar ATR per contract, using a 4- to 20-day ATR depending on the source; preferred when stops are tighter than the daily range Nine Position-Sizing Models (Equal Units, % Risk, % Volatility, Kelly) Van Tharp's Four Position-Sizing Models.
  4. Standard-deviation normalisation: market risk = 30-day standard deviation of closes × big point value; contracts = floor((equity × risk %) ÷ market risk). Worked example: $100,000 at 2% with $750 market risk gives 2.67, floored to 2 contracts The Money Manager Trading Strategy.
  5. Kelly / optimal f with a payoff ratio A: f = [(A + 1)p − 1] ÷ A. Worked example: p = 0.33, A = 5 gives f = 0.20 Optimal f, Fixed-Fractional Sizing, and Risk-of-Ruin Allocation A New Interpretation of Information Rate.
  6. Empirical optimal f from a trade history: HPR = 1 + f × (−trade ÷ biggest loss); TWR is the product of all HPRs; search f from 0.01 to 1.00 and take the peak. Convert with dollars per contract = biggest loss ÷ −f (a −$100 worst loss at f = 0.25 gives one contract per $400) Optimal f and the TWR/Geometric Mean Framework.
  7. Fixed Ratio: add the next contract at previous required equity + (contracts held × delta), with delta set near half the expected worst-case per-contract drawdown. At a $5,000 delta from $10,000: 2 contracts at $15,000, 3 at $25,000, 4 at $40,000 Fixed Ratio Position Sizing.
  8. Blended per-contract capital: (optimal f $ + largest overnight gap $ + (max drawdown $ + margin)) ÷ 3; without a backtest, substitute margin × 3 The Blended Account-Sizing Formula.
  9. Take the lower of the margin-based and risk-based contract counts so neither constraint is breached, and round fractional contracts down Optimal f, Fixed-Fractional Sizing, and Risk-of-Ruin Allocation.

Risk limits

  1. Per-trade risk: 1% or less when managing other people’s money, up to about 3% on your own, beyond which Tharp calls it gunslinger territory Special Report on Money Management Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom. Professional norms are cited at 1-3% The Truth About Money Management, with interviewees ranging from 0.25-0.5% up to 5% Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits.
  2. Competing caps for the same decision: 2% per trade inclusive of commission and slippage Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System Managing Your Money; 2% as standard with 5% as a ceiling Money Management and Risk Control for Traders; 1% preferred and 3% never exceeded Forex Money Management; 5% as an outer bound The Four Biggest Mistakes in Futures Trading.
  3. Aggregate open risk: 6% total with 2% per sector Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System; 20% of equity as a portfolio cap Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits; portfolio heat of 20-25%, derived as roughly 80% of the Kelly-implied fraction and divided by the expected number of simultaneous positions Nine Position-Sizing Models (Equal Units, % Risk, % Volatility, Kelly); a 50%-of-equity open-risk gate before any new trade Secrets of Today's Top Traders (Interview With C.V.).
  4. Margin-to-equity below roughly 30% as a leverage proxy The Four Biggest Mistakes in Futures Trading; managed futures funds typically run 5-30% Understanding the Relationship Between Risk, Reward and Margin/Equity Ratio in Managed Futures; a 5:1 leverage cap regardless of what the broker offers The Magic of Forex Trading: Money Management.
  5. Drawdown stop-points: 12% of initial capital Money Management and Risk Control for Traders; 20-25% maximum acceptable Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits; roughly 15% for managed accounts against 40-60% an individual might accept The Truth About Money Management.
  6. Capitalise a systematic strategy at 2-5× its historical worst drawdown, and expect live profits near half the backtested figure Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits.
  7. Fund the account with risk capital only, never borrowed money, and keep it to 10-20% of investable capital Money Management and Risk Control for Traders Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System.

Stops, scaling and adding

  1. Set the stop before entry and size the position to it, rather than picking a size and forcing a stop to fit Stop Losses Are For Sissies (And Other Myths About Forex Trading) Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System.
  2. Size stops to recent volatility — around 3-4 days’ average range — since stops tighter than normal noise convert an edge into a near-certain loss Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits; one source specifies at least two standard deviations, referenced to a higher timeframe than the entry Money Management and Risk Control for Traders.
  3. Stops may be tightened but never loosened, and a hit stop is taken immediately rather than overridden Managing Your Money Stop Losses Are For Sissies (And Other Myths About Forex Trading) Money Management and Risk Control for Traders.
  4. Size new trades off core equity — total equity minus dollar risk open in all positions — so capacity shrinks automatically as exposure grows Managing Your Money Forex Money Management.
  5. Two-for-one management: once open profit exceeds the initial risk, exit half and move the remaining stop to breakeven Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits The 15-Rule Put Option Crash Strategy.
  6. Pyramid only into winners, financed from locked-in profit: additional contracts = (chosen fraction × assured unrealised profit × current contracts) ÷ permissible loss per contract. Never average down Optimal f, Fixed-Fractional Sizing, and Risk-of-Ruin Allocation The Five Minute Investor.
  7. Reverse Scale: add an equal dollar amount at each fixed percentage increment above the last purchase, and exit the whole position on a retrace through the previous decision point The Five Minute Investor.
  8. Risk bands tied to trend stage: stop 2.5-5% of price in a strong trend, 5-10% early, 10-15% choppy, 15-20% late, with risk of 1-5% falling to 1-2.5% in late-stage conditions CAI Discretionary Risk Matrix: Speed vs. Impact.
  9. Cap a speculative options sleeve at about 5% of the portfolio, staged across roughly a year rather than deployed at once The 15-Rule Put Option Crash Strategy.

Common mistakes

  • Trading a fixed number of shares or contracts regardless of stop distance and equity, so real risk varies trade to trade without the trader noticing Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System The Truth About Money Management.
  • Treating unrealised gains as “house money” and risking them more loosely than original capital; a stop left unmoved as a winner runs can let a 2% risk balloon to 9% or more Managing Your Money Money Management.
  • Averaging down, and rationalising it with costs already sunk into the trade — commissions, research, or the wait for the setup Money Management The Five Minute Investor Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits.
  • Setting stops too tight for the instrument’s normal range, which converts ordinary noise into realised losses Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits Money Management and Risk Control for Traders.
  • Sizing each position independently and ignoring correlation, so a book of related markets carries far more aggregate risk than any single calculation shows Van Tharp's Four Position-Sizing Models Forex Money Management.
  • Trading at or near calculated optimal f, which is acutely sensitive to the single largest historical loss and to the ordering of trades The Truth About Money Management The Mathematics of Money Management.
  • Relying on mental stops at exactly the moment execution is psychologically hardest The Four Biggest Mistakes in Futures Trading Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits.
  • Undertrading as well as overtrading: risk small enough to be swamped by costs wastes a real edge Managing Your Money The Mathematics of Gambling: Section Four — Money Management.
  • Adopting a fraction the account can bear arithmetically but the trader cannot bear psychologically, then abandoning the system partway into the drawdown Money Management Strategies for Serious Traders The Four Biggest Mistakes in Futures Trading.
  • Judging a fund or system by raw volatility when volatility is largely a leverage setting Understanding the Relationship Between Risk, Reward and Margin/Equity Ratio in Managed Futures.

Best books for this topic

  1. Money Management Strategies for Futures Traders — the most complete single treatment: risk of ruin, fixed-fractional and Kelly sizing, multi-commodity allocation and pyramiding, all worked numerically. Best for anyone who wants the whole framework in one place.
  2. The Mathematics of Money Management — the rigorous derivation of optimal f, TWR and the geometric-mean objective, and unusually candid about the drawdowns its own answer implies. For quantitative readers.
  3. Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom — expectancy and R-multiples, plus four sizing models demonstrated on identical signals. The clearest demonstration that sizing dominates entry choice.
  4. Special Report on Money Management — nine sizing models compared on one reference system, with the drawdown-recovery table and portfolio heat. The best menu-style reference.
  5. The Trading Game: Playing by the Numbers to Make Millions — Fixed Ratio and the delta formula, and the sharpest critique of fixed-fractional sizing for small accounts.
  6. The Four Biggest Mistakes in Futures Trading — the most practical account-sizing method for futures, blending optimal f, overnight gap and drawdown, with leverage made concrete.
  7. A New Interpretation of Information Rate — the 1956 original. Short, and worth reading to see exactly what the criterion does and does not claim.
  8. Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits — a compact set of 17 guidelines plus practitioner interviews; the fastest route to a workable default for a discretionary trader.
  9. The Truth About Money Management — the best short statement of why individual traders and fund managers should not use the same sizing targets.
  10. Position-Sizing Effects on Trader Performance: An Experimental Analysis — a controlled experiment rather than assertion: smaller positions cut bankruptcy rates roughly tenfold without raising average profit.
  11. Crash Profits: Make Money When Stocks Sink and Soar — the defensive end of the subject: how to move capital out of harm’s way in stages rather than in one decision, with a single explicit market filter (the S&P against its 20-day average) and a staged re-entry plan.

Open debates

  • Fixed Ratio versus fixed fractional. Jones argues fixed-fractional sizing either compounds too slowly to matter or courts ruinous drawdowns, and that Fixed Ratio dominates both Fixed Ratio Position Sizing. His comparisons use selected bounded-loss scenarios rather than full simulation, and he concedes the advantage narrows at fund scale; the percent-risk camp remains the default elsewhere Nine Position-Sizing Models (Equal Units, % Risk, % Volatility, Kelly).
  • How close to optimal f to trade. Vince derives it as the growth-maximising fraction while stating it implies 30-95% retracements The Mathematics of Money Management. Tharp rejects it in favour of Kelly on the grounds that it assumes the worst loss has already happened Special Report on Money Management; Ruggiero calls it a theoretical ceiling, never a target The Truth About Money Management; Kaeppel uses it only as one input among three The Blended Account-Sizing Formula; one practitioner reports trading about 10% of full Kelly Secrets of Today's Top Traders (Interview With C.V.).
  • What number belongs in the percent-risk rule. The library offers 0.25%, 1%, 2%, 3% and 5% as “the” limit, mostly as convention rather than derivation Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits Forex Money Management Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System Money Management and Risk Control for Traders. Only the Kelly-derived approaches attempt to compute it from a system’s own statistics A New Interpretation of Information Rate Money Management Strategies for Futures Traders.
  • Whether an acceptable drawdown is universal. Ruggiero argues individuals can rationally target 40-60% while managed accounts need roughly 15%, because the objectives differ The Truth About Money Management Understanding the Relationship Between Risk, Reward and Margin/Equity Ratio in Managed Futures; most retail-facing sources prescribe a single ceiling around 12-25% for everyone Money Management and Risk Control for Traders Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits.
  • Scaling out versus holding the full position. Partial exits are advocated as stress reduction that converts marginal trades into small winners Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System Money Management: Controlling Risk and Capturing Profits, but they cut exactly the outsized trades that carry a trend-following system’s expectancy Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom, and require divisible size and liquidity most small accounts lack.
  • Percent risk versus percent volatility. Risk-based sizing is criticised because a fixed percentage can mean one contract or many depending on stop distance, so nominally equal risks differ in real exposure Van Tharp's Four Position-Sizing Models; volatility-based sizing normalises across instruments but ignores where the stop actually sits The Money Manager Trading Strategy.
  • Whether stops are always required. Most sources treat a stop as non-negotiable Stop Losses Are For Sissies (And Other Myths About Forex Trading) Managing Your Money, but Weiss deliberately omits stops from a capped-size options sleeve on the grounds that the size cap is itself the risk control The 15-Rule Put Option Crash Strategy.
  • Whether a locked-in profit still counts as risk. McDowell frees a position from the risk budget once a trailing stop is past breakeven Fine-Tuning Your Money Management System, and Balsara formalises this as negative effective exposure Optimal f, Fixed-Fractional Sizing, and Risk-of-Ruin Allocation; gap risk means a resting stop is not a guaranteed exit, which neither source addresses.
