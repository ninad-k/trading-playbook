## Summary

This Deutsche Bank strategy report presents the “Fed’s Stock Valuation Model,” which compares the S&P 500 forward earnings yield (FEY) with the 10-year Treasury yield. Fair value is defined as FEY=TBY, or an index fair-value price of expected earnings divided by the Treasury yield (pp. 2–3). An improved model replaces the Treasury yield with Moody’s A-rated corporate bond yield minus a weighted five-year earnings-growth forecast: `FEY = CBY − b·LTEG`, with historical average `b=0.1` and examples using 0.1, 0.2, and 0.25 (p. 4). The report then maps valuation bands to stock-bond mixes.

## Key points

- The report says overvaluation can persist and is therefore not a precise market-timing signal (pp. 3–5).
- Historical examples include a 34% overvaluation peak in September 1987 and a 70% reading in January 2000 before the technology bubble unwound (pp. 3–4).
- The stated allocation model is 60/40 stocks/bonds above 20% overvaluation, 70/30 at 10%–20% overvaluation, 80/20 below 10% overvaluation or undervaluation, 85/15 at 10%–20% undervaluation, and 90/10 beyond 20% undervaluation (p. 5).
- Forward earnings use 12-month consensus operating earnings; the report notes analysts often begin too optimistic (pp. 2, 9–11).
- The improved model makes risk and long-term growth assumptions explicit rather than burying them in the simple earnings-yield comparison (pp. 4–5, 12).
- U.S. forward earnings are shown as closely related to industrial production and capacity utilization; profits tend to rise when utilization exceeds 79% in the cited chart (p. 24).

## Actionable rules

1. Calculate fair-value price as expected forward earnings divided by the 10-year Treasury yield; compare actual index level with that estimate (pp. 2–3).
2. For a richer model, use `FVP = E ÷ [CBY − b·LTEG]` and state the chosen growth weight `b` explicitly (p. 4).
3. Use the report’s valuation bands as a long-horizon allocation guide: 60/40, 70/30, 80/20, 85/15, or 90/10 across the five bands (p. 5).
4. Treat the valuation gap as a 12–24 month return context, not a short-term timing trigger (p. 5).

## Caveats

The Fed never officially endorsed the model (p. 2). Consensus earnings are subject to forecast error and structural changes in rates, risk premia, taxes, and index composition. The allocation percentages are explicitly subjective, and the report is dated August 2001. Charts are descriptive and do not establish a tradable timing edge.

## Who it is for

Investors and macro traders who need a simple valuation dashboard for equity exposure and bond diversification.
