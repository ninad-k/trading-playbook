## Summary

A self-published, informally written forex trading guide by a trader identified only as "J. Heat." The core idea is "Mechanical Discretion" (MD): starting from a mechanical trading system and then applying the trader's own discretionary judgment to entries and exits, on the theory that no two traders can execute the same mechanical system identically because of individual psychology ("diff factors"). To ground MD in something concrete, the author presents his own base system, the Jetset Trading Method (JTM), built on a three-timeframe chart setup borrowed explicitly from Alexander Elder's Triple Screen, combined with three sub-strategies: Crossout, Boll Breakers, and Stochastic Divergence.

## Key points

- **Triple Screen setup** — three charts per pair: Day, 1 Hour, and 5 Minute, all candlestick.
- **Day chart** — 100-period exponential moving average (EMA) plus trendlines, Fibonacci projections, and support/resistance, used to gauge the prevailing trend direction.
- **1 Hour chart** — 5, 13, and 100-period EMAs (the 5/13 combination credited to Rob Booker).
- **5 Minute chart** — Bollinger Bands (20 period, 2 deviations), RSI (14, with 70/30 lines), Stochastic (15,3,3 with 80/20 lines), yesterday's high/low lines, a daily floor-trader pivot, and Camarilla pivots (credited to Rob Walton).
- **Crossout** — traded only on the 1 Hour chart: enter on a break of the prior candle's high/low by 1-2 pips in the direction of an EMA cross, targeting the daily EMA 100 if at least 10 pips away.
- **Boll Breakers** — traded on the 5-minute chart around Bollinger Band and Camarilla pivot touches, with separate stop rules for aggressive vs. non-aggressive traders.
- **Stochastic Divergence** — 5-minute divergence signals confirmed against Bollinger Bands or the other two strategies.
- The "Beginner's Trap": abandoning a system after a normal losing streak (the author notes 8-9 consecutive losses can occur) rather than evaluating it over a full sample period.

## Actionable rules

1. Crossout entry: buy 2 pips above the high of an up candle (or sell 2 pips below the low of a down candle) within 2 bars of a moving-average cross, on the 1 Hour chart.
2. Crossout stop: initial stop-loss 35 pips from entry; move stop to breakeven after 10 pips profit (7 pips if price is moving slowly).
3. Boll Breakers (aggressive trader): stop-loss 10-15 pips beyond the high/low formed at the Bollinger Band touch; exit at the middle Bollinger Band if no stop is used.
4. Boll Breakers (non-aggressive trader): stop-loss 2 pips beyond the high/low formed at the Bollinger Band touch; move to breakeven after 7-10 pips if trading against the daily/hourly trend.
5. Stochastic Divergence: initial stop-loss 15-20 pips from entry.
6. Only take counter-trend 5-minute setups (Boll Breakers/Stochastic Divergence) in the direction of the 1 Hour Crossout trend; if signals disagree, prefer the higher-timeframe (Crossout) direction.
7. Do not abandon a system after a short losing streak; evaluate performance over an extended period before concluding it is faulty.

## Caveats

Self-published and written in an admittedly informal, unedited style with no table of contents by the author's own design; several rules (e.g., exact Boll Breakers entry triggers, full worked trade examples) rely on figures/screenshots not captured in the extracted text. The "Mechanical Discretion" concept itself is not testable — it explicitly tells the reader to override the mechanical rules with personal judgment, which makes the system's historical performance impossible to verify or reproduce.

## Who it is for

Discretionary forex day traders comfortable combining a multi-timeframe, multi-indicator mechanical base (Triple Screen style) with subjective judgment, rather than traders wanting a fully rules-based, backtestable system.
