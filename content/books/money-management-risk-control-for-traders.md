## Summary

A short educational report (originally a web page from Stan Grist's Cycles of Wealth site) arguing that money management, not trade selection, drives most of the variance in trading results, citing the claim that up to 90% of fund manager performance variance is attributable to it. It walks through a concrete percent-risk position-sizing method with a running numerical example, then lists ten supplementary risk-control tips.

## Key points

- Cites Larry Hite's rule from Market Wizards: never risk more than 1% of total equity on one trade, framed as making the trader "indifferent" to any single trade's outcome.
- Recommends a 2% risk module as the standard for the techniques taught (up to 5% for traders willing to accept larger equity swings), scaled up slightly for very small accounts ($5,000-$10,000).
- Position size formula: risk amount (% of current equity) ÷ risk per share (entry price minus stop price) = number of shares/contracts; always round the share count down, never up.
- Worked example: a $50,000 account risking 2% ($1,000) on a trade with $1.50/share risk yields a 666-share position (rounded down to 600-650 for ease of execution).
- Recommends keeping a trading account separate from an investment account, sized at only 10-20% of total investable capital, since trading is explicitly high-risk capital.
- Recommends diversifying across at least 5-10 positions of roughly equal dollar size so no single loser materially damages the account.
- Hard drawdown limit: never let account drawdown exceed 12% of initial capital, noting that recovering from a 25% drawdown requires a 33.3% gain just to break even.
- Stops should be placed at least 2 standard deviations from current price and referenced to a higher timeframe than the one used for entries (e.g., use weekly data to place a stop when trading off daily data); stops should not be loosened as price approaches them.
- Reports that most successful traders lose on roughly 60% of trades but make up for it with the other 40%, and that only a handful of trades per year typically account for most of the profit.

## Actionable rules

1. Risk per trade: 2% of current account equity as the standard risk module (up to 5% maximum; smaller accounts under $10,000 may go slightly higher).
2. Position size = (equity × risk%) ÷ (entry price − stop price); always round the resulting share/contract count down.
3. Maximum account drawdown: 12% of initial capital before reassessing the approach.
4. Stops: place at least 2 standard deviations from market price, referenced to a higher timeframe; never move a stop closer once it's set, and never move it against an open profit.
5. Diversification: hold at least 5, preferably 10, roughly equal-sized positions rather than concentrating capital.
6. Never let a winning trade turn into a loss — move the stop to lock in profit as soon as a trade is favorable.
7. Trading capital should be a separate account representing only 10-20% of total investable capital.

## Caveats

This is a short promotional web report (with a call-to-action for the author's paid "Cycles Report" at the end) rather than a rigorous or independently sourced text; the "90% of fund manager variance" claim and the 60/40 win-rate generalization are stated without citation. Commission estimates ($2,500/year on a $25,000 account trading ~20 times) reflect early-2000s discount-broker pricing and are dated. The content overlaps heavily with standard percent-risk position-sizing guidance found in many other trading books; it adds no novel technique beyond the worked example and the specific numeric thresholds (2%, 12% drawdown limit).

## Who it is for

Beginner traders who have not yet formalized a position-sizing rule and want a simple, numbers-first introduction to percent-risk sizing and basic account-level risk controls.
