## Summary

A four-chapter introductory forex manual: (1) world financial markets and FOREX mechanics — sessions, quoting conventions, margin trading, and P&L formulas; (2) fundamental analysis — central banks, interest rates, and macro indicators that move exchange rates; (3) technical analysis — chart types, trend/support-resistance, classical patterns, Fibonacci and Elliott Wave, and mathematical indicators (moving averages, Bollinger Bands, RSI, Stochastics, MACD, Parabolic SAR); (4) trading psychology and capital-management tactics.

## Key points

- FOREX runs 24 hours across Asia (Tokyo/Hong Kong/Singapore), Europe (Frankfurt/London), America (New York/Chicago), and Pacific (Wellington/Sydney) sessions; London handles ~32% of turnover, USD is on one side of ~70% of trades.
- Quote convention: Bid/Ask spread; base currency first, quoted currency second (e.g., USD/CHF); EUR and (usually) GBP are always base currencies, JPY is always the counter currency.
- Margin trading lets small accounts control much larger notional (leverage cited up to 40-100x); P/L = N lots × 100,000 × (Sell − Buy) − commission − overnight financing.
- Three technical-analysis axioms: price discounts everything, prices move in trends, history repeats (because human psychology is stable).
- Fibonacci ratios (0.382, 0.5, 0.618) used for retracement/extension targets; Elliott Wave: 5 impulse waves + 3 corrective waves per cycle, with strict alternation and channel rules (Wave 4 low must not overlap Wave 1 high).
- Moving averages (SMA/WMA/EMA), Bollinger Bands (2 std-dev bands), RSI (70/30 or 80/20 overbought/oversold), Stochastics (%K/%D), MACD, and Parabolic SAR are the core mathematical indicators covered.
- Oscillators work best in range-bound (lateral) markets; moving averages work best in trending markets and generate false signals in choppy conditions.

## Actionable rules

1. RSI: overbought/oversold at 75/25 in general use, 80/20 in strong trends, 70/30 in sideways markets.
2. Stochastic %K formula: (Close − Low_n)/(High_n − Low_n), typically smoothed over 5-21 periods with a 3-period SMA for %K and %D.
3. Trade in the direction of the intermediate-term trend: buy dips in an uptrend, sell rallies in a downtrend.
4. Use protective stops on every trade; close losing positions before winning ones when trimming risk.
5. When pyramiding: add only to profitable positions, reduce size at each level, never add to a loser, and move the stop toward breakeven.
6. Never trade against the dominant trend ("the trend is your friend").
