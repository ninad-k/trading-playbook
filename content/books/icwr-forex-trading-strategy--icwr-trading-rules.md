## What it is

The ICWR Trading Rules generate objective long/short signals from Fibonacci retracement levels drawn around the most recent significant price swing ("active wave"), filtered by a daily-chart RSI momentum reading, with a fixed hard-pip stop-loss as backstop. The same rule structure is used for two trading horizons that differ only in their parameter values: the **Intraday** variant (5-minute candlesticks) and the **Long-Term** variant (4-hour candlesticks). Both are documented below.

## Rules

### Step 1 — Identify the active wave

The active wave is the nearest price swing to the current time whose height (high minus low) meets or exceeds a minimum threshold:
- **Intraday**: minimum wave height of 40 pips, measured on a 5-minute candlestick chart.
- **Long-Term**: minimum wave height of 150 pips, measured on a 4-hour candlestick chart.

Procedure: identify candidate upward and downward swings on the chart; connect each swing's extreme values (high-to-low for downward swings, low-to-high for upward swings); enumerate them starting from the swing nearest the current time; calculate each swing's height; select the nearest swing meeting the minimum-height threshold as the active wave. If none qualifies, look further back in time. Each time a new qualifying swing forms, it replaces the previous active wave and its Fibonacci levels are redrawn.

### Step 2 — Draw Fibonacci levels

From the active wave's extremes, draw Fibonacci retracement levels at 0.000 (the wave's starting/lower extreme for measurement), 0.250, 0.382, 0.618, 0.750, and 1.000 (the wave's other extreme). The 0.382–0.618 band is the **retracement channel**; the 0.250 and 0.750 levels are the **confirmation levels**. The 0.000 and 1.000 levels carry no trading signal (they exist only to confirm correct construction).

### Step 3 — Wait for the retracement channel to trigger

No signal is valid until a candlestick's closing price falls inside the 0.382–0.618 retracement channel. Once triggered, only the confirmation levels (0.250, 0.750) matter going forward for that active wave.

### Step 4 — Read the signal (four cases)

A signal requires a **full candlestick** (entire candle body and wick) to close beyond a confirmation level, after the retracement channel has been triggered:

| Active wave direction | Candlestick closes fully... | Wave type | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Downward | below 0.250 | Impulsive (continuing the down-trend) | Bearish |
| Upward | above 0.750 | Impulsive (continuing the up-trend) | Bullish |
| Downward | above 0.750 | Corrective (against the down-trend) | Bullish |
| Upward | below 0.250 | Corrective (against the up-trend) | Bearish |

### Step 5 — Confirm with the daily RSI filter

Both variants use the same filter: a 14-period Relative Strength Index (RSI) on the **1-day** chart, referenced against the 50 midline (not the conventional 30/70 overbought/oversold levels).
- A bullish ICWR signal is only actionable if the daily RSI(14) is above 50.
- A bearish ICWR signal is only actionable if the daily RSI(14) is below 50.

### Step 6 — Entry

Enter long on a confirmed bullish signal (ICWR signal + RSI > 50); enter short on a confirmed bearish signal (ICWR signal + RSI < 50).

### Step 7 — Exit

Two independent exit triggers, whichever occurs first:
1. **Opposite ICWR signal** — an opposite-direction signal generated by the same process (Steps 1–4) as the position is held, tracked continuously as new active waves form.
2. **Hard stop-loss** — a fixed-pip stop placed immediately on entry:
   - **Intraday**: 50 pips from entry (above entry for shorts, below entry for longs).
   - **Long-Term**: 100 pips from entry.

There is no fixed profit target; the system is explicitly designed to "let profits run" by staying in the trade through multiple corrective retracements as long as none breaches the opposite confirmation level.

### Parameter summary

| Parameter | Intraday | Long-Term |
|---|---|---|
| Chart timeframe | 5-minute | 4-hour |
| Minimum active-wave height | 40 pips | 150 pips |
| Hard stop-loss distance | 50 pips | 100 pips |
| RSI filter | Daily RSI(14) vs. 50 | Daily RSI(14) vs. 50 |
| Confirmation levels | 0.250 / 0.750 | 0.250 / 0.750 |
| Retracement channel | 0.382–0.618 | 0.382–0.618 |

## Risk

Risk per trade is capped by the hard stop (50 pips intraday, 100 pips long-term), but position sizing in dollar/percentage-of-equity terms is left to the trader — the book's own examples size trades in fixed notional units (e.g., 10,000 units of the quote currency) combined with a stated leverage ratio (commonly 1:20 in examples) rather than a percentage-of-equity risk formula. Because exits rely on waiting for an opposite full-candlestick confirmation signal rather than a trailing stop, open profit can give back a meaningful amount before the exit triggers — the book's own comparison example shows this cost 147 pips of foregone profit relative to a simple trailing stop in one specific trade, in exchange for capturing a larger overall move. The authors' own parameter-sensitivity charts (varying active-wave threshold, stop distance, and RSI cutoff) show the reported backtest net-profit results are not highly sensitive to small parameter changes, which the book presents as evidence of robustness — though this is self-reported and not independently replicated.

## Caveats

The reported backtest performance (net pips over a "5 months" test averaged across EUR/USD, GBP/USD, and CAD/USD, informed by "two years" of historical data) does not disclose the specific date range, spread/commission/slippage assumptions, maximum drawdown, or out-of-sample testing — treat the figures as promotional illustrations rather than validated results. The "active wave" identification step retains an element of subjective judgment (choosing which swing on the chart qualifies) despite the system's mechanical framing, which could produce different signals between two traders applying the rules to the same chart. The RSI(1-day; 50/50) filter and the specific Fibonacci confirmation levels (0.250/0.750) were selected by the authors' own internal comparison against several alternative filters (moving averages, CCI, other RSI thresholds) reported only as summary bar-chart results, not as a rigorously cross-validated parameter search.
