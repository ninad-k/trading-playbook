## Summary

A beginner-oriented forex ebook covering market basics (majors, leverage, spreads, 24-hour sessions), technical vs. fundamental analysis, chart types, trading psychology, broker selection, common beginner mistakes, and money management, before presenting one concrete short-term trend-following system in its final section ("The System").

## Key points

- **Majors** — USD, JPY, EUR, GBP, CHF, CAD, AUD account for over 85% of daily Forex transaction volume.
- **Leverage** — ranges 40:1 to 400:1 in the broker examples given; risk scales directly with leverage used, not just position size.
- **Four types of stops** — equity stop (risk a fixed % of account), chart stop (swing high/low), volatility stop (Bollinger-band-based), and margin stop (subdividing capital into small allocations so a single trade can't blow up the account).
- **Money-management math** — a 50% equity loss requires a 100% gain to recover; a 75% loss requires 400%; the book cites Larry Hite's rule of never risking more than 1% of equity per trade.
- **Stochastic oscillator** (George Lane, 1950s) — %K (fast line) and %D (moving average of %K); standard interpretation buys below 20 and sells above 80, but in a trending market only oversold signals (uptrend) or only overbought signals (downtrend) should be taken.
- **Top day-trading mistakes** — chasing the market, trading on gut feeling, poor equity management, floating between systems, and skipping preparation before a session.
- **1-2-3 trend system** — described in full under Actionable rules below.

## Actionable rules

1. **Trend filter:** compare price to a 200-period moving average on both a 10-minute and an hourly chart; a trend is in effect only when price sits consistently on the same side of the MA on both timeframes.
2. **Entry trigger:** on the 10-minute chart, price must be within 20 points of the 200-period MA, and the fast stochastic line must cross above the slow line while below 20 (buy) or cross below the slow line while above 80 (sell).
3. **Stop placement:** initial stop-loss 10 points beyond the 200-period MA on the 10-minute chart (below it for longs, above it for shorts).
4. **Trailing stop:** as the trade moves favorably, trail the stop; the book's worked examples trail 25 points behind each new price extreme.
5. **General risk rule:** never use more than 10% of account equity on any one position; a commonly cited equity-stop size is 2% of account risked per trade.

## Caveats

The 1-2-3 system's two worked examples (EUR/USD and USD/JPY, June 2002) are illustrative case studies, not a statistically validated backtest — no win rate, sample size, or drawdown data is given beyond the two trades shown. Broker recommendations (Saxo Bank, GAIN Capital, GCI Financial, CMS Forex) and cited leverage limits (up to 400:1) reflect mid-2000s retail forex conditions that regulators have since restricted in most jurisdictions. General educational/psychology content (discipline, cutting losses, not "marrying" trades) overlaps heavily with other beginner forex/trading-psychology books.

## Who it is for

Complete beginners to forex wanting a single primer covering market mechanics, basic money management, and one concrete (if lightly tested) short-term trend system to start from.
