## Summary

Part 1 of a two-part Elliott Wave International ebook by EWI analyst Wayne Gorman, based on a March 2008 webinar. It reviews the mathematics of the Fibonacci sequence and Golden Ratio, then works through how Fibonacci ratios and multiples apply to the internal structure of Elliott waves: how corrective waves retrace prior waves, how impulse wave 5 relates to waves 1-3, how wave 4 divides a move's price/time range, and how zigzags, flats, and triangles relate their sub-waves by fixed Fibonacci ratios. It closes with a Q&A section clarifying common misconceptions.

## Key points

- **Golden Ratio (phi)** — 0.618 (and inverse 1.618) derived by dividing adjacent Fibonacci numbers to infinity; appears in nature, DNA structure, and a documented 62/38 split in human binary decision-making under uncertainty.
- **Retracement guideline** — second waves commonly retrace 0.618 or 0.786 (deep) of wave 1; fourth waves commonly retrace 0.382 or 0.236 (shallow) of wave 3.
- **Wave 5 projection (no extension)** — wave 5 ≈ 0.618 × (net distance of waves 1-3) applied from the end of wave 4, or alternatively wave 5 ≈ 0.382 × the same net distance.
- **Wave 5 with extensions** — third-wave extension: wave 5 ≈ wave 1; fifth-wave extension: wave 5 ≈ 1.618 × net distance of waves 1-3; first-wave extension: waves 2-5 ≈ 0.618 × the extended wave 1's length.
- **Fibonacci dividers** — wave 4 (its start or end) commonly splits the whole impulse move's price range, and separately its time duration, into the Golden Section (0.382/0.618) or occasionally 50/50.
- **Corrective-wave ratios** — zigzags: wave C ≈ wave A (or 0.618×/1.618× wave A); flats: wave C ≈ wave B ≈ wave A; expanded flats: wave C ≈ 1.618 × wave A, wave B ≈ 1.236 or 1.382 × wave A; triangles: alternating legs relate by 0.618 (1.618 if expanding).
- **Scale choice** — use log scale for long-horizon/wide-range charts (because Fibonacci relationships reflect percent change), but check log values, arithmetic values, and multiples separately since a ratio may only appear in one representation.
- **Fibonacci cluster** — the most reliable turning-point signals occur where multiple independent Fibonacci relationships (in price and/or time) converge at the same level or date.
- **Hard rule (not a guideline)** — wave 2 must retrace less than 100% of wave 1, or it is not a valid wave 2 by Elliott Wave Principle's definition.

## Actionable rules

1. To estimate a wave 5 top/bottom, multiply the net distance traveled of waves 1 through 3 by 0.618 and add/subtract from the end of wave 4 (use 0.382 as an alternate/lower target).
2. To estimate a retracement low for wave 2, look for 0.618 or 0.786 of wave 1's length; for wave 4, look for 0.382 or 0.236 of wave 3's length.
3. For triangles, project the next leg as 0.618 × the leg two positions prior (alternating legs), or 1.618× if the triangle is expanding.
4. When charting on log scale and no Fibonacci relationship is visible, re-check the same points using price multiples, then raw arithmetic values.
5. Treat a single Fibonacci hit as weak evidence; only treat a level/date as a high-confidence turning point when at least two independent Fibonacci relationships (price and/or time) cluster there, with roughly a one-day tolerance for time-based projections.

## Caveats

This is guideline-based pattern-fitting, not a mechanical system: the author repeatedly notes ratios are "common" or "guidelines," not guarantees, and several worked examples show the actual turning point missing the exact Fibonacci level by a few points or pips. Requires the reader to already be competent at Elliott wave counting (identifying impulse/corrective structure, degree labeling) since wave counts are assumed rather than taught here. Part 2 (trading application) is a separate ebook not included in this excerpt.

## Who it is for

Traders already familiar with basic Elliott Wave Principle who want a structured reference to the standard Fibonacci price and time ratios used to project wave targets and identify high-probability turning points.
