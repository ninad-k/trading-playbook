## Summary

A promotional bonus report from swing-trading author Alan Farley that walks through three dated, real-time trade write-ups from late 2001 — a long setup in Amazon.com, a range/breakout setup in Nvidia, and a short setup in Alkermes built around a head-and-shoulders pattern — then closes with an alphabetical glossary of dozens of swing-trading terms (many coined by Farley himself) covering candlestick patterns, Bollinger Band behaviors, gap types, and performance metrics.

## Key points

- Amazon example: a post-bubble bounce stock approaching the $10 "market number," where clearing $10 would confirm both a cup-and-handle on the short-term chart and a double bottom on the daily chart; resistance is flagged near $12 at the 200-day moving average and the post-bubble downtrend line.
- Nvidia example: a stock stuck below a rising trendline after a false breakout ("bull trap") on a volume spike; the setup hinges on whether it can clear resistance and fill the gap above $54.
- Alkermes example: a textbook head-and-shoulders top with a projected decline to ~$20 (measured from the neckline break by the head's height), where the author advises waiting for a weak bounce into resistance (broken 50-day MA, prior lows, $25 market number) for a better-timed short entry rather than shorting the initial neckline break.
- Three short-selling tips given: never chase a selloff, prefer less volatile (lower short-interest) markets, and short in an established downtrend rather than trying to pick a top.
- "Market numbers" (round levels like 10, 20, 25, 50, 100) are treated as real support/resistance because large numbers of traders cluster orders around them.
- The glossary defines dozens of terms including Cup and Handle, Head and Shoulders, NR7 (narrowest range of 7 bars), Fibonacci retracement levels (38%/50%/62%), and Farley-specific concepts like "Cross-Verification" and "Charting Landscape."

## Actionable rules

1. On a head-and-shoulders setup, project the downside target as the neckline-break price minus the height from the head to the neckline.
2. Prefer entering a head-and-shoulders short on a weak retracement rally into resistance rather than on the initial neckline break, to avoid the whipsaws caused by widely-known patterns.
3. Use round "market numbers" (10, 20, 25, 50, 100, etc.) as reference levels for confirming breakouts or resistance.
4. Combine multiple confirming signals (e.g., moving average resistance + trendline + market number + oscillator reading) before entering — Farley calls a 4-way confluence "Cross-Verification x4," his highest-probability setup.
5. Keep stops tight on lower-liquidity or small-price stocks given the outsized impact of small price moves.

## Caveats

This is a short promotional/bonus report tied to marketing for Farley's other paid material (site branding "fx1618.com" appears on every page); the three trade examples are dated, single-instance case studies with no aggregate win-rate or backtest data. Some ticker-specific commentary (Amazon near $10, Nvidia near $54) is time-bound to late 2001 market conditions and has no lasting relevance beyond the pattern-recognition lesson.

## Who it is for

Beginner-to-intermediate swing traders who want worked, real-time examples of chart-pattern-based entries/exits alongside a reference glossary of common swing-trading terminology.
