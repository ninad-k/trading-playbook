## What it is

Four niche intraday chart patterns Wolff identified as repeating "a good percentage of the time" in Nasdaq stocks reacting to news: the gainer pattern, the dumper pattern, end-of-day gap plays, and (as a variant within slow-momentum stocks) the breakout play. All four are read off Level 1/Level 2 quote screens and time-of-sales, not off indicators, and all four require the trader to keep a daily "tracking diary" of how the pattern behaved that day so tomorrow's expectations are evidence-based rather than assumed.

## Rules

**1. Gainer pattern** — a stock up 20% or more from the previous close on genuinely positive news (earnings beat, upgrade, FDA approval, hot-sector theme).
- *Qualify it*: at least 50,000 pre-market shares traded across multiple prints (one large block does not count), a real price gap up confirmed on the Level 2/time-of-sales (not just a stale Market Maker quote), and a spread no wider than 0.15 point.
- *First-day entry*: buy the first up-tick at the open if the tracking diary shows first-day gainers historically climb with no meaningful dip; otherwise wait for the first pullback bottom to confirm before entering.
- *Second-day entry*: look to fade — short the first high — once momentum shows signs of waning; watch for a stair-step continuation pattern, which argues against shorting.
- *Exit*: scale out — sell roughly half the position at a reasonable profit target derived from the tracking diary, trail a stop on the remainder, exit the rest when the trailing stop is hit. Do not short a "runner" stock (fast pace, only 0.50–1 point pullbacks, continuous new highs) — treat wide stops or avoidance as mandatory on these.

**2. Dumper pattern** — a stock down 20% or more from the previous close on moderately negative news (missed earnings by a penny or two, an analyst downgrade, a lost contract). Explicitly avoid devastating news (fraud, SEC investigation, bankruptcy, halted-stock risk).
- *Strong-pattern entry*: buy the first or second bottom, expecting value buyers to bid the stock back up; take profits at the upside target implied by the tracking diary.
- *Weak-pattern entry*: short the first run-up instead of buying dips; do not chase the short more than 0.25 point past the first top made.
- *Stop discipline*: take at most two stop-losses hunting for the reversal bottom on a single name, then stand aside for the rest of the session rather than continuing to average in.

**3. End-of-day gap plays** — held overnight, so treated as higher-risk than the intraday patterns.
- *Entry window*: assess only in the final few minutes (not 20–30 minutes before the close) — momentum often reverses in the last minutes, and entering too early risks holding a stock that gaps down instead of up.
- *Gainer-for-the-gap criteria*: majority of trades in the last 5 minutes are buys; stock closes near the top of its daily range; the tracking diary shows gainers currently gapping up a good percentage of the time; check for pre-market economic releases or negative headlines that could reverse the mood.
- *Dumper-for-the-gap criteria*: at least a 50/50 buy/sell split in the last 5 minutes (does not need majority buying); stock closes near the bottom of its daily range; news is not severely damaging; tracking diary shows dumpers currently gapping up.
- *Exit*: the safe, recommended exit is at or just prior to the next morning's open; less experienced traders should always take this exit rather than holding through the open.

**4. Breakout play** (a scalp-style variant traded within slow-momentum stocks) — triggered by a new intraday high.
- *Setup*: stock moves from a level (e.g., 20) to a prior high (e.g., 21), pulls back, pauses, then buying returns toward the prior high.
- *Entry*: buy at or just above the prior high (e.g., 21) once buying pace increases, on the expectation the level will be penetrated.
- *Exit*: if the level fails to break, take a small predefined stop immediately. If it breaks, exit at least half the position within roughly 0.25–0.50 point (breakouts often move only fractionally before pulling back sharply); expect fast, small profits (a scalper trading 1,000 shares nets real money even on a 0.13-point move).

## Risk

- **Position sizing by dollar risk, not fixed share count**: choose the stop distance first (typically 0.15–0.40 point depending on the stock's normal spread), then size shares so the resulting dollar loss is an acceptable percentage of total account equity — Wolff's example: 1,000 shares at $50 with a 0.25-point stop risks $250, roughly 0.5% of a $50,000 account; the same $250 loss is a much larger percentage of a smaller account, so cut share size accordingly.
- **Spread filter as a hard gate**: avoid any setup where the quoted spread exceeds 0.25 point; illiquid, wide-spread stocks can cost a full point or more before a stop can even be executed.
- **Two-stop cap**: never take more than two stop-losses chasing a single reversal (dumper bottoms, breakout retests) — a third attempt after two failures indicates the pattern isn't working today, not that the third try will.
- **News-severity filter**: never trade dumper patterns on stocks facing fraud allegations, SEC investigation, or bankruptcy risk, regardless of how attractive the technical bounce looks — halted stocks trap capital for unknown periods.
- **Runner-short prohibition**: never short a stock exhibiting runner characteristics (fast pace, sub-1-point pullbacks, continuous new highs); if a short must be taken, use a deliberately wide stop and reduced size.
- **Exit-when-invalid rule**: exit the instant the reason for entry is no longer true (support breaks, buying dries up) — this overrides any profit target or price level.

## Caveats

The specific numeric filters (50,000-share pre-market threshold, 20% gap threshold, 0.15/0.25-point spread gates) come from Wolff's own pre-decimalization, SOES/SelectNet-era Nasdaq tracking around 2000–2002 and are not validated against a modern, decimalized, algorithmically-traded market — treat them as illustrative starting points requiring re-calibration, not literal thresholds to hardcode. All four setups assume a human reading Level 2/time-of-sales in real time; they predate high-frequency trading and payment-for-order-flow routing changes that materially altered how retail orders interact with these price levels. The end-of-day gap play in particular carries acknowledged overnight risk that the book says should only be traded with dedicated risk capital.
