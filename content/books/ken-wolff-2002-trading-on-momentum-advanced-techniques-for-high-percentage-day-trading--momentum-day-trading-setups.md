## What it is

Four niche intraday chart patterns Wolff found repeating "a good percentage of the time" in Nasdaq stocks reacting to news: the gainer pattern, the dumper pattern, end-of-day gap plays, and (a scalp-style variant within slow-momentum stocks) the breakout play. All four are read off Level 1/Level 2 screens and time-of-sales, not indicators, and require a daily "tracking diary" of pattern behavior so the next day's expectations are evidence-based.

## Rules

**1. Gainer pattern** — stock up 20%+ from the previous close on genuinely positive news.
- *Qualify*: 50,000+ pre-market shares across multiple prints (one block doesn't count), a real gap confirmed on time-of-sales, spread ≤0.15.
- *First-day*: buy the first up-tick at the open if the diary shows a no-dip climb; otherwise wait for the first pullback bottom.
- *Second-day*: fade — short the first high — once momentum wanes; a stair-step pattern argues against shorting.
- *Exit*: sell roughly half at a diary-derived target, trail a stop on the rest. Never short a "runner" (fast pace, sub-1-point pullbacks, continuous new highs).

**2. Dumper pattern** — stock down 20%+ on moderately negative news (penny earnings miss, downgrade, lost contract). Avoid devastating news (fraud, SEC investigation, bankruptcy).
- *Strong pattern*: buy the first or second bottom expecting value buyers; take profits at the diary-implied target.
- *Weak pattern*: short the first run-up; don't chase the short more than 0.25 past the first top.
- *Stop discipline*: at most two stop-losses hunting the bottom, then stand aside for the session.

**3. End-of-day gap plays** — held overnight, higher risk.
- *Entry*: assess only in the final few minutes — momentum often reverses right before the close.
- *Gainer-for-the-gap*: majority buys in the last 5 minutes; closes near the day's high; diary shows gainers currently gapping up.
- *Dumper-for-the-gap*: 50/50 buy/sell split in the last 5 minutes; closes near the day's low; news not severely damaging.
- *Exit*: at or just prior to the next open — the recommended default for less experienced traders.

**4. Breakout play** — a new intraday high in a slow-momentum stock.
- *Setup*: stock reaches a prior high, pulls back, pauses, then buying returns.
- *Entry*: buy at or just above the prior high once pace increases.
- *Exit*: small predefined stop if the level fails; if it breaks, exit at least half within 0.25–0.50 point — breakouts often move only fractionally before pulling back.

## Risk

- **Size by dollar risk**: pick the stop distance (0.15–0.40) first, then size shares to an acceptable equity percentage — e.g., 1,000 shares at $50 with a 0.25 stop risks $250 (0.5% of $50,000); a smaller account should cut size.
- **Spread gate**: avoid any setup with a spread over 0.25.
- **Two-stop cap**: never take more than two stop-losses chasing one reversal.
- **News-severity filter**: never trade dumpers facing fraud, SEC investigation, or bankruptcy risk.
- **Runner-short prohibition**: never short a runner; if forced to, use a wide stop and reduced size.
- **Exit-when-invalid rule**: exit the instant the entry reason is no longer true, overriding any price target.

## Caveats

The numeric filters (50,000-share threshold, 20% gap, 0.15/0.25 spread gates) come from Wolff's pre-decimalization, SOES/SelectNet-era Nasdaq tracking around 2000–2002 and aren't validated against a modern, decimalized, algorithmic market — treat as illustrative starting points, not literal thresholds. All four setups assume a human reading Level 2 in real time; they predate the HFT and order-routing changes that altered how retail orders interact with these levels. The gap play carries acknowledged overnight risk the book says to trade only with dedicated risk capital.
