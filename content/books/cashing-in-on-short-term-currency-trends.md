## Summary

A short trading-strategy article stating that markets trend only about 30% of the time but that trends are worth chasing when they occur, especially in the "major" currency pairs (85%+ of forex volume). It presents a three-step mechanical method combining a 200-period moving average on two timeframes (10-minute and hourly) with a 14-bar slow stochastic to time entries, illustrated with two worked trade examples (EUR/USD and USD/JPY).

## Key points

- Trend confirmation requires price to sit consistently above (or below) the 200-period moving average on BOTH the 10-minute and hourly charts simultaneously.
- Entry trigger: price must be within 20 points of the moving average on the 10-minute chart AND the fast stochastic line must cross the slow stochastic line while below 20 (buy) or above 80 (sell).
- The stochastic %K formula: %K = 100×(Ct − Ln)/(Hn − Ln); %D is a 3-period moving average of %K; a "slow" stochastic smooths this further.
- Initial stop-loss: 10 points below the 200-period moving average (long) or 10 points above it (short).
- Once profitable, the stop trails behind each new swing high/low — the article's examples use a 25-point trailing distance.
- Worked EUR/USD example: long entry at .9883, initial stop at .9858, closed for an 84-point ($840) gain after trailing.
- Worked USD/JPY example: short entry at 119.57, stop at 119.86, closed for a 99-point gain after the stop trailed down through a decline.
- The method is presented as adaptable to other timeframe pairs and other liquid markets, not exclusively forex.

## Actionable rules

1. Only look for trades when price is consistently on one side of the 200-period moving average on both the 10-minute and hourly charts (defines the trend direction).
2. Enter long when price is within 20 points of the 10-minute moving average and the fast stochastic crosses above the slow stochastic while below the 20 level; mirror for shorts (cross below the slow line above 80).
3. Place the initial stop 10 points beyond the 200-period moving average on the 10-minute chart.
4. Trail the stop by roughly 25 points from each new swing extreme once the trade is in profit.
5. Avoid forcing trades outside the major currency pairs (EUR/USD, USD/JPY, GBP/USD, USD/CHF, CAD, AUD) where the approach was demonstrated.

## Caveats

Only two illustrative trades are shown; no multi-trade backtest, win rate, or drawdown statistics are given, so the edge is unproven at scale. The 20-point/25-point/10-point parameters are specific to the currency pairs and spreads of 2002 and may not transfer directly to modern spreads or other instruments without re-calibration.

## Who it is for

Beginner-to-intermediate forex traders who want a simple, fully mechanical multi-timeframe trend-and-pullback entry method built from two widely available indicators.
