## What it is

A "perfect order" is a configuration of simple moving averages (SMAs) stacked in strict sequential order by length, indicating a strong, aligned trend across multiple timeframes simultaneously. For an uptrend: 10-day SMA > 20-day SMA > 50-day SMA, with the 100-day SMA below the 50-day and the 200-day SMA below the 100-day. A downtrend is the mirror image, with the 200-day SMA highest and the 10-day SMA lowest. Because each moving average also acts as a layer of dynamic support (in an uptrend) or resistance (in a downtrend), a perfect order formation indicates not just directional momentum but multiple technical levels reinforcing the trend. The strategy is a trend-following, momentum-confirmation play meant to capture a trend near its beginning; perfect orders are described as infrequent, so setups are relatively rare.

## Rules

1. Identify a currency pair whose moving averages have just formed a sequential perfect order (10 > 20 > 50 > 100 > 200-day SMA for an uptrend, or the reverse for a downtrend).
2. Confirm with ADX pointing upward, ideally above 20.
3. Enter the trade five candles (days) after the initial formation of the perfect order, provided it still holds at that point.
4. Set the initial stop at the low of the day the crossover initially formed (for longs) or the high of that day (for shorts).
5. Exit the position when the perfect order no longer holds — i.e., when the moving averages lose their sequential order (commonly signaled first by the shortest-term pair, e.g., the 10-day SMA crossing back through the 20-day SMA).

Worked examples (EUR/USD, USD/CHF, USD/CAD, all daily charts, 2004–2005) show holding periods measured in months and gains in the 400–550 pip range against initial risk of roughly 100–150 pips — implied reward-to-risk ratios in the 3:1 to 4.5:1 range in those specific cases.

## Risk

Because the stop is placed at the extreme of the crossover-formation day (rather than a fixed pip amount) and the position is held until the trend structure itself breaks (rather than a fixed target), both risk and holding period vary considerably by trade — this is a swing/position-style trend trade rather than a fast scalp, despite appearing in a day-trading book. The five-candle entry delay is a deliberate trade-off: it avoids entering on noise immediately at the crossover, but it also means the trader enters after some of the initial move has already occurred and gives back a defined amount of edge for confirmation.

## Caveats

The three worked examples span a bull market period for the specific currencies shown; because perfect orders are explicitly described as infrequent, historical frequency and hit-rate data across more pairs or periods are not provided. The exit rule ("when the perfect order no longer holds") is somewhat ambiguous in application — the text uses the shorter moving averages crossing as the practical trigger, but does not specify precisely which of the five moving-average relationships must break first to count as invalidation. As with other strategies in the book, results are hand-picked illustrations, not a systematic, cost-inclusive backtest.
