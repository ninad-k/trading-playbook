## Summary

Titled on its own cover page "THE GUIDE FOR BEGINNER TECHNICAL ANALYSIS (English Edition)" and branded to the "IQ Trader" handle, this short beginner text explains candlestick anatomy and market sentiment, then walks through 13 candlestick patterns and 14 chart patterns in narrative paragraphs (not the boxed formation/entry/stop format used in this batch's other IQ Trader titles), followed by four indicators (Moving Average, MACD, RSI, ADX) and a closing trading-discipline note. The explicit "(English Edition)" subtitle, together with occasional slightly non-native phrasing ("This is called a kicker signal, also known as a professional gap," "don't get fooled by whom"), makes this read as an English rendering of material originally prepared in another language rather than a text composed natively in English — treat it as a translated/adapted edition rather than an original English work.

## Key points

- **Candlestick catalogue (13 patterns)** — Hammer, Inverted Hammer, Bullish Engulfing, Piercing Signal, Bullish Harami, Morning Star, Bullish Kicker, Hanging Man, Shooting Star, Bearish Engulfing, Dark Cloud Cover, Bearish Harami, Evening Star — each explained narratively (what the pattern means and why) rather than boxed.
- **Chart pattern catalogue (14 patterns)** — Double/Triple Top/Bottom, Ascending/Descending/Symmetrical Triangle, Pennant, Wedge (rising/falling), Flag, Head and Shoulders, Inverse Head and Shoulders, Cup With Handle, Inverted Cup and Handle.
- **Timeframe-specific guidance** — a few patterns get explicit timeframe suggestions not seen in this batch's other titles: Morning Star is suggested on a 10-minute chart for intraday trading or a 1-day chart for swing trading; Shooting Star is suggested on 5/10/15-minute charts intraday or daily/weekly charts for positional trading.
- **Indicator toolkit (4 indicators)** — Moving Average (7/14/21-day for intraday, 21-50 for swing, 50-100 for positional, 100-200 for investing), MACD (12/26 EMA, 9-EMA signal line), RSI (30/70), and ADX with the most concrete rule in the book: ADX below 20 = weak trend, 20-25 = no clear signal, above 25 = strong trend; a buy signal requires ADX above 25 with +DI crossing above −DI (mirrored for a sell), with the stop-loss set at the low (buy) or high (sell) of the signal candle and a standard 14-day ADX look-back period.
- **A "never invest more than 10% of your savings" caution** in the book's own introduction, alongside its standard "learn first, trade with your own research" disclaimer.

## Actionable rules

1. **Bullish Engulfing follow-through**: if a gap-up candle follows a bullish engulfing pattern, treat it as a buy signal immediately; otherwise wait for price to exceed the engulfing pattern's prior candle high before buying, with the stop below that same prior candle's low.
2. **Kicker signal definition**: a gap where the next candle's open does not match the prior candle's close in the direction opposite the prevailing trend is read as a kicker — a bullish kicker after a decline (price drops, then gaps up the next day) or a bearish kicker after a rally (price rises, then gaps down), read as a sign that "professional investors" are rapidly repositioning.
3. **Dark Cloud Cover / Bearish Harami confirmation**: after a green (bullish) candle in an uptrend, a following red candle whose open is above the first candle's high but whose close falls roughly 50% or more into the first candle's body confirms the pattern and signals a short-selling opportunity.
4. **ADX trend-strength filter**: do not treat a moving-average or price-action signal as high-confidence unless ADX is above 25 and confirmed by a +DI/−DI crossover in the trade's direction; use the crossover candle's low/high as the stop.
5. **Moving-average period selection by style**: match the moving-average length to trading style — roughly 7-21 days for intraday, 21-50 for swing, 50-100 for positional, 100-200 for long-term investing — rather than using one fixed period across all timeframes.

## Caveats

Most patterns in this book are described only qualitatively ("the market may change its trend from here") without the entry/stop/target specificity found in some of this batch's sibling titles — the ADX section is a clear exception with an actual numeric rule and look-back period, but it is the only section that reaches that level of precision. No pattern or indicator claim in the book is backed by a stated win rate, sample, or backtest. The "(English Edition)" framing and occasional awkward phrasing support treating this as a translated or adapted edition of source material rather than a text originally composed in English, which is worth bearing in mind when the prose reads unusually or repeats itself (as in the near-duplicated Hammer description in its own opening section). As with this batch's other IQ Trader titles, embedded social-media handles throughout mark it as a lead-generation product rather than an edited, professionally reviewed trading text.

## Who it is for

A true beginner to intraday trading who wants prose explanations of common candlestick and chart patterns rather than a boxed reference, plus one genuinely specific indicator rule (ADX with a defined threshold, crossover and stop-loss) worth extracting even if the rest of the book stays qualitative. Not a source for backtested or quantitatively validated intraday setups.
