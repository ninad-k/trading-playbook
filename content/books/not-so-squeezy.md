## Summary

Frames trading as reading the market's "mood" along three axes — trend (trending vs. ranging), acceleration (momentum/excitement), and volatility (the Bollinger Squeeze) — using a stack of indicators on one chart. The core timing tool is the Bollinger Squeeze (Bollinger Bands moving inside Keltner Channels), which flags an impending big move but not its direction; direction and confirmation come from a rainbow of moving averages and two further Bollinger Band overlays.

## Key points

- Squeeze setup: Bollinger Bands (20, 2 std dev) plus Keltner Channels (20-period, 1.5 multiplier); bands moving inside the channels signals compressed volatility and a likely large move soon.
- Rainbow Multiple Moving Average (RMMA): 88 EMAs plotted together (à la Daryl Guppy's GMMA) — slope shows direction, spread shows strength, compression warns of a weakening trend.
- 100- and 200-period SMAs (on close) serve as dynamic support/resistance and stop references, chosen because they are widely watched by other participants.
- A second Bollinger Band overlay (20, 2, close) flags overbought/oversold extremes, trusted only in the direction of the RMMA trend (e.g., only oversold signals in an uptrend).
- Two entry triggers suggested: a 50-period CCI zero-line cross, or specific candlestick formations (bullish engulfing, three-inside-up, hammer) at the relevant Bollinger Band.
- Exit rule: close at least half the position at the opposite Bollinger Band; optionally let the rest run to the 161.8% Fibonacci extension of the prior swing.
- Stops should reflect where the trade thesis is proven wrong — commonly behind the 100/200 SMA or the 78.2% retracement — rather than immediately behind the last swing high/low, which the author argues invites stop-hunting.

## Actionable rules

1. Treat a Bollinger Squeeze (bands inside Keltner Channels) as a volatility-expansion warning only, not a direction signal.
2. Use the 88-EMA RMMA to establish trend direction/strength before acting on any squeeze or oscillator signal.
3. In an established trend, only take oversold Bollinger touches as longs in uptrends, overbought touches as shorts in downtrends.
4. Trigger entries with a 50-period CCI zero-line cross or a reversal candlestick at the relevant Bollinger Band.
5. Exit half at the opposite Bollinger Band; let the remainder run to the 161.8% extension if acceleration was high.
6. Place stops behind the 100/200 SMA or the 78.2% retracement, not immediately behind the prior swing high/low.

## Caveats

Self-published, informal retail forex manual with no backtested statistics; the author explicitly states money management and trade management are not covered here. The system stacks many indicators (Bollinger Bands twice, Keltner Channels, 88 EMAs, two SMAs, CCI or candlesticks) with no quantified edge for the combination as a whole.

## Who it is for

Retail forex/CFD traders comfortable with visually dense, multi-indicator charts who want a framework for timing entries around volatility contraction.
