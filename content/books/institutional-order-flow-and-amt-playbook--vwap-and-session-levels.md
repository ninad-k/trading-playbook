## What it is

VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price) is the running average price of a session, weighted by volume at each price: VWAP = Σ(price × volume) ÷ Σ(volume), computed bar-by-bar from a session anchor (or, as an **Anchored VWAP**, from any trader-chosen pivot — a swing high/low, a news release, the start of a week). Institutions benchmark to it because it is the industry-standard execution-quality scoreboard: beating VWAP on a buy order is, by definition, a better-than-average fill. VWAP's *slope* tells you the net direction volume-weighted flow has moved in (rising = buyers have been willing to pay up); price's *position relative to* VWAP separately tells you who is "winning" right now (above VWAP = buyers currently paying a premium to be involved). **Standard-deviation bands** (±1σ, ±2σ, ±3σ, using the volume-weighted variance Σ(V×(P−VWAP)²)/ΣV) quantify how statistically stretched price is from that volume-weighted center, adaptively for each session's own volatility. Neither VWAP nor a single price level means anything read alone — the book pairs VWAP with a full **structural reference-level stack** (PDH/PDL/PDC, weekly/monthly extremes, overnight range, settlement, prior Value Area, Initial Balance) on the premise that the market has "memory": levels where large capital was previously positioned attract resting liquidity and stop orders on repeat visits, because algorithmic benchmarking and human habit both re-anchor to the same visible coordinates every session.

## Rules

1. **Classify the regime before touching a σ band.** In a *balance* regime (flat VWAP slope, stable/contracting σ), fade a rejected touch of ±2σ (confirmed by a footprint absorption signature) back toward VWAP or the opposite band. In a *trend* regime (rising/falling slope, σ expanding session-over-session), do not fade ±2σ/±3σ — instead treat a ±1σ touch as a with-trend pullback entry, since in a trend the bands reflect genuine, sustained, expanding one-sided participation rather than statistical over-extension.
2. **Combine Value-Area location with VWAP location into one 3×3 grid.** Premium (above prior VAH) crossed with below-session-VWAP is the single richest short context; discount (below prior VAL) crossed with above-session-VWAP is the richest long context — because the higher-timeframe read (cheap/expensive to yesterday) and today's own intraday flow (buyers/sellers already back in control) are agreeing rather than conflicting.
3. **Rank structural levels by a fixed priority hierarchy** (highest to lowest): Previous Month High/Low, Previous Week High/Low, Settlement/PDC, Previous Day High/Low, prior session VAH/VAL/POC, Overnight High/Low, Initial Balance High/Low. On a first test, expect a level to be defended in proportion to its tier; a break-and-hold-then-retest that holds with declining opposing delta is a continuation signal, while a wick through a level that closes back on the original side ("sweep-and-reclaim") is a trapped-late-breakout reversal signal.
4. **Score levels by confluence, not by tier alone.** Build the level stack pre-market, set a fixed tick tolerance (the book's example: 6 ticks on a 0.25-tick instrument), and count how many independent levels cluster inside that band — a 3-way cluster (e.g., PDH + Overnight High + prior VAH all within tolerance) outranks any single isolated Priority-1 level and should receive both more size and a wider stop (sized to the width of the whole cluster, not one level's usual noise buffer).
5. **Use the Opening Range (5, 15 or 30 minutes) as an early regime tell.** A close beyond the OR on expanding, same-direction volume/delta is a breakout (join on the first shallow pullback that holds the OR boundary); a push beyond the OR that fails to attract follow-through and rotates back inside within a few bars is an OR failure/stop-run (enter the reversal once price closes back inside with opposing delta) — always read the OR's location against yesterday's Value Area (into premium, inside prior value, or a full outside gap) rather than in isolation.
6. **Classify gaps before trading a "fill."** A gap into prior value (open beyond the prior close but still inside yesterday's Value Area or range) fills at a materially higher rate than a full gap (open entirely outside the prior range, with no volume yet transacted at the new price) — and even then, only fade the fill once the Opening Range shows an explicit failure footprint against the gap's direction; a full gap whose OR extends with expanding same-direction delta should be traded as acceptance of new value, not faded.
7. **Weight order-flow signals by the 24-hour session clock.** Volume traces a "U-shape" — peaks at the New York open and the close/settlement, a trough through the U.S. midday and Asia session; the same-looking footprint or imbalance signal carries materially less weight in the low-volume trough than at a volume peak. The recurring "London-sweep / New York-reversal" pattern — London builds the session high/low on real directional flow, then New York flow fails to confirm and reverses through it — is traded by waiting for the first 15-30 minutes of the New York open to show opposing delta before entering back through the swept level.

## Risk

VWAP-based entries are stopped a defined number of ticks (or one prior swing) beyond VWAP itself, invalidated if price closes and holds on the other side of it; band-touch fades are stopped just beyond the next band outward (±3σ if fading ±2σ), while trend-regime pullback entries are stopped beyond the prior swing low/high rather than beyond a band. Confluence-zone trades size their stop to the width of the whole tolerance band rather than a single level's typical wick allowance, on the premise that a multi-source cluster gets defended harder and chopped through more slowly. Premium/discount entries are stopped beyond the swing that defined the "flow already reversed" read (the swing low inside a discount zone, or the swing high inside a premium zone) — a break of that swing means the reversal-of-urgency thesis, not just the price level, has failed. Throughout, targets step from one structural level or band to the next in the trade's direction rather than being picked as a fixed point count, and every rule explicitly refuses to fade a level that is breaking with expanding volume and delta rather than stalling.

## Caveats

The VWAP-vs-POC distinction is worth holding onto precisely because the book warns about it directly: VWAP is a volume-weighted *average* (a single computed statistic) while POC is the volume-weighted *mode* (the single busiest row) — they often sit near each other but are different calculations and can diverge sharply in a skewed, one-sided session. The specific probability language used for level behavior ("historically defended," "a large majority," the London-sweep pattern's frequency) is asserted without a cited sample or backtest, consistent with the rest of the source — these are heuristics inherited from standard order-flow-desk practice, not statistics validated in this text. The full 24-hour session-clock framework (Asia/London/New York volume shape, the settlement window) is built around U.S. index futures trading nearly continuously; it needs real adaptation, not a literal read-across, for instruments with different listed hours or for equities, which the book's own instrument-specifics material (outside this sub-page's scope) partially addresses.
