## Summary

A short opinion essay, written by a forex system vendor, arguing that the flood of short forex "education" products (weekend seminars, one-week courses, DVD trainings) cannot substitute for real trading skill. The author's central claim is that no meaningful trading competence can be built in days or weeks — professionals in other fields spend years in training — and that marketers exploit traders' desire for easy money by selling shortcuts. The piece closes by recommending one-on-one mentorship, where an instructor can correct a student's support/resistance and trend-line judgment in real time, as the only training format that actually works, and promotes the author's own coaching/signal service.

## Key points

- Doctors and lawyers train for years; forex traders are often expected (by marketing) to become competent in a single week-long course.
- Many forex "education" products are one to two days long, or offered free specifically to upsell other products.
- The demand for "Currency Trading for Dummies"-style shortcuts is attributed to human greed and the appeal of easy-money promises.
- DVD-based or classroom (40+ student) training is compared to a medical student watching a heart transplant on video and then being expected to perform surgery — passive viewing does not transfer skill.
- One-on-one training is claimed to be the only format allowing individualized correction of chart-reading mistakes (support/resistance, trend lines) and pacing suited to the student.
- Most educators avoid one-on-one formats because group classes and DVDs are more profitable to produce and sell.

## Actionable rules

None given — this is an opinion/marketing essay about the forex education industry, not a trading method. The only practical takeaway is a caution: treat short paid forex courses skeptically and prioritize mentorship or hands-on practice (e.g., a demo account with feedback) over passive video/classroom training.

## Caveats

Extremely short (2 pages) and written by a vendor to promote his own one-on-one coaching and automated signal service (ctsforex.com); no trading rules, numbers, or methodology are given. The argument is entirely anecdotal/persuasive, not backed by data, and should be read as marketing commentary rather than an educational resource in itself.

## Who it is for

Beginners who are shopping for a forex course and want a (self-interested but not unreasonable) warning against expecting fast results from short, mass-produced training products.
