## Summary

This is an anthology, not a single-author book: eleven short articles from Futures Magazine's website, each by a named professional. Highlights: Joe Duffy's "3x5ATR" support/resistance indicator; George Pruitt (Futures Truth) on which exit type suits which S&P system; Frank Alfonso's five rules for vetting a day-trading system; Tom DeMark on applying TD Sequential intraday; Mitchell Holland's T-bond "bracket" event trade; Jon Najarian's options market-maker pre-market routine; Holter/Darby/Greenberg on what makes a market day-tradable; Mark Etzkorn on gap behavior, Market Profile, and pivot formulas; Darrell Jobman's 1997 data/software vendor survey; Mark D. Cook's and Jake Bernstein's personal rules and style taxonomy; Chris McGinnis on the broker/clearing "team."

## Key points

- **3x5ATR (Duffy)**: resistance = 3-day SMA of lows + 5-day ATR; support = 3-day SMA of highs − 5-day ATR; used only after MACD and Slow Stochastic agree on direction.
- **Exit selection depends on trade frequency (Pruitt)**: high-frequency S&P systems do best with tight ($300-750) protective stops; low-frequency systems need larger stops and targets; pure fixed profit targets tend to underperform a trailing stop.
- **Five rules for a valid system (Alfonso)**: 5-10+ years of test data, robust (not fragile) variable ranges, 5-6 rule limit, out-of-sample testing, and walk-forward validation.
- **TD Sequential (DeMark)**: setup (9 consecutive closes vs. the close 4 bars earlier), intersection, and countdown (13 closes vs. the low/high 2 bars earlier) — works intraday as well as daily.
- **T-bond bracket trade (Holland)**: place a buy and sell order on either side of the prior close before a scheduled news event during a volatility contraction; the filled side becomes the position, the other the stop.
- **Pivot formula (Etzkorn)**: P=(H+L+C)/3, R1=2P−L, S1=2P−H, R2=(P−S1)+R1, S2=P−(R1−S1); the first penetration of a level is most significant.
- **Four day-trading styles (Bernstein)**: trend/breakout, support-resistance, daily seasonal (pre-holiday bias), and daily sentiment (contrarian at 90%/10% extremes).
- **Cook's daily rules**: no new S&P trades in the last hour, exit any trade you "don't like," and trust predefined signals fully.

## Actionable rules

1. Combine MACD/Slow Stochastic agreement with 3x5ATR levels before a dip-buy or rally-sell (Duffy).
2. Use tight ($300-750) stops on high-frequency systems, wider stops with targets on low-frequency ones (Pruitt).
3. Require 5+ years of data, a limited rule count, and walk-forward validation before trading any system (Alfonso).
4. Enter on TD Sequential countdown-13 completions for low-risk zones (DeMark).
5. Place bracket orders around the prior close before scheduled news during a volatility squeeze; cap risk at a small fixed tick amount (Holland).
6. Do not day-trade the last hour of the S&P session (Cook).

## Caveats

Published in 1997/2000 on Futures Magazine's site; software, data-vendor pricing, and commission figures (e.g., specific vendor monthly fees) are entirely obsolete. Several contributors' claimed track records (Cook's 563% year, Raschke's later article referenced within) are self-reported and not independently audited. The anthology format means depth per idea is shallow — each article is a magazine feature, not a full manual.

## Who it is for

Futures/index day-traders who want a quick survey of period-appropriate professional techniques (TD Sequential, pivots, Market Profile, event-based bracket trades) and system-testing discipline, understanding this is a historical snapshot rather than a current playbook.
