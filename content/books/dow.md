## Summary

The Dow TS4 guide says its authors analyzed daily Dow data back to 1930, comparing percentage changes with weekday, date, month, working-day counts, and related calendar variables (p. 3). It claims a recurring monthly movement pattern linked to institutional investment schedules, salary flows, quarterly activity, and year-end or election adjustments. The guide reports more than 4,151 Dow points over 2001-2004 and says low trade frequency reduces spread drag (p. 4). Its contents include system criteria, stop loss and stop profit, staking, fixed-odds betting, warnings, and an appendix of past results (p. 2), but the assigned extract does not establish a reproducible statistical test or full parameter table.

## Key points

- The claimed edge is calendar based and tied to monthly institutional flows (p. 3).
- The system is described as rigid to reduce discretionary psychological errors (p. 4).
- Fewer trades are presented as beneficial when bid-offer costs are material (p. 4).
- The guide explicitly says the Dow is not totally predictable and not every position wins (p. 3).
- Reported historical points are author claims, not independently verified results.
- The system buys at the close before the first working day of a month and closes at that first trading day’s close; August is excluded in the stated criteria (pp. 11-12, 18-19).
- The source specifies a 150-point stop below the opening level for each monthly position and advises entering the stop at or shortly after entry (pp. 14-15).
- Its illustrative staking method starts at a £1 minimum, increases the next stake by £1 after a loss, and resets to the minimum after a win; the author says January and February movements are less predictable (pp. 16-17).
- For fixed-odds bets, the source says risk is limited to the initial stake and reports 65% historical accuracy for winning Up bets, while noting that an exact expiry-level close loses the bet (pp. 20-22).

## Actionable rules

1. Treat calendar effects as hypotheses to retest on current data, including spread, slippage, and structural breaks.
2. Read the system-criteria, stop, and staking sections together before attempting reproduction; the source’s stated monthly schedule, 150-point stop, and illustrative stake progression appear on pp. 11-18.
3. Do not infer current fixed-odds or spread-betting suitability from a 2005 guide.

## Caveats

The source is promotional and its central performance claim is historical. The cash-index research, futures implementation, spread costs, guaranteed-stop premiums, and fixed-odds payout mechanics are distinct; futures can diverge from cash and the guide itself notes funding and dividend effects (pp. 12-13). Calendar regularities can decay when institutional behavior or market structure changes, and the 65% claim is not independently tested in the extract (pp. 20-23).

## Who it is for

Researchers testing seasonality claims in index markets, with careful skepticism about historical marketing results.
