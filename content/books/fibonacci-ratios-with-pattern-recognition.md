## Overview

Larry Pesavento, a former Drexel Burnham broker and CTA who traded through Commodity Corporation and later ran Astrocycles Newsletter, presents ten specific chart patterns that he argues underlie most classical technical formations (double tops/bottoms, head and shoulders, triangles): each is a swing sequence labeled X-A-B-(C-D) whose legs relate to each other by Fibonacci ratios and their square roots (0.618, 0.786, 1.272, 1.618) rather than by shape alone. The centerpiece is the "Gartley 222" pattern, taken directly from H.M. Gartley's 1935 course *Profits in the Stock Market*, plus Pesavento's own "Butterfly" pattern (co-discovered with Bryce Gilmore). The book also covers "harmonic" or "vibratory" price numbers specific to individual futures markets, the statistical tendency of the daily opening price to sit near the day's high or low, and a short section of practical entry techniques. Pesavento states explicitly that this is "not a trading system" on its own but a set of ratio-based pattern tools requiring discretionary judgment and separate risk control.

## Core thesis

Markets are not random; recurring Fibonacci-ratio relationships between consecutive price swings (and, secondarily, between the number of time bars in those swings) repeat with enough regularity to define tradable, risk-quantifiable patterns, independent of and simpler than full Elliott Wave labeling. Because the ratios pin down where a swing should terminate, a trader does not need to correctly predict future direction — only to recognize when a pattern is completing and define the maximum acceptable loss if the ratio-based projection is wrong. Symmetry (both in price and in time bars) is the visual/quantitative tell that a real pattern, rather than noise, is present.

## Key concepts

- **X-A-B-C-D notation** — the labeling convention for all ten patterns: X is the pattern's origin, A the first reactive extreme, B a retracement of X-A, C-D the completion leg (some patterns use only X-A-B).
- **AB=CD pattern** — the core building block inside several patterns: the C-D leg equals the A-B leg in price about 60% of the time, or extends to 1.27 or 1.618 times AB the other 40%.
- **Gartley "222" pattern** — named for the page in Gartley's book; a continuation-after-correction pattern where D completes at the 0.618 or 0.786 retracement of X-A roughly 75% of the time.
- **Butterfly pattern** — Pesavento and Bryce Gilmore's pattern found only at major tops/bottoms, built from two connected triangles with an internal AB=CD leg extended to 1.27 or 1.618 of XA; correct "more than 80% of the time" per the author.
- **Three Drives pattern** — a rare, highly symmetrical three-push exhaustion pattern (also called "Three Little Indians" by Linda Bradford Raschke) where each drive extends 1.27 or 1.618 beyond the prior one.
- **Harmonic/vibratory number** — a market-specific, roughly fixed price increment (e.g., 20 ticks in T-Bonds, 27/54 points in the Swiss franc) that recurring swings and their multiples tend to respect; found by frequency-distribution analysis of historical swing sizes.
- **Opening Price Principle** — the daily open sits within about 10% of that day's high or low roughly 85–90% of the time; trading in the direction the open is meandering is framed as a standalone statistical edge.
- **Key of the Day** — (Open + High + Low) / 3; only take longs above it, only shorts below it.
- **Shapiro Iteration ("Five Minute Rule")** — a discipline rule: wait one full bar period after deciding to enter before actually placing the trade, to filter out emotion-driven, chase-the-market entries.

## Rules and setups

The ten primary patterns, with the ratios that define each (all figures from the text; "X→A" etc. denote the swing being measured):

| # | Pattern | Defining ratios |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Trend-line/channel base pattern | Foundational parallel-channel pattern from Gartley p.249; not independently ratio-defined |
| 3–4 | **Gartley "222"** (bull/bear) | D = 0.618 or 0.786 of X-A (75% of cases; else 0.382/0.500/0.707); AB=CD present in A-D; BC = 0.618 or 0.786 of AB (0.382/0.500 in strong trends); time from X-A : A-D also in Fibonacci proportion; if X is exceeded, expect continuation to 1.27 or 1.618 of X-A |
| 5–6 | Reaction pattern | X-A spans 5–13 (rarely 21) time bars; retracement to A at 0.382, 0.500, 0.618, 0.707, 0.786, or 1.00 (double top/bottom); move protective stop to point A once price has moved 61.8% in the trade's favor; minimum objective = distance from A to the prior swing point |
| 7–8 | Extension pattern | X-A spans 5–13 (rarely 21) bars; no swings between points 1 and A; stop placed at the 1.27 extension of X-1; if price reaches 1.27 within 5 bars, expect extension to 1.618; profit objective = distance between points 1 and A; move stop to breakeven once price reacts in the trade's favor |
| 9–10 | **Three Drives** (top/bottom) | Points 2 and 3 are 1.27 or 1.618 price expansions of the A and C swings; A and C legs retrace 0.618 or 0.786 of the prior swing (0.382 in a vertical/blow-off move); time frames A-to-2 and C-to-3 should be symmetrical |
| Bonus | **Butterfly** | AB=CD present in the extension leg; extension = 1.27 or 1.618 of X-A (anything beyond 1.618 negates the pattern and signals a strong continuation instead); internal retracements typically 0.618/0.786; found only at significant tops/bottoms; correct >80% of the time per the author |

Additional standalone rules:
1. Buy a 0.618 retracement from the day's opening price with a protective stop at the 0.786 retracement (a day-trading setup independent of the ten swing patterns).
2. Only enter the 38.2% retracement level of a pattern if the initiating X-A swing showed 3–5× normal-range thrust bars.
3. Entry techniques, in the author's stated order of preference: the Shapiro Iteration (wait one bar before executing), limit orders placed a few ticks beyond the exact ratio price with a fixed dollar stop (his example: ~$600), candlestick confirmation (doji, hammer, shooting star, tweezers) at the ratio price, and a Volatility Stop (`Range = (Range × (N−1) + High − Low) / N`; stop = highest close − Range × C for longs, lowest close + Range × C for shorts; constant C between 2.5 and 4.0).

## Risk and money management

The one explicit portfolio-level rule given is that stop protection should be no more than 3% of total trading equity per trade. Beyond that, risk is defined pattern-by-pattern via the specific price levels above (e.g., stop at the pattern's X point, at a 0.786 retracement, or at a fixed dollar amount such as $600 on the Volatility Stop / limit-order technique) rather than through a single formula. Several patterns include an explicit rule to move the stop to breakeven or to point A once the trade has moved partway in its favor, converting the position to a "risk-free" trade. The author repeatedly frames the core skill as knowing the maximum loss in advance, not predicting direction: "it is not necessary to know what is going to happen next... what is necessary is to determine how much risk and profit potential is available."

## Psychology and discipline

Pesavento's central discipline tool is the Shapiro Iteration — forcing a mandatory wait of one bar period between the decision to trade and the actual order, specifically to filter out FOMO-driven entries made after price has already started moving. He frames pattern failure as informative rather than a personal loss: when the "222" or Three Drives pattern fails (price exceeds point X or a drive extreme), it signals a stronger continuation move is underway, and the trader's job is to recognize the failure and act on it rather than fight it. He is explicit that the material is "not a trading system" that can be followed mechanically without judgment and risk control, and repeatedly credits a quote from Amos Hostetler: "take care of your losses and the profits will take care of themselves."

## Strengths and caveats

The ratio definitions for each pattern are stated with unusual numeric precision (specific percentages of cases, specific stop levels) for a discretionary pattern-recognition book, and the Gartley "222" and Butterfly definitions here are the commonly cited source for the harmonic-pattern trading later popularized under names like the "Gartley pattern." Caveats: several of the book's claimed hit rates (75% for Gartley 222, "more than 80%" for the Butterfly, 85–90% for the Opening Price Principle) are stated without disclosed sample size, methodology, or out-of-sample testing, so they should be treated as the author's experience-based estimates rather than validated statistics. The market-specific harmonic numbers were derived from 1970s–1980s futures data (pre-decimalization, different volatility regimes) and would need independent re-verification before use today. The astrology/planetary-cycle material in the early chapters is presented as background color and explicitly said to be "not necessary for profitable trading," but takes up a notable share of the book's front matter. The author is candid that this is not a complete, standalone trading system — position sizing beyond the single 3%-of-equity rule and broader trade-management rules are largely left to the reader.

## Who should read it

Swing and day traders already familiar with basic Fibonacci retracement who want the specific XABCD ratio definitions behind Gartley- and butterfly-style harmonic patterns, told directly by one of the people who popularized them. Less suited to readers wanting a fully mechanical, backtested system, or readers uninterested in wading through astro-cycle background material to get to the pattern rules.
