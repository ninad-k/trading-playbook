## Summary

A self-published mechanical trading manual presenting the "EZ Trade FOREX" day-trading system, mainly used on the 60-minute chart across EUR/USD, USD/CHF, GBP/USD, AUD/USD, and (cautiously) USD/JPY. The system stacks four indicators — a Detrended Price Oscillator (DPO) as the lead signal, MACD and the Directional Movement Index (DMI) as confirming filters, and a 3-period/15-period simple moving average crossover as a further filter — into a single trend-following entry rule, then walks through roughly a dozen annotated real trade examples (wins and losses) plus a FAQ.

## Key points

- **DPO (Detrended Price Oscillator)** — the lead indicator; used two ways: overbought/oversold reversal levels in ranging markets, and the primary buy/sell trigger when it crosses its "zero line."
- **MACD (12/26 EMA, 9 EMA signal)** — a confirming filter; only take DPO buy signals when the MACD line is above its signal line, and DPO sell signals when MACD is below signal.
- **DMI (+DI/–DI)** — Wilder's Directional Movement Index; ideally +DI/–DI cross at or near the same candle as the DPO cross, but a trade can still be taken if the two DI lines are close together even without a fresh cross.
- **3/15 moving average cross ("the 3/15")** — a 3-period and 15-period simple MA crossover used as the final filter; must cross within 3 candles (before or after) of the DPO's zero-line cross to validate the signal.
- **Trailing stop rule** — stop is placed and continually trailed 10 pips below (long) or 15 pips above (short) the 15 MA, or at a flat 30-pip cap (35–40 pips on USD/CHF), whichever is closer.
- **Breakeven rule** — once a trade is up 50 pips, the stop is moved to breakeven (entry price) and left there for the duration of the trade.
- **Exit rule** — close the trade when the 3 MA crosses back through the 15 MA against the position.
- Advanced confirming tools layered on top of the core system: trendlines, Fibonacci retracements (38.2/50/61.8), and blank candlestick charts for spotting chart patterns (head-and-shoulders, flags, triangles).
- The author states the system trades ~4 times per week per pair, expects roughly 30–40% losing trades, and cautions against USD/JPY unless the pair is clearly trending.

## Actionable rules

1. Buy when: DPO crosses above its zero line, MACD line is above its signal line, +DI is above (or just crossing above) –DI, and the 3 MA crosses above the 15 MA within 3 candles of the DPO cross (mirror rules for sell).
2. Trade primarily on the 60-minute chart; most active/valid signals occur between roughly 8 PM and 12 PM EST (London/early US hours); avoid the 12 PM–8 PM EST window.
3. Initial stop: 10 pips below the 15 MA (long) or 15 pips above the 15 MA (short), capped at a maximum 30-pip stop (35–40 pips for USD/CHF).
4. Move stop to breakeven once the trade is up 50 pips.
5. Exit when the 3 MA crosses back through the 15 MA against the position.
6. Reduce position size (rather than skip the trade) when a poor entry price forces a wider-than-normal stop, e.g., around news releases.
7. On USD/JPY, only take signals confirmed by every indicator simultaneously and only when price is already visibly moving in the signal's direction.

## Caveats

Self-published retail forex manual with no independent verification, formal backtest, or published win-rate/expectancy statistics — all evidence is a curated set of the author's own annotated trade screenshots, which is subject to selection bias. Written in 2003 on then-current spreads and a 60-minute-chart 2003–2004 dataset; pip-based stop sizes (30–40 pips) may not translate directly to modern spreads, execution costs, or currency pairs beyond the five discussed.

## Who it is for

Beginner-to-intermediate forex traders who want a fully mechanical, indicator-stack day-trading system with explicit entry, stop, and exit rules rather than discretionary chart reading.
