## Summary

A Stocks & Commodities magazine article (V.10:12, 1992) by Tim Ord, a follow-up to his earlier NYSE TICK-index buy/sell method. It defines four rules for reading the intraday and closing TICK index (NYSE upticking stocks minus downticking stocks) to identify market turns and confirm trend continuations, then walks through two real examples on the June 1992 S&P futures contract (a December 19, 1991 low and an April 8, 1992 low). A short sidebar also explains basic candlestick chart construction.

## Key points

- **TICK definition** — the difference between the number of NYSE issues trading on an uptick and those trading on a downtick at a given moment (e.g., 500 up − 250 down = 250 TICK reading).
- **Base buy/sell method (from an earlier article)** — a buy signal occurs when two intraday 600+ downtick readings appear at roughly the same DJIA price level, 1-10 days apart (double bottom); the mirror pattern with 600+ upticks signals a sell.
- **Rule 1 (continuation)** — a high intraday TICK reading (>600 up or down) plus a close near the day's high/low implies the move will likely continue.
- **Rule 2 (narrowing range)** — a narrow open-to-close range combined with an intraday TICK reading >600 signals an approaching turn.
- **Rule 3 (extreme closing TICK)** — a closing TICK reading exceeding 600 signals an approaching turn.
- **Rule 4 (breakaway confirmation)** — at breakaways from tops/bottoms, TICK usually posts intraday readings above 500, confirming the new trend direction.
- **Worked example** — on Dec 19, 1991, a 670 downtick reading triggered a buy per Rule 2; the following days saw a conflict between Rules 2 and 4 (high upticks with both a narrowing range and a breakout), which the author treats as an important inflection point requiring tightened stops.
- **Reliability caveat from the author's own example** — in the April 1992 case, the tick method identified the low but failed to signal the eventual top; a trailing stop (hit for a gain) is what actually closed the trade.

## Actionable rules

1. Buy signal: two intraday TICK readings of 600+ downticks at a similar DJIA price level, 1-10 days apart (double bottom); mirror for sell signals with 600+ upticks (double top).
2. Tighten stops whenever Rule 2 (narrow range + high TICK) and Rule 4 (breakaway high TICK) appear to conflict on the same day — the author calls this an important inflection point.
3. Always carry a stop order on TICK-based positions; the method can identify entries but, per the author's own example, does not reliably flag exits.
4. Treat intraday TICK >500 at a breakaway from a top/bottom as trend-direction confirmation (Rule 4).

## Caveats

Based on only two illustrative examples (Dec 1991 and Apr 1992) from one instrument (S&P futures) — no systematic backtest or win-rate statistics are presented. The author's own second example shows the method missing the actual top, underscoring that TICK signals need independent confirmation and firm stop discipline. TICK dynamics reflect early-1990s NYSE market structure and breadth composition, which has changed substantially since (ETF/algorithmic flow effects on TICK were not a factor in 1992).

## Who it is for

Futures/index day traders already using intraday breadth indicators (TICK, TICKI) who want a specific, rule-based framework for reading extreme readings rather than eyeballing the indicator.
