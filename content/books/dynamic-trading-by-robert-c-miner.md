## Overview

Dynamic Trading is Robert Miner's comprehensive manual for combining three independent forms of technical analysis: Elliott Wave pattern recognition, Fibonacci-ratio price projection, and Fibonacci-ratio time projection. Miner argues that no single method (pattern, price, or time) is reliable enough alone, but that when all three point to the same zone, the odds of a trend reversal are very high. The second half of the book shifts from analysis to execution: a full catalogue of daily-bar entry triggers, initial stop-loss placement rules, and stop-adjustment techniques designed to keep losses small while staying in trends for their duration. Miner was a professional futures trader and advisory-newsletter publisher (Dynamic Traders Group), and the book is built almost entirely from his own real-market chart examples rather than theory.

## Core thesis

Every market swing is part of a larger-degree trend and contains smaller-degree sub-swings; trend-reversal zones can be projected in advance, not just recognized after the fact, by measuring how prior swings of similar degree relate to each other in both price and time through Fibonacci and related ratios. A single price or time projection is weak evidence; a "cluster" of several independent price projections falling in the same zone, coinciding with a time projection and a completed Elliott Wave pattern, is high-probability evidence. Trading decisions should follow strict, pre-defined entry and stop rules rather than discretion, and the protective stop-loss (not the profit target) is the only variable a trader actually controls.

## Key concepts

- **Wave degree** — three practical degrees (major, intermediate, minor) are enough; each larger wave subdivides into the next smaller degree, and smaller-degree projections should confirm larger-degree ones.
- **Impulse wave** — a five-wave structure in the direction of the larger trend; waves 1, 3 and 5 are themselves impulsive and should each subdivide into five waves.
- **Corrective wave (ABC)** — a three-wave structure against the trend; may be a zigzag (5-3-5), flat, irregular, or running correction; complex corrections beyond a simple ABC are treated as unpredictable and not labeled.
- **Elliott "rules"** — wave 2 cannot retrace past the start of wave 1; wave 3 is never the shortest of waves 1/3/5; wave 4 cannot close in the price territory of wave 1 (a "pattern stop-loss").
- **Overbalance** — a counter-trend swing that exceeds prior counter-trend swings in both price and time, signaling a reversal of larger degree than previous corrections.
- **Time Cycle Ratio (TCR)** — Miner's term for projecting a Fibonacci ratio of one prior time span forward from a pivot (time retracements and alternate time projections).
- **Time Rhythm Zone (TRZ)** — the overlap of the 80% historical range of low-to-low and high-to-low (or high-to-high/low-to-high) swing durations, used to bracket the probable window for the next trend change.
- **Alternate Price Projection (APP)** — projecting one wave's price range from the end of another wave using a Fibonacci ratio (e.g., wave C = 0.618 × wave A).
- **Parallel channel** — a trendline drawn through waves 1–3 (or 2–4) with a parallel line from wave 2 (or 3) used to give a confirming, not primary, target for wave 4 or 5 completion.
- **Pattern stop-loss** — the Elliott rule violation that would invalidate the current wave count, used directly as the logical stop-loss level.
- **Three-Day Low/High (3DL/3DH)** — the lowest (highest) price of the three days measured from the most recent extreme, excluding inside days; used to trail stops through a trend.

## Rules and setups

Miner's price-projection ratios by wave (fixed values used throughout the book):

| Comparison | Typical ratios |
|---|---|
| Wave 2 retracement of Wave 1 | 50%–78.6% (rarely less than 50%, rarely more than 78.6%) |
| Wave 3 vs. Wave 1 | minimum 100%, then 162% or 262% |
| Wave 4 retracement of Wave 3 | 23.6%–50% (never into Wave 1's range) |
| Wave 5 vs. Wave 1 (Wave 3 extended) | 62%, 100%, or 162% |
| Wave 5 vs. Waves 1–3 (Wave 3 extended) | 38% or 62% |
| Wave 5 vs. Wave 4 | 127%, 162%, 200%, or 262% (162% most common) |
| Wave C vs. Wave A | 62%, 100%, or 162% |
| Wave C vs. Wave B | 162%, 200%, or 262% |

Entry-trigger catalogue (all defined on daily bars, described for a short entry at a top; reverse for longs):

1. **Reversal Day (RD)** — new high, close below both the current day's open and the prior close. Variants: Key Reversal Day (opens below prior close too — stronger), Outside Reversal Day. Stop: one tick above the RD high.
2. **Signal Day (SD)** — opens in the top third of the day's range, makes a new high, closes in the bottom third (close need not be below the prior close). Gap Signal Day: the whole range gaps above the prior day's range — one of the strongest single-day signals. Stop: one tick above the SD high.
3. **Snap-Back Reversal Day (SBRD)** — a two-day pattern: day 1 opens low/closes high (bullish-looking), day 2 opens high/closes low. Stop: one tick above the higher high of the two days.
4. **Reversal-Confirmation Day (RCD)** — used when no RD/SD/SBRD forms at the actual pivot; enter on a close below the current open and the prior close (stronger version also requires the day's low to exceed the prior day's low). Maximum stop: one tick beyond the pivot extreme.
5. **Inside-Day set-up (trend-continuation)** — only in the direction of the established trend; buy one tick above the high of the day *preceding* the inside day, as long as that day's low hasn't been exceeded. Stop: one tick below the lower of the inside-day or entry-day low.
6. **Outside-Day set-up** — if price first exceeds the prior day's low without exceeding its high, buy one tick above the prior day's high; exit on the close if the close is below the day's open and the prior close. "OSD+1": same idea entered the following day if the outside day itself wasn't tradeable in real time.
7. **Gann Pull-Back (GPB)** — after three consecutive higher-highs (or two plus an inside day) against an established uptrend, sell one tick below the prior day's low, adjusting the stop up each new high; buy-side is the mirror image. Initial stop: one tick above the higher of the entry day or the day before it. Exit on the close if the close is above the day's open and prior close.

Stop-loss adjustment as a trend matures: keep the stop at the swing extreme (or the 3-Day Low/High, one tick beyond) through the bulk of the trend, then tighten to the two-day and finally one-day extreme once time/price/pattern projections indicate the trend is near completion.

## Risk and money management

Miner rejects fixed reward:risk ratios as meaningless ("risk is a probability, not a dollar figure") and instead defines risk purely as the distance from entry to the level that invalidates the trade thesis. The trading plan sets a maximum acceptable dollar capital-exposure per trade (his worked example uses $500 on a gold contract); if the entry-and-stop rule combination would require more capital exposure than the plan allows, the trade is skipped entirely rather than the stop moved closer. He recommends trading two to three units per position: covering the short-term unit near the minimum objective for what the move would be if it were only a correction, and holding the intermediate/long-term unit for the greater trend, letting the market's own stop rules (not a fixed profit target) end the trade. No percent-of-equity position-sizing formula is given; sizing is left to the trader's own capital-exposure ceiling.

## Psychology and discipline

The book's discipline message is process-first: define the protective stop before entry, never move it further away, and let the market — not a subjective profit guess — end the trade. Miner repeatedly warns against forcing an Elliott Wave count when the pattern isn't clean ("don't become an Elliott Wave obsessive"), against curve-fitting a forecast to a pre-existing bias, and against becoming a "trading junkie" who must always have a position. He frames a failed reversal signal as itself informative — a stop-and-reverse condition — rather than a reason for frustration, and insists that skipping a trade that doesn't meet the plan's capital-exposure rule is itself correct trading, not a missed opportunity.

- Ch 1 — Introduction to Dynamic Trading — the case for combining time, price and pattern rather than relying on lagging indicators.
- Ch 2 — Getting Started — wave degree, Fibonacci ratio table, the three dimensions (time/price/pattern) of market activity.

- Ch 8 — The Real World of Dynamic Trading — Dynamic Trader Report case studies across a dozen markets.

## Strengths and caveats

The price and time ratio tables are unusually explicit and internally consistent for an Elliott Wave book, and the entry/stop catalogue in Chapter 6 is coded-implementable as written — a rarity in discretionary pattern-trading literature. Miner is candid about Elliott Wave's limits (a clean count is identifiable "about 50% of the time") and explicitly tells readers not to force counts, which is more intellectually honest than most Elliott Wave material. Caveats: the book predates decimalization and electronic order books in most futures markets, so "one tick" stop placement needs re-scaling to modern minimum price increments; examples are drawn from 1990s futures markets (bonds, currencies, grains, S&P) and the multi-day/weeks-long Time Rhythm Zone examples assume daily-bar swing trading, not intraday execution; and several of the wave-ratio "rules" (e.g., wave 4 alternation) are explicitly labeled tendencies rather than hard constraints, which the reader must track carefully since Miner sometimes states them more forcefully in summary boxes than in the surrounding prose.

## Who should read it

Swing traders and position traders already comfortable with basic Elliott Wave and Fibonacci retracement concepts who want a systematic, rule-based upgrade rather than a discretionary "read the chart" approach. Less suited to pure discretionary chartists uninterested in ratio arithmetic, or to short-term intraday scalpers, since the worked examples and time-cycle methods are calibrated to daily and weekly bars.
