## Summary

A brief promotional lesson (credited to both "Mark Crisp" and "Mark McRae" in the text) distributed via tradingforbeginners.com, presented as 20 numbered maxims for building a trading plan. It covers discipline, realistic expectations, risk sizing, diversification across positions, cutting losses versus letting profits run, and staying with the trend. No specific trading system, entry method, or market is discussed — it is a checklist of principles rather than a method.

## Key points

- Discipline in following a plan matters more than the trading philosophy itself; a plan's value is in holding up over time.
- No system is 100% accurate — the goal is a small, repeatable edge, not certainty.
- Admit mistakes quickly; avoid emotional or financial attachment to a losing trade.
- Diversify positions so no single trade risks catastrophic loss; smaller per-trade capital allocation raises the odds of long-run success.
- Common trader error: taking small profits while letting losses run — the opposite of sound practice.
- Simplicity is valued over complex, stressful systems.
- Declining volume can signal the market is rejecting the current price direction — a possible turn.
- Move stops in the direction of profit only, to lock in gains; never loosen a stop to increase risk.
- Avoid trading from anxiety (boredom) or impatience; only act when the fundamental/technical picture gives a real signal.

## Actionable rules

1. Divide trading capital into six equal parts; never risk more than one-tenth of total capital on any single trade.
2. Trade in five or six different stocks at once rather than concentrating capital in one position.
3. Cut losses short and let profits run; take the loss and wait for the next opportunity rather than averaging into a loser.
4. Never increase trading size or frequency immediately after a loss or after a long winning streak.
5. Raise (never lower) a stop-loss as a winning position moves in your favor.
6. Trade the most active names and avoid slow-moving markets; use market orders when possible rather than fixed limit prices.
7. Establish a separate "surplus account" after winning streaks, reserved for emergencies.

## Caveats

Undated tearsheet-style document with no publisher information, and the two sizing numbers given are internally inconsistent (item 11 says to divide capital into six equal parts, implying about 16.7% per position, and in the same breath caps risk at one-tenth of capital per trade) and both are far looser than the 1-2%-of-equity risk now standard and are not derived from any backtest or case study in the text. Item 15 ("trade at the market whenever possible") is generic advice that predates decimalization and modern electronic execution, and is dated given today's tighter spreads and slippage concerns. No entry/exit signal, indicator, or timeframe is specified anywhere in the document.

## Who it is for

Absolute beginners looking for a short, plain-language checklist of trading-plan discipline points before building an actual system.
