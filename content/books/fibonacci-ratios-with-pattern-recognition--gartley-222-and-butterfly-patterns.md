## What it is

A pair of harmonic, ratio-defined reversal patterns built on the same X-A-B-C-D swing skeleton. The Gartley "222" (named for its page number in H.M. Gartley's 1935 course) is a continuation-after-correction pattern found after an established trend has made a first correction; the Butterfly (co-discovered by Pesavento and Bryce Gilmore) is a reversal pattern found only at major tops and bottoms. Both require an internal AB=CD sub-pattern and are defined entirely by Fibonacci retracement/extension ratios between the labeled swing points, not by visual shape alone — which is what makes them mechanically checkable rather than purely discretionary.

## Rules

**Gartley "222" (bullish version; mirror all levels for the bearish version):**

1. Identify an X-A decline (the setup requires a prior trend into point A).
2. A B-wave rally retraces the X-A decline; the subsequent C-wave decline should equal 0.618 or 0.786 of the A-B rally (expect only 0.382 or 0.500 in a strongly trending market).
3. Point D — the pattern's completion and entry trigger — should fall at the 0.618 or 0.786 retracement of the entire X-A swing, roughly 75% of the time (the other 25% of cases complete at 0.382, 0.500, or 0.707).
4. Confirm that an AB=CD sub-pattern is visible within the A-to-D move (the B-C-D leg should echo the A-B leg in proportion).
5. Check time symmetry: the number of time bars from X to A should be in Fibonacci proportion to the number of bars from A to D (the book's example: 17 bars X-to-A against 11 bars A-to-D, approximately a 1.618 ratio).
6. Entry: at or near point D, using one of the entry techniques below.
7. Invalidation / stop: if point X is exceeded, the pattern has failed — this is not treated as a minor loss to absorb passively but as a signal that a strong continuation move (to at least 1.27 or 1.618 of the X-A range) is now underway in the direction through X.
8. A rarer variant completes the AB=CD projection exactly at point X, forming a true double top or double bottom.

**Butterfly pattern (found only at significant tops/bottoms):**

1. Formed by two connected triangles sharing an XABCD structure, with an internal AB=CD leg in the extension move (C to D).
2. The C-D extension leg must equal 1.27 or 1.618 times the X-A leg. Any move beyond 1.618 negates the Butterfly interpretation entirely and instead signals a strong continuation move through the pattern.
3. Internal retracement legs (A-B, B-C) typically fall at the 0.618 and 0.786 ratios.
4. Time bars are generally in ratio and proportion to the price bars (symmetry is a visual/quantitative confirmation, not a separate hard rule).
5. Entry: at or near point D, once the 1.27/1.618 extension is reached and confirmed (see entry techniques below) — do not anticipate the level without confirmation, since price can extend to the full 1.618 before reversing.
6. Invalidation: a sustained move beyond the 1.618 extension of X-A voids the pattern.

**Entry techniques (either pattern), in the author's stated order of preference:**

1. **Shapiro Iteration** — after price reaches the projected D-point ratio, wait one full bar of whatever timeframe is being traded (e.g., 5 minutes on a 5-minute chart) before entering, to filter emotional, chase-the-price entries.
2. **Limit order** — place a limit order a few ticks beyond the exact calculated ratio price, with a predetermined fixed-dollar stop (the author's own example uses roughly $600 per position on futures).
3. **Candlestick confirmation** — wait for a doji, hammer, shooting star, or tweezers (double top/bottom candle pair) forming at the D-point before entering.
4. **Volatility Stop** — for strongly trending markets: `Range = (Range × (N−1) + High − Low) / N`; long stop = highest close − Range × C; short stop = lowest close + Range × C, with the constant C set between 2.5 and 4.0.

## Risk

The pattern's own structure supplies the stop: for the Gartley 222, the maximum acceptable adverse level is point X (a break of X invalidates the trend-continuation reading entirely, so a stop beyond X is not a "wider stop," it is trading a different, opposite thesis). For the Butterfly, the invalidation level is a sustained break of the 1.618 extension of X-A. Beyond the pattern-specific invalidation point, the book's one stated portfolio-level rule applies: stop protection should be no more than 3% of total trading equity per trade — if the distance from entry to the pattern's invalidation point implies a larger loss than that, position size must be reduced rather than the stop moved closer than the structural invalidation level. Reaction-pattern variants elsewhere in the book add a specific stop-management refinement usable with either pattern: once price has moved 61.8% of the way toward the profit objective, move the stop to the nearest confirmed swing point (converting the trade to risk-free).

## Caveats

Pesavento states the Gartley 222 completes within its stated ratio zone about 75% of the time and the Butterfly is correct "more than 80% of the time," but neither figure is accompanied by disclosed sample size, date range, or out-of-sample testing — treat these as the author's experience-based estimates, not audited statistics. Both patterns require the trader to first correctly identify points X and A, which is itself a discretionary judgment; a wrongly-identified X point invalidates every downstream ratio calculation. The Butterfly is explicitly described as rare and found only at major turning points — the book's own reviewers reportedly suggested cutting the chapter because the pattern is easy to state but hard to reliably spot in real time. The AB=CD sub-pattern requirement inside both patterns adds a second, nested ratio check (C-D equals A-B about 60% of the time, or extends to 1.27/1.618 the other 40%) that must also hold for a valid signal, which further narrows how often a textbook-clean setup actually appears.
