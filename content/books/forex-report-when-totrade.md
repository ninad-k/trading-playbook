## Summary

Question: which hours, sessions, and days produce the most price movement (and by implication, trading opportunity) in the major forex pairs? Data: four years of historical tick data from a forex dealer, aggregated into average pip range tables. Method: simple descriptive statistics (average high-low range) broken down by hour of day (Eastern Time), trading session (Australia/Japan/London/New York), day of week, and day of month, for EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/CHF, and USD/JPY. Finding: volatility is highly uneven across time — it concentrates in the London/New York overlap hours and on Tuesday/Wednesday, is markedly lower on Sunday and in the late New York afternoon/evening, and shows no strong repeatable pattern by day of the month.

## Key points

- Highest average hourly ranges cluster around 8–11 AM Eastern Time (the London/New York session overlap): e.g., USD/CHF averaged 39–45 pips/hour in that window versus roughly 12–16 pips/hour in the late afternoon (3–5 PM ET).
- Lowest average hourly ranges occur late in the New York session and into the Sydney/early Asia session, around 4 PM–12 AM ET (e.g., EUR/USD averaging 8–11 pips/hour versus 20–30 pips/hour during the London open).
- By session, London (UK) produced the largest average ranges across all four pairs (e.g., 80 pips EUR/USD, 121 pips USD/CHF), followed by New York, Japan, and Australia in descending order for most pairs.
- By day of week, Tuesday and Wednesday showed the widest average ranges (e.g., GBP/USD ~123–128 pips), Sunday the narrowest (24–36 pips across pairs), with Monday building up and Thursday/Friday tapering off.
- Day-of-month breakdown showed no consistent, strongly repeatable pattern — ranges fluctuated within a roughly 65–110 pip band for most pairs across all 31 days without an obvious monthly cycle.
- The report explicitly frames itself as a "data brief" meant to "stimulate thinking," not a strategy — it includes a disclaimer that the content does not suggest any particular tradable action.

## Actionable rules

1. Concentrate active/discretionary trading attention in the London and London/New York-overlap hours (roughly 8–11 AM ET) where average pip ranges were 2–4x the late-afternoon lows.
2. Expect reduced volatility (and correspondingly, use tighter targets/stops or reduce activity) during the late New York session and Sunday, when average ranges were smallest.
3. Favor Tuesday–Wednesday for range-dependent strategies and treat Sunday as a low-opportunity trading day, based on the observed day-of-week pattern.
4. Do not expect a repeatable day-of-month edge; the data showed no clear, consistent monthly pattern worth building a rule around.
5. No specific entry, exit, or stop-loss rules are given — this is timing/volatility context to layer onto an existing system, not a standalone strategy.

## Caveats

This is marketing collateral for a now-largely-historical automated forex platform (FX Engines, 2004) rather than an independently published or peer-reviewed study; methodology detail (data source, exact statistical treatment) is minimal, and the report itself disclaims that it offers no actionable trading advice. The underlying volatility patterns (session overlaps, weekday clustering) are broadly consistent with well-documented forex market microstructure, but the specific pip figures are from 2000–2004 tick data and should not be taken as current.

## Who it is for

Forex traders who want a quick historical reference on when volatility (and thus intraday opportunity) has tended to concentrate, to use as a filter alongside an existing entry/exit system.
