## Summary

This is a broker-produced (Royal Forex) educational manual rather than a proprietary trading system. It moves from Forex market structure (spot/forward/futures/options sectors, major currencies, broker vs. direct dealing) through risk categories (exchange rate, interest rate, credit, dictatorship/sovereign risk), then fundamental analysis (exchange-rate theories, GDP/inflation/employment indicators), and finally a lengthy technical analysis section: Dow Theory, chart types, support/resistance, reversal and continuation patterns, gaps, a full suite of indicators, and closes with Fibonacci constants and Elliott Wave theory.

## Key points

- Classifies Forex risk into exchange rate risk, interest rate risk, credit risk (with replacement and settlement sub-types), and dictatorship (sovereign) risk.
- Covers spot, forward, futures, and options market sectors and the difference between broker-intermediated and direct interbank dealing.
- Fundamental analysis section catalogs the standard U.S. macro release calendar: GDP, industrial production, PPI/CPI, trade balance, employment cost index, retail sales, consumer sentiment.
- Technical analysis section covers Dow Theory, percentage retracement rules, and the three main chart types (line, bar, candlestick).
- Full pattern catalog: head-and-shoulders (and inverted), double/triple tops and bottoms, rounding patterns, flags, pennants, triangles, wedges, rectangles, and all four gap types (common, breakaway, runaway, exhaustion).
- Indicator reference includes moving averages, envelopes, Bollinger Bands (20-period, 2 standard deviations), Average True Range, CCI, DMI, Stochastics, MACD, Momentum, RSI, Rate of Change, Williams %R, and Ichimoku.
- Gives a concrete moving-average crossover example: a 9-day/18-day MA pair, where a buy signal is confirmed when the 9-day average also turns up.
- Closes with Fibonacci ratio constants (0.382, 0.618, etc.) and an introduction to Elliott Wave theory.

## Actionable rules

1. When evaluating credit exposure, separate replacement risk (counterparty failure before settlement) from settlement risk (time-zone gaps between currency legs).
2. Use a 20-period simple moving average with bands at 2 standard deviations above/below as the standard Bollinger Band setup.
3. Confirm a moving-average crossover signal (e.g., 9-day/18-day) only once the faster average has also turned in the signal's direction, not on the crossover alone.
4. Treat gaps by type before acting: common gaps in quiet/illiquid conditions carry little information, while breakaway and runaway gaps mark the start and continuation of a real move.
5. No single unified entry/exit/stop system is given — this is a reference text; readers must combine the pattern and indicator definitions themselves into a rule set.

## Caveats

This is a generalist educational reference assembled by a retail forex broker (2001), not a tested trading system — it defines dozens of indicators and patterns without ranking or backtesting them, and offers no specific position-sizing or stop-loss rules. Content is standard textbook material available in many other sources; treat it as a glossary/primer rather than an edge.

## Who it is for

Complete beginners to Forex who want one document covering market structure, macro fundamentals, and the vocabulary of technical analysis before specializing in any single method.
