## What it is

A discretionary, Elliott-wave-driven campaign system for entering a new trend near its start and adding to the position as the trend develops, then exiting on a confluence-based exhaustion signal. Unlike the Alligator-gated system of the sequel book (*New Trading Dimensions*), this earlier version has no moving-average envelope filter — trend identification and timing come from Elliott wave counting, the Market Facilitation Index (MFI), and the fractal/thumb price patterns, layered together into a single "campaign" rather than one all-at-once entry.

## Rules

**Step 1 — Find Point Zero.** Identify the end of a completed 5-wave Elliott sequence (a-through-e correction finished) with reasonable confidence. This becomes the anchor for the whole campaign; do not initiate the system without it.

**Step 2 — First entry (wave 1).** Take a standard fractal breakout: buy stop one tick above (sell stop one tick below) the middle bar of a 5-bar fractal pattern forming off Point Zero. In a bracketed/ranging market, use the 3-bar "thumb" variant instead — valid only when at least 2 of its 3 bars are squat bars (unusually low range for their volume) or green bars (rising volume versus the prior bar) — entering one tick beyond the thumb bar's extreme for a better price than the standard fractal signal.

**Step 3 — Wave-3 add.** Target the end of wave 3 by dropping to a smaller timeframe and watching for a confluence of the five magic bullets there (see Risk/definitions below). Initial wave-3 length target: 1x to 1.62x the length of wave 1; it is rarely shorter than wave 1.

**Step 4 — Wave-4 add.** Do not add for wave 5 until the 5/34 (or signal-lined 5/34/5) moving-average momentum oscillator has crossed back through its zero line. Corroborate the wave-4 low/high with (a) a completed a-b-c or triangle correction shape and (b) a Fibonacci time projection: wave 4 tends to end 1.38x–1.62x the time span from the wave-1 peak to the wave-3 peak, measured from the wave-2 low. Add to the position once wave 4 is judged complete.

**Step 5 — Exit / campaign close.** Treat a reversal as high-confidence only once 2 or more of the five magic bullets align: (1) MFI divergence against price, (2) price inside the Elliott retracement/target zone (roughly the 62–100% zone referenced elsewhere in the book), (3) a fractal forming at the price extreme, (4) a squat bar at the extreme, (5) a change in the momentum oscillator's direction. Close the full campaign (not just the latest add) once this confluence appears.

**Position sizing across the campaign**: size is not committed all at once. A worked soybean example scales in with additional contracts at the wave-1 length level and again at roughly 110% of the wave-1 length, trailing stops up to the prior wave's extreme as each new leg confirms.

## Risk

No fixed percentage-of-equity or dollar-per-trade formula is given; the book scales positions off Elliott levels instead of a sizing rule. Two independent stops are used together:
- **Structure stop**: placed at the extreme of the triggering pattern (the fractal/thumb bar, or the prior Elliott wave's high/low), tightened as each new campaign leg confirms.
- **Air bag stop**: a second, higher-timeframe stop placed one tick beyond the high/low of the second-back bar on a significantly higher timeframe (using an approximate 1:5 ratio between adjacent timeframes — e.g., a hold on the daily chart references a bar two back on a weekly-equivalent view), meant to survive gap moves and violent single-bar spikes that a normal stop would not.

The worked example gives real figures: a per-contract risk of $458 on an early campaign leg, and a closed-out profit of $1,275 per contract on part of the position, illustrating that risk is evaluated leg-by-leg as the campaign is built rather than fixed at the outset.

## Caveats

This system is reconstructed from a sampled OCR pass over a scanned 251-page book, so several supporting mechanics referenced by name are not fully defined on the pages that were available: the exact MFI formula, the precise Elliott retracement percentages used for the target zone, and the definitions of the "four Profitunity windows" (an MFI/volume bar classification the book tests readers on but whose specific rules were not present in the sample). No backtest, win-rate, or drawdown statistic is given beyond the single worked soybean walkthrough, so the system's edge is illustrative rather than statistically validated. Entry, add, and exit timing all depend on a discretionary Elliott wave count, which is inherently more subjective than the purely mechanical Alligator/Awesome-Oscillator system in the companion book, [[bill-williams-new-trading-dimensions-how-to-profit-from-chaos-in-stocks-bonds-and-commodit]] — traders wanting a fully mechanical rule set should use that system instead and treat this one as a discretionary overlay.
