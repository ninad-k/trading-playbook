## Summary

Robert Kiyosaki's home-study workbook organizes personal finance into “Think,” “Learn,” and “Do” sections. The contents span attitudes toward work and risk, financial literacy, cash-flow statements, investor types, business ownership, paper securities, mutual funds, real estate, debt reduction, and action worksheets (pp. 1-10). It is distinct from *Rich Dad Poor Dad*: it converts the same philosophy into exercises, checklists, and planning prompts.

The investing material starts with goals. Readers are asked whether they seek security, comfort, or wealth, then to select investments as parts of a coherent plan rather than accumulate unrelated products (p. 148). The source favors income-producing businesses and real estate, but it also discusses stocks, bonds, mutual funds, notes, and intellectual property as possible assets (p. 51). A real-estate worksheet separates initial cash requirements, gross income, vacancy allowance, operating costs, taxes, insurance, and debt service (p. 187).

## Key points

- Financial education, goal selection, and action planning are treated as separate stages (pp. 1-10).
- The asset checklist includes investments that produce income, or appreciate in value and can be readily sold (p. 51).
- Investment products should follow the investor's desired lifestyle and risk tolerance (p. 148).
- Real estate requires financing, insurance, maintenance, tenant management, and verification of cash-flow claims (p. 167).
- The workbook includes practical debt, stock, fund, and property worksheets (pp. 8, 187, 264, 283).

## Actionable rules

1. Before choosing products, state the financial outcome sought and assemble investments into one plan; do not treat a collection of pensions, insurance, and funds as a complete plan by itself (p. 148).
2. For rental property, calculate total cash required, realistic rent and other income, vacancy and bad-debt allowance, every operating expense, and mortgage interest before judging cash flow (p. 187).
3. If possible, the workbook says to pay unsecured debt from the rainy-day reserve and replenish it promptly. Without a reserve, cover listed essentials first, then list card balances and APRs and repay from the highest APR downward (p. 264).
4. Before buying a stock, review the company's finances, prospectus, and regulatory filings; stay within risk tolerance and retain complete transaction records (p. 283).

## Caveats

Only the recovered pages listed above were reviewed. The material is motivational and sometimes makes broad claims without evidence. Its preference for concentration and entrepreneurial assets can raise risk, while tax, retirement, and product details may be dated or jurisdiction-specific. Professional advice is repeatedly suggested, but the workbook is not a substitute for individualized planning.

## Who it is for

Beginners who want structured exercises for household cash flow, debt reduction, and basic investment due diligence.
