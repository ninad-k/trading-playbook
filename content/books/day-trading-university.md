## Summary

This compilation preserves early-2000s Daytrading University web lessons organized into at least five modules. The opening material covers broker and software selection, Nasdaq versus NYSE trading, basic technical analysis, opening gaps, stock screening, stops, and chart patterns (pp. 1–9). Later sampled lessons move through Level 2 and time-and-sales reading, trade journaling, psychology, swing trading, position scaling, and short selling (pp. 60–335). The method combines a market and sector view with a five-day chart, one-minute chart, volume, support and resistance, then uses Level 2 as a short-horizon execution aid rather than the sole reason for a trade (pp. 26, 112, 129). The course repeatedly favors strong stocks for longs, weak stocks for shorts, and patience when the setup is unclear (pp. 43, 181, 284). A separate module extends the framework to two-to-twenty-day swing trades, wider structural stops, and 38%, 50%, and 62% retracement areas (pp. 215, 232).

## Key points

- Direct-access brokerage, multiple screens, Level 2, and period-specific software reflect 1999–2001 market structure (pp. 3–7, 249).
- The source treats volume and support/resistance as primary context; oscillators are secondary (p. 26).
- It distinguishes rapid intraday stops from wider swing-trade stops (pp. 77, 215).
- Scaling in and out is presented as a way to manage entry and exit uncertainty (p. 267).
- Many probability and income statements are personal claims rather than tested results.

## Actionable rules

1. Decide from the market, sector, chart, volume, and time-and-sales evidence first; use Level 2 mainly to time the entry or exit within roughly two minutes (p. 112).
2. In the illustrated SBUX trade, place the initial stop two to three spreads below entry and raise it after each favorable two-spread move; these fixed fractional examples are historical and pre-decimal (p. 77).
3. For swing trades, the course proposes 100–300 shares, a two-to-twenty-day horizon, buying the lower third of a channel or a resistance breakout, and using prior two-to-three-month support for the stop (p. 215).

## Caveats

Only the OCR-recovered pages listed above were reviewed; unsampled pages may contain additional modules or qualifications. The course is promotional, uses selected examples, and supplies no audited sample. Fractions, the uptick rule, named brokers, software, hardware, and Level 2 tactics are historically dated.

## Who it is for

Useful as a historical study of discretionary stock-day-trading workflow and execution discipline, especially alongside the related seminar workbook.
