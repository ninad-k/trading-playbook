## What it is

A two-track relative-valuation screening system for large-cap U.S. equities (roughly 1,000 names with market cap over $3 billion), designed to identify statistically cheap stocks without relying on absolute valuation cutoffs that break down as market regimes change. Dividend-paying stocks are screened with Relative Dividend Yield (RDY); low- or non-dividend-paying growth stocks are screened with Relative Price-to-Sales Ratio (RPSR). Both are purely valuation-timing tools and are explicitly stated to say nothing about company quality, so every stock that screens as a buy candidate must additionally clear a Twelve Fundamental Factors qualitative/quantitative checklist before entering the portfolio. The system concludes with mechanical position-sizing and stop-loss rules meant to remove discretion from both the buy and the sell decision.

## Rules

**Step 1 — Universe and track selection**
1. Universe: roughly 1,000 U.S. large-cap stocks with market capitalization over $3 billion (RPSR can also be applied to mid- and small-cap names given sufficient sales history).
2. Route dividend-paying stocks to the RDY screen; route low- or non-dividend-paying growth stocks to the RPSR screen. A stock can be tracked on both if applicable.

**Step 2 — Relative Dividend Yield (RDY) screen**
3. Compute: Stock yield = indicated annual dividend / current stock price. Market yield = index annual dividend / current index value. RDY = stock yield / market yield.
4. Buy candidate only if RDY ≥ 125% (absolute floor; never buy below this regardless of the stock's own historical range).
5. Default sell trigger: RDY falls back to approximately the market's own yield (RDY ≈ 100%).
6. Otherwise, set the specific buy/sell threshold lines from the stock's own historical RDY range, and hold them fixed unless the company's fundamental profile changes materially (merger, sale of a business line, large shift in growth rate).
7. Treat RDY as a multi-year signal: expect roughly one to three years (or more) between a stock entering the buy range and reaching the sell range.

**Step 3 — Relative Price-to-Sales Ratio (RPSR) screen**
8. Compute: Stock PSR = stock price / sales per share. RPSR = stock's PSR / S&P 500's PSR.
9. Using monthly data anchored from 12/31/1992 to the current month, compute the mean RPSR and its standard deviation over that window.
10. Buy line = mean RPSR − 1 standard deviation. Sell line = mean RPSR + 1 standard deviation. Recompute both lines monthly as new data arrives.
11. Do not apply RPSR to companies without a meaningful multi-year sales history (new IPOs, newly merged entities whose combined sales history doesn't reflect the new business).

**Step 4 — Twelve Fundamental Factors gate (applies after Step 2 or 3 signals a buy)**
12. Qualitative (require ≥ 2 of 3 passing): Buggy Whip (no material product/service obsolescence risk), Franchise or Niche Value (market-leading pricing/purchasing power, growing or defensible share), Top Management and Board (accountable management, compensation tied to shareholder value, an independent board of roughly ≤12 members with only 1–2 insiders).
13. Quantitative (require ≥ 5 of 9 passing): Sales/Revenue Growth, Operating Margins (stable or improving, favorable vs. industry), Relative P/E (reasonable vs. own history and peers), Positive Free Cash Flow (Operating Net + DD&A − Capital Spending − Common Dividends, positive in at least one of the last two years and competitive vs. peer free-cash-flow yield), Dividend Coverage and Growth (sustainable payout ratio, yield in line with peers), Asset Turnover (stable or improving vs. industry), Investment in Business/ROIC (ROIC consistently above weighted average cost of capital), Equity Leverage (leverage growth justified by earnings/margin growth, no history of large acquisition write-offs), Financial Risk (reasonable debt/equity including off-balance-sheet items, adequate coverage ratio, stable credit rating).
14. For bank stocks, substitute Overhead/Efficiency, Liquidity/Funding Mix, Asset Quality, Asset-Liability Management, and Capital Adequacy for the quantitative factors that don't apply to lenders (keep the three qualitative factors unchanged).
15. Reject the stock if it fails the minimum qualitative or quantitative pass count, even if the valuation screen signals a strong buy ("terminally cheap" trap).
16. Exclude, regardless of screen result: newly merged companies combining a high-growth and a slow-growth business (their combined historical sales/earnings base is not statistically meaningful) and companies with too short a public operating history for a reliable RPSR range.

**Step 5 — Portfolio construction**
17. Concentrate the portfolio in roughly 25–35 holdings (research cited in the text finds 10–20 stocks near-optimal for diversification, but 25–35 is used as a practical institutional compromise).
18. Blend both RDY and RPSR names: RDY names anchor the portfolio with dividend income and lower volatility; RPSR names provide capital-gains upside from a return to a prior growth trajectory.
19. Favor sector pairs with historically negative return covariance (e.g., technology vs. pharmaceuticals, technology vs. financials) to reduce overall portfolio volatility.
20. Cap any single sector at roughly 2x the S&P 500's weighting in that sector, absent a deliberate, conditions-based override.
21. Initiate a new position at up to 0.5% of portfolio value; average in over time toward a target weighting of approximately 3–5%; hard cap any single holding at 6% of portfolio value (trim back to comply if price appreciation pushes it over).
22. Expected holding period once purchased: roughly one to three years, continuously re-ranked against the current universe of stocks passing Steps 2–4; rotate out a current holding if a new candidate offers materially more upside on the same screens.

**Step 6 — Stop-loss rules**
23. Trigger 1: a relative price decline of 10% in one trading day, or 15% over two trading days, automatically trims the position to half its pre-decline size; follow with a full reassessment (sell out entirely, or add back at a newly identified rock-bottom valuation with an identified catalyst).
24. Trigger 2: a relative price decline of 20% over the trailing week, or over the trailing 1, 3, 6, or 12 months, triggers an immediate Twelve Fundamental Factors re-review; an additional 5% decline from that point triggers the same automatic half-size trim as Trigger 1.
25. Sell discipline on the valuation side: once a stock reaches its RDY or RPSR sell line, begin selling with the explicit goal of exiting before the stock's price decline pushes it back out of the sell range (i.e., don't wait for the "top tick").

## Risk

Risk is controlled at three layers. At the position level: staged entry (0.5% initial, 3–5% target, 6% hard cap) prevents any single name from dominating the portfolio, and the two stop-loss triggers (10%/15% one-two-day decline; 20% multi-period decline plus a further 5%) force an automatic half-size cut and mandatory reassessment rather than allowing a loss to run on hope. At the sector level: no sector may exceed roughly twice its S&P 500 weighting without a deliberate override, limiting concentration risk from a single macro theme. At the portfolio level: concentration is deliberately bounded to roughly 25–35 names — enough to avoid over-concentration in any one idea, but few enough to avoid index-like dilution of returns — and sector pairs with negative historical covariance are favored to reduce aggregate volatility without sacrificing the value/GARP mandate. The text notes that under the original RDY-only version of the discipline, stocks rarely fell more than 50% after entering the buy range; extending to RPSR (a statistically more volatile, growth-oriented universe) increased downside variance, which is the stated reason the two stop-loss triggers were formally added.

## Caveats

Both formulas depend on institutional-grade historical data (Compustat in the source text) that individual investors may not have ready access to; RPSR in particular requires constructing and maintaining a monthly time series with a fixed 12/31/1992 anchor date, recalculated every month, which is a nontrivial ongoing data-engineering task. The specific numeric thresholds (RDY ≥ 125% floor, RPSR ±1 standard deviation bands, the 10/15/20/5% stop-loss percentages, the 0.5/3–5/6% position-sizing bands) were calibrated to market conditions and data availability through 2002 and are not validated against post-2003 markets in the text; a practitioner should expect to re-derive or at least re-test these constants on current data. Both screens are pure valuation-timing indicators and say nothing about business quality on their own — the book is explicit that skipping the Twelve Fundamental Factors gate risks buying "terminally cheap" stocks that are cheap because they are genuinely impaired, not because they are undervalued. RPSR is explicitly unreliable for companies without several years of consistent, comparable sales history (recent IPOs, freshly merged entities), which the book illustrates with a costly real losing position (Qwest) that directly motivated adding the stop-loss rules.
