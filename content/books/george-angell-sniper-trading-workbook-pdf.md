## Summary

A companion study guide to George Angell's *Sniper Trading*, structured as short question-and-answer drills rather than continuous prose. Each chapter poses a problem, then walks through the calculation. The core method is George Douglas Taylor's "Book Method" (rally, decline, buying-high, buying-under numbers) extended by Angell into the "LSS 3-Day Cycle" with Trend Reaction (pivot) buy/sell numbers, applied mainly to S&P 500 and Treasury bond futures.

## Key points

- **Taylor's four numbers** — rally (today's high minus yesterday's low), decline (yesterday's high minus today's low), buying high (today's high minus yesterday's high), buying under (yesterday's low minus today's low); averaged over 3 days to project tomorrow's range.
- **Buy/sell envelopes** — the buy envelope = last low, average 3-day decline, average 3-day buying-under, and the LSS pivot buy number; the sell envelope mirrors this with rally/buying-high numbers and the pivot sell number.
- **LSS Pivotal (Trend Reaction) numbers** — X = (High+Low+Close)/3; pivot sell = 2X − High; pivot buy = 2X − Low. Support becomes resistance and vice versa depending on whether the market is choppy or trending.
- **Early range / anticipated range** — after 30-60 minutes of trading, the day's expected range (pivot sell minus pivot buy) is added to the early low or subtracted from the early high to project a target top/bottom.
- **5-day LSS oscillator** — (Highest 5-day price − open 5 days ago) plus (last close − lowest 5-day price), divided by 2×(5-day range), expressed as a percentage; readings are tracked with a 3-day difference to gauge whether momentum is accelerating or decelerating.
- **Day-of-week bias** — Thursdays tend to be the weakest day in bull markets (best short after 2-3 up days) and the counter-trend rally day in bear markets; Mondays favor buying after a strong prior-day close and a steady-to-higher open.
- **Stops** — never place stops just above prior highs/below prior lows (stop-running zones); trail stops only in the position's favor; allow up to a 0.618 retracement of the initial move before the stop is hit.
- **Opening gaps** — fade (sell) upside gaps and buy downside gaps, since gaps fill roughly 75% of the time; do not fade extreme gaps, which tend to continue.

## Actionable rules

1. Trend reaction pivot buy/sell numbers: X=(H+L+C)/3, Sell=2X−H, Buy=2X−L.
2. Stop-loss placement: cap adverse retracement at 0.618 of the initiating leg; move stops only in the direction of profit.
3. Time-and-price trading: expect the second leg of a move to roughly match the first leg's size and duration (example in the text: an 11-minute first leg implies an ~11-minute second leg).
4. Fade opening gaps under normal conditions (gap fill ~75% of the time); do not fade extreme/outsized gaps.
5. One-day strength index = (Close − Low)/(High − Low) × 100, used to gauge whether yesterday's close was bullish or bearish for today.

## Caveats

The extracted text is heavily garbled in places (OCR/formatting artifacts scramble some numeric examples), so a few worked calculations could not be fully verified here — cross-check formulas against the original *Sniper Trading* text before coding them. The system is built around S&P 500 and Treasury bond futures of the 1990s trading floor era; day-of-week and seasonal edges described may not hold in modern electronic markets.

## Who it is for

Intermediate futures/index day traders already familiar with (or willing to adopt) Angell's Taylor-derived numeric system, looking for drilled practice problems rather than a first introduction to the method.
