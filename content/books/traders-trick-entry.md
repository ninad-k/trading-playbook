## Summary

A slide-deck course from Trading Educators (Joe Ross) that assumes prior knowledge of "The Law of Charts" — specifically 1-2-3 formations, ledges, consolidations, and Ross hooks. It teaches the Trader's Trick Entry (TTE), a technique for entering a position before the actual breakout of a 1-2-3 formation's #2 point or a Ross hook's point, on the logic that if the breakout is real the early entry captures extra profit, and if it's false the trade has already banked enough to cover costs — effectively a "free trade."

## Key points

- The TTE entry is a stop order placed one tick above (for a buy) or below (for a sell) the extreme of a "correcting" price bar that forms after the #2 point or Ross hook point.
- Correcting bars only need to make lower highs (for a buy setup) — they do not need to make lower lows — to still count as corrective.
- Rule: there must be enough room between the TTE entry price and the #2/hook point to cover costs and bank some profit; otherwise wait for the next correcting bar.
- Rule: a 1-2-3 formation must occur at the end of a trend, not inside a consolidation — 1-2-3s inside consolidations are meaningless because they occur in both directions.
- Rule: with one exception, no more than 3 bars of correction may occur before price starts moving toward the #2/hook point; beyond that, odds favor consolidation and the TTE is abandoned.
- Exception: double or triple highs (or lows) count as only one correcting bar for the 3-bar rule.
- A Ross hook TTE must occur after the violation of a 1-2-3's #2 point, a consolidation, or a ledge — this establishes or re-establishes the trend the hook trades with.
- As price moves away from the #2/hook point, the entry stop is trailed to one tick beyond the extreme of the newest correcting bar.
- The second correcting bar is identified as offering the best overall probability of success: about 5% less chance of reaching the breakout point than the first bar, but a materially better chance of having room to cover costs.
- The technique's edge is attributed partly to "insiders" whose own order-clustering and momentum toward the breakout point can't easily be reversed, plus reinforcing buying from Fibonacci and Gann retracement traders.

## Actionable rules

1. Entry: buy (sell) one tick above (below) the high (low) of a correcting bar following a #2 point or Ross hook, only after the formation's preconditions are met.
2. Formation filter: 1-2-3 must form at trend's end, not in consolidation; Ross hook must follow a violated #2 point, ledge, or consolidation.
3. Correction-bar limit: no more than 3 correcting bars (double/triple highs or lows count as 1) before abandoning the setup.
4. Profit filter: only take the TTE if there's enough room to the #2/hook point to cover costs and take some profit; otherwise wait for a later correcting bar (up to the 3-bar limit).
5. Trail: move the entry stop to one tick beyond each new correcting bar's extreme as price moves toward the breakout point.
6. Preferred bar: the second correcting bar offers the best tradeoff of probability versus room for profit.

## Caveats

This is a promotional slide presentation for Trading Educators' broader "Law of Charts" curriculum, not a standalone book — it explicitly requires prior study of that separate material to use the 1-2-3, ledge, and Ross hook definitions it builds on. No backtested win rate, sample size, or drawdown statistics are given; support for the method's edge rests on Joe Ross's reasoning about order clustering and Fibonacci/Gann trader behavior rather than empirical testing.

## Who it is for

Traders already familiar with Joe Ross's Law of Charts (1-2-3 formations, ledges, Ross hooks) who want a tighter, lower-risk entry technique for trading breakouts of those formations.
