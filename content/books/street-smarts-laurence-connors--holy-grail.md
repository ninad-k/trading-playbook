## What it is

A low-risk trend-continuation entry built on the observation that once a market is confirmed to be in a strong trend (via Welles Wilder's ADX), the first pullback to a key moving average tends to offer a high-probability re-entry point rather than a reversal. The authors half-joke about the "Holy Grail" name but present it as one of the simplest, most repeatable patterns in the book, workable across any market and timeframe.

## Rules

**For buys (sells are reversed):**
1. A 14-period ADX must be greater than 30 and rising, confirming a strongly trending market.
2. Wait for price to retrace to the 20-period exponential moving average — usually accompanied by a turn-down in the ADX itself (a normal, expected pause, not a reversal signal).
3. When price touches the 20-period EMA, place a buy stop above the high of the previous bar.
4. Once filled, place a protective sell stop at the newly formed swing low. Trail the stop as profit accrues; the trade objective is a retest of the most recent swing high. To manage the exit, consider taking partial profits at that swing high and tightening the stop on the remainder in case the trend continues further.
5. If stopped out, re-enter on a new buy stop at the original entry price.
6. After a completed trade, the ADX must turn back up above 30 before another retracement-to-the-moving-average setup can be traded — this prevents repeatedly trading a market that has stopped trending.

## Risk

Risk is defined by the distance from entry (a break of the prior bar's high) to the protective stop at the newly formed swing low/high — one worked orange juice intraday example shows a trader risking no more than $150 on the setup. The pattern typically offers two possible outcomes once triggered: the retest of the prior extreme fails and only a small profit results, or a full continuation leg develops — either way, the authors note the entry point itself carries very low risk relative to those outcomes. No fixed percent-of-equity sizing is specified beyond the book's general rules (full position at entry, hard stop immediately, trail on profit).

## Caveats

The authors explicitly warn against a common misreading of the ADX: a turndown in the ADX during the pullback is normal and does not by itself signal a trend reversal — the ADX typically peaks just as the retracement/consolidation begins, and traders who exit or reverse on ADX weakness alone will frequently be wrong. Rule 6 (waiting for ADX to turn back above 30 before re-trading) limits how often the setup can fire in a given market, trading frequency for selectivity. No independent backtested win rate or drawdown is cited for the Holy Grail specifically; the evidence given is a run of annotated wheat, Citicorp, Russell 2000, and orange juice chart examples, with the authors recommending readers study additional charts themselves to build confidence in the pattern.
