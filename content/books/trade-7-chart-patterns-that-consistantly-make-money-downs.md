## Summary

This is a presentation/workbook (originally a seminar slide deck) by OmniTrader developer Ed Downs, identifying seven chart patterns he argues are statistically predictive of price behavior, and laying out a common rule set for trading all of them: establish a reference scale, use "eighths" (38%/50%/62%) for trailing stops, and only take trades with at least a 2:1 reward:risk ratio. It cites a 2000 Economist summary of MacKinlay and Lo's academic finding that technical patterns like head-and-shoulders and triangles occur far more often than random chance would predict, especially on Nasdaq shares.

## Key points

- The 7 patterns: Support & Resistance, Trend Line Reversal & Break, Saucer Formations, Fibonacci Retracements, Price Gaps (breakaway/measured/exhaustion), Volume Climax & Volume Trend, and Consolidations.
- Each pattern can resolve as a "break" (continuation) or a "reverse" (failure) — the workbook stresses being prepared to trade both outcomes, not just one direction.
- Consolidations are called the most powerful pattern; each projects a move roughly equal to its own width, measured from the last significant high/low.
- Fibonacci levels used are the "rule of eighths": 3/8 (38%), 4/8 (50%), 5/8 (62%) — described as where markets typically reverse.
- Gap taxonomy: breakaway gaps mark the start of a move, measured gaps mark the middle, exhaustion gaps mark the end.
- Once a timeframe is chosen based on a pattern's visible size, the trader should not switch timeframes mid-trade.
- Avoid setups that reverse across 2+ reference lines within 5 bars — treated as high-volatility, low-reliability.
- Only take setups with reward:risk of at least 2:1 (preferably 3:1), measured before entry.

## Actionable rules

1. Trade a pattern only once a clear size/reference scale is established (e.g. a defined high/low for a saucer or consolidation).
2. Enter on a confirmed break of the support/resistance, trendline, saucer, or consolidation boundary in the setup's direction.
3. Place trailing stops using the eighths: enter near 1/8, stop near the zero (origin) reference, then trail via 3/8, 4/8, 5/8 as price moves favorably.
4. Reject any setup below 2:1 reward-to-risk based on the measured target vs. initial stop.
5. If stopped out, re-entry is allowed on the next "eighth" level provided reward:risk stays ≥ 2:1.

## Caveats

The document is a slide deck with minimal prose per page, so several patterns are illustrated mainly through annotated charts rather than fully written rules. No aggregate backtest statistics or win rate are given for the method as a whole, only a cited third-party academic finding that chart patterns in general are non-random. All chart examples use OmniTrader-generated signals, so results may not generalize to manual pattern identification.

## Who it is for

Discretionary chart-pattern traders who want a compact, repeatable rule framework (reference scale, eighths-based stops, 2:1 reward:risk filter) to apply consistently across different classical patterns, rather than trading each pattern with ad hoc rules.
