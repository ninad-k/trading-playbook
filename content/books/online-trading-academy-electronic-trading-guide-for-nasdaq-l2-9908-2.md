## Summary

A direct-access day-trading course built around the NASDAQ Level 2 Market Maker window, written when SOES (Small Order Execution System) and SelectNet were the primary NASDAQ order-routing tools and ECNs like Island (ISLD) were emerging. It covers market awareness, chart basics, reading Level 2 for buy/sell pressure, order types, short-selling rules, and risk management, then walks through common trading styles (scalping, trend/swing trading, multiple positions, scaling in/out) and preparation for going live from demo.

## Key points

- Level 2 shows Market Maker and ECN quotes stacked at each price; watching size and movement at the inside bid/ask gives short-term clues to buying or selling pressure before price moves.
- Scalping targets quick 1/8 and 1/4 point profits, is commission-heavy, and works best at the open and near the close when trends and liquidity are clearest.
- Trend (swing) trading holds from minutes to about two weeks, follows the daily/10-day intraday chart trend, and requires letting winners run rather than exiting at the first tick of profit.
- Multiple-position trading spreads exposure across two or three stocks in the same directional bias (long or short) but does not reduce per-share risk, so share size on each position must stay small.
- Scaling in/out — adding to a position as it moves favorably, or trimming as it moves against you — is presented as one of the best risk-management techniques.
- The NASDAQ Short Sale ("uptick") Rule, shorting against the box, and boxed accounts are covered as mechanical constraints on short positions.
- Golden rules of risk management: capital preservation first, small share size, never add to a losing trade, don't take losers home overnight, and always honor a predetermined stop.

## Actionable rules

1. Keep every stop loss under one point; a one-point loss is treated as an early warning that could become a 5-10 point loss if ignored.
2. Require a reward-to-risk ratio of at least 2:1, and for trend trades 3:1 to 4:1, before entering.
3. Use share size, not wide stops, as the primary risk control — trade smaller size in volatile stocks.
4. Raise (trail) the stop as a trend trade moves into profit; never widen a stop against the position.
5. Avoid trading 40 minutes-to-hours around major news and avoid stocks with abnormally wide spreads or erratic volume.
6. Write down every trade manually as a backup to platform records and for post-trade review.

## Caveats

Thoroughly dated to the late-1990s NASDAQ market structure: SOES, SelectNet, fractional pricing (points and teenies), and the specific ECN landscape (ISLD) no longer exist in this form after decimalization and Reg NMS. The mechanics of order routing and short-sale rules described are obsolete, though the underlying risk-management and trading-style concepts (small size, tight stops, scaling, reading order-flow pressure) still translate to modern Level 2/order-book reading.

## Who it is for

Readers wanting a historical view of pre-decimalization NASDAQ scalping and Level 2 tape-reading, or beginners who want the risk-management and trading-style framework (scalping vs. trend vs. multiple positions) independent of the outdated order-routing mechanics.
