## Summary

An 18-chapter institutional reference covering the hedge fund industry end to end: trading mandate taxonomy, the full spectrum of hedge fund risk types (market, liquidity, credit, leverage, operational, model, systemic), quantitative risk standards built around Value at Risk (VaR), fraud-detection and rating methodologies, and the evolving compliance/regulatory landscape, closing with due-diligence practices and statistical appendices.

## Key points

- **Trading mandates covered** — convertible arbitrage, distressed securities, emerging markets, equity hedge/market-neutral/statistical arbitrage, event-driven, fixed-income variants, global macro, merger arbitrage, sector strategies, short selling, funds of funds, and CTAs.
- **Risk types owned by a hedge fund risk manager** — pricing/NAV, market, liquidity, counterparty credit, leverage, operational, compliance/legal/governance, model, systemic, technology, and fiduciary risk.
- **VaR** — the maximum expected portfolio value change at a given confidence level over a given holding period; e.g., a 95% one-day VaR of $1,000,000 means losses should exceed that only ~5 of every 100 trading days.
- Three VaR methods: variance-covariance (assumes normal returns, least intensive), historical simulation (uses actual P&L history), Monte Carlo (randomized repricing, better for nonlinear strategies).
- Standard confidence levels: 95%, 97%, rarely 99%; standard holding periods: 1, 3, 5, 10 days. VaR is not "coherent" (ignores diversification) and captures only one point of the distribution — pair it with stress testing and back-testing.
- **Leverage** danger depends on the volatility/liquidity of the leveraged instrument, not the ratio alone (10:1 on T-bills is less risky than 2:1 on the S&P 500).
- **Omega** and **Sortino** ratios are offered as Sharpe-ratio alternatives that better capture skewed, non-normal hedge fund returns; Sortino replaces total volatility with downside deviation.

## Actionable rules

1. Use VaR confidence levels of at least 95% and standard holding periods of 1/3/5/10 days; keep the confidence level consistent across strategies for aggregation.
2. Never rely on VaR alone — pair it with stress testing, scenario analysis, and back-testing of actual P&L against the VaR limit.
3. Judge leverage by the volatility/liquidity of the underlying instrument and the fund's capacity to absorb losses, not by the ratio alone.
4. For skewed/fat-tailed strategies (most hedge funds), prefer Omega or Sortino over the Sharpe ratio.
5. Segment risk monitoring by position, strategy, and geography for granular, aggregatable visibility.

## Caveats

Written in 2006-2007, before Dodd-Frank/AIFMD reshaped hedge fund regulation — the compliance chapters are dated. Heavy use of academic citations and regression/hazard-ratio tables assuming a quant background. This note covers the overview and core risk-measurement chapters (7-9); the fraud-detection, ratings, and compliance-case chapters were not reviewed in depth.

## Who it is for

Hedge fund risk managers, compliance officers, and due-diligence analysts needing a structured VaR-based risk framework, rather than traders looking for entry/exit systems.
