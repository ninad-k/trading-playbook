## Summary

A January 2001 investor-education booklet from The Options Clearing Corporation covering equity option fundamentals (long/short, open/close, time decay, exercise/assignment, volatility) and then presenting nine standard strategies with a market-opinion label, a profit/loss diagram, break-even formula, and maximum profit/loss for each.

## Key points

- Option basics: a call/put conveys the right (not obligation) to buy/sell 100 shares at a strike price by expiration; the writer (seller) has the obligation and collects the premium.
- Time decay accelerates in an option's final weeks; higher volatility raises premium (positive effect on long options, negative on short) via the time-value component.
- Long call: bullish; max loss limited to premium paid, max profit unlimited; BEP = strike + premium paid.
- Long put: bearish; max loss limited to premium paid, max profit limited by stock falling to zero; BEP = strike − premium paid.
- Married put (put bought with the stock): bullish/hedged; unlimited upside, downside capped near stock cost minus put's protective value; acts as "insurance" on a new stock position.
- Covered call (long stock + short call) and covered put (short stock + short put): income/limited-protection strategies; covered call caps upside at the strike plus premium received.
- Bull call spread and bear put spread: debit spreads combining a purchased and a written option at different strikes on the same expiration; both max profit and max loss are limited.
- Collar (long stock + protective put + covered call): defines a price floor and ceiling simultaneously, financing the put's cost with the call premium.

## Actionable rules

1. Long call/put break-even: strike price ± premium paid (add for calls, subtract for puts).
2. A written (short) option can be assigned at any time before expiration; close the position before market close to avoid assignment on a given day.
3. Deep in-the-money puts are frequently exercised early by professional traders — short put writers in that position should expect assignment risk.
4. Covered call writers should track dividend dates: in-the-money short calls face elevated early-assignment risk ahead of an ex-dividend date.
5. Spread strategies (bull call, bear put) cap both maximum profit and maximum loss at initiation — net debit paid defines the loss ceiling; strike-width minus debit defines the profit ceiling.

## Caveats

Written for U.S. exchange-listed equity options as of 2001; the exchange list (AMEX, CBOE, ISE, PCX, PHLX) and some venue references are dated by industry consolidation since. All P&L examples explicitly exclude commissions, transaction costs, and taxes, and the booklet does not address early exercise economics, implied volatility, the Greeks, or multi-leg strategies beyond the nine listed. It is a definitional glossary and strategy primer, not an trading edge or entry/exit system.

## Who it is for

Beginners who need a clear, vendor-neutral reference for how basic equity option strategies are structured and their risk/reward profiles, before moving to more advanced options texts in this library.
