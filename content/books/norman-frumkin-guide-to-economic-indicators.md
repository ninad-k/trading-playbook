## Summary

A structured reference work (published by M.E. Sharpe) covering more than 60 US government economic indicators organized into 49 alphabetically-arranged chapters — from Average Weekly Earnings through Value of the Dollar — each explaining what the series measures, which federal agency (BLS, BEA, Census Bureau, Federal Reserve) produces it, when it is released, and how to interpret its movements. Frumkin groups the same 49 indicators into thematic categories in the preface (economic growth, household income/expenditure, business profits, labor, inflation, production, housing, finance, government, international, cyclical/forecasting, well-being, and psychology) to help readers navigate by topic rather than alphabet.

## Key points

- **Chapter structure** — each entry follows a consistent format: definition, "Where and When Available" (source agency and release schedule), and interpretation/context, often with an accompanying data table.
- **Thematic groupings** — the same indicators are cross-referenced across 13 broad categories (e.g., CPI appears under both "Household Income and Expenditure" and "Inflation and Deflation"), reflecting that most indicators matter for more than one purpose.
- **Leading/Coincident/Lagging Indexes chapter** — covers the Conference Board's composite indexes used for business-cycle forecasting, a core cyclical-analysis tool.
- **Data-sharing context (2002 legislation)** — the preface explains a US legal change (CIPSEA/E-Government Act 2002) enabling BLS, BEA, and Census to share confidential business survey data, improving indicator quality and coverage going forward.
- **Coverage breadth** — includes not just headline releases (GDP, CPI, Unemployment, Retail Sales) but secondary/niche series such as Farm Parity Ratio, Housing Affordability Index, Job Openings and Labor Turnover, and International Investment Position of the United States.
- **Appendix on survey methodology** — a dedicated appendix explains sampling and non-sampling error in the government surveys underlying these indicators, useful context for judging how much noise to expect in any single release.

## Actionable rules

None given — this is an encyclopedic reference on what each indicator measures and how it is produced, not a trading or forecasting system. It does not rank indicators by market impact, give release-time trading tactics, or provide entry/exit rules (contrast with the shorter, impact-graded reference u-s-key-economic-indica in this library).

## Caveats

At 305 pages and 49 chapters, this is a dense reference text meant to be consulted per-indicator rather than read cover to cover; this note is based on the table of contents, preface, and the opening chapter (Average Weekly Earnings) rather than a full read of all 49 chapters, so chapter-specific numeric details (formulas, historical tables) are not reproduced here. The edition catalogued (following a 2004 Employment Cost Index table and other circa-2004/2005 data references) appears to be the mid-2000s edition; series definitions and release schedules for individual indicators may have been revised by their source agencies since.

## Who it is for

Traders, analysts, or students who want an authoritative one-stop reference for what a specific US economic indicator actually measures, which agency publishes it, and when — best used as a lookup reference alongside a market-impact-focused source like u-s-key-economic-indica.
