## What it is

The Impulse System identifies bars where market "inertia" (trend direction, measured by a moving average) and "momentum" (rate of change, measured by MACD-Histogram) point the same way — the inflection points where a move speeds up. It works on any timeframe, including intraday, and is designed to be combined with Triple Screen rather than used as a standalone system: apply Triple Screen (or another method) to pick the higher-timeframe trend direction, then use the Impulse System on the intermediate chart to filter entries and, especially, to force fast exits.

## Rules

**Indicators**: a 13-day EMA of closing price (inertia) and a 12-26-9 MACD-Histogram (momentum), plotted on the intermediate (e.g., daily) chart.

**Higher-timeframe filter**: first determine the long-term trend using Triple Screen's Screen One (e.g., 26-week EMA slope on the weekly chart, or weekly MACD-Histogram slope). Only take Impulse System entries in that direction.

**Buy signal**: both the 13-day EMA and MACD-Histogram are rising on the same bar. Color that bar green (or otherwise flag it) — this identifies an accelerating uptrend. Enter long, or stay long, as long as both indicators keep confirming.

**Sell signal**: both the 13-day EMA and MACD-Histogram are falling on the same bar (flag red). Enter short, or stay short, as long as both keep confirming.

**No-signal bars**: when the EMA and MACD-Histogram disagree, the bar is unmarked — do not treat it as a trade signal in either direction.

**Exit rule**: exit the instant either indicator stops confirming the trend — do not wait for a full reversal signal from both. In practice MACD-Histogram usually turns first as momentum fades; when the buy (or sell) coloring disappears, close the position without waiting for the opposite-color signal.

**Negative-rule filter for other systems**: even when another system (e.g., Triple Screen) flags a trade, check the Impulse System first. If Impulse currently shows a sell signal, do not buy (even on an otherwise-valid Triple Screen buy) — wait for the sell coloring to clear. Mirror this for shorts against an Impulse buy signal. This "hold off" rule is presented as one of the most useful risk-reduction filters in the book.

**Parameter testing**: Elder explicitly invites testing variable EMA lengths and different entry timing (enter at the close 15 minutes before the bell, at tomorrow's open, or attempt to anticipate the signal) since the book does not fix these choices as final.

## Risk

The system is built to "enter cautiously, exit fast" — position sizing and stop placement still follow the parent book's 2%/6% Rules; a hard stop should sit below the most recent low (for longs) at entry, and the Impulse System's own exit rule (loss of dual confirmation) is typically hit well before a technical stop, functioning as a second, faster exit layer. Elder warns explicitly against trading this system with any discipline weakness: "Do not touch this system if you have the slightest problem with discipline" — because momentum trades move fast and taking profits early is psychologically hard to accept without regret.

## Caveats

Like Triple Screen, the Impulse System is a filter/method rather than a fully mechanical, backtested system in the book; no formal win-rate or expectancy statistics are given, only chart illustrations of the EMA/MACD-Histogram combination tracking real bull and bear moves. It performs best in markets with a clear directional impulse and is explicitly described as unreliable in indecisive, choppy markets where buy and sell colorings alternate rapidly ("if in doubt, stay out"). It should not be traded in isolation from a higher-timeframe trend filter (Elder repeatedly ties it back to Triple Screen's weekly analysis).
