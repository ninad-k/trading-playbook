## Summary

A short FXCM/DailyFX research report updating a 2005 study of which US economic releases produce the biggest USD price moves. It compares rankings from 2004 data against a fresh 12-month window (June 2005-June 2006), measuring dollar reaction at three horizons — 20 minutes, 60 minutes, and full daily range — and finds that ISM Manufacturing had risen to become the single most market-moving release on the daily-range measure, overtaking Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP).

## Key points

- On the 20-minute knee-jerk measure, Unemployment (NFP) and the FOMC interest rate decision remained the top two movers in both 2004 and 2006 rankings, but Trade Balance, Inflation (CPI), and Retail Sales rose in importance while Foreign Purchases of US Treasuries (TIC) fell from 3rd to 6th.
- On the full daily-range measure, ISM Manufacturing became the #1 mover in 2006 (up from not appearing in the 2004 top rankings' #1 spot, where NFP had led), attributed to ISM being an early-month leading indicator whose prices-paid and employment subcomponents foreshadow later CPI/PPI and payrolls releases.
- Average daily move on release day: ISM Manufacturing 130 pips (2006, #1) vs. Non-Farm Payrolls 193 pips (2004, #1) — reflecting a broader decline in EUR/USD volatility, not just a change in which indicator leads.
- EUR/USD average daily trading range fell from 111 pips (2004) to 104 pips (2005-2006), attributed partly to narrowing global interest-rate differentials.
- Some widely-watched releases (Industrial Production, University of Michigan Consumer Confidence) don't rank highly because they are chronically released alongside other competing data, diluting their standalone market impact.
- The 60-minute measure largely mirrors the 20-minute list, with the main change being Trade Balance and the FOMC decision swapping relative rank — suggesting the initial reaction to top indicators tends to persist or extend rather than reverse.

## Actionable rules

None given as trading rules — this is a fundamental-analysis reference table, not a system. The practical takeaway for a news/fundamentals-aware trader: as of mid-2006, ISM Manufacturing (released first business day of the month) and Non-Farm Payrolls were the two releases most likely to produce a significant same-day USD move, with Trade Balance, CPI, and the FOMC decision as secondary tier-one events to watch.

## Caveats

This is a point-in-time snapshot (data through June 2006) from a broker-affiliated research desk (FXCM/DailyFX); the specific rankings and pip-range figures are now purely historical and would need to be re-run against current data to be useful for present-day trading — economic-release market impact shifts with the prevailing macro regime (rate cycle, inflation focus, etc.), as the report itself demonstrates by comparing 2004 vs. 2006. No methodology detail is given beyond "average daily movement," so exact abnormal-return or event-study rigor (e.g., controlling for concurrent releases) is unclear.

## Who it is for

Forex traders who want a concrete historical illustration of how to rank and monitor economic-release impact on a currency, as a template for building or updating their own release-impact watchlist.
