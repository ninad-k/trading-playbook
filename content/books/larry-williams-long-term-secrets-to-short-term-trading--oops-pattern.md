## What it is

Williams calls this "the most reliable of all short-term patterns I have researched and traded," first taught to his students in 1978 and later popularized by other authors (some crediting him, some not). It fades an overextended opening gap: when the open itself moves further than the market's normal overnight range, the resulting panic (short sellers on a down gap, chasers on an up gap) tends to reverse once price claws back into the prior day's range, catching the emotional crowd on the wrong side just as their broker calls to say "Oops!"

## Rules

**Buy setup:** today's open is below the previous day's low (a rare, "unusual" occurrence since price almost always opens within the prior day's range). Entry trigger: price rallies back up to the previous day's low — buy.

**Sell setup:** today's open is above the previous day's high. Entry trigger: price falls back down to the previous day's high — sell.

**Stops and exits (as backtested):**
- S&P 500 buys, any day except Wednesday/Thursday: $2,000 stop, exit next day's opening; 1990–1998 sample showed >82% accuracy, $42,687 total profit, $438 average profit per trade (average trade length ~1.5 days).
- Bond buys, any day except Wednesday: $1,800 stop, bailout exit; 86% accuracy, $27,875 profit, $201 average profit per trade after $50 commission.
- Bond sells, Wednesday only: $1,000 stop, 4-day bailout exit; since 1990, 55 trades / 31 winners, $9,875 net.
- S&P sells, best day Thursday: 78% winners, $14,200 profit.
- Layered with a 9-day moving-average filter (buy Bonds any day but Thursday if Friday's 9-day MA < Thursday's; exit close of first profitable opening after 3 days in trade): 81% accuracy, $24,625 profit, $373 average profit per trade. Sell side (9-day MA rising Tuesday vs. Monday, sell Wednesday): 79% accuracy, $13,406 profit, $394 average profit per trade.
- S&P layered with 9-day MA filter: buy days Tuesday/Wednesday/Friday, 81% accuracy, $22,650 profit, $456 average profit per trade; sell Wednesday, 89% accuracy on 35 trades, $18,962 profit, $486 average profit per trade.
- Calendar overlay: taking Oops! signals only in the first-of-month rally window (Bonds: first TDM through the 5th) produces comparably strong results; Williams flags this himself as a possible curve-fitting risk but notes the underlying end-of-month rally tendency (documented since Art Merrill's 1965 work) predates his own discovery of it in Bonds.

## Risk

- Every quoted backtest pairs the pattern with a fixed dollar stop (ranging $1,000–$2,000 depending on market and variant) plus the bailout exit (close on first profitable opening, delayed for slower markets).
- Trade duration is short by design — often under 2 days — which the book treats as a risk-reducer versus holding through unrelated overnight news.
- No aggregate portfolio heat or correlation guidance is given specific to Oops!; use the book's general fixed-fraction sizing formula (risk% of equity ÷ largest historical loss per contract).

## Caveats

- All results are in-sample backtests over specific historical windows (1990–1998 for Bonds/most tests, 1982–1998 for the S&P) with no out-of-sample validation shown.
- Adding the calendar (TDM) filter narrows the sample considerably; Williams acknowledges this raises legitimate curve-fitting concerns even while defending the underlying seasonal logic.
- Commission and slippage are only explicitly netted out in the Bond example ($50/trade); other cited profit figures do not state whether they are net or gross of costs.
