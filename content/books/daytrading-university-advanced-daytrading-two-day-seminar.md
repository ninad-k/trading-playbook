## Summary

This is the participant handout from a two-day live Nasdaq day-trading seminar (August 2001), built around table activities and worksheets rather than continuous prose. It teaches a multi-monitor scanning workflow (Nasdaq futures, sector indices, TRINQ, individual stock charts) for finding tradable stocks at the open, breaks the trading day into named timing windows (9:30–10:00 "10-minute reversals," 10:00–11:30 "15–20 minute trends"), and covers chart-pattern entries (cup breakouts, consolidation breakouts), Fibonacci retracement exits, and a checklist of red flags for staying out of a trade. Content is aimed at active/scalp traders using Level 2 and time & sales, in the pre-decimalization-transition Nasdaq market structure.

## Key points

- Premarket workflow: track Nasdaq futures trend into the open (a swing from, e.g., +32 down to +11 before 9:30 is treated as a bearish signal; moves under ~10 points either way are "no signal"), and classify each sector's gap size (under 0.5% = no action; 0.5–1.0% = look for trend continuation; over 1.0% = watch for gap reversal).
- Divides the trading day into distinct tactical windows, each with its own stop width and holding time: 9:30–10:00 ("10-minute reversals," max 8-minute round trips, very tight 2–3 spread stops, trade off the tape), 9:50–10:00 (pivot/second reversal), and 10:00–11:30 ("15–20 minute trends," wider 3–4 spread stops, chart patterns matter as much as tape).
- Two core breakout setups: the "cup" pattern (enter 0.3–0.5 above the previous day's high, favored for high follow-through probability) and consolidation/triangle breakouts (confirm with volume, watch for false breakouts in choppy markets).
- Fibonacci retracement levels used are 24/38/50/62/78%; the taught "Fibo shorting strategy" estimates the stock's average daily range, marks the 50% retracement from the prior day's low, and trails a cover stop as price nears that level.
- A written pre-trade checklist of red flags that should keep a trader out of a setup: price already extended 70–90% of its typical daily range or run length, TRINQ above 1.5 for longs (or below 0.7 for shorts), a choppy/non-clean chart, trading late in a run ("minute 8 of a 10-minute trend"), and personal fatigue or impatience.
- Stops are described as tight and structural: roughly 2–3 bid/ask spreads for the fastest opening-window trades, widening to 3–4 spreads with chart support/resistance for the mid-morning window; the author favors "the red band, 3–4 levels outside on Level 2" as a stop reference.
- Recommends trading stocks with a 2–4 point average daily range and using a non-standard 22-period simple moving average on a 1-minute chart, plus 20/80 (or safer 10/90) stochastic crossovers, as secondary confirmation/exit tools, not primary signals.

## Actionable rules

1. Only take a trade in the direction of the sector and Nasdaq Composite (COMPQ) trend; do not fade the major index/sector direction without strong contrary tape evidence.
2. Set breakout alerts 0.3–0.5 points above/below the prior day's high/low; avoid buying at round "whole number" resistance (e.g., avoid entries right at .9x prices) until it clears.
3. Disqualify a stock from that day's trading list if it stayed inside its prior 2-day high/low range, or if the prior day was a "chop chart," unless its range exceeds roughly 3 points.
4. Cap opening-window (9:30–10:00) trade duration at about 8 minutes with 2–3 spread stops; widen to 3–4 spreads with chart-based stops for 10:00–11:30 trend trades.
5. Walk through the full red-flag checklist before entering; skip the trade if two or more items apply (e.g., price already 70%+ through its typical daily range, TRINQ outside the stated bounds, or a choppy Composite).
6. Use Fibonacci 50% retracement of the average daily range from the prior close as a trailing-stop reference point on bounce/short trades.

## Caveats

Written for the specific 2001 Nasdaq SuperSOES/decimalization-transition market microstructure (Island ECN sweeps, bid/ask "spreads" as the stop-sizing unit, TRINQ, Level 2 order books) — the execution mechanics are dated and would need translation to modern order-routing and decimal-cent tick sizes. The material is structured as seminar worksheets and discussion prompts (table activities, fill-in-the-blank exercises) rather than a fully narrated system, so several techniques are sketched rather than completely specified. No formal backtest, win rate, or risk-of-ruin data is presented; the guidance is experience-based trader lore from the instructor.

## Who it is for

Active/scalp traders already familiar with Level 2 quotes and time & sales who want a structured intraday timing framework and a pre-trade red-flag checklist, rather than a beginner introduction to day trading mechanics.
