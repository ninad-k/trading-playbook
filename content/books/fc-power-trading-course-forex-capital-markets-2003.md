## Summary

This 2003 course manual introduces retail foreign exchange from market structure through execution, macro drivers, chart tools, currency profiles, and risk control. Early sections argue that liquidity, transparent executable prices, low transaction costs, and persistent trends make a market tradeable (PDF pp. 4-6). The curriculum then covers quotes, profit and loss, rollover, orders, central banks and country indicators, candlesticks, Fibonacci retracements, moving averages, Bollinger Bands, stochastics, and MACD. Its last section turns the material into 14 broad rules for survival.

## Key points

- Liquidity affects both slippage and the ability to exit; headline volume is not a substitute for executable depth.
- Spot FX is decentralized and participants receive different access and pricing according to credit and size (PDF p. 18).
- The manual's 100:1 leverage example shows how a 0.5% currency move can become a 50% change on posted margin; the dated marketing tone understates the danger (PDF p. 12).
- Fundamental analysis links currencies to interest rates, growth, external balances, policy, and risk appetite.
- Technical tools are presented as timing aids rather than self-sufficient forecasts.
- Money management means determining loss before entry and enforcing the exit when invalidated.
- Taking profits too quickly while allowing losses to expand creates a structurally poor payoff profile (PDF p. 121).
- **Session structure and volumes as given in 2003** — Tokyo 7:00pm-3:00am EDT with average daily volume around $150bn; London 3:00am-11:00am EDT with around $570bn and roughly 30% of all transactions, described as the most influential session (PDF p. 29).
- **Equity-index links** — the Dow is presented as the index with most influence on the dollar, with the Nikkei mapped to JPY, the FTSE to GBP and the DAX to EUR (PDF p. 46); the carry-trade discussion notes that carry positions are most likely to fail in risk-averse periods, when the high-yielding currencies they depend on tend to depreciate (PDF p. 52).
- **Indicator conventions taught** — Fibonacci retracements at 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% drawn from a base to a recent high (PDF p. 69); stochastics read conventionally, selling above 80 and buying below 20 (PDF p. 75).
- Part 4 profiles each major currency's economy and drivers (the New Zealand profile, for example, notes Australia and Japan together account for about 30% of its trade), and the manual closes with a long glossary of FX terms (PDF pp. 81-120, 132-143).

## Actionable rules

1. Calculate the maximum dollar or percentage loss before placing a trade, then enter the corresponding stop with the order.
2. Exit when the trade premise fails. The manual's breakout example treats a return through the breakout level as invalidation rather than “noise” (PDF p. 126).
3. After four or five consecutive losing trades, stop and reassess; the exact pause should match the trading horizon (PDF p. 126).
4. Use limit entries for pullbacks and stop entries for breakouts; do not confuse the two order logics.
5. Research the reason for each trade and keep the strategy adaptable as conditions change.

## Caveats

The note is based on the scan's extractable text, which covers roughly 29 of the 143 pages — the file is a sampled OCR, so intervening pages could not be read. Market-size figures, spreads, leverage, platform descriptions, and session practices date from 2003. The text was produced by a broker and often presents retail FX favorably, so its claims need independent testing.

## Who it is for

Beginners who need a structured overview of FX terminology, macro context, technical tools, order handling, and basic risk discipline.
