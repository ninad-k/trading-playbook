## Summary

Terry combines two chart timeframes: the longer timeframe defines trend direction and a continuation or countertrend pullback, while the shorter timeframe supplies a reversal pattern and trigger. The examples use one-minute/five-minute and daily/weekly combinations (p. 1). In the SPY example, a five-minute rising channel contains a one-minute head-and-shoulders top; the short trigger is a break of the right-shoulder trendline or neckline (p. 2). A stop is placed just above the right shoulder. Profit-taking uses a 50% retracement, a head-to-neckline measured objective, and the prior major low, with a trailing stop at declining resistance (p. 3).

## Key points

- The approach trades with the longer trend while using the shorter pattern to time entry (pp. 1-2).
- Support and resistance help anticipate the right shoulder and validate the pattern.
- Multiple confirmations can improve trade structure but cannot guarantee success.
- Partial profits and a trailing stop allow management as a trade progresses (p. 3).
- The same logic is reversed for long trades.

## Actionable rules

1. Choose a longer trend chart and a shorter trigger chart, such as 5-minute plus 1-minute or weekly plus daily (p. 1).
2. Enter on penetration of the shorter reversal trendline or neckline and set the stop beyond the right shoulder (pp. 2-3).
3. Consider partial profits at a 50% retracement, then a measured head-to-neckline target and major support (p. 3).

## Caveats

The examples are chart illustrations rather than a quantified test. Timeframe pairs and targets are choices from the examples, and execution costs or gaps can change results.

## Who it is for

Discretionary traders who want a structured multi-timeframe chart-pattern workflow.
