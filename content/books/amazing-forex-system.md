## Summary

Borowski’s system attempts to capture the rapid FX move that often follows a fundamental announcement. The trader selects an announcement from an economic calendar, places one buy-stop and one sell-stop shortly before release, and lets the initial jump determine direction. The manual claims moves of 30–60 pips and sometimes 100+, while recommending a default 20-pip limit or discretionary trailing (pp. 4–5). It also emphasizes demo practice and equity limits, although its marketing claims are not supported by a controlled study.

## Key points

- The core idea is direction-neutral: the trader needs the announcement time and a suitable pair, not a forecast of good or bad news (pp. 4–5, 10–12).
- The recommended stop is 10 pips, paired with a 20-pip default limit for a nominal 2:1 reward-to-risk ratio (p. 5).
- The manual prefers multiple announcements at the same time, especially at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, and names EUR/USD and GBP/USD as primary pairs (pp. 41–43).
- After a 10-pip gain, the stop can be moved to breakeven; after a 30-pip gain, it can be moved to lock 20 pips (p. 35).
- A “baby-sitting” mode trails by 15 pips, or 10 pips before pullbacks; “sailing on” keeps a protected position for days when the broader trend agrees (pp. 35–38).
- The author instructs traders to close before Friday afternoon and avoid weekend gaps (pp. 37–38).

## Actionable rules

1. Review the next day’s announcement calendar and select the strongest five or six opportunities; favor two or more same-country releases (pp. 11, 41–43).
2. Shortly before release, place buy and sell entries around the recent range with a 10-pip stop and a 20-pip limit; examples use 10-pip entry-to-stop and 20-pip entry-to-target distances (pp. 5, 45–46).
3. Cancel the unfilled side once one order triggers; if price has not jumped within about three minutes, cancel the setup (p. 41).
4. Use no more than 2% of margin equity per trade, except the author’s temporary 5% allowance for very small mini accounts (p. 8).
5. At a 10-pip favorable move, shift the stop to breakeven; at 30 pips, lock 20. Then take the fixed limit or trail, and close all open trades before the weekend (pp. 35–38).

## Caveats

The source claims high win rates and rapid account growth but provides no audited sample. News releases can produce spread widening, slippage, whipsaws, and both orders filling. The 10-pip stop and 2%/5% risk guidance may be unrealistic around volatile releases. The recommendation to compound 100%–200% monthly gains is promotional rather than evidence-based (pp. 3–8, 40–48).

## Who it is for

Beginners studying the mechanics of event-driven orders. Use a broker-specific demo and independent execution statistics before considering live risk.
