## Summary

This transcript records NickTrader explaining a “no price” approach to Woodie’s CCI signals. He says traders can focus on CCI patterns rather than watching price, treating regular divergence as a reversal clue and reverse divergence as a continuation signal taken with the trend (pp. 1–5). The discussion uses CCI trendlines, trendline breaks (TLB), high-frequency extremes (HFE), and triple divergence. NickTrader says the first point should be above the +100 or below the −100 line for one setup, a divergence trendline becomes invalid after CCI spends more than five bars on the opposite side, and a triple divergence remains valid when points stay on the same CCI side within roughly 10–12 bars (pp. 5–9). Turbo CCI is described as an early warning or trendline aid, not the signal itself (p. 10). For execution, he prefers limit orders—on the bid for longs and ask for shorts—and gives a fixed 10-tick YM stop as his safety net, while acknowledging that volatility can make fixed stops vulnerable (p. 5). Failed signals should be exited quickly, and a “ghost” head-and-shoulders pattern is treated as reversal information that argues against a continuation setup (pp. 11–12).

## Key points

- The method deliberately removes price context, relying on CCI shape and prior trend.
- The transcript’s numerical windows are speaker heuristics, not measured probabilities (pp. 5–9).
- Participants explicitly debate whether price and volatility should filter the signals (pp. 2, 5, 11).

## Actionable rules

1. Treat reverse divergence as a continuation setup with the trend and regular divergence as a reversal setup; the transcript bases both on CCI pattern shape (pp. 1–4).
2. In the described divergence setup, require the first point beyond ±100, invalidate the line after more than five bars on the opposite CCI side, and keep triple-divergence points on the same side within roughly 10–12 bars (pp. 5–8).
3. Use Turbo CCI only to draw a trendline or as an early warning, not as the entry signal; exit a failed signal quickly (pp. 9–11). The 10-tick YM stop is NickTrader’s personal safety net, not a volatility-adjusted rule (p. 5).

## Caveats

This is anecdotal chat-room instruction with screenshots unavailable in the extracted text, no trade log, and no performance study. “99%” correctness is a participant claim, not evidence. Fixed ticks, limit fills, and pattern windows may not transfer across instruments or regimes.

## Who it is for

Experienced CCI users evaluating whether visual simplification improves execution discipline.
