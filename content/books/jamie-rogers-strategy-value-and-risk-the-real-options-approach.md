## Summary

This is a corporate strategy and valuation book, not a trading manual. It argues that traditional discounted cash flow (DCF) and NPV analysis undervalue strategic investments because they ignore management's flexibility to defer, expand, contract, abandon, or switch a project as uncertainty resolves — flexibility that financial option theory can price. The book surveys the evolution of strategy and valuation thinking, introduces the mechanics of option pricing (Black-Scholes, binomial lattices, Monte Carlo), and works through three recurring case studies (software, energy, pharmaceuticals) comparing DCF-only valuation against a real-options valuation.

## Key points

- **Real option** — the right, not the obligation, to take a business action (invest, expand, abandon) in the future, priced using the same no-arbitrage logic as financial options.
- Six standard real-option types covered: option to defer, to expand/contract, to abandon, to switch, growth options, and compound options (options on options); rainbow options for multiple sources of uncertainty are also introduced.
- No-arbitrage/replicating-portfolio pricing logic requires that a traded "twin security" exist whose risk profile matches the project; when it doesn't, the gap is called "private risk" (per Robert Merton's 1997 Nobel lecture framework).
- Distinguishes real options analysis (market-based, risk-neutral pricing) from decision-tree/decision-analysis methods (subjective utility-based valuation).
- Covers option pricing mechanics: Black-Scholes formula, binomial lattices, and numerical/Monte Carlo techniques, plus spot-price and forward-curve models for implementing real options on physical assets (e.g., energy).
- Game theory is presented as a complement to real options when competitor actions can erode the value of waiting to invest.
- Case studies apply the framework to an IT restructuring decision, an energy spread option, and pharmaceutical R&D staging (using Agouron Pharmaceuticals as an example, comparing model output to actual stock prices).
- Frames the CFO's evolving role as integrating real-options-based strategic analysis into corporate finance, not just external reporting.

## Actionable rules

None given as trading rules — this is a corporate investment-valuation methodology, not a market-trading system. Its closest transferable idea for options/derivatives traders: real options make the differences between an option to defer, expand, or abandon investment explicit and price them with the same call/put logic used in listed options, which is a useful mental model when reasoning about optionality in any risk position.

## Caveats

Written for corporate finance practitioners, CFOs, and investment analysts valuing strategic projects and M&A — not for retail or systematic traders. Assumes familiarity with DCF, CAPM, and basic option pricing. No entries, exits, stops, or position sizing of any kind; several sections (pharmaceutical case study, DCF assumptions) reflect early-2000s data and drug-development economics.

## Who it is for

Corporate finance, M&A, and valuation professionals wanting to apply option-pricing theory to real investment decisions; useful background for derivatives traders wanting a rigorous framework for optionality outside listed markets, but not a source of trading rules.
