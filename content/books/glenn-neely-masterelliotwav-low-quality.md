## Summary

Glenn Neely reorganizes Elliott-wave analysis into a procedure intended for real-time use. The book delays conventional progress labels such as 1-2-3-4-5 and a-b-c until after more basic observations, arguing that premature labeling and subjective degree assignments cause confusion (p. 13). Its contents move from monowaves and structural labels through polywaves, impulsive and corrective patterns, Fibonacci relationships, channeling, and advanced “Neely Extensions” (pp. 3-6).

The sampled material shows a method built around conditional classification rather than a single entry signal. Adjacent price movements are compared for similarity in price and time before they may be joined into a larger pattern; satisfying that comparison makes a shared degree possible, not certain (p. 59). Later chapters use internal and external Fibonacci relationships as diagnostic levels, and treat a reversal at a projected level as confirmation of its relevance (p. 153).

## Key points

- Structure is established before conventional wave labels and degree judgments (p. 13).
- The Rule of Similarity and Balance tests whether adjacent waves can plausibly belong to the same degree (p. 59).
- The framework distinguishes internal length comparisons from external price projections (p. 153).
- External projections shown in the sample use 61.8%, 100%, and 161.8% of a reference wave (p. 153).
- Multiple valid counts are treated as information about location within a larger pattern, not merely analyst failure (p. 161).

## Actionable rules

1. Do not combine adjacent movements into one Elliott pattern until their price and time proportions satisfy the book's similarity test; passing the test is permission to investigate, not proof of equal degree (p. 59).
2. For an external projection, calculate 61.8%, 100%, and 161.8% of the reference wave and add or subtract those amounts from its terminal price. Treat the results as potential support or resistance, confirmed only if price stops and reverses there (p. 153).
3. When several counts remain equally acceptable, avoid a new wave-count trade until the alternatives narrow. If already profitably positioned from a previously clear count, an increase in alternatives can indicate the middle of a larger formation rather than its end (p. 161).

## Caveats

Only the OCR-recovered pages listed above were reviewed. The scan is noisy, and many decision trees depend on diagrams and intervening rules that were not continuously available. Elliott-wave counts remain interpretation-sensitive; the book provides a classification framework, not independently tested performance evidence.

## Who it is for

Advanced Elliott-wave students who want a more formal vocabulary and a disciplined way to revise counts.
