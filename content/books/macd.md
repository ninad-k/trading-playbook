## Summary

A very short educational lesson on the Moving Average Convergence-Divergence (MACD) indicator, originally constructed by analyst Gerald Appel. It explains how MACD is built (the difference between two moving averages, plus a signal line that is a moving average of that difference), the standard default settings, and the three common ways traders use it: line crossovers as buy/sell signals, distance from the zero line as an overbought/oversold gauge, and price/indicator divergence as a reversal warning. The author then describes his own personal variant: using MACD on a higher timeframe purely as a trend filter (not for entries) while executing trades on a lower timeframe, illustrated with a same-day 30-minute/5-minute Cash DJIA example.

## Key points

- MACD is the difference between two exponential moving averages (EMAs), plotted as a fast line, against a signal line that is itself an EMA of that difference.
- Standard/default settings: 12 and 26-period EMAs for the MACD line, with a 9-period EMA as the signal line.
- **Crossover signal** — buy when the faster (signal) line crosses above the slower (MACD) line; sell on the reverse cross.
- **Overbought/oversold** — the further both lines move above the zero line, the more overbought; the further below, the more oversold.
- A crossover that occurs while overbought/oversold (signal line turning down from overbought, or up from oversold) is described as a stronger signal than a crossover alone.
- **Divergence** — bullish divergence: MACD makes a new low while price does not; bearish divergence: MACD makes a high and turns down while price continues up — both flagged as possible reversal warnings.
- Author's personal settings: 8 and 18-period EMAs with a 9-period signal line, used specifically as a higher-timeframe trend filter rather than for direct entries.
- Multi-timeframe approach: check MACD trend direction on a higher timeframe (e.g., weekly for a daily trader, daily for an hourly trader) and only take trades in that direction on the lower timeframe as long as the higher-timeframe signal line stays on its side of the MACD line.

## Actionable rules

1. Standard MACD settings: 12-period and 26-period EMA with a 9-period EMA signal line.
2. Enter long when the signal line crosses above the MACD line; enter short on the reverse cross.
3. Treat a crossover as a higher-conviction signal when it occurs from an overbought (for a sell) or oversold (for a buy) zone relative to the zero line.
4. Watch for bullish divergence (MACD higher low vs. price lower low) or bearish divergence (MACD lower high vs. price higher high) as an early reversal warning.
5. Author's own multi-timeframe filter: use MACD (8, 18, 9) on a higher timeframe strictly to define the tradeable direction; only take same-direction entries on the lower/execution timeframe; close all positions in the trade direction if the higher-timeframe MACD signal line crosses back.
