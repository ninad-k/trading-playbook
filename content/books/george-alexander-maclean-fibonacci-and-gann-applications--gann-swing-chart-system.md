## What it is

A standalone charting and trading method from Chapter 9 ("Gann, The Misunderstood Analysis"), built around a single derived line — the Trend Indicator Line (TIL) — plotted from nothing more than daily highs and lows. The author presents it as one of Gann's most approachable and underused tools, simple enough to build by hand, and unlike the book's other Fibonacci/Gann tools (retracements, fanlines, arcs), it comes with its own complete entry, exit, and stop-loss logic rather than serving only as a support/resistance overlay.

## Rules

**Constructing the Trend Indicator Line (TIL)**
1. Compare each day's high/low to the prior day's: if today's high exceeds yesterday's high, move the TIL up to today's high; if today's low is below yesterday's low, move the TIL down to today's low.
2. **Inside days** (today's high and low both fall within yesterday's range) are generally ignored, except when a reversal follows an inside day.
3. **Outside days** (today's high and low both exceed yesterday's range on both ends): draw the TIL to whichever extreme (the high or the low) occurred first during the session, then reverse it to the opposite extreme.

**Entry**
4. A **buy signal** fires once the swing chart has formed a base (a period of TIL consolidation) and price breaks out above the top of that range.
5. A **sell signal** fires once the swing chart has formed congestive highs and price breaks out below the bottom of that range.

**Stop-loss**
6. Place the protective stop at the level of the TIL's most recent change of direction (i.e., the last swing point where the line reversed) — a structural, market-defined stop rather than a fixed percentage or dollar amount.

## Risk

Risk per trade is defined entirely by chart structure: the distance between the breakout entry price and the TIL's last reversal point sets the initial stop distance, so risk varies with each setup's volatility and consolidation width rather than being fixed in advance. The source gives no position-sizing, percent-of-equity, or profit-target rule for this system — it is presented purely as an entry/exit/stop mechanism, leaving trade sizing and profit-taking to the trader's own money-management framework (the parent book provides none of its own).

## Caveats

The author calls this "a fairly simple application" that gives "excellent entry and exit points," but offers no backtested win rate, sample size, or drawdown data — the endorsement is experiential, not statistical. Because entries depend on visually identifying a "base" or "congestive highs" before the breakout, the pattern-recognition step retains some subjectivity despite the otherwise mechanical high/low construction rule. The method also inherits the book's general Gann caveat: it was designed around daily bars in markets with five-day trading weeks (or a 360-day Gann interest year for the fanline/square tools elsewhere in the book), and the author notes this form of charting is "even less popular than Point and Figure charting" today, suggesting limited independent modern validation outside this text.
