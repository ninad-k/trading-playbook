## Overview

This is the printed training manual that accompanies Jake Bernstein's four-part video seminar series "Trade Your Way to Riches Now!" Unlike a conventional prose book, it is organized almost entirely as a slide deck: each page is a labeled chart or diagram (with occasional handwritten annotations reproduced from the original slides) rather than paragraphs of explanatory text. The manual is split into four modules — Introduction to Futures Trading, Cycles in Futures Trading, Introduction to Seasonals, and Seasonals Part II — moving from futures-contract basics (leverage, contract specs, the trading floor) through cyclical price analysis, seasonal tendency charts, and finally Bernstein's "Key Date Seasonal" system, which pairs a specific calendar entry/exit window with a historical win-rate and average-profit table for a given futures market.

## Core thesis

Futures prices exhibit recurring, statistically measurable calendar-based and cyclical tendencies (seasonals and cycles) that can be identified from historical data and traded with defined entry dates, exit dates, and stop-losses. Bernstein presents this as complementary to, not a replacement for, standard chart-based technical analysis (gaps, moving averages, trendlines) and market-sentiment measurement (his Daily Sentiment Index). The manual's implicit argument is that a trader who combines seasonal/cyclical timing with basic technical confirmation and sentiment extremes has a statistical edge that a purely discretionary or purely trend-following trader lacks.

## Key concepts

- **Seasonal tendency / seasonal array** — the historical tendency of a commodity's price to rise or fall in a given calendar month or window, computed by averaging percentage price changes across many years (e.g., corn cash prices shown for 1963-1993+); presented as month-by-month arrays flagging "high odds up" and "high odds down" months.
- **Key Date Seasonal** — Bernstein's specific tradable system: a fixed calendar entry date and exit date (e.g., buy 4/6, sell 4/16) tested across every available historical year for a market, with a stop-loss percentage and a summary of win rate, average profit, and average loss (full spec in the system sub-page).
- **Seasonal spread** — a seasonal tendency expressed as the price difference between two related contracts/markets (e.g., April Live Cattle vs. April Live Hogs, June/April Live Hogs, December Wheat/December Oats) rather than an outright position.
- **Cycles in futures trading** — recurring price cycles of a measured length (e.g., a "predictable 5.6-year cycle" shown for corn futures) distinct from seasonals, which are annual/calendar-based.
- **Daily Sentiment Index (DSI)** — Bernstein's proprietary daily reading of the percentage of traders bullish on a given futures market, tracked with 3-day and 5-day moving averages across dozens of markets (T-Bonds, currencies, metals, grains, softs, meats); used as a contrarian sentiment-extreme indicator.
- **Moving Average Channel timing (MAC)** — buy/sell signals generated when price crosses above or below a moving-average-derived channel; illustrated with support/resistance examples but without a single numerically specified parameter set in the sampled pages.
- **Gap types** — the manual illustrates a basic gap and a "2-day breakaway gap" as chart patterns worth recognizing for timing entries.
- **Why traders lose vs. why traders win** — a paired checklist slide: losers lack a defined methodology, ride losses, take profits too quickly, misunderstand their own system, stay too close to the market, and cannot tolerate more than a few consecutive losses; winners develop specific procedures, understand how their system works, are sufficiently capitalized, avoid taking quick profits (unless day-trading), can accept six or more consecutive losses, avoid overthinking, and "play your own game" by avoiding news-driven decisions.

## Rules and setups

1. **Key Date Seasonal system (full detail in the [[jake-bernstein-trade-your-way-to-riches--key-date-seasonals|Key Date Seasonals system sub-page]]):** for a given market, buy (or sell) on a fixed calendar date, exit on a second fixed calendar date roughly one to five weeks later, with a percentage stop-loss; the manual shows fully worked, year-by-year historical results for at least two instances (Dow Jones Industrial Average, April 6-April 16; Live Hogs, October 30-November 27).
2. **Seasonal spread trades:** hold a long-one/short-another position between two related contracts (e.g., April Live Cattle long vs. April Live Hogs short) over a calendar window identified as historically reliable from a seasonal array; specific entry/exit dates for the spread trades beyond the cattle/hogs example are not recoverable from the sampled pages.
3. **"Why traders win" checklist as an operating rule set:** be sufficiently capitalized before trading; do not take quick profits unless day-trading; be willing to withstand six or more consecutive losing trades without abandoning a system; avoid trading off news headlines.
4. **Sentiment extremes (DSI) as a contrarian filter:** the manual presents the DSI readings across markets but does not give a specific numeric threshold (e.g., "above 80% bullish = sell") in the sampled pages; the mechanism (percentage of traders bullish, smoothed with 3- and 5-day moving averages) is documented, but the exact trigger level is not stated where sampled and should not be assumed.

The manual is chart/slide-based rather than prose-based; beyond the Key Date Seasonal tables, most "rules" are illustrated visually (annotated chart examples) rather than spelled out as numbered text, so this list reflects what could be extracted with confidence from the sampled OCR pages.

## Risk and money management

The only explicit, numeric risk control in the sampled pages is the stop-loss percentage attached to each Key Date Seasonal trade (shown as "SL:" on the results tables — e.g., a 5.0% stop on the Dow Jones April 6-16 trade, 6.0% on the Live Hogs October 30-November 27 trade). No general percent-of-equity risk-per-trade rule, position-sizing formula, or margin guidance is given elsewhere in the sampled material. The "why traders win/lose" checklist frames risk indirectly through behavioral rules (being sufficiently capitalized, tolerating a run of six or more losses, not overtrading) rather than a quantitative sizing method.

## Psychology and discipline

Psychology is addressed briefly through the paired "Why Traders Lose" / "Why Traders Win" slide (module 1). Losing behaviors listed are: lack of a defined methodology, riding losses, taking profits too quickly, a poor understanding of one's own system, staying too close to the market (overmonitoring), and an inability to tolerate more than a few consecutive losses. Winning behaviors listed are: developing specific, repeatable procedures; understanding how the trading system actually works; adequate capitalization; not taking quick profits outside of day-trading; willingness to accept six or more consecutive losses without abandoning the method; not overthinking each decision; and "playing your own game" by ignoring news-driven noise. No deeper psychological discussion (e.g., of fear, discipline components, or trader case studies as in Bernstein's Market Masters) appears in the sampled pages of this manual.

## Chapter map

- Video Seminar Series #1 — Introduction to Futures Trading: what a futures contract is, market participants, trading-floor structure, delivery months, contract leverage, gap patterns.
- Video Seminar Series #2 — Cycles in Futures Trading: long-term interest-rate cycles, individual-market price cycles (soybeans, British Pound, pork bellies, corn, shell eggs).
- Video Seminar Series #3 — Introduction to Seasonals: seasonal arrays and monthly seasonal tendency charts across grains, softs, energy, currencies, and stock indexes; weekly and daily seasonal composite charts.
- Video Seminar Series #4 — Seasonals Part II: Key Date Seasonal system with full historical trade tables (Dow Jones, wheat, T-Bonds, Live Hogs) and inter-market seasonal spreads (cattle/hogs, oats, soybeans, corn/oats, wheat/oats).
- Market Timing section (undated in TOC, appears after seminar modules) — moving averages, moving-average-channel (MAC) support/resistance examples, and the Daily Sentiment Index report format.

## Strengths and caveats

The Key Date Seasonal tables are a genuine strength: they show full year-by-year historical trade results (entry price, exit price, profit/loss, percent profit, cumulative total) rather than just an asserted win rate, which makes the method's historical performance auditable in principle. That said, this is a scanned, OCR-sampled training manual whose text consists overwhelmingly of chart labels and slide annotations rather than connected prose; explanatory context for how a Key Date Seasonal window is originally identified (e.g., minimum number of years, statistical significance threshold, selection process to avoid data-mining/curve-fitting across thousands of possible date pairs) was not found in the sampled pages and should not be assumed. The seasonal and cycle charts reference historical data mostly from the 1960s-1995 and are dated in market context (pre-decimalization, pit-trading era); the specific commodities and interest-rate regimes shown may not transfer to current market structure. No discussion of transaction costs, slippage, or how the historical win rates would change if adjusted for those costs appears in the sample.

## Who should read it

Traders who already have Bernstein's Market Masters or Stock Market Strategies That Work and want the source material for his seasonal/cyclical timing approach, especially the Key Date Seasonal method with its worked historical tables. Less useful as a standalone introduction to technical analysis, since the manual assumes the video seminar's spoken narration to fill in context that the slides alone do not fully convey, and much of the explanatory prose was not present in the source pages sampled for these notes.
