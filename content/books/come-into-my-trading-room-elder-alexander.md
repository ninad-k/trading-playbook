## Overview

Come Into My Trading Room is Alexander Elder's follow-up to Trading for a Living, organized around his "three M's": Mind, Method, Money. Part One covers the basics of choosing markets and getting equipped; Part Two updates his technical toolkit (Triple Screen, Impulse System, SafeZone stops, Market Thermometer, Chandelier Exit) and formalizes his money-management rules (the 2% and 6% Rules); Part Three walks through the organizational habits of a professional trader, including a real trading diary. The book assumes familiarity with basic chart reading and is written as a practical update rather than an introduction.

## Core thesis

No single "holy grail" indicator or system makes a trader profitable. Sustainable trading rests equally on three pillars — psychological discipline, a tested technical method, and strict money management — and a weakness in any one of them will eventually sink an otherwise sound approach. Elder repeatedly stresses that indicators only filter and time trades; survival is a function of position sizing and stop discipline, not signal quality.

## Key concepts

- **Triple Screen** — a three-timeframe trading method: use a long-term chart (intermediate timeframe × 5) with trend-following indicators to pick a trade direction, an intermediate chart with oscillators to time entries, and a third screen (intraday breakout/pullback orders) to execute.
- **Impulse System** — a two-indicator (13-day EMA + 12-26-9 MACD-Histogram) color-coded system that flags bars where trend inertia and momentum agree; used as a "go/no-go" filter that blocks counter-impulse trades.
- **Force Index** — Elder's own oscillator, Force Index = (Close(today) − Close(yesterday)) × Volume(today), smoothed with a 2-day EMA for short-term signals and a 13-day (or longer) EMA for longer-term divergence analysis.
- **SafeZone stop** — a noise-adaptive trailing stop that places the stop a multiple (typically 2–3×) of the average penetration below the previous day's low (uptrend) or above the previous day's high (downtrend).
- **Chandelier Exit** — a volatility-based trailing stop: highest high (or lowest low) of the last N days minus (or plus) a coefficient × Average True Range (Elder uses Hhv(hi,22) − 3×ATR(22)).
- **Market Thermometer** — measures how far today's bar protrudes beyond yesterday's range; used to gauge whether a market is "hot" (wide, volatile) or "quiet" (narrow, better for entries).
- **2% Rule** — never risk more than 2% of trading equity on a single trade.
- **6% Rule** — stop trading for the rest of the month once open+closed risk/losses reach 6% of the equity measured at the start of that month.
- **Elder-ray** — Bull Power (High − EMA) and Bear Power (Low − EMA), plotted as two histograms against a price EMA to show the relative strength of bulls and bears within a bar.
- **Circle of five** (Time — the Factor of Five) — Triple Screen's rule of thumb that the "long-term" chart is roughly five times the period of the chosen "intermediate" chart (e.g., daily → weekly, 10-minute → hourly).

## Rules and setups

1. **Triple Screen, screen one (strategic)**: choose an intermediate timeframe; multiply by 5 for the long-term chart. Use the slope of a 26-week EMA (or weekly MACD-Histogram) to decide long, short, or stand aside.
2. **Triple Screen, screen two (tactical)**: on the intermediate chart, take oscillator signals only in the direction of the long-term trend — e.g., buy when a rising weekly trend coincides with the 2-day EMA of Force Index dipping below zero on the daily chart; reverse for shorts.
3. **Triple Screen, screen three (entry)**: without live data, place a buy stop at the previous day's high (or a tick above) when the first two screens agree bullish; a sell stop at the previous day's low for bearish setups. With live data, use an opening-range breakout.
4. **Impulse System entries**: on the intermediate timeframe, in the direction of the higher-timeframe trend, go long when both the 13-day EMA and MACD-Histogram turn up together; go short when both turn down together.
5. **Impulse System exits**: exit as soon as either the EMA or MACD-Histogram stops confirming (do not wait for a full reversal signal); the system is designed to enter cautiously and exit fast.
6. **Force Index exit rule**: in an uptrend, sell the day after the 2-day EMA of Force Index turns negative (short-term) or when it traces a bearish divergence against price (swing); mirror for shorts.
7. **SafeZone stop calculation**: pick a lookback (10–20 days typical), average the downside penetrations of the prior day's low during an uptrend, multiply by a coefficient (start at 2, test to 3), and subtract from yesterday's low; stops may only be raised, never lowered, in an uptrend (mirror for downtrends using upside penetrations of highs).
8. **Chandelier Exit**: Chandelier = highest high(N) − coefficient × ATR(N), typically N=22 and coefficient=3; used as a wide trailing stop for strong trends, sometimes combined with partial profit-taking at a channel line.
9. **Market Thermometer filter**: prefer new entries when today's thermometer reading is below its 22-day EMA (quiet market); take profits when the reading spikes to roughly 3× its EMA (panic/exhaustion).
10. **"OK to trade" filter for futures**: only trade a market whose 22-day EMA of SafeZone noise, multiplied by 2, is less than 2% of account equity (i.e., average noise should be under about 1% of equity).

## Risk and money management

The **2% Rule**: never risk more than 2% of trading-account equity (cash plus open positions, excluding savings/retirement) on any single trade; risk is defined as entry price minus stop, times shares, including commission/slippage headroom. Elder notes professionals typically risk far less (0.5–1%). The **6% Rule**: total risk across all open positions plus realized losses for the current calendar month may not exceed 6% of equity measured on the first of that month; once hit, close all positions and stop trading until the next month. Together the rules bound single-trade risk (the "shark") and cumulative drawdown (the "piranhas"). Position sizing follows directly: divide the 2% dollar amount by the per-share/contract risk to get maximum size, rounding down for costs. Elder recommends a stepped position-sizing ladder for beginners (e.g., start at the smallest round lot, increase only after a profitable two-week/two-month period, and drop back immediately after a losing period) and references Ralph Vince's "optimal f" only as a cautionary ceiling to stay well under, not a target.

## Psychology and discipline

Elder frames trading psychology around self-destructive impulses: undisciplined traders "sleepwalk" through decisions driven by fear and greed rather than a written plan. He recommends keeping a detailed trading diary with entry/exit screenshots and a rationale for every trade, tracking an equity curve, and treating money-management rules as non-negotiable externally-imposed discipline (mimicking the role a trading manager plays for institutional traders, which private traders lack). He distinguishes a "businessman's risk" (an acceptable, planned equity fluctuation) from a "loss" (a rule violation), and argues that overtrading — in size or frequency — is the single most common way undisciplined traders destroy accounts, since risk tolerance and decision quality both degrade as position size grows.

- Ch 10 — Come Into My Trading Room: annotated excerpts from Elder's own trading diary.

## Strengths and caveats

The book gives exact, codeable parameters for every system it introduces (EMA lengths, MACD settings, SafeZone coefficients, ATR multiples), which is unusually rare among discretionary-trading books and makes it genuinely useful as a reference. It is explicitly a sequel — it assumes the reader already knows basic technical analysis from Trading for a Living and reads best alongside that book. Some material is dated (day-trading-floor anecdotes, pit-trading mechanics, pre-decimal quoting in eighths), and several signature indicators (Force Index, Elder-ray, SafeZone) are proprietary to Elder and not independently validated against other well-known systems in the text; performance claims rest on chart illustrations rather than systematic backtests.

## Who should read it

Intermediate swing and position traders who already understand basic charting and want concrete, parameterized rules for trend/oscillator combination systems (Triple Screen, Impulse) and for stop placement (SafeZone, Chandelier); also useful for any trader looking for a workable, arithmetic-based position-sizing framework (2%/6% Rules) rather than vague risk-management advice.
