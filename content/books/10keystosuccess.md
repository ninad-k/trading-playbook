## Summary

Martinez’s introductory manual frames successful trading as disciplined habit: create a plan, continue education, make logical decisions, and manage equity. It recommends studying and paper trading for at least one month, trading no more than 20% of a margin account, and exposing no more than 5% of the account on a single trade (p. 4). The manual introduces the spot FX market, currency pairs, leverage, spreads, and round lots, using dated claims such as a roughly $1.5 trillion daily market and approximately four-pip spreads (pp. 5–8). A candlestick section defines bullish, bearish, neutral, and reversal formations, including hammers, engulfing lines, stars, doji, and harami (pp. 10–17). The order section distinguishes market, limit, and stop orders, explains that stop orders become market orders, and recommends pairing a protective stop with a projected limit target through an OCO procedure (pp. 21–23). It also illustrates cancel-and-replace by moving a stop to breakeven and then into profit (p. 23).

## Key points

- The “10 keys” theme is process control and repeatable habits rather than a tested entry edge (pp. 2–4).
- Market orders and stop orders can slip; limits seek a specified price or better (pp. 21–22).
- Candlestick patterns are presented as context-dependent formations, with confirmation advised for doji stars (pp. 11–17).

## Actionable rules

1. Paper trade for at least one month; the manual says to use no more than 20% of account margin and expose no more than 5% of the account to one trade (p. 4).
2. Pair a protective stop with a profit-taking limit through an OCO order where the broker supports it (pp. 21–22).
3. Use cancel-and-replace to advance the protective stop as a trade moves favorably; the 1.3900-to-1.4100-to-1.4400 sequence is the source’s historical illustration, not a universal spacing rule (pp. 22–23).

## Caveats

This is educational and promotional material with no audited performance evidence. Its leverage, market-size, broker, and execution claims are dated, and the suggested 5% per-trade exposure can be far too large for a volatile leveraged product. Candlestick definitions do not establish predictive power.

## Who it is for

Beginners learning FX terminology, chart conventions, and basic order handling.
