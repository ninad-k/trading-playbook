## What it is

Relational Analysis is du Toit's framework for reading the fundamental/sentiment side of forex price action: "the study of the relationships between price, time and driving forces behind price changes." It rests on a three-way interaction, PET (Price, Event, Time), and works alongside - not instead of - the 4x1 strategy and Median Trading: PET tells the trader what is happening and why, while 4x1/median grid decide trade mechanics. Its practical output is a habit of daily fact-tracking and session-to-session sentiment reading (the "Japanese Follow-Through Indicator") rather than a fixed indicator.

## Rules

**The PET relationships**
1. **Price-Time**: volatility requires elapsed time; the longer a move takes, the lower its effective volatility - use this to judge whether a move is significant for your timeframe.
2. **Event-Time**: an event's impact depends on proximity (a rate decision a week out trades differently than one tomorrow); markets progressively discount an approaching event.
3. **Event-Price**: events cause temporary volatility/liquidity shifts that fade as they're absorbed - expect spike-and-normalize, not a permanent regime change, for most releases.
4. Treat the trader's own psychological state (stress, a losing streak) as a legitimate "event" on equal footing with an external announcement - if it affects decisions, it counts.

**Separating storyline from flavour**
5. Identify the "storyline": the durable, multi-week/month fundamental bias that should determine trading direction under 4x1's one-directional rule.
6. Treat individual releases as "flavour of the day" - short-lived noise - unless a sequence consistently points one direction across multiple periods, at which point flavour may be starting to change the storyline.
7. Weight repeated, confirming releases far more than a single surprising one; treat one unexpected release as noise pending confirmation.
8. Track recurring macro drivers of the pair traded: interest-rate differentials, employment, consumer confidence, production data, inflation (CPI/PPI); in a downturn the market watches for recovery signs, in an upturn for overheating signs.

**Session-based sentiment**
9. Track the three overlapping sessions in sequence - Asia, then London, then New York - noting how each session's close is likely to be extended or reversed by the next.
10. Read the Tokyo close specifically: a pair trading consistently one way across several Asian sessions is more likely to see London extend than reverse that bias.
11. Keep a daily checklist before trading: prior-day US equity close/direction, today's scheduled releases, the traded currency's prior close and move since, gold's close, whether US data was USD-positive/negative, USD-equity correlation at the close, and recent relevant news.

**Applying it**
12. Use Relational Analysis only to confirm or question the storyline feeding 4x1's directional rule - not to generate standalone entries or timing.
13. Avoid trading into or right after major announcements; expect ~15 minutes of high volatility, up to ~2 hours elevated, then normal - let the window pass.
14. Prefer raw facts over pundit opinions; form an independent read rather than deferring to commentators.

## Risk

This is a qualitative, discretionary overlay, not a signal generator - it does not define position size, entry price, or stops (those come from 4x1/median grid). Its risk-management value is behavioral: it gives a structured reason to sit out ambiguous periods ("no opinion" is a legitimate output) and a discipline for recognizing when personal psychological state, not the market, is the real obstacle - the author describes halting live trading for weeks when his own state of mind was unreliable, treating that as good risk management.

## Caveats

The framework gives no quantitative threshold for "several confirming releases" or how many sessions of Tokyo bias justify a bet - the checklist and session-reading rules are judgment aids, not testable rules; du Toit says this is built through practice ("gut feel"), not formula. The macro backdrop and named 2003-2004 commentators are dated; the durable content is the general method (separate trend drivers from noise, respect event-proximity, track session hand-offs), not the specific market calls used as illustrations.
