## What it is

Relational Analysis is Dirk du Toit's framework for reading the fundamental and sentiment-driven side of forex price action, defined as "the study of the relationships between price, time and driving forces behind price changes." It rests on a three-way interaction he calls PET - Price, Event, Time - and is meant to work alongside (not replace) his 4x1 strategy and Median Trading methodology: PET tells the trader what is happening in the market and why, while 4x1/median grid decide the mechanics of the trade. The framework's practical output is a habit of daily fact-tracking and session-to-session sentiment reading (his "Japanese Follow-Through Indicator") rather than a fixed indicator or formula.

## Rules

**The PET relationships**
1. **Price-Time**: volatility requires elapsed time; the longer the period over which an absolute price move occurs, the lower its effective volatility - use this to judge whether a given move is significant for your timeframe.
2. **Event-Time**: an event's market impact depends on its proximity in time (a rate decision a week away trades differently than one tomorrow); markets progressively discount an approaching event as it nears ("worked through the system").
3. **Event-Price**: events cause temporary volatility/liquidity shifts that fade as the event is absorbed; expect a spike-and-normalize pattern, not a permanent regime change, for most single data releases.
4. Treat the trader's own psychological/personal state (stress, a losing streak, fatigue) as a legitimate "event" within the framework on equal footing with an external announcement - if it affects trading decisions, it counts.

**Separating storyline from flavour**
5. Identify the "storyline": the durable, multi-week/month fundamental bias (example given: strong euro/weak dollar in early 2004) that should determine trading direction under the 4x1 one-directional rule.
6. Treat individual data releases and news items as "flavour of the day" - short-lived noise - unless a sequence of releases consistently points the same direction for multiple periods, at which point flavour may be starting to change the storyline itself.
7. Weight repeated, confirming releases much more heavily than a single surprising one: one unexpected release is treated as noise pending confirmation; two or more consecutive confirming releases signal a possible storyline shift.
8. Track the core recurring macro categories that drive the pair being traded: interest rate differentials (absolute and expected direction), employment data, consumer confidence, manufacturing/production data, inflation (CPI/PPI), and note that in a downturn the market watches growth/employment signs of recovery while in an upturn it watches inflation signs of overheating.

**Session-based sentiment ("Japanese Follow-Through Indicator")**
9. Track the three overlapping trading days in sequence: Asia (Tokyo/Sydney) opens first, hands off to Europe/London, which hands off to New York; note how each session's close (bullish/bearish/flat) is likely to be read and extended (or reversed) by the next session.
10. Specifically read the Tokyo close: if the Asian session has traded a pair up (or down) consistently across several days and closes still biased that way, expect London to extend rather than reverse that bias absent a contrary catalyst.
11. Maintain a simple daily checklist of cross-market facts before trading: prior-day US equity index close and direction, scheduled economic release times for the day, the traded currency's prior close and move since, gold's close, whether the day's US data was USD-positive or -negative, whether USD correlated with equities at the close, and any relevant news in the last few hours.

**Applying it to trading decisions**
12. Use Relational Analysis only to confirm or question the fundamental direction (the "storyline") that feeds the one-directional rule in 4x1 - it is not used to generate standalone entry signals or precise timing.
13. Avoid trading directly into or immediately after major scheduled announcements; expect roughly 15 minutes of high volatility, up to about 2 hours of elevated volatility, then a return to normal - let this window pass before entering.
14. Prefer raw facts (index levels, data prints, closing prices) over third-party opinions/pundit calls; form an independent read rather than deferring to commentators, since their track records are frequently no better than random.

## Risk

Relational Analysis is explicitly a qualitative, discretionary overlay, not a signal generator - it does not by itself define position size, entry price, or stop placement (those come from the 4x1/median grid rules). Its main risk-management value is behavioral: it gives the trader a structured reason to sit out ambiguous or event-heavy periods ("no opinion" is treated as a legitimate, sometimes optimal, output) and a discipline for recognizing when personal psychological state, not the market, is the actual obstacle - the author describes deliberately halting live trading for weeks when his own state of mind was unreliable, treating that as good risk management rather than a failure.

## Caveats

The framework does not specify quantitative thresholds for "several confirming releases" or how many sessions of Tokyo bias justify a directional bet - the daily checklist and session-reading rules are judgment aids, not testable rules, and du Toit says explicitly that this skill is built through repeated practice ("gut feel") rather than a formula. The macro backdrop and named commentators (2003-2004 CNBC personalities, specific EUR/USD levels) are dated and country/era-specific; the durable content is the general method (separate durable trend drivers from daily noise, respect event-proximity effects, track session hand-offs) rather than any of the specific market calls used as illustrations.
