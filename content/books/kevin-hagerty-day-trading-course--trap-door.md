## What it is

A short-term (typically first-hour) reversal trade that fades an emotional down-opening in an otherwise strong stock. Haggerty's premise is that a specialist (NYSE market-maker) facing a weak opening will let the stock trade lower to "take out the emotional sellers" before the real buying returns, leaving a "trap door" of stale sell orders below the market that the reversal will run through quickly. The setup is restricted to a narrow universe of stocks (high relative-strength, liquid, already trending) precisely because it depends on that underlying strength reasserting itself once weak-handed sellers are cleared out.

## Rules

**Candidate filter (apply before the trading day, or at the open):**
1. Stock must have high relative strength (RS) versus the S&P 500 — a name institutions ("the Generals") are already accumulating.
2. Average daily trading range of at least 1½–2 points.
3. Stock must be trading above both its 50-day and 200-day EMAs (this is usually already true of a high-RS name).

**Entry sequence (long case; reverse all conditions for the short case):**
4. Bar 1 (the open): the stock opens below the previous close and trades down at least 1 point from that close.
5. Bar 2: price makes both a lower high and a lower low than Bar 1.
6. Entry trigger: enter long as soon as a subsequent bar trades above the high of Bar 2. This can technically happen on Bar 3 (the earliest possible entry), but under normal conditions the trigger more often comes on the 4th or 5th bar.
7. Do not anticipate the entry — wait for the actual break above the Bar 2 high; the point of the setup is that the market has already "proven" the down-move is exhausted by the time this trigger fires.

**Confirmation / what you are watching for:**
8. On a 5-minute chart, roughly 90% of valid Trap Doors show a 3–5 bar reversal pattern — i.e., the reversal off the low completes within a handful of bars, not a slow grind.
9. Watch for the specialist's behavior: after the emotional sellers are cleared, a "reflex rally" typically follows as buyers return and programs re-engage, sometimes moving the stock quickly as the specialist unwinds inventory built during the flush-out.

## Risk

Haggerty classifies Trap Doors (alongside Slim Jims) as "contra trades" — reflex trades against the immediate intraday direction — and explicitly warns they can reverse direction quickly if the underlying dynamics don't stay positive, so they require more active monitoring than a plain trend-continuation entry. No fixed stop-distance or percent-of-equity risk figure is given specifically for this setup in the sampled material; the course's general day-trading risk rules apply — enter a hard stop-loss or stop-limit order immediately on confirmation of the fill rather than relying on a mental stop, and exit immediately if the market fails to "prove the trade correct" with a move into profit shortly after entry, rather than waiting for the stop to be hit. Because the setup is explicitly restricted to high-RS, high-liquidity S&P 500 names with a meaningful average daily range, it is not intended to be applied to thin or low-momentum stocks.

## Caveats

These rules come from a scanned course with OCR run on only a sample of pages; the Trap Door section itself (Week 4) was well recovered, but no aggregated win-rate or backtest statistics for the setup were found in the sampled material — the only evidence given is the "90% show a 3–5 bar reversal" observation and a single worked example (MRK). The setup's mechanism depends on late-1990s NYSE specialist order-handling behavior (a single market-maker absorbing and then releasing order flow); in modern all-electronic, multi-venue equity markets this specific specialist dynamic no longer exists in the same form, though the general pattern — a gap-down open that fails to extend and reverses through the second bar's high — is a structure that can still appear for other microstructure reasons (algorithmic liquidity provision, opening-auction imbalances). Traders applying this today should treat the specialist-behavior explanation as historical color rather than a mechanism to rely on, and validate the entry/reversal statistics against current market structure before sizing it aggressively.
