## Summary

A short article by trader Ray Barros arguing that trading breakouts from sideways congestion is harder than it looks (many breakouts fail and retrace), then presenting three ways to set a profit target once a valid breakout occurs: two simple traditional methods (measured-move and time-based) and a more detailed six-step method based on Dr. Alan Andrews' work, which uses "X" marks at zone re-entries to calculate a price objective.

## Key points

- **Premise** — markets spend roughly 80% of the time in congestion by the author's estimate, and breakouts from congestion fail often enough that a target/exit strategy matters as much as the entry.
- **Traditional method 1 (measured move)** — downside target = low of congestion range minus the range's height; upside target = high of range plus range height.
- **Traditional method 2 (time-based)** — downside target = low of range minus the number of trading days the congestion took to form (upside is the mirror).
- **Andrews six-step method** — define the congestion boundaries ("A" and "B"), split the range into eighths to define Primary Sell/Buy Zones, mark price re-entries into the opposite zone with an "X," multiply the range by the number of "X"s to get a "Product," then subtract the Product from "A" (primary target) and from "B" (secondary target).
- **Zone-marking rule** — only mark an "X" the first time price re-enters the opposite Primary Zone after having been in the current zone; intervening highs/lows between zone visits are not marked.
- **Secondary target** — rarely reached; used only as a fallback if price does not reverse at the Primary Price Objective.

## Actionable rules

1. Determine whether the breakout is upside or downside before applying any target method.
2. Measured-move target: subtract (downside) or add (upside) the congestion range's point-height to/from the range's low/high.
3. Time-based target: subtract (downside) the number of trading days congestion took to form from the range low (mirror for upside).
4. Andrews method: divide the congestion range into eighths from boundaries "A" (top) and "B" (bottom) to define Primary Sell Zone (A to A minus ⅛) and Primary Buy Zone (B to B plus ⅛).
5. Mark each valid zone re-entry with an "X"; count the "X"s in the Primary Buy Zone (for a downside breakout) or Primary Sell Zone (for an upside breakout).
6. Primary Price Objective = "A" minus (congestion range × number of "X"s); Secondary Price Objective = "B" minus the same product.

## Caveats

Uses one worked numerical example (an unnamed market, likely an index, with a 1518-point congestion range) without naming the instrument, timeframe, or currency of the price levels, which limits reproducibility. The Andrews method requires subjective judgment in placing the "A"/"B" boundaries and identifying valid zone entries. No win-rate or backtest data is provided; article ends with a promotion for the author's November seminar (omitted here per source policy).

## Who it is for

Traders working with congestion/consolidation ranges who want a more rigorous, rule-based way to set profit targets after a breakout than a simple measured-move guess.
