## Summary

Third edition of McGraw-Hill's "All About..." series entry on stocks — a survey textbook, not a trading system. It walks beginner-to-intermediate investors through what common and preferred stocks are, risk/return measurement, how exchanges and orders work, stock valuation, financial statement analysis, technical analysis basics, growth/value/momentum styles, fund vehicles, foreign stocks, and portfolio management, across 18 chapters.

## Key points

- Covers common vs. preferred stock, market indexes, and trading mechanics (order types, short selling).
- Valuation chapter covers P/E, PEG, price-to-cash-flow, price-to-book, and price-to-sales, with worked examples (P/E of 8 × $4 EPS = $32 price).
- Financial statement chapter covers liquidity, turnover (receivables, inventory, payables), profitability (gross/operating/net margin, ROE, ROI), and leverage ratios; one worked example shows leverage raising ROE from 14% to 21%.
- Technical analysis chapter covers chart types, volume/price mood combinations (rising volume + rising price = bullish), moving averages, and named patterns, while noting the method lacks academic credibility.
- Separate chapters distinguish growth (high P/E), value (low P/E, low price-to-book), and momentum investing styles.
- Distinguishes open-end mutual funds, closed-end funds, and ETFs by pricing and liquidity mechanics.
- Cites historical S&P 500 P/E range of roughly 5.9–46.5 (average ~15.3) from 1935–2002 as valuation context.

## Actionable rules

1. Compare a stock's P/E to its own historical range and to peers, not in isolation; PEG above 1.0 suggests a rich P/E relative to growth, below 1.0 suggests cheap.
2. Read gross, operating, and net margins separately — a rising net margin can mask a falling operating margin driven by one-time items.
3. Watch receivables/inventory turnover for red flags: low turnover ties up cash; a spike in finished goods versus raw materials signals unsold product.
4. Combine volume and price direction: rising price + rising volume is bullish; rising price + falling volume warns of a top.
5. Diversify across stocks, funds, and (optionally) foreign holdings, and evaluate the portfolio against a benchmark rather than judging trades in isolation.

## Caveats

General-audience investing education, not a proprietary strategy — it presents standard finance-textbook ratios alongside mainstream technical-analysis concepts, explicitly flagging that technical analysis lacks academic support even while devoting a chapter to it. Examples are dated to the 2000s market. Only the valuation, fundamental-analysis, and technical-analysis chapters were read in detail; fund and portfolio-management chapters were not examined page by page.

## Who it is for

Beginning stock market investors who want one comprehensive reference on terminology, valuation math, and fund vehicles before building a portfolio, rather than active traders seeking an edge.
