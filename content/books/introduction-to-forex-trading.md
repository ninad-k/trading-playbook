## Summary

A short marketing/education brochure from TradeStation Securities (2002–2005) introducing absolute beginners to forex mechanics: what forex is, who trades it, how quotes and pips work, and how to open and close a position on the TradeStation platform. It is platform-oriented rather than strategy-oriented.

## Key points

- Identifies four market participant groups: retail/novice traders, dealers (market makers), institutional traders (banks/government agencies settling real business flows), and advanced/professional discretionary traders.
- "Interbank" describes quoting/trading between banks and large institutions; forex has no central exchange, unlike equities or futures.
- States zero commissions when trading forex via TradeStation, with cost embedded in the bid-ask spread instead.
- Cites leverage available up to 100x account value, with an explicit warning that leverage magnifies both gains and catastrophic losses.
- Cites average daily forex volume of over $1.3 trillion, framed as enabling entry/exit "at will" in most conditions.
- Explains standard currency pair notation (e.g., EURUSD, USDJPY) and that a Forex contract is quoted as units of the second currency per one unit of the first (base) currency.
- Standard contract size cited as 100,000 units of the base currency; a pip is defined as the minimum price increment.
- To close a position, execute an equal and opposite trade in the same pair (e.g., close a long EURUSD by selling EURUSD).
- Defines bid (what a buyer will pay) vs. ask/offer (what a seller will accept); a retail trader buys at the ask and sells at the bid.

## Actionable rules

None given — this is a pure mechanics/vocabulary primer with no entry, exit, sizing, or system rules. The only operational point offered is trade execution mechanics: to exit, place an equal and opposite trade in the same currency pair.

## Caveats

This is vendor-produced platform marketing material (TradeStation Securities) rather than independent education; the $50,000 minimum-capital caution and zero-commission claims are specific to TradeStation's own account structure circa 2005 and are not generalizable to other brokers. Contains no risk-management framework beyond a general leverage warning.

## Who it is for

Absolute beginners who need the vocabulary and mechanics of a forex trade (pairs, pips, bid/ask, leverage, opening/closing) before evaluating any actual trading system.
