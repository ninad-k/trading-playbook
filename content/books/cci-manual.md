## Summary

This manual explains Woodie’s CCI chart language and its beginner trade patterns. The chart uses a 14-period CCI, a 6-period Turbo CCI, a 25-period LSMA represented at the zero line, and ±100/±200 reference levels (pp. 3–5). A trend exists when CCI remains on one side of zero for at least six time bars; mixed bars mean no trade (pp. 4, 7). The core trend-agreement patterns are Zero-Line Reject (ZLR), Reverse Divergence, Trend Line Break, and Horizontal Trend Line Break (pp. 8–14).

## Key points

- A ZLR requires CCI to retrace toward zero through at least the ±100 level, preferably ±50, then reverse with the established trend (pp. 9–10).
- Reverse Divergence uses two CCI peaks or valleys and a break of the connecting line as a trend-continuation signal (p. 11).
- A Trend Line Break starts from a peak or valley beyond ±100, preferably ±200/−220, and is strongest when it confirms a ZLR (p. 12).
- A Horizontal Trend Line Break joins two or more similarly placed bumps within the ±100 boundary (p. 13).
- Beginners are told to wait until a signal bar has about 20 seconds left, although the text acknowledges aggressive intrabar entry as an alternative (pp. 15–16).
- The stated beginner hard stop is one to two ticks beyond the entry bar (p. 16).
- Beginner exits include CCI hooking or flattening, Turbo CCI crossing CCI, and a hook from an extreme beyond ±200 (pp. 17–20).

## Actionable rules

1. Define trend only after six or more CCI bars above or below zero; if no clear trend exists, do not trade (pp. 4, 7).
2. In an uptrend, buy a CCI ZLR or confirmed line-break signal; in a downtrend, sell the corresponding bearish pattern (pp. 8–14).
3. Enter near bar completion, with the manual’s beginner timing rule of roughly 20 seconds remaining, and place a price stop one to two ticks beyond the entry bar (pp. 15–16).
4. Exit when CCI hooks or flatlines, or when Turbo CCI crosses CCI. For a hook after CCI has crossed ±200, exit at the first hint of a turn without waiting for the bar close or the normal 20-second point (pp. 17–20).

## Caveats

The source is a pattern and terminology guide, not a statistical performance study. Exact bar duration, contract, tick value, and position sizing are not specified. Intrabar signals can disappear before a bar closes (p. 15), and the recommended one-to-two-tick stop may be too tight for many markets.

## Who it is for

Intraday traders learning CCI vocabulary and a structured pattern checklist. It requires independent testing of bar length, market, and execution costs.
