## Summary

A short, freely distributed ebook (published by Day-Trading-Mind.com, 2002) aimed at real-time/day traders struggling with discipline. It has no technical-analysis content — it is entirely about the psychological and behavioral side of trading, presented as ten short chapters, each a page or two, moving from probability theory through position sizing, language/self-talk, session timing, and journaling.

## Key points

- **Trading is probability, not certainty** — like a coin with a 51/49 edge, a trading system only shifts odds slightly in your favor; the current trade is one of a long series and "hardly matters" on its own.
- **Write the trading plan down** — a plan needs three explicit parts: Setup, Entry, and Exit; write it, post it at the trading desk, and follow it "religiously."
- **Risk small per trade** — undercapitalized traders should either use tight stops or trade a shorter timeframe (e.g., 1-minute chart) rather than oversizing positions.
- **Think in points/percentages, not dollars** — reframing P&L as "risk-to-reward ratio" and "percentage of account" reduces the emotional charge of watching dollar amounts move.
- **Pre-market visualization** — mentally rehearsing calm, non-emotional responses to both winning and losing trades before the session starts.
- **Reframe loss/win language** — replace "loss" with "expense" and "win" with "income" to treat trading as a business rather than an emotional contest.
- **Limit trades per day** — follow a "3 strikes and you're out" rule (max ~3 trades/day) and trade mainly the first two hours after the open (and optionally the last half hour) when momentum/volume are highest.
- **Keep a trading diary** — record facts (ticker, time, size, entry, stop, exit) plus feelings before/during/after each trade; review weekly and monthly to spot recurring behavioral patterns.

## Actionable rules

1. Cap trading to a maximum of ~3 trades per day.
2. Concentrate trading in the first two hours after the open (and optionally the final 30 minutes before the close).
3. Size positions so that a loss is an "expense" you can absorb without emotional reaction — reduce size or tighten stops if undercapitalized.
4. Write Setup/Entry/Exit rules down before the session; do not deviate intraday.
5. Log every trade (facts + emotional state) and review the log weekly and monthly for behavioral patterns.

## Caveats

Contains no market data, backtests, or specific instrument guidance — it is a general psychology checklist, largely reused/generic advice (probability framing, journaling, session timing) that overlaps with better-known trading-psychology books in this library. Publisher and specific author are not identified beyond the "Day-Trading-Mind.com" website credit.

## Who it is for

Beginner-to-intermediate day traders who already have a method but struggle with discipline, overtrading, or emotional decision-making.
