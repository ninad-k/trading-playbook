## Summary

A short promotional-style explainer (attributed to reseller SureFireThing.com) on the Camarilla equation, said to have been devised in 1989 by bond trader Nick Stott. The equation derives 8 intraday support/resistance levels ("L1" through "L4" above and below the prior close) purely from yesterday's open, high, low and close, on the premise that markets revert to the mean after a wide daily range. The piece explains two ways to trade the levels: fading price at the L3 levels, and trading breakouts through the L4 levels. The exact formula/constants for computing L1–L4 are not given in the text.

## Key points

- 8 levels are calculated from the prior day's OHLC and split into two groups (1–4) above and below the close, roughly symmetrical.
- L3 levels are the primary reversal levels; L4 levels act as both stop-loss for reversal trades and as breakout levels.
- Reversals off L3 are claimed to occur "as often as 4 times out of 5" intraday.
- A breakout through L4 on the S&P is claimed to run up to 7 points further.
- The author's own suggested stop on an S&P breakout trade is 2 points or less.
- Trading style differs by where the market opens: inside the L3 range (wait for price to tag an L3 level) versus outside the L3 range (wait for price to retreat back through L3).

## Actionable rules

1. If price opens between the two L3 levels, wait for price to reach either L3 level, then fade it (go short at the upper L3, long at the lower L3), using the corresponding L4 level as the stop.
2. For a more conservative entry, wait for price to bounce back through the L3 level (trading with the short-term move) rather than entering right at first touch.
3. If price opens outside the L3 range, wait for it to retreat back through L3 before entering with the trend, again using L4 as the stop.
4. Treat an L4 break as a breakout signal and trade with it; exit is subjective since the equation defines no levels beyond L4.
5. On the S&P, the author used roughly a 2-point stop for breakout trades through L4.

## Caveats

The document does not supply the actual Camarilla formula (the multipliers applied to the daily range), so the levels cannot be reproduced from this text alone. It is written as marketing copy for a specific vendor's calculator and reprinted chart examples, with unverified claims ("astounding accuracy," "4 times out of 5") and no out-of-sample statistics. Treat the win-rate and profit-target figures as anecdotal.

## Who it is for

Day traders who already know what Camarilla pivot levels are and want a quick refresher on the two basic ways to trade them (fade at L3, breakout at L4); not a substitute for a source with the actual formula.
