## Summary

A promotional e-booklet by futures market commentator Jim Wyckoff (of jimwyckoff.com), collecting eight short standalone articles: the top 10 trader mistakes, seasonality in agricultural/livestock markets, Fibonacci retracements and the Golden Ratio, ignoring market "noise," futures order types, swing trading sideways markets, the Williams %R indicator, and trading nuggets from Jesse Livermore. It is a grab-bag of educational tips rather than one unified system.

## Key points

- Top-10 mistakes list: no trading plan, undercapitalized/poor money management, unrealistic early expectations, no protective stops, lack of patience/discipline, fighting the trend or picking tops/bottoms, letting losers ride, overtrading, not owning one's results, and ignoring longer-term chart context.
- Seasonality primer covers corn, soybeans, wheat, live/feeder cattle, hogs, cocoa, and coffee, each with a described seasonal high/low window driven by harvest, farrowing, or weather cycles (e.g., corn's "February Break," the mid-summer-to-harvest price decline).
- Fibonacci section: the 0.618/1.618 "Golden Ratio" derived from the Fibonacci sequence; the key retracement levels tracked are 38.2% and 61.8%, with 33%, 50%, and 75% also used.
- Market-noise article argues day-to-day fundamental headlines are mostly irrelevant; traders should focus on the overall trend rather than reacting to daily news.
- Order-types glossary: market, limit, "or better," market-if-touched (MIT), stop, stop-limit, stop-close-only, market-on-opening, market-on-close, fill-or-kill, one-cancels-other (OCO), spread orders, and good-till-cancelled.
- Swing-trading method: trade off clearly defined support/resistance boundaries of a sideways channel, going long near support and short near resistance, with a tight stop just outside the boundary; a stop-out on a breakout is treated as a signal to reverse into a breakout trade.
- Williams %R: an oscillator (0–100, inverted scale) built on a lookback range (Larry Williams' original used 10 days; many platforms default to 14); simple rule is sell when %R reaches 20 or below, buy at 80 or above, waiting for the indicator to turn before acting.
- Livermore's rules (paraphrased from "How to Trade Stocks"): never average a loser, never meet a margin call, cap losses at roughly 10% of capital, trade only in the direction of the trend, and wait for market confirmation ("Pivotal Points") before acting.

## Actionable rules

1. Track the 38.2% and 61.8% Fibonacci retracement levels of a trend leg as support/resistance; secondary levels are 33%, 50%, and 75%.
2. Williams %R basic signal: sell when the indicator reaches 20 or lower (overbought), buy when it reaches 80 or higher (oversold); wait for the indicator to turn before entering.
3. Williams' original stricter %R rule: buy after %R reaches 100 and, five trading days later, falls back below 85–95%; sell after %R reaches 0 and, five trading days later, rises back above 5–15%.
4. In a swing trade, place the protective stop just outside the channel boundary being traded (e.g., just above resistance when short, just below support when long).
5. Per Livermore: never add to a losing position, never meet a margin call (exit instead), and never risk more than about 10% of capital on a single position.

## Caveats

This is a marketing e-booklet (opens with a pitch for the author's paid newsletter and seminar CD) assembled from short columns, not a tested or unified system — no backtests or statistics are given for any rule. Seasonality and Fibonacci sections are general folk-technical-analysis claims common across many trading books; the seasonality windows are approximate and commodity-market specific, so they don't transfer directly to equities or forex. No publication year is given.

## Who it is for

Beginner-to-intermediate futures/commodity traders wanting a quick, readable tour of several classic technical tools (Fibonacci, Williams %R, order types, swing trading) and discipline reminders in one short document.
