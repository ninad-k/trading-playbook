## Summary

This PDF covers the "Knowing Yourself" and "The Wall Street Herd" portions of Pring's Investment Psychology Explained, roughly the first two-thirds of the book (this scan is incomplete: it breaks off partway through Part III without reaching the printed table of contents' final chapters). Pring's core argument is that beating the market is secondary to "beating yourself" — mastering fear, greed, and herd instinct — since natural responses (buying strength, doubting a system after a losing streak) are usually wrong. He covers objectivity, independent thinking, patience, staying with a tested method through losses, and reading crowd sentiment and speculative manias, closing (in this excerpt) with profiles of Buffett and Templeton.

## Key points

- **No Holy Grail** — a recurring theme: no system or indicator works consistently enough to eliminate losses; plan for failure rates.
- **Objectivity over emotion** — decisions made after money is committed are corrupted by emotion; the antidote is a written, pre-committed plan followed mechanically.
- **Patience/staying the course** — abandoning a validated system after a normal losing streak (illustrated with a moving-average crossover example) is common and costly.
- **Trend-following example** — a British Pound system (long when price is above its 10-week MA and both 13-week and 6-week rate-of-change are positive; short on the reversed conditions) shows profits concentrate in trend-direction trades.
- **Contrary opinion** — an "art, not a science": the goal is gauging when a consensus has become so one-sided it's already priced in, not mechanically betting against the crowd.
- **Bubble/mania traits** — a "believable" revolutionary concept, surplus capital chasing scarce opportunity, an unfalsifiable story, fear of missing out, loosening lending standards, a cult figure.
- **Great investors profiled** — Buffett's business-quality checklist (return on capital, understandable, pricing power, predictable earnings, owner-oriented management); Templeton's contrarian, globally-diversified, multi-year-hold approach.

## Actionable rules

1. Don't abandon a back-tested system after a normal losing streak; judge performance over the full tested period.
2. When you have a firm view on the main trend, don't position against it.
3. Example rule set (GBP): long when price is above its 10-week MA and both 13-week and 6-week rate-of-change are positive; short when all three reverse.
4. Treat contrary opinion as confirmed only when a view is so widely held it's already priced in.
5. Multiple simultaneous bubble traits (revolutionary story, easy credit, fear of missing out, a cult figure) are a warning sign, not a buy signal.
