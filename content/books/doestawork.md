## Summary

A short educational article (distributed via tradingforbeginners.com) that answers the "is TA voodoo science?" objection by arguing technical analysis works because a large enough population of traders acts on the same signals, creating predictable, self-reinforcing price behavior that a minority of "winning" traders can anticipate and trade against. It then uses standard pivot points as a worked example of a widely-followed level.

## Key points

- **Core argument** — TA is not predictive because markets are inefficient; it works because enough participants use the same tools, so their collective reaction becomes the tradable signal.
- **Losing-trader mechanism** — a price crossing a moving average triggers stop-loss selling, which triggers more technical selling, creating a self-reinforcing move that "winning" traders eventually fade once they judge the move as artificially driven.
- **No single indicator is sufficient** — the article states there is no standalone TA tool or "Holy Grail" signal; combining indicators with discipline and capital is described as the actual edge.
- **Pivot point formula** — Pivot Point (PP) = (previous day's High + Low + Close) / 3.
- **Resistance/Support from PP** — Resistance = 2×PP − Low; Support = 2×PP − High.
- **Two derived levels** — previous day's last-two-hour high and low (L2HrHigh, L2HrLow) are also tracked as reference points.
- **Swing trading definition** — waiting for price to reverse after testing a pivot support/resistance level (illustrated on a 5-minute S&P E-mini chart).
- **Breakout trading definition** — trading in the direction of price that moves cleanly through a known support/resistance level after two or more tries.

## Actionable rules

1. Calculate the daily pivot point as PP = (H + L + C) / 3 using the prior day's high, low, and close.
2. Calculate resistance as 2×PP − L and support as 2×PP − H.
3. If price approaches a pivot level on increased volume and pushes through, treat it as continuation (breakout trade); if price tests the level and fails to move through after two or more attempts, treat it as reversal (swing trade).
4. No stop-loss, position-sizing, or profit-target numbers are given for either the swing or breakout approach.

## Caveats

This is a short promotional/educational piece (links out to a paid course) rather than a tested strategy: the "TA works because everyone uses it" thesis is an assertion, not backed by data in the article, and the pivot-point example is illustrated with a single anecdotal chart rather than statistics. Treat the reflexivity argument as a conceptual framing tool, not a proven edge.

## Who it is for

Beginners who want a plain-language answer to "does technical analysis actually work" and a first introduction to the standard pivot-point formula used for intraday support/resistance.
