## Summary

A magazine article by full-time futures trader Christopher Terry, built around a case study of "Mary," an intelligent trader who kept losing money despite sound entries, stops, and targets. Terry's diagnosis: her problem wasn't technique but a mismatch between her trading style (products, timeframe, holding period) and her personality. The article walks through the questions he used to help her define her "right game" — what to trade, on what timeframe, and with what risk per trade — rather than prescribing one universal method.

## Key points

- **Style-personality fit matters more than technique** — a sound methodology (breakouts, pullbacks, stochastics, MACD) fails if it doesn't match the trader's psychological tolerance for volatility, holding time, and pace.
- **Define your product list** — decide which markets/instruments you trade best (NYSE vs. Nasdaq stocks, index ETFs like QQQ/SPY/DIA, or E-mini futures) based on fill quality and comfort, not just opportunity.
- **Match timeframe to temperament** — Mary couldn't tolerate the "heat" of 60- or 120-minute swing charts but found 1-minute scalping too fast/noisy; she settled on 5-minute (primary) and 15-minute charts.
- **Overtrading is a symptom of mismatch** — trading the wrong timeframe led Mary to scalp in and out ~50 times a day, racking up commissions on small wins.
- **Position size follows fixed dollar risk** — size is derived from a target dollar risk per trade divided by the point risk of the setup; the larger the point risk, the smaller the position.
- **Pre-define exits before entry** — decide the loss exit and the profit-taking plan before putting on the trade, not while it's open.
- **Trending intraday environment as the preferred setup** — Mary's chosen pattern was a bull-flag continuation in a trending intraday session, with the stop raised to just under the last low as price advances and profit taken on a retest of prior highs.

## Actionable rules

1. Full position size example given: 5 E-mini S&P contracts (or 1 full S&P contract) with an average risk of ~2 points ($250/point x 2 = $500 per full position).
2. As point risk per trade rises, reduce contract/share size proportionally to hold dollar risk constant.
3. On a bull-flag long, raise the stop to just under the most recent swing low as the position moves in your favor; take profit on a retest of the prior high.
4. Set exit price (both loss and profit target) before entering the trade, not after.
5. Limit the trading day to a small number of setups (Mary moved from ~50 scalps/day to 3–5 trades/day) once style is matched to personality.

## Caveats

Single-trader case study, not a tested system — there are no aggregated statistics, win rates, or sample sizes, and the "right style" prescribed for Mary (5-/15-minute E-mini/Nasdaq trading) is not a universal template. The article is light on specific indicator settings (mentions Stochastic and RSI only in passing) and assumes the reader already has basic technical-analysis vocabulary.

## Who it is for

Traders with a technically sound system who are underperforming and suspect the issue is fit (timeframe, instrument, or holding period) rather than the rules themselves.
