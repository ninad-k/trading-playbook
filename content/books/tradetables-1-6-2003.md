## Summary

This document is not a strategy explanation — it is a raw trade-by-trade ledger for what the source labels the "WWW Trading System," covering EUR/USD trades from January through June 2003. Each row lists the open/close side, price, date, any remark ("stop order"), lot size, and running account balance. No entry, exit, or risk rules are stated anywhere in the document.

## Key points

- Trades are exclusively on EUR/USD, entered and closed within the same day in most cases (some spanning a few days), often multiple round trips per day.
- Position size increases steadily over the six months: it begins at 1 lot in January and grows to 27–30 lots by late June, tracking the growing account balance.
- Account balance grows from roughly $11,338 (start of January) to roughly $159,741 (end of June) — over a 14x increase in six months, per the log.
- Some closes are explicitly marked "stop order," indicating a defined stop-loss was used on at least some trades, though the stop distance is never stated.
- One entry notes a weekend/overnight gap that forced a worse-than-planned close price, an explicit reminder that stop orders can be skipped by gaps.
- The pattern of scaling lot size directly with account equity (rather than keeping position size fixed or using fractional/percentage risk) is a de facto aggressive compounding approach, though it is never described or justified in the text.

## Actionable rules

None given. This is a performance record, not a rulebook — there is no stated entry signal, stop-loss distance, profit target, or position-sizing formula. The only inferable "rule" is that lot size was scaled up roughly in proportion to account equity, which is a risky practice if applied naively (it increases dollar risk per trade as the account grows without an explicit cap).

## Caveats

No system logic, indicator, or reasoning is disclosed — this is pure output data with no way to verify how trades were selected or whether the results are representative, cherry-picked, or hypothetical/backtested versus real. The aggressive equity-proportional lot scaling shown here (1 lot to 30 lots in six months) is a red flag pattern often associated with unsustainable, high-variance systems that eventually suffer a large drawdown; readers should not infer that scaling position size with balance in this way is safe or repeatable.

## Who it is for

Readers studying real (or claimed) trade logs and equity curves as case studies in aggressive position-size scaling, rather than traders looking for a system to copy.
