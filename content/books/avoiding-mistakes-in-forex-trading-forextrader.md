## Summary

Cofnas frames forex trading as a problem of perspective and controllable behavior. He advises analyzing the message and incentives behind news rather than accepting a release at face value; a government statement can be a contrarian clue when officials want a currency to move in a particular direction (p. 1). He also says traders should avoid entering during panic or surprise surges, let the market digest the shock, and wait for confirmation because indicators become distorted during sudden moves (p. 1). A third recommendation is simplicity: use only tools that clarify trend, support, resistance, and buying or selling pressure. Point-and-figure charts are offered as one compact option (p. 1).

## Key points

- News interpretation includes source incentives and crowd psychology (p. 1).
- Surges can retrace toward pre-event ranges if no fundamental change persists (p. 1).
- Too many indicators can add redundant information (p. 1).
- The example point-and-figure chart uses 25-pip boxes and a 3-box reversal (p. 1).
- The second page is primarily broker advertising, not additional analysis.

## Actionable rules

1. Analyze the message behind a release and consider whether the public statement serves a policy objective (p. 1).
2. Wait for post-surge direction confirmation instead of trading the first panic move (p. 1).
3. Keep a compact toolset covering trend, support/resistance, and pressure; the example uses 25-pip/3-box point-and-figure settings (p. 1).

## Caveats

This is a brief 2002 advice article with promotional advertising. Contrarian interpretation is not a standalone signal, and the point-and-figure settings are an example.

## Who it is for

New forex traders learning to control news reactions and indicator overload.
