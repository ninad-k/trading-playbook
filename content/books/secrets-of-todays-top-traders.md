## Summary

An excerpt from Neal Weintraub's "Trading Chicago Style," an interview with an anonymous full-time futures trader ("C.V.") who left law practice to trade a long-term trend-following breakout system across a ten-market futures portfolio. It centers on his "Test 48" system, Kelly-criterion position sizing, and his verified three-year track record of 30–40% annual returns.

## Key points

- Trades a 10-market liquid futures portfolio (ED, TY, US, DM, JY, CD, CL, NG, C, CT), same system parameters across all markets to avoid curve-fitting to any one instrument.
- Kelly formula: Kelly = ((WP × W/L) − (1 − WP)) / W/L, where WP = win rate and W/L = average win / average loss.
- Test 48 backtest (1/91–6/96, 10-market portfolio, $60/contract commission+slippage): 42% win rate, 2.68 win/loss ratio, 20.3% Kelly.
- Out-of-sample extension (7/96–6/97): 53.2% win rate, 1.38 win/loss ratio, 19.4% Kelly — held up out of sample.
- Risking 2% of equity per trade (~10% of full Kelly) produced roughly 23% compound annual return with ~16.5% drawdown over 1/91–6/97; return eventually turns down past the system's Kelly-implied capacity while drawdown keeps rising.
- Defines a trend as 12 consecutive closes above (or below) the close from 24 trading days earlier.
- Cites a study of ~100 traders in which only 5% were profitable, mainly from cutting winners early while letting losers run.
- Live results run worse than backtests: a 20% historical drawdown could become 25–30% live, since live trading sees more extreme events than the sample window captured.

## Actionable rules

1. Position size using a fraction of full Kelly (this trader used roughly 10% of Kelly, i.e., about 2% risk per trade) rather than full Kelly, which maximizes return but produces unacceptable drawdowns.
2. Cap total open portfolio risk: no new trade is taken if existing open-position risk (entry price to stop, across the whole portfolio) exceeds 50% of equity.
3. Trade one simple breakout system with identical parameters across a diversified 6–10 market futures portfolio rather than optimizing separately per market.
4. Entry/exit logic (Test 48): enter long/short on a 48-day high/low breakout; exit on a 12-day trailing stop (opposite high/low).
5. Avoid placing entry orders on major scheduled report days until after the report is released.

## Caveats

This is a single trader's self-reported track record, not a peer-reviewed study; results depend heavily on the specific 1991–97 sample period and the ten markets chosen. No publication year is stated in the excerpt.

## Who it is for

Systematic futures traders and anyone wanting a concrete, numeric illustration of Kelly-based position sizing and long-term trend-following breakout mechanics from a real (if anonymized) practitioner.
