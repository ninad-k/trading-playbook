## Summary

A forex trading model by a self-described former CME floor trader using the pseudonym "Vegas," shared free on a trading forum. It trades breakouts of a "tunnel" formed by two EMAs (144 and 169 period) on the 1-hour chart, using a 12-period EMA as a filter. On a break above or below the tunnel, the trader enters in that direction with a stop on the opposite side, then scales out partial profits at successive Fibonacci pip levels (55, 89, 144, 233, 377) from the EMAs, letting a final portion ride to the largest target or a tunnel re-cross. The 144/169 EMA choice is framed via Fibonacci-number square roots and W. D. Gann's price/time work, though the author's real justification is that retracements empirically stop near those EMAs on 1-hour charts.

## Key points

- **The tunnel** — band between 144- and 169-period 1-hour EMAs; 144 is the only Fibonacci number with a whole-number square root (12), and 169 is the square of the nearest Fibonacci number (13).
- **12 EMA filter** — when the 12 EMA, price, and tunnel sit within about 5 pips of each other, a strong breakout is considered more likely.
- **Fibonacci pip targets** — 55, 89, 144, 233, 377 pips from the EMA, as successive partial-profit levels (34 added for less volatile pairs).
- **Preferred pairs** — GBP/USD and USD/CHF, which reach the larger extremes (233, 377) more often than EUR/USD or USD/JPY.
- **Session filter** — no new entries during the Asian session (5pm-midnight NY); open positions still managed normally.
- **News filter** — no new entries around US Non-Farm Payrolls.
- **Trend filter** — don't fade a strong prevailing trend on a minor tunnel signal; re-enter with the major trend on a re-cross.

## Actionable rules

1. Chart setup: 1-hour candlesticks with 12, 144, and 169-period EMAs.
2. Entry: long on a break above the tunnel; short on a break below.
3. Stop-loss: opposite side of the tunnel (typically 10-25 pips risk in the author's use).
4. Scale out roughly 1/3 of the position at each successive Fibonacci level reached; let the last unit ride to 377 or a tunnel re-cross.
5. Exit remaining position on a tunnel re-cross or the largest relevant target.
6. Skip entries during the Asian session and around major news (e.g., US NFP).
7. Don't take a tunnel breakout against a strong established trend.

## Caveats

Informal forum material from an anonymous author; performance claims are anecdotal, not independently backtested. Specific pip levels reflect mid-2000s GBP/USD and USD/CHF volatility, which has since changed materially. The Gann/Fibonacci rationale is speculative by the author's own admission.

## Who it is for

Trend/breakout forex swing traders comfortable with multi-target scaling on higher-volatility major pairs, willing to validate parameters on current data before trading live.
