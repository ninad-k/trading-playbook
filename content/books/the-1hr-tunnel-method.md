## Summary

A forex trading model, written by a self-described former CME floor trader using the pseudonym "Vegas," shared informally (free, no upsell) with a trading forum. The system trades breakouts of a "tunnel" formed by two exponential moving averages (144 and 169 period) on the 1-hour chart, using a 12-period EMA as a filter. When price breaks above or below the tunnel, the trader enters in that direction with a stop on the opposite side of the tunnel, then scales out partial profits at successive Fibonacci pip-distance levels (55, 89, 144, 233, 377) measured from the EMAs, letting a final portion of the position ride toward the largest target or until price re-crosses the tunnel. The author frames the moving-average periods (144, 169) as chosen for their relationship to Fibonacci numbers and their square roots, citing W. D. Gann's work on price/time/number relationships as inspiration, though he states the real justification is that retracements empirically stop near those EMAs on 1-hour charts.

## Key points

- **The tunnel** — the band between a 144-period and a 169-period 1-hour EMA; 144 is chosen because it is the only Fibonacci number with a whole-number square root (12), and 169 is the square of the nearest Fibonacci number to that root (13).
- **12-period EMA filter** — used to flag high-probability setups: when the 12 EMA, current price, and the tunnel are all within about 5 pips of each other, a strong breakout move is considered more likely.
- **Fibonacci pip targets** — 55, 89, 144, 233, 377 pips measured from the EMA (tunnel median or boundary), used as successive partial-profit levels; 34 is added for less volatile pairs.
- **Preferred pairs** — GBP/USD and USD/CHF, chosen for reaching the larger Fibonacci extremes (233, 377) more often than EUR/USD or USD/JPY.
- **Session filter** — no new tunnel entries during the Asian session (5pm-midnight NY time); existing positions are still managed and profit targets still honored.
- **News filter** — no new entries around US Non-Farm Payrolls (released 8:30am NY, first Friday of the month); existing positions still managed normally.
- **Trend filter** — do not fade a strong prevailing trend by taking the minor/contra-major tunnel breakout signal; only re-enter in the major trend direction when price re-crosses back through the tunnel.
- **Clean-move filter** — prefer setups where price crosses the tunnel in a single bar (an immediate, decisive break) over choppy multi-bar consolidation through the tunnel.

## Actionable rules

1. Chart setup: 1-hour candlestick chart with 12, 144, and 169-period EMAs plotted.
2. Entry: go long on a break above the upper tunnel boundary (169/144 EMA band); go short on a break below the lower boundary.
3. Stop-loss: placed on the opposite side of the tunnel from entry (typical risk cited as roughly 10-25 pips per trade in the author's own use).
4. Profit-taking: scale out roughly 1/3 of the position at each of the first Fibonacci levels reached (e.g., 3-unit trade: exit thirds at 55, 89, 144; 5-unit trade: exit fifths at 55, 89, 144, 233, and let the last unit ride to 377 or a tunnel re-cross).
5. Exit remaining position when price re-crosses the tunnel to the opposite side, or when the largest relevant Fibonacci target (233 or 377 pips) is reached.
6. Skip new entries during the Asian session and around major scheduled news (e.g., US NFP).
7. Do not take a tunnel breakout against a strong established trend; only trade tunnel breaks in the direction of the dominant trend during strong trending conditions.

## Caveats

Self-published, informal forum material from an anonymous author ("Vegas"); numeric performance claims (e.g., "19 of 20 trades profitable" in one filter example) are anecdotal, not from an independently verified backtest, and the recommended tools (fxtrek/Intellicharts, a paid charting service) and specific pip levels reflect mid-2000s GBP/USD and USD/CHF volatility, which has since changed materially (both pairs trade with far smaller typical ranges post-2015 than described here). The Gann/Fibonacci rationale for the 144/169 EMA choice is presented as numerology-adjacent speculation by the author's own admission ("I am not a Gann disciple") rather than a rigorously tested causal explanation.

## Who it is for

Trend/breakout forex swing traders comfortable with multi-target scaling and Fibonacci-based profit-taking on higher-volatility major pairs, willing to validate the specific EMA and pip parameters on current market data before trading live.
