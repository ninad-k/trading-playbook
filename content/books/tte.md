## Summary

A Trading Educators / Joe Ross slide deck teaching the Traders Trick Entry (TTE), a supplementary entry layered onto Ross's "Law of Charts" 1-2-3 formation and Ross Hook (assumed prior knowledge). Instead of waiting for price to break the obvious #2/hook point — where other traders' orders are bunched — the TTE enters earlier by buying (or selling) the violation of a smaller "correcting bar" that forms after the #2 point. The deck frames this as front-running the larger move's own order-triggering momentum, reinforced by Fibonacci and Gann retracement traders acting at similar levels.

## Key points

- Correcting bars are bars after the #2/hook point that make lower highs (for a long setup) — they need not make lower lows.
- Provision 1: there must be enough room between the TTE entry and the #2/hook point to cover costs and bank some profit.
- Provision 2: the 1-2-3 must form at the end of a trend, not inside a consolidation — those are meaningless.
- Provision 3: no more than 3 correcting bars are allowed before abandoning the setup (a double/triple high or low counts as one bar).
- Trade-off: violating the 1st correction bar gives the highest odds of continuation but least profit room; the 3rd bar gives the lowest odds but the most room.
- Rationale: the trader driving price toward the #2/hook point (where resting orders sit) can't easily stop that momentum without hurting himself, so TTE rides it; Fibonacci (0.382/0.5/0.618) and Gann (1/3, 1/2, 2/3) retracement traders add reinforcing pressure at similar levels.
- If the breakout proves false, Provision 1's cost-covering room typically still leaves a small profit or breakeven — described as a "free trade."

## Actionable rules

1. Confirm the 1-2-3 (or Ross Hook, itself following a 1-2-3, Ledge, or Consolidation) formed at trend end, not in a consolidation.
2. Once the #2/hook point is set, enter 1 tick above (long) or below (short) the extreme of the correcting bar(s).
3. Take the trade only if there is room to cover costs and bank profit before the #2/hook point; otherwise wait for the 2nd or 3rd correcting bar.
4. Abandon the setup past 3 correcting bars (double/triple extremes count as one).
5. Trail the entry/stop to 1 tick beyond each new correcting bar's extreme as price moves away.
6. Rules apply symmetrically to short setups. No explicit profit target or exit-stop rule is given here — that is referred to Ross's separate money-management material.

## Caveats

A promotional deck for Ross's Trading Educators business — roughly the last 15% of pages advertise other paid products (newsletters, seminars). No backtest, win rate, or real trade sample; all charts are idealized schematics. The technique supplements, and requires, Ross's proprietary 1-2-3/Ross Hook pattern definitions, which are not included in this file.

## Who it is for

Traders already familiar with Joe Ross's 1-2-3 and Ross Hook patterns who want a rules-based way to enter ahead of the obvious breakout level.
