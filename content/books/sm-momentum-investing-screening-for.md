## Summary

Thorp translates Richard Driehaus’s momentum approach into a stock screen combining improving earnings, positive surprises, price strength, industry strength, and tradability. The earnings filter requires year-over-year growth rates from year 4 to year 3, year 3 to year 2, year 2 to year 1, and the latest 12 months to increase sequentially, plus positive latest-year growth (p. 2). Applied to 9,269 companies, those criteria leave 220. The final eight sample companies have median latest 12-month earnings growth of 68.4%, versus 8.5% for the database, but median prior growth was 4.5% and −21.9%, illustrating turnaround cases rather than uniformly strong histories (p. 2). The next filter requires an earnings surprise at least 10% above consensus and at least three analysts; the 59 survivors become 40 (p. 3). Momentum filters require a positive four-week price change, 26-week company relative strength above the S&P 500, and a strong industry; the eight selected companies had median four-week price gain of 48% and median relative strength of about 216%, versus 1% and −8% for the full database (pp. 3–4). The screen then narrows to $50 million–$3 billion market capitalization, excludes ADRs, and requires top-half daily volume, ending with eight stocks (p. 5). The author stresses that screening is only the first step and that exits should limit losses while allowing momentum winners to run.

## Key points

- Large growth rates can result from tiny base earnings, so underlying dollars matter (p. 2).
- Industry and market context can overwhelm company-specific momentum (pp. 1, 4–5).
- The 2000 screen is descriptive and does not report subsequent returns.

## Actionable rules

1. Require sequential improvement in continuing-operations EPS growth from year 4→3, 3→2, 2→1, and year 1→trailing 12 months, with positive trailing-12-month growth (p. 2).
2. Require a latest-quarter earnings surprise of at least 10% above the median estimate and coverage by at least three analysts (p. 3).
3. Require positive four-week price change, positive 26-week relative strength versus the S&P 500, and a strong industry relative-strength reading (pp. 4–5).
4. Limit the screen to domestic non-ADR firms with $50 million–$3 billion capitalization and daily volume in the database’s top half; then perform further fundamental review (p. 5).

## Caveats

The screen is historical, data-source dependent, and vulnerable to survivorship, look-ahead, and selection bias. Thresholds such as 10% surprise and 26 weeks are design choices, not demonstrated optimal values.

## Who it is for

Investors building auditable momentum screens for small and mid-cap equities.
