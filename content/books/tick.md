## Summary

An *Active Trader* magazine article (April 2002) by Christopher Terry explaining how to combine the NYSE TICK breadth indicator with price-pattern and Fibonacci-retracement analysis to find low-risk intraday reversal and continuation trades, illustrated with five SPY (S&P 500 Index Trust) one-minute chart examples.

## Key points

- **TICK definition** — the difference between the number of NYSE stocks trading on an uptick versus a downtick at a given moment; not to be confused with a "tick" as a minimum price increment.
- **Overbought/oversold thresholds (author's own, condition-dependent)** — TICK above +500 = overbought, below −500 = oversold; readings beyond ±1000 are "extreme"; in a strong trend, the effective oversold/overbought threshold can shrink to ±100.
- **TICK divergence** — price makes a higher high (lower low) while TICK makes a lower high (higher low); signals weakening internal participation and a possible reversal — attributed to Laurence Connors and Linda Bradford Raschke's *Street Smarts*.
- **Range-trade setup** — at resistance, an overbought TICK reading (>+500) plus a short-term double top signals a short; at support, an oversold TICK plus a double bottom signals a long; the protective stop sits just beyond the pattern's extreme.
- **Trend pullback setup** — during a strong uptrend, wait for TICK to dip toward zero (or below, briefly) while price pulls back to a Fibonacci retracement (commonly 38% or 50%), then buy on a break above a countertrend channel line drawn on the pullback; mirror for downtrends.
- **Smoothing** — because TICK is a volatile snapshot, it is commonly smoothed with a 10-period moving average to see direction and pattern more clearly.
- **Usage caveat stated by the author** — TICK should confirm a setup (price pattern, channel break, Fibonacci level), not be traded as a standalone signal; patience is required for TICK to fully top/bottom before acting.

## Actionable rules

1. Short at resistance when intraday TICK exceeds +500 and price forms a short-term double top; stop above the double-top high.
2. Long at support when intraday TICK falls below −500 and price forms a double bottom; stop below the double-bottom low.
3. In a strong uptrend, buy when TICK dips toward/below zero at a 38%/50% Fibonacci retracement and price then closes back above a countertrend channel line; stop below the entry bar's low.
4. Watch for TICK divergence (price extreme not confirmed by a matching TICK extreme) as an early warning of a stalling move.
5. Smooth raw TICK with a 10-period moving average to reduce noise before reading direction.

## Caveats

All five illustrative examples use SPY one-minute charts from a single source (TradeStation) around 2001-2002 — no systematic backtest, win rate, or out-of-sample test is presented. TICK breadth composition and typical magnitude have shifted since 2002 with the growth of ETF and algorithmic flow, so the specific ±500/±1000 thresholds may need recalibration on modern data.

## Who it is for

Intraday index/ETF traders who already read price patterns and Fibonacci retracements and want a breadth-based confirmation tool layered on top.
