## Summary

A short, informally written ebook by forex trainer Rob Booker debunking four common myths about stop-loss orders: that there is a "magic" win/loss ratio, that stops aren't necessary, that brokers systematically "hunt" stops, and that a losing trader is doomed regardless of what they do. The core message is that a stop-loss is meaningless in isolation — it only works as part of a complete, written trading plan that also defines entries, exits, profit-taking, and position sizing, decided before the trade is placed rather than as an afterthought.

## Key points

- Myth 1: there is no universal "magic" win/average-loss ratio; a system's required ratio depends on its win rate and bet size, not a fixed number like "2:1."
- Myth 2: claiming you "don't need" a stop loss is dangerous — without one, a losing trade that would have reverted in a ranging market can instead be destroyed in a trending one.
- A stop-loss doesn't have to be a resting order with the broker; a strictly-obeyed mental stop is acceptable, though harder to execute in practice.
- Myth 3: most "broker is hunting my stops" complaints are paranoia rather than fact; if it happens repeatedly with one broker, switch brokers rather than assume a global conspiracy.
- Myth 4 / "Truth 1": traders who lose without a system or rules "deserve" the loss in the sense that trading has consequences like any rule-based discipline; most losses trace back to having no written system.
- A complete trading plan needs five components: (1) entry/exit rules, (2) profit-taking rules, (3) loss-taking (stop) rules, (4) bet/trade-size rules, (5) a description of the overall system.
- The author reports (anecdotally, from working with over 150 traders) that pessimistic/negative-thinking traders are consistently the worst at setting and honoring stops.
- No successful trader the author has personally observed traded profitably for more than a year without a written plan.

## Actionable rules

1. Decide your stop-loss level before entering a trade, never after.
2. Never trade without some form of stop — mental, platform order, or broker instruction — that you obey unconditionally.
3. Do not size a trading account with "it's not scared money" reasoning as a substitute for a stop-loss discipline.
4. Build a full plan with five parts (entry/exit, profit target, stop-loss, position size, system description) rather than trading opportunistically.
5. If a broker's stops get hit unjustifiably on a repeated basis, switch brokers rather than removing stops.

## Caveats

This is a short (11-page), informal, free promotional ebook with a casual, joke-heavy writing style and no specific numeric stop-loss formula, indicator, or backtested system — it's a psychology/discipline piece about the role of stops, not a technical method. No data or study is cited to support claims about trader behavior (e.g., the "150 traders" observation); these are the author's personal anecdotes.

## Who it is for

Beginner-to-intermediate forex traders who lack a written stop-loss discipline or who have talked themselves out of using stops; not for traders seeking a specific stop-placement technique or indicator.
