## Summary

A short opinion piece by Winsor A.G.A. Hoang, founder of a managed-forex signal/software service (ctsforex.com), arguing that marketing claims about trading forex profitably in "15 minutes a day" are unrealistic because forex is a 24-hour market that professional full-time traders monitor 10-12 hours daily. The article's main content is a set of due-diligence questions readers should ask before buying a forex course, book, or trading system: whether the seller actually earns their income from trading or from selling the product, how long a genuine mentor believes it would take to become a competent trader (comparing this to multi-year medical/professional apprenticeships), and skepticism toward vendors selling proprietary "expert advisors" cheaply when a genuinely profitable system would not need to be sold. It closes by promoting the author's own managed-account / signal service as the stated solution for people who cannot trade full-time.

## Key points

- Forex is a continuous 24-hour market; the article argues no one can meaningfully trade it well in only 15 minutes a day.
- Suggested vetting question for any trading book/course/system seller: "Is the author making money from trading or from the profits of selling the item?"
- Suggested vetting question for a paid mentor: ask how long they believe it would take you to become a good trader under their full-time mentorship — a "few months" answer is presented as a red flag.
- Analogy to licensed professions: doctors require two years of residency, and many trades require 12-18 month apprenticeships, implied as a more realistic timeline for developing trading competence.
- Red flag on commercial expert advisors (EAs): if a $300-$1,200 EA genuinely produced $1,000/month, the article argues the seller would have no reason to sell it that cheaply.
- Recommends looking for six-to-nine-month live track records rather than trusting forum testimonials or short (e.g., three-week) performance claims.
- The article's own proposed solution to the "15 minutes a day" problem is renting/using a managed forex trading software service from a "creditable and trusted" vendor rather than self-directed part-time trading.

## Actionable rules

1. Before buying any forex course, book, or system, ask whether the seller's primary income is from trading or from selling the product.
2. Before engaging a paid mentor, ask them directly how long they estimate it would take you to become consistently profitable under their guidance; treat a "few months" answer skeptically.
3. Be skeptical of commercial expert advisors priced at $300-$1,200 that claim large monthly returns — question why a genuinely profitable system would be sold rather than traded privately.
4. Require six-to-nine-month verified live track records before trusting a vendor's performance claims, not short (multi-week) samples or forum testimonials.
5. If unable to commit to full-time market monitoring, consider a reputable managed account or signal service rather than attempting part-time self-directed trading (the article's own recommended path, disclosed as coming from a vendor of such a service).

## Caveats

This is a short promotional article from the founder of a managed-forex-signal business (ctsforex.com), and its concluding recommendation — use a managed trading software service — directly promotes the author's own product, which is a clear conflict of interest given the article's own stated test ("is the author making money from trading or from selling the item?"). It contains no trading rules, indicators, or system content; its value is limited to a short list of consumer-protection style questions for vetting forex vendors.

## Who it is for

Beginners evaluating whether to buy a forex course, EA, or mentorship program, looking for basic due-diligence questions rather than any trading technique.
