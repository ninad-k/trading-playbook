## Summary

A short SFO Magazine feature (February 2003) by 20-year CBOT pit scalper Douglas Zalesky, laying out his "25 Rules of Trading Discipline." The core argument: trading success is roughly 90% discipline, and Zalesky's own edge came not from technical or fundamental analysis (he states he has no formal training in either) but from strict, repeatable behavioral rules around position sizing, loss-cutting, and consistency. Written as a daily pre-market checklist for his mentees.

## Key points

- **Wheel of Success** — three elements of trading: content (live time-and-price information), mechanics (fast, error-free execution), and discipline (practiced 100% of the time, not "most trades").
- Author's own daily trade mix: roughly 33% losers, 33% scratches, 33% winners, yet consistently profitable because losers are cut small.
- Found his own comfort zone between 10-20 lots, never traded more than 50; beyond his comfort zone execution quality ("butchering" the trade) suffered.
- Distinguishes a "real" losing trade from an acceptable one: taking a loss is fine, staying in a trade you already know is bad is the actual failure.
- Advocates scaling out of winners (selling half at the first target, moving the stop to breakeven) but exiting losers in full, never partially.

## Actionable rules

1. Lower trade size after 2 consecutive losers; only raise it back after 2 consecutive winners.
2. Never let a winning trade turn into a loser — treated as absolute, though no specific stop distance is given.
3. Never let your biggest loser on the session exceed your biggest winner.
4. Set a hard daily loss limit (his example: $500) and stop trading once it's hit.
5. Require a proven track record (his example: 10 consecutive profitable days at 1 lot) before increasing size.
6. Exit losing trades immediately once recognized as bad; never average down or wait hoping for a reversal.
7. Scale out of winners at a first target, moving the stop on the remainder to entry ("playing with the house's money").
8. Write down the specific setup/prerequisites required before taking any trade.
9. Ignore financial news commentary — by the time it airs, professionals have already acted on it.

## Caveats

Written from the perspective of a floor/electronic futures scalper (bonds, S&P, e-Mini, Dow futures) with no stated backtest or performance statistics beyond the author's personal claims. Dollar figures ($500 daily loss limit, 10-bond-tick daily target) are specific to his own account size and era and should be rescaled, not copied literally. No entry signal or methodology is given — the piece is entirely about risk and behavior management around trades however they're generated.

## Who it is for

Short-term futures/day traders and scalpers looking for a concise, checklist-style discipline framework rather than a chart-pattern or indicator system.
