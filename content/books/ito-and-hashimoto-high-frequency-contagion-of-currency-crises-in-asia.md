## Summary

Ito and Hashimoto study whether the 1997–99 Asian crisis spread from an identifiable first-hit country to other markets at daily frequency. They use daily exchange rates for Indonesia, Korea, Malaysia, Philippines, Taiwan, and Thailand from 1997–99, and stock indexes from 1994–99 (pp. 3, 8). The paper labels the “origin” as the country with the largest five-day weighted depreciation, provided the decline exceeds 2%; the weights are 0.5, 0.25, 0.125, 0.0625, and 0.0625 from the current day through four lags (pp. 11–12). Dynamic OLS regressions and trade-link measures test whether the origin’s move predicts affected markets.

## Key points

- The weighted origin measure identifies Thailand in July 1997, Indonesia in August–September, and Korea in late 1997, broadly matching the crisis chronology (pp. 12–14).
- The contagion coefficient is `CC(t,i) = DRR(t,i) / DOR(t,0)`; positive values mean the affected market depreciates or falls with the origin (pp. 14–15).
- Exchange-rate contagion is statistically positive for several pairs, especially shocks originating in Indonesia or Korea; Taiwan is relatively weakly affected (pp. 16–19).
- Pooled stock-price contagion is significant across the sample and becomes stronger after the crisis begins, although country-specific signs vary (pp. 17, 20–21).
- The exchange-rate regression’s pooled-origin coefficients are about 0.12–0.19 for Korea, Malaysia, and the Philippines, while Taiwan’s is insignificant (pp. 18–19; Table 4-1, p. 47).
- Correlations between contagion and export share, direct trade linkage, income effect, and cheap-import effect are 0.329, 0.258, 0.357, and 0.384 respectively (pp. 23–26).
- The authors interpret the positive trade relationships as evidence that competitive and income channels dominated any welfare benefit from cheaper imports (pp. 24–27).

## Actionable rules

1. None given. The paper estimates conditional spillovers after a crisis origin is observed; it does not propose an entry, exit, stop, or position-sizing system.
2. For research replication, use the stated 2% threshold and five-day declining weights, rather than treating these as validated live-trading signals (pp. 11–12).

## Caveats

The sample is historical and limited to six Asian markets during one crisis episode. The 2% cutoff and weighting scheme are described as arbitrary (pp. 11–12), and daily data cannot establish a structural causal mechanism by itself. Correlation with trade indices supports a channel but does not rule out common shocks, financial similarity, or investor-creditor effects. Results also vary materially by origin country and subperiod (pp. 16–21).

## Who it is for

Researchers and macro or cross-market traders studying crisis transmission, regional risk, and event-driven correlation. It is useful for regime awareness, not a standalone signal generator.
