## What it is

Gary Smith's core method for trading (mostly no-load) mutual funds, and by extension stocks or index shares, by reacting to a handful of recurring multi-day and intraday momentum patterns rather than forecasting or using lagging technical indicators. It is discretionary rather than mechanical — patterns are confirmed against sentiment/breadth indicators before being acted on — but the entry triggers, position-building increments, and exit thresholds are stated as specific numbers throughout the book (see [[gary-smith-how-i-trade-living]]). It produced real, audited returns of 40.55% (1996), 46.88% (1997), and 84.90% (1998) in Smith's tracked INVESCO fund account, against 23.25%/33.55%/28.56% for the S&P 500 over the same years.

## Rules

**Entry 1 — V-bottom reversal.** The Dow trades down at least 0.75% intraday, then reverses to close near unchanged or higher. Only actionable after a prior down day or downtrend; the later in the session, the stronger the signal.

**Entry 2 — late-day upside surge.** A choppy session rallies in its final 2-2.5 hours to close at least 0.50% above the prior close, again only after prior weakness.

**Entry 3 — extreme momentum day / Friday-to-Monday.** A day's price action more extreme than the past several months tends to see continuation. Separately, strong (weak) Friday action is expected to carry into Monday; when it doesn't (a "momentum break"), trade the new direction instead.

**Entry 4 — index divergence.** Track a rolling point-ratio between the Dow, cash S&P, and Nasdaq 100/Composite (or Russell 2000 for small-cap rotation), recomputed periodically. When one index outruns what the ratio implies, trade toward the stronger index — wait until mid-morning to trust it, and treat it as good for the next 1-2 hours, not the whole day. After a V-bottom or late-day surge, technology funds show the most next-day follow-through.

**Seasonal overlays** (add conviction, not standalone signals): January Barometer — the fund/sector with the strongest gain over the first 5-7 trading days of January tends to lead for 2-6 months. Thanksgiving-to-January — buy a fund near 52-week highs with strong 4-week momentum on the Friday before Thanksgiving (this window has produced ~40% of the market's total gain since 1928). New-fund effect — buy newly launched, little-publicized funds immediately or within 1-2 weeks of early momentum; exit within 3 days if none appears. Small-cap divergence — after a large-cap rally (typically following a 7%+ Dow/S&P decline), rotate into small caps the first day the Russell 2000 clearly outperforms.

**Position sizing.** Risk no more than 1-2% of the position on the initial purchase. Build the full position in 25-33% increments as it moves favorably (scale-up); never average down.

**Exits.** Hold as long as the position doesn't react more than 3-4% from its recent high (tight-rising-channel stop). Separately, after 2+ weeks of rising prices, if the Dow, S&P, Nasdaq 100, and Russell 2000 all close down 1%+ on the same day (tolerance: one index at 0.75-1% if the others are 1.5-2%+), sell 25-50% immediately, and the rest if selling continues or a second such day occurs within a week — this market-wide trigger overrides the individual position's 3-4% channel. If the true-selling-day signal proves false (no carryover selling), re-enter promptly rather than staying defensive.

## Risk

Because positions scale up as they move favorably, realized dollar risk on a mature position is materially larger than the 1-2% entry-stage figure — the 3-4% trailing-channel exit is the real risk control on winners. The true-selling-day exit is also the least reliable signal, so false signals are expected and must be met with fast re-entry, not a defensive posture; failing to re-enter after a false signal is the method's biggest cited source of lost opportunity. The system assumes fund-switching without redemption fees or settlement delay — restrictive fund families degrade its exit timing.

## Caveats

These are discretionary pattern-recognition heuristics (index ratios are "rules of thumb," recalculated periodically, not fixed constants), not a mechanical, backtestable system — Smith says his own attempts to automate this style failed. The fund-switching mechanics (no redemption fees, orders to the 4:00 p.m. close) reflect late-1990s industry practice that many fund families have since restricted. The seasonal statistics rest on personal observation and a handful of late-1990s third-party studies, not an independent updated backtest.
