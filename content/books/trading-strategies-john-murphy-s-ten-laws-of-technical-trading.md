## Summary

A StockCharts.com educational compendium built around John Murphy's "Ten Laws of Technical Trading," followed by several independently authored short pieces: Richard Rhodes' 18 trend-following trading rules, a "Last Stochastic" (39-period, unsmoothed) technique with OBV confirmation, Arthur Hill's notes on goals/style/strategy and moving-average crossover systems, and a detailed eight-strategy gap-trading tutorial with a historical "pre-holiday effect" study.

## Key points

- **Murphy's Ten Laws**: map long-term trends first (monthly/weekly before daily/intraday) and trade in that direction; use support/resistance (old highs become new lows after a break, and vice versa); measure retracements (50% typical, 33-67% range, Fibonacci 38%/62%); draw trendlines (valid once touched ≥3 times); use MA crossovers (popular pairs: 4/9, 9/18, 5/20 day) and oscillators (RSI 70/30, Stochastics 80/20) in ranging markets; use MACD and ADX (rising ADX favors MAs, falling ADX favors oscillators) to gauge trend strength; confirm with volume and open interest.
- **Richard Rhodes' rules**: in bull markets stay long or flat, never short; buy strength, sell weakness; never average into a losing position (each new buy higher, each new sell lower); add only 1/4-1/2 of current size when scaling in; take small quick losses, let winners run; the final 10% of a bull run's time can hold 50%+ of its price movement.
- **"Last Stochastic" (K39)**: an unsmoothed 39-period stochastic; buy when K crosses above 50% with close above prior week's high close, sell/short on the mirror condition; confirm with On Balance Volume plus a 30-day MA.
- **MA crossover examples**: 30/100 EMA (Xircom) and 20/60 EMA with a ±2% buffer around zero on the PPO differential (3Com) to reduce whipsaws.
- **Gap trading**: defines Full/Partial Gap Up/Down; eight strategies (long/short × 4 gap types); enter on a breakout of the first hour's range with a stop 2 ticks beyond it; 8% trailing stop for longs, 4% for shorts.
- **Pre-holiday effect**: a historical study (1928-1975 base) finding stocks bought 1-2 days before major US holidays and held to year-end showed positive average returns in most cases (e.g., New Year's: 19.6% buying 1 day before vs. 31.1% buying 2 days before).

## Actionable rules

1. Use the direction of the longer-timeframe chart to determine trend direction; use the shorter timeframe only for entry timing.
2. RSI: overbought above 70, oversold below 30 (9-14 period); Stochastics: overbought above 80, oversold below 20 (14-period).
3. Gap trading: enter on a stop 2 ticks beyond the first hour's high (long) or low (short); use an 8% trailing stop for longs and 4% for shorts.
4. Never add to a losing position; only add to winners, and only 1/4 to 1/2 of the existing position size.
5. Require ADX direction to choose indicator type: rising ADX → trade with moving averages; falling ADX → trade with oscillators.

## Caveats

This is a compilation of separately authored, promotional/educational web content (StockCharts.com, c. 2000) rather than a single coherent, backtested system; the gap-trading and pre-holiday statistics are presented without transaction-cost-adjusted, statistically rigorous validation, and several examples use dot-com-era stocks and price levels (pre-decimalization "ticks" of 1/8-1/4 point) that are now obsolete market structure.

## Who it is for

Retail technical traders wanting a single-document survey of classic trend-following rules, momentum oscillators, and short-term gap-trading mechanics before specializing further.
