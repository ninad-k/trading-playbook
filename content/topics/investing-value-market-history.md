## What it is

This category holds the part of the library that buys businesses rather than trading prices. Its horizon is years, its unit of analysis is the company, and its central question is what something is worth rather than where it is going next week. Three traditions run through it. Value investing starts from Graham: a price meaningfully below a conservatively estimated intrinsic value, with the gap — the margin of safety — doing the work that forecasting cannot. Growth investing starts from the opposite end, screening for accelerating earnings and price leadership and accepting a high multiple for it. And market history supplies the frame: long cycles, bear markets, manias and the recurring observation that whichever style dominated the last decade tends to be the one crowded and overpriced at the start of the next. Almost nothing here uses a stop; risk is managed by what you pay and how much you diversify.

## Core principles

- Price is not value. Graham's Mr. Market offers a mood-driven quote daily, and the investor is free to accept, reject or ignore it [[the-intelligent-investor-benjamin-graham]] [[dean-lebaron-s-treasury-of-investment-wisdom-30-great-investing-minds-wiley]].
- The margin of safety substitutes for forecasting accuracy: it makes being roughly right survivable rather than guaranteeing a profit [[the-intelligent-investor-benjamin-graham]] [[daniel-a-strachman-essential-stock-picking-strategies]].
- Every asset's value is the present value of its expected cash flows discounted at a rate reflecting their risk; every multiple is a shortcut derived from that same logic [[investment-valuation-damodaran]].
- A multiple is only cheap after controlling for the growth, risk and payout differences a full DCF would have priced [[investment-valuation-damodaran]] [[streetsmart-guide-to-valuing-a-stock]].
- Statistically cheap baskets outperform because analysts and investors extrapolate the recent past too far, and the reappraisal is slow — often taking years [[david-dreman-contrarian-investment-strategies-the-next-generation]].
- Every investment story has a kernel of truth and a specific failure mode: low P/E often signals low growth or poor earnings quality rather than mispricing, high yield can be a bond substitute without the contract [[damodaran-aswath-investment-fables]].
- Naive value screens concentrate: a high-yield or low-multiple screen can put 40% of the portfolio into REITs and 20% each into utilities and financials [[damodaran-aswath-investment-fables]].
- Cheap-looking stocks often carry more leverage, so screen with an explicit debt ceiling before buying the multiple [[damodaran-aswath-investment-fables]] [[kenneth-l-fisher-super-stocks]].
- Selling matters as much as buying. O'Neil treats an inviolable loss limit as more important than stock selection because it caps the majority of picks that will be wrong [[how-to-make-money-in-stocks-pdf-forex]].
- Asset allocation, not security selection, drives most of a portfolio's return — Hayden cites up to 90% [[getting-an-investing-game-plan-creating-it-working-it-winning-it-wiley-2003]].
- Discipline consistently applied beats intelligence or prediction, and which discipline matters less than the consistency [[daniel-a-strachman-essential-stock-picking-strategies]].
- Markets do not pick managers' styles for them — each style works in some environments and fails in others, and yesterday's dominant approach tends to end up concentrated in the same overpriced names [[dean-lebaron-s-treasury-of-investment-wisdom-30-great-investing-minds-wiley]].
- Bear markets are a recurring structural feature, not an aberration; Schultz puts the market in bear conditions roughly 35% of the time [[harry-d-schultz-bear-market-investing-strategies]].
- Backward-looking fund ratings are a hidden risk: top-rated funds underperformed in the year after being rated in the studies cited [[divorcing-the-dow-using-revolutionary-market-indicators-to-profit-from-the-stealth-boom-ah]].
- Value investing has no natural stop: a falling price is reinterpreted as a better opportunity, which is exactly what technical triggers supply and fundamentals do not [[deborah-owen-mapping-the-markets]].

## Concrete rules and setups

### Graham's screens

1. Defensive allocation: never less than 25% or more than 75% in either stocks or bonds, defaulting to 50/50 and rebalanced as it drifts [[the-intelligent-investor-benjamin-graham]].
2. Defensive stock rules: 10 to 30 issues, each company large, prominent and conservatively financed, with a long record of continuous dividends and a price carrying a cushion against a conservative value estimate [[the-intelligent-investor-benjamin-graham]].
3. Graham's numeric filters — current ratio, dividend history, P/E and P/B caps — were calibrated to 1970s balance sheets and exclude almost the entire modern large-cap market if applied literally; most readers use them as a relative ranking [[the-intelligent-investor-benjamin-graham]].

### Quantitative value screens

4. Magic Formula: from the largest ~3,500 US-listed companies excluding utilities, financials and ADRs, rank every company by return on capital (EBIT ÷ [net working capital + net fixed assets]) and again by earnings yield (EBIT ÷ enterprise value), sum the two ranks, and hold 20-30 of the lowest combined ranks on a rolling one-year-per-position schedule [[the-little-book--magic-formula]].
5. Dreman's contrarian screens: within a large-cap universe, buy the cheapest quintile by P/E, by price-to-cash-flow, or by industry-relative price-to-book, or the highest-yielding quintile — with low P/E as the preferred core. Diversify to roughly 16 names [[david-dreman-contrarian-investment-strategies-the-next-generation--contrarian-value-screens]].
6. Dreman's significance testing: low P/E and low P/CF each showed roughly a 1-in-200 probability of the historical outperformance being chance, low P/BV about 1-in-100 [[david-dreman-contrarian-investment-strategies-the-next-generation]].
7. Fisher's Price/Sales screen: buy Super Companies at a PSR of 0.75 or less, avoid above 1.5, never buy above 3.0, and sell between 3.0 and 6.0. Basic-industry names use a separate band — buy under 0.4, sell approaching 0.8 [[kenneth-l-fisher-super-stocks--price-sales-and-price-research-screen]].
8. Fisher's balance-sheet cushion: five years of worst-case losses covered, three years of negative cash flow, debt under 40% of assets, and management ownership at least ten times salary [[kenneth-l-fisher-super-stocks]].
9. Tengler's relative value: route dividend payers to a Relative Dividend Yield screen (stock yield ÷ market yield, requiring at least 125%) and low- or non-payers to a Relative Price-to-Sales screen, within a universe of large caps above $3bn [[n-tengler-new-era-value-investing-a-disciplined-approach-to-buying-value-and-growth-stocks--relative-value-discipline]].
10. Manager-level filters from practice: a 15% minimum return on book value, 3-5 year growth faster than inflation, no more than 12.9× the manager's own forward earnings estimate, and a preference for companies with more cash than debt; Tweedy Browne's caps of 3-4% per position and 15-20% per industry are the only explicit portfolio limits in that book [[daniel-a-strachman-essential-stock-picking-strategies]].

### Growth screens

11. CAN SLIM: current quarterly EPS up at least 20-50% year over year on like quarters (8-10% is explicitly inadequate); annual EPS compounding 25-50% over three to five years with at most one down year; a new catalyst plus a new price high out of a proper base; a small float; relative strength at or above 70-90 — the 500 best performers from 1953-1993 averaged 87 just before their advance; institutional sponsorship; and a market-direction filter [[how-to-make-money-in-stocks-pdf-forex--can-slim-system]].
12. O'Neil's sell rules: cut every loss at 7-8% below cost without exception, and take profits around 20-25%. Being wrong twice at 8% and right once at 20% still nets a gain [[how-to-make-money-in-stocks-pdf-forex]].
13. Buffett's twelve tenets as a checklist: the business simple and understandable, with a consistent operating history and favourable long-term prospects; management rational, candid, and resistant to the institutional imperative; plus the financial and value tenets [[r-g-hagstrom-the-warren-buffett-way-2nd-edition--twelve-tenets-checklist]].
14. Lynch's rules for individual stock-pickers: keep a written log of why you own each stock, favour facts over forecasts, require a 3:1 reward-to-risk before buying, check the balance sheet, avoid margin and options, treat clustered insider buying as positive, hold 5-10 companies you can actually monitor, and expect three to four years for the biggest winners [[dean-lebaron-s-treasury-of-investment-wisdom-30-great-investing-minds-wiley]].

### Valuation

15. Model choice by cash-flow policy: use the dividend discount model only when dividends track free cash flow to equity closely, FCFE when payout persistently differs from what the firm can afford, and FCFF at the cost of capital when leverage is changing or the whole firm is being valued [[investment-valuation-damodaran--dcf-valuation-method]].
16. Terminal growth cannot exceed the long-run nominal growth rate of the economy the firm operates in [[investment-valuation-damodaran]].
17. Fundamental growth: g = retention ratio × return on equity, so a growth assumption implies a reinvestment assumption and cannot be set independently [[investment-valuation-damodaran]].
18. Because terminal value dominates the answer, stress-test across a range of terminal growth rates and competitive-advantage-period lengths rather than trusting one point estimate — one worked example swings from roughly $25 to over $60 on a few points of growth [[investment-valuation-damodaran]].
19. FCFF checklist: forecast revenue growth, net operating profit margin, tax rate, net investment rate and working-capital rate over the excess-return period, then discount at the cost of capital [[streetsmart-guide-to-valuing-a-stock--fcff-valuation-checklist]].

### Timing, cycles and sentiment

20. Best Six Months: hold the index from 1 November to 30 April. The almanac's Dow illustration grows $10,000 to $492,060 across the favourable halves against $9,682 across May-October, before costs and taxes; Nasdaq's favourable span runs November through June [[john-wiley-sons-stock-trader-almanac-2005-38th-ed]].
21. Williams's Dow rotation: in the last trading week of October buy the five lowest-priced, highest-yielding Dow Industrials; in the first week of April rotate into the five lowest-priced, highest-yielding Dow Utilities, optionally filtering on price-to-sales below 1.0 [[l-williams-the-right-stock-at-the-right-time-prospering-in-the-coming-good-years--dow-seasonal-rotation-system]].
22. Crowd sentiment extremes: AAII bull share below 44% is bullish and above 61% bearish; Investors Intelligence thresholds differ by rate regime — below 51% and above 81% in a falling-rate regime, below 31% and above 58% in a rising one. Treat a crossing as a flag, not a trigger [[mcgraw-hill-the-triumph-of-contrarian-investing-crowds-manias-and-beating-the-market-by-go--ndr-crowd-sentiment-indicators]].
23. Schultz's bear tactics: in a late bull market sell across the board without trying to catch the top, using stops 5-15% below price set from the short-term trendline; during a mid-bear secondary reaction sell into strength in stages, and cap any counter-trend buy at 10-25% of a normal position and 25-30% of total commitment [[harry-d-schultz-bear-market-investing-strategies]].
24. The 50% principle: rallies and reactions tend to retrace about half the preceding primary move; holding above the midpoint favours the prior trend resuming, and a break below signals reversal [[harry-d-schultz-bear-market-investing-strategies]].
25. Cycle scaffolding: a roughly 50-60 year Kondratieff wave, a 9-11 year Juglar business cycle, and a 4-year Kitchin cycle tied to the US electoral calendar, with the 200-day moving average defining the primary trend and 20-30% deviation from it marking stretched conditions [[deborah-owen-mapping-the-markets]].
26. Insider setup: the strongest bottom combines heavy insider buying, extreme pessimism, high mutual-fund cash, an oversold market and panic news — with insider signals often leading price by months [[georgemuzea-the-vital-few-vs-the-trivial-many-invest-with-the-insiders-not-the-masses]].

### Portfolio construction

27. The five-year rule: money that may be needed within five years stays out of stocks and bonds entirely [[getting-an-investing-game-plan-creating-it-working-it-winning-it-wiley-2003]].
28. Take only the risk needed to reach the target return for the goal and horizon, cap any speculative sleeve at 5%, and diversify at three nested levels — asset class, fund style and individual manager [[getting-an-investing-game-plan-creating-it-working-it-winning-it-wiley-2003]].
29. Exclude sub-$2 to sub-$5 stocks from value screens, where transaction costs can exceed 25% of the position [[damodaran-aswath-investment-fables]].
30. Real diversification means holding multiple market-cap and style segments rather than several funds that turn out to hold the same names [[divorcing-the-dow-using-revolutionary-market-indicators-to-profit-from-the-stealth-boom-ah]].

## Common mistakes

- Buying a low multiple without asking why it is low — poor earnings quality, low growth, or genuine distress rather than mispricing [[damodaran-aswath-investment-fables]] [[david-dreman-contrarian-investment-strategies-the-next-generation]].
- Running a screen and accepting the sector concentration it produces without a second diversification pass [[damodaran-aswath-investment-fables]].
- Treating a yield inflated by a collapsing share price as income rather than as a warning [[damodaran-aswath-investment-fables]].
- Comparing a quarter to the one immediately before it rather than to the same quarter a year earlier [[how-to-make-money-in-stocks-pdf-forex--can-slim-system]].
- Holding a losing growth position past the loss limit, which O'Neil treats as the error that destroys the whole expectancy [[how-to-make-money-in-stocks-pdf-forex]].
- Setting a terminal growth rate above the economy's long-run nominal growth, which values the firm as eventually larger than everything [[investment-valuation-damodaran]].
- Reading one DCF output as an answer when terminal value dominates it and the inputs are judgement calls [[investment-valuation-damodaran]].
- Setting a growth assumption independently of the reinvestment it requires [[investment-valuation-damodaran]].
- Applying Graham's 1970s numeric filters literally to a modern market where almost nothing passes [[the-intelligent-investor-benjamin-graham]].
- Chasing recently hot managers, funds or narrow specialty indexes [[david-dreman-contrarian-investment-strategies-the-next-generation]] [[divorcing-the-dow-using-revolutionary-market-indicators-to-profit-from-the-stealth-boom-ah]].
- Mistaking several funds for diversification when they hold overlapping positions [[getting-an-investing-game-plan-creating-it-working-it-winning-it-wiley-2003]] [[divorcing-the-dow-using-revolutionary-market-indicators-to-profit-from-the-stealth-boom-ah]].
- Committing money needed within five years to the risk portfolio [[getting-an-investing-game-plan-creating-it-working-it-winning-it-wiley-2003]].
- Buying into a mid-bear secondary reaction as though it were the reversal — Rhea's data shows these retrace 40-85% and more [[harry-d-schultz-bear-market-investing-strategies]].
- Reverse-engineering criteria from known winners without asking how many stocks met the same screen and failed, which is the acknowledged gap in the CAN SLIM research [[how-to-make-money-in-stocks-pdf-forex]].

## Best books for this topic

1. [[the-intelligent-investor-benjamin-graham]] — the origin of margin of safety, Mr. Market and the defensive/enterprising split. Read the framework; treat the numeric filters as historical.
2. [[damodaran-aswath-investment-fables]] — the most useful book here for a sceptic: every popular story stated, tested against real cross-sections, and shown exactly where it breaks.
3. [[investment-valuation-damodaran]] — the reference for actually valuing something, and honest that terminal value dominates the answer.
4. [[david-dreman-contrarian-investment-strategies-the-next-generation]] — the behavioural case for value with unusually well-documented backtests and explicit significance testing.
5. [[how-to-make-money-in-stocks-pdf-forex]] — every criterion attached to a number, and the clearest statement anywhere that the sell rule matters more than the buy.
6. [[the-little-book]] — the most reproducible system in the category: two ratios, a rank-sum, and no judgement required.
7. [[r-g-hagstrom-the-warren-buffett-way-2nd-edition]] — the twelve tenets turn a famously qualitative approach into something close to a checklist.
8. [[kenneth-l-fisher-super-stocks]] — the origin of the price-to-sales ratio, with explicit entry and exit bands rather than vague guidance.
9. [[dean-lebaron-s-treasury-of-investment-wisdom-30-great-investing-minds-wiley]] — the best map of the philosophy landscape, with a deliberate counterargument in every chapter.
10. [[harry-d-schultz-bear-market-investing-strategies]] — the one book here written entirely for the 35% of the time the rest of the category struggles.
11. [[deborah-owen-mapping-the-markets]] — connects long-cycle macro theory to a practical technical primer, and is candid that the cycle theories are contested.
12. [[n-tengler-new-era-value-investing-a-disciplined-approach-to-buying-value-and-growth-stocks]] — relative dividend yield as a discipline: never buy below an RDY of 125%, and sell by default when the stock's yield falls back to the market's, with relative price-to-sales standing in for non-payers.
13. [[steven-bolten-stock-market-cycles]] — the cleanest single explanation of *why* the market cycles: whichever is changing faster, expected earnings or the required return, sets direction, reduced to a nine-cell matrix.
14. [[mcgraw-hill-the-triumph-of-contrarian-investing-crowds-manias-and-beating-the-market-by-go]] — sentiment measured rather than asserted, with numeric thresholds (the Rydex bull/bear asset ratio above 82.5% or below 51%, the COT speculator index against its 78-week range).
15. [[l-williams-the-right-stock-at-the-right-time-prospering-in-the-coming-good-years]] — the decennial and seasonal studies behind the calendar folklore, including the October-bottom statistics and the Dow-yield valuation bands.
16. [[mcgraw-hill-buy-the-rumor-sell-the-fact-85-maxims-of-wall-street-and-what-they-really-mean]] — 85 Wall Street sayings taken apart one at a time; useful mainly for noticing how many of them contradict each other.
17. [[when-buy-means-sell-mcgraw-hill]] — a rules-based way to use analyst recommendations against the crowd, with the backtests laid out.

Two of these overlap deliberately with the timing material above: Bolten supplies the valuation mechanism, [[mcgraw-hill-the-triumph-of-contrarian-investing-crowds-manias-and-beating-the-market-by-go]] supplies the crowd measurement, and [[l-williams-the-right-stock-at-the-right-time-prospering-in-the-coming-good-years]] supplies the calendar evidence — three different answers to the same question of when the odds shift.

## Open debates

- **Whether a multiple can substitute for a valuation.** Dreman, Greenblatt and Fisher all trade multiples directly and back it with data [[david-dreman-contrarian-investment-strategies-the-next-generation]] [[the-little-book--magic-formula]] [[kenneth-l-fisher-super-stocks]]; Damodaran shows every multiple is derivable from a DCF and therefore only meaningful after controlling for growth, risk and payout — making him markedly more sceptical of pure multiple investing [[investment-valuation-damodaran]].
- **Value or growth.** Dreman's evidence is that statistically cheap, out-of-favour stocks beat expensive ones over six decades [[david-dreman-contrarian-investment-strategies-the-next-generation]]; O'Neil argues explicitly against buying low P/E and screens for accelerating earnings at new highs [[how-to-make-money-in-stocks-pdf-forex]]. Damodaran's answer is that both stories are partly true and both have a failure mode their advocates omit [[damodaran-aswath-investment-fables]].
- **Concentrate or diversify.** Buffett's circle of competence argues for a small number of deeply understood holdings, and Lynch for 5-10 monitorable names [[r-g-hagstrom-the-warren-buffett-way-2nd-edition]] [[dean-lebaron-s-treasury-of-investment-wisdom-30-great-investing-minds-wiley]]; Graham requires 10-30 issues, Dreman about 16, Greenblatt 20-30 [[the-intelligent-investor-benjamin-graham]] [[the-little-book--magic-formula]]. The LeBaron anthology deliberately places the diversification chapters against Buffett's concentration without resolving it.
- **Whether an investor should ever use a stop.** O'Neil's 7-8% rule is non-negotiable and Schultz sets stops the day a position opens [[how-to-make-money-in-stocks-pdf-forex]] [[harry-d-schultz-bear-market-investing-strategies]]; none of the value managers profiled uses price stops at all, controlling risk by not overpaying in the first place [[daniel-a-strachman-essential-stock-picking-strategies]] [[kenneth-l-fisher-super-stocks]].
- **Whether timing is possible or even necessary.** Graham rejects timing the general market outright, substituting allocation and margin of safety [[the-intelligent-investor-benjamin-graham]]; the seasonal and cycle literature offers specific dated rules with long backtests [[john-wiley-sons-stock-trader-almanac-2005-38th-ed]] [[l-williams-the-right-stock-at-the-right-time-prospering-in-the-coming-good-years--dow-seasonal-rotation-system]]; and none of those backtests accounts for transaction costs, taxes or, in several cases, dividends.
- **Whether long-cycle frameworks forecast anything.** The Kondratieff and phase-cycle models supply a coherent narrative and a genuinely useful market history [[deborah-owen-mapping-the-markets]] [[divorcing-the-dow-using-revolutionary-market-indicators-to-profit-from-the-stealth-boom-ah]]; the one book here that made a dated, falsifiable prediction from a single historical analogy — NASDAQ at 30,000 by 2011 — was wrong by an order of magnitude, which is the clearest evidence available on how much weight the method bears.
- **Whether active management is worth the search.** Strachman's conclusion is that most investors should hire a disciplined manager rather than pick stocks [[daniel-a-strachman-essential-stock-picking-strategies]]; Hayden argues for searching out the minority who outperform, on a stretch favourable to active management [[getting-an-investing-game-plan-creating-it-working-it-winning-it-wiley-2003]]; the LeBaron anthology's indexing chapter and the efficient-markets counterpoint make the opposite case in the same volume [[dean-lebaron-s-treasury-of-investment-wisdom-30-great-investing-minds-wiley]].
