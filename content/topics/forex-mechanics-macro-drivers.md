## What it is

Foreign exchange is a decentralised, nearly 24-hour, over-the-counter dealer market rather than an exchange: there is no central book, no consolidated volume, and no closing price. Prices are relative — a currency cannot rise without something falling against it — so every quote is a statement about two economies at once. That structure produces the category's distinctive concerns. Macro drivers (interest-rate differentials, growth, inflation, capital flows, central-bank policy) set the medium-term direction. Scheduled releases and session hand-offs set the hour. And because retail access came bundled with leverage of 50:1 and above, the literature's most consistent warning is not about entries at all: it is that gearing, not signal quality, determines whether an account survives long enough for any edge to matter.

## Core principles

- Currency values are relative and must be read as parity between two things, not as the price of one [[3-how-to-access-and-trade-the-world-s-biggest-market]].
- The market is an OTC dealer network with no central exchange, running around the clock from Sydney through Tokyo, London and New York [[all-about-forex-market-in-usa-eng]] [[1fta-forex-trading-course]].
- Gearing is the primary risk control, ahead of the stop. Du Toit's worked case shows a 20:1 trader wiped out by a cumulative 175-pip adverse move where a 1:1 trader would need 2,500 pips [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]].
- No single discipline forecasts currencies. Henderson's answer is a four-way signal grid — economics, flow, technicals and valuation — acted on only when a majority agree [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting]].
- The grid is inconclusive most of the time, which is itself the finding: when it is, fall back to a risk-appetite or carry read rather than forcing a trade [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting--signal-grid-and-active-currency-overlay]].
- Macro sets the trend, order flow and news set the hour — and the news adjustment happens fast enough that daily or even minute data can miss it [[what-moves-the-currency-market]] [[chinn]].
- Short-term FX price action contains a large random component, so a method requiring pinpoint entries is fighting order flow it cannot see [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]].
- Volatility is concentrated by clock: the London and London/New York overlap carries 2-4× the average hourly range of the late New York session [[forex-report-when-totrade]].
- Because the market is dominated by participants not seeking profit — central banks, corporate treasuries, tourists — classical efficient-market reasoning fits it poorly [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting]].
- Interest-rate differentials are a tradable return in their own right, not just a directional input, and drive capital toward higher-yielding currencies [[day-trading-the-currency-market--leveraged-carry-trade]].
- The carry trade's risk is not the rate spread but the unwind: it works while risk appetite is high and reverses violently when it is not [[day-trading-the-currency-market--leveraged-carry-trade]] [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting]].
- A forecast is not a strategy. Gotthelf's formulation: every strategy is a forecast plus a decision rule plus a money-management plan [[3-how-to-access-and-trade-the-world-s-biggest-market]].
- COT positioning is context, not timing: it is delayed, the sign conventions differ from the spot pair, and commercial hedging often happens in spot rather than futures [[cot]].
- Counterparty and dealer risk are real in an OTC market — the dealer is the counterparty, the spread is not the whole cost, and regulatory protection varies by entity [[forex-nfa]].
- Practitioners themselves largely reject purchasing power parity as a trading input: 63% of surveyed dealers called it academic jargon and 81% would not act on a PPP signal, though 40% believed it matters beyond six months [[chinn]].
- Most of the retail literature is broker- or vendor-produced education rather than research, and its leverage, spread and market-size figures are usually a decade or more out of date [[forex-nfa]] [[quick-guide-to-trade-forex]].

## Concrete rules and setups

### Macro and positioning

1. Henderson's signal grid: score currency economics, flow analysis, technicals and long-term valuation as independent buy/sell readings, and open a major new position only when at least three of the four agree [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting--signal-grid-and-active-currency-overlay]].
2. Trend-following overlay: 32-, 61- and 117-day moving averages, chosen to span short, medium and long trader horizons, readable either as directional trades or as a variable hedge ratio [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting--signal-grid-and-active-currency-overlay]].
3. Widely used technical defaults in institutional FX: 20-, 55- and 200-day moving averages (and 55/200-week), with a 55-over-200 golden cross read as bullish; 14-day RSI at 70/30; 12/26/9 MACD [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting]].
4. Carry trade: buy the higher-yielding currency funded by the lower-yielding one. The worked example is long AUD/CHF at 4.75% against 0.25%, a 450 basis-point spread, which 5× leverage turns into roughly 22.5% annualised from carry alone. Enter when aggregate risk aversion is low and exit or reverse when it spikes [[day-trading-the-currency-market--leveraged-carry-trade]].
5. Hedging benchmarks: avoid polar 0% or 100% hedge ratios in favour of a partial or symmetrical benchmark, typically 50%, which cuts tracking error to roughly 70% of the polar level and leaves room to add value in either direction [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting]].
6. COT discipline: record both the report date and the underlying Tuesday position date; map futures direction to the pair's base currency before labelling a position long or short (CHF futures and USD/CHF carry opposite signs); treat extremes as crowding signals and rising open interest as trend confirmation [[cot]].

### News and timing

7. Concentrate discretionary trading in the London session and the London/New York overlap, roughly 8-11am Eastern, where USD/CHF averaged 39-45 pips an hour against 12-16 in the late afternoon [[forex-report-when-totrade]].
8. Prefer Tuesday and Wednesday for range-dependent strategies and treat Sunday as low-opportunity; the same data showed no reliable day-of-month effect worth trading [[forex-report-when-totrade]].
9. Release ranking as of mid-2006: ISM Manufacturing had overtaken Non-Farm Payrolls on the full daily-range measure, while NFP and the FOMC decision remained the top two on the 20-minute knee-jerk measure, with Trade Balance, CPI and Retail Sales rising in importance [[mostmarketmoving08-09-06]].
10. Booker's NFP alternative to scalping the release: mark the high and low between midnight and 7am Eastern on NFP Friday, trade a close beyond that box, stop 100 pips beyond the opposite side, and reverse with a 1-pip stop beyond the original box if hit [[nfp]].
11. Do not enter during a panic or surprise surge; let the market digest the shock and wait for confirmation, since indicators distort during sudden moves [[avoiding-mistakes-in-forex-trading-forextrader]].
12. Read the incentive behind an official statement rather than the statement itself — a government comment can be a contrarian clue about the direction officials want [[avoiding-mistakes-in-forex-trading-forextrader]].

### Discretionary frameworks

13. The 4x1 strategy: one currency pair at a time (EUR/USD, occasionally GBP/USD), low gearing with staggered entries, one direction only established from a medium-term fundamental view, and a one-percent profit edge [[the-bird-watching-lion-country--4x1-median-grid]].
14. Median grid: on a 60-minute chart with three to four weeks of history, draw the clearest support and resistance — roughly 300-400 pips on EUR/USD, 400-500 on GBP/USD, about 2.5-3% of price — identify the 20-40 pip median band, divide into four quadrants, and buy predominantly in the bottom half during an uptrend [[the-bird-watching-lion-country--4x1-median-grid]].
15. Du Toit's gearing ceiling: about 3:1 per entry with total exposure sometimes reaching 10-15:1 across staggered entries; set a maximum drawdown tolerance in advance (his example 25%) but begin actively reducing at around 10% [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]].
16. PET: separate the durable storyline from daily news flavour, weigh an event by its proximity, expect spike-and-normalise rather than regime change from most releases, and treat your own psychological state as an event on equal footing [[the-bird-watching-lion-country--relational-analysis]].
17. Gotthelf's $600 or 2% rule: trades drawing down more than roughly 2% of position value rarely recover, so cap automatic loss exposure there — which on $10,000 of capital allows roughly 16-17 consecutive losses before ruin [[3-how-to-access-and-trade-the-world-s-biggest-market]].

### Retail systems

18. Sure-Fire: three nested timeframes, with trend set by 89- and 144-period EMA bands of highs and lows, entry only on a 38.2% Fibonacci retracement, stop just beyond the 61.8% level, a 2:1 minimum reward-to-risk, and no more than 3% of equity risked in total across all open trades [[mcrae-mark-sure-fire-forex-trading]].
19. Tunnel method: trade breaks of the band between the 144- and 169-period 1-hour EMAs, stop on the opposite side of the tunnel (typically 10-25 pips), scale out roughly a third at each of 55, 89, 144, 233 and 377 pips, and skip the Asian session and major news [[the-1hr-tunnel-method]].
20. 5/13/62: buy when the 5 EMA crosses the 13 on a 15- or 30-minute chart, stronger when both also cross the 62; set the initial stop 20 pips beyond the 62 EMA rather than 20 pips from entry; avoid the whipsawing "DNA spiral" and new positions after 14:00 GMT on Friday [[5-13-62]].
21. Session and margin defaults common across the compiled retail systems: trade the Asian/European and European/US overlaps, close by early Friday afternoon to avoid weekend gaps, and keep margin usage under roughly 10% of equity [[9-forex-systems]].
22. Retail leverage guidance where it is coherent: 5:1 to 10:1 for newcomers rather than the 50:1-400:1 available [[1fta-forex-trading-course]]; a 5:1 cap regardless of what the broker offers [[the-magic-of-forex-trading]].

### Mechanics

23. Spot settles within two days; anything beyond that is a forward carrying interest-rate and counterparty risk, with forward points reflecting the rate differential under covered interest parity [[all-about-forex-market-in-usa-eng]] [[3-how-to-access-and-trade-the-world-s-biggest-market]].
24. Before opening a position, compute notional exposure, security deposit, spread, rollover charges and the possible loss — and verify the dealer, introducing firm and account manager with the regulator, including disciplinary history [[forex-nfa]].
25. Pair a protective stop with a profit limit through an OCO order where the broker supports it, and advance the stop by cancel-and-replace rather than leaving it static [[10keystosuccess]].
26. Prefer limit orders in fast conditions: a market or stop order can slip, while a limit guarantees price but not execution [[10keystosuccess]] [[forex-nfa]].

## Common mistakes

- Trading at leverage the account cannot survive, which the literature names as the single dominant cause of retail failure [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]] [[forex-nfa]] [[1fta-forex-trading-course]].
- Using tight, timing-based stops in a volatile decentralised market, where normal noise reaches them before the thesis resolves [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]].
- Counter-trend trading a currency because it looks overbought, rather than trading with the established fundamental direction [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]] [[abcdelforex]].
- Treating a forecast as a trade without a stated decision rule and sizing plan [[3-how-to-access-and-trade-the-world-s-biggest-market]].
- Trading the seconds after a major release, where slippage and 100-pip moves make the execution risk larger than the edge [[nfp]].
- Assuming a late move after a release is still a news response, when the adjustment happens almost immediately [[chinn]] [[what-moves-the-currency-market]].
- Reading COT positioning as a timing signal rather than delayed context, or mis-mapping futures signs onto the spot pair [[cot]].
- Trading thin non-USD crosses, where liquidity is lower and the drivers less transparent [[the-bird-watching-lion-country--4x1-median-grid]].
- Assuming the spread is the whole cost, when commissions, markups and rollover also apply [[forex-nfa]].
- Taking a broker's or vendor's education as research: much of this category is marketing with no audited results, and some sources contradict themselves on leverage within a single document [[1fta-forex-trading-course]] [[managed-forex-trading-software]].
- Applying a 5%-per-trade exposure figure to a leveraged product, which several retail manuals recommend and which is far too large in this context [[10keystosuccess]].
- Carrying mid-2000s carry-trade economics into a near-zero-rate regime, where the available spreads and the trade's basic logic change [[day-trading-the-currency-market--leveraged-carry-trade]].

## Best books for this topic

1. [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting]] — the most serious book here: a four-discipline framework with named academic sources for its overlay strategies, written for people who actually hedge currency risk.
2. [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]] — the clearest and most numerically worked case that gearing, not entries, decides retail outcomes. Discretionary and honest about it.
3. [[3-how-to-access-and-trade-the-world-s-biggest-market]] — parity as an organising lens, plus the forecast-plus-rule-plus-sizing formulation that most of this category omits.
4. [[all-about-forex-market-in-usa-eng]] — the New York Fed's own institutional primer; the best source for how spot, forward and swap pricing actually relate.
5. [[day-trading-the-currency-market]] — the most codeable retail FX book, and the carry-trade chapter is the clearest short statement of the macro trade.
6. [[chinn]] — what FX dealers themselves believe about speculation, intervention and PPP, from survey data rather than assertion.
7. [[forex-report-when-totrade]] — four years of tick data on when volatility actually occurs; useful regardless of method.
8. [[mostmarketmoving08-09-06]] — a measured ranking of which releases move the dollar, rather than the usual undifferentiated calendar.
9. [[norman-frumkin-guide-to-economic-indicators]] — the reference for what roughly sixty US releases actually measure, when the newsletter glossaries are not enough.
10. [[forex-nfa]] — short, free, and the only source here with no product to sell; read it before funding an OTC account.
11. [[mcrae-mark-sure-fire-forex-trading]] — a representative and unusually complete retail system, with explicit reward-to-risk and equity-risk caps.

## Open debates

- **Whether stops or gearing are the primary risk control.** Du Toit argues tight stops in a noisy market guarantee being stopped out and that low gearing plus staggered entries is the real defence [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]]; Gotthelf derives a hard 2%-of-position loss cap from observation that deeper drawdowns rarely recover [[3-how-to-access-and-trade-the-world-s-biggest-market]]; most retail sources simply assert a fixed pip stop with no reasoning [[5-13-62]] [[9-forex-systems]].
- **Whether short-term FX is tradable at all.** Du Toit calls short-horizon price action largely random noise and builds a method around not needing precise entries [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]]; the intraday systems in the category assume the opposite without testing it [[the-1hr-tunnel-method]] [[9-forex-systems]]; the one attempt at measurement here is a continuation-probability table its own authors call descriptive, not a strategy [[forex-report-predicting-price-movement]].
- **Whether valuation models have any use.** Henderson includes long-term valuation as one of four grid disciplines [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting]]; surveyed dealers overwhelmingly dismiss PPP as unusable while a substantial minority still think it binds beyond six months [[chinn]].
- **Whether averaging in is discipline or disaster.** Du Toit's grid explicitly stages multiple entries into a position moving against him, defended by low gearing [[the-bird-watching-lion-country--4x1-median-grid]], and several retail systems permit adding at a fixed adverse distance [[241forex]]; the money-management literature treats adding to a loser as the cardinal error.
- **Which timeframe defines the trend.** The multi-timeframe systems set trend on the higher chart and enter on the lower [[mcrae-mark-sure-fire-forex-trading]] [[the-1hr-tunnel-method]]; du Toit sets it from a fundamental view and treats charts only as a reference grid [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]]; Henderson requires agreement across four independent disciplines before either matters [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting--signal-grid-and-active-currency-overlay]].
- **How much leverage a beginner should use.** The same course recommends both 5:1-10:1 for newcomers and 100:1 as the "best" balance [[1fta-forex-trading-course]]; du Toit caps at roughly 3:1 per entry [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]]; the carry-trade case study models 5× and 10× as ordinary [[day-trading-the-currency-market--leveraged-carry-trade]].
- **What counts as evidence in this category.** Only the Fed primer, the dealer survey, the tick-range study and the release-impact ranking rest on data [[all-about-forex-market-in-usa-eng]] [[chinn]] [[forex-report-when-totrade]] [[mostmarketmoving08-09-06]]. Nearly everything else is broker-produced education or a self-published system with no sample, and at least one document in the category is a compounding trade log with the rules withheld entirely [[tradetables-1-6-2003]].
