The library is large enough that reading it at random is worse than reading a fraction of it in order. Each path below is a sequence: earlier entries set up later ones. Nothing here is a recommendation to trade any method — several paths deliberately end at the evidence that the method does not survive testing.

Two notes before starting. First, almost every book in this library was written between the 1930s and the mid-2000s, so its parameters, costs and market structure are dated even where its logic is not; treat specific numbers as illustrations of method. Second, the single most useful page in the library for judging everything else is the [Quant, Microstructure & Academic Research](topics/quant-microstructure-academic-research.html) synthesis, and it is worth reading early rather than last.

## By experience level

### Beginner — first fifteen hours

Start with how markets work, then with why traders lose, and only then with methods.

1. [[nasdaq-education-foundation-a-guide-to-u-s-stock-markets-eng]] — how an order actually reaches a market and gets filled. Written by an academic rather than a vendor.
2. [[john-j-murphy-charting-made-easy]] — the standard vocabulary of trends, support, resistance and patterns, stated clearly and briefly.
3. [[trading-hill-arthur-introduction-to-candlesticks]] — candlestick construction and the reversal set, with explicit confirmation rules.
4. [[managing-your-money]] — position sizing from a stop, in a few pages, before any of it becomes habit.
5. [[trade-your-way-to-financial-freedom]] — expectancy and R-multiples: why win rate is the wrong thing to optimise.
6. [[disciplined-trader]] — why sound analysis still loses, and the predefine-and-execute rule pair.
7. [[stops]] — a short, plain argument for why the stop is part of the plan rather than an afterthought.
8. [[damodaran-aswath-investment-fables]] — the antidote to everything else you will read: each popular story tested and its failure mode named.

Then read the [Money Management & Position Sizing](topics/money-management-position-sizing.html) and [Trading Psychology & Discipline](topics/trading-psychology-discipline.html) syntheses, which map what comes next.

### Intermediate — building a method

You have a rough approach and want to make it explicit and survivable.

1. [[come-into-my-trading-room-elder-alexander]] — the three pillars, and the 2% and 6% rules that turn discipline into arithmetic.
2. [[a-short-course-in-technical-trading]] — every technique with a reproducible formula and a real backtest table rather than a chart picture.
3. [[street-smarts-laurence-connors]] — the most completely specified short-term setups in the library, and honest that none is a standalone system.
4. [[alan-farley-the-master-swing-trader]] — cross-verification and multi-timeframe reading, which generalise well beyond his named setups.
5. [[trading-in-the-zone]] — probabilistic thinking and the 20-trade exercise, once you have an edge to be disciplined about.
6. [[turtlerules]] — a complete system, free, short, and specified tightly enough to code.
7. [[wayne-a-thorp-testing-trading-success]] — how much of a backtest survives commissions, slippage, margin cost and taxes.
8. [[taleb-nassim-fooled-by-randomness]] — read after your first good stretch, not before.

### Advanced — testing and institutional practice

1. [[mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies]] — the methodology chapter first, then the results. The most consequential book here.
2. [[perry-kaufman-smarter-trading]] — why short backtests systematically understate risk, and how to test components independently.
3. [[mathematicsmoneymanagement]] — optimal f derived properly, including the drawdown it implies.
4. [[balsara-nauzer-j-money-management-strategies-for-futures-traders]] — risk of ruin, Kelly sizing and multi-commodity allocation, all worked numerically.
5. [[madhavan-market-microstructure-a-survey]] — how prices actually form, and what the empirical literature has established.
6. [[hull-options-futures-and-other-derivative-securities-5th-ed]] — pricing and hedging from no-arbitrage first principles.
7. [[marketneutralstrategies]] — institutional long-short construction, with a candid post-mortem of the Askin and LTCM failures.
8. [[douglas-hubbard-the-failure-of-risk-management]] — the argument that the standard risk toolkit is unvalidated, and what to use instead.

## By goal

### I want to day trade

1. [[wyckoff-richard-d-the-day-trader-s-bible-or-my-secret-in-day-trading-of-stocks-2]] — a century old, and still the clearest account of reading flow and placing stops at structure.
2. [[mark-b-fisher-the-logical-trader-applying-a-method-to-the-madness]] — ACD: the most completely specified opening-range framework here, every entry paired to a stop.
3. [[kevin-hagerty-day-trading-course]] — the Traders Equation, and why loss size is the only variable you fully control.
4. [[larry-williams-long-term-secrets-to-short-term-trading]] — patterns with dated, falsifiable backtest statistics attached.
5. [[bernstein-jake-the-compleat-day-trader-vol-ii]] — read the opening chapter on why sold systems fail once fills and commissions are counted.
6. [[andrews-scott-understanding-gaps]] — a decade of data on the most popular intraday setup, and an unflattering profit factor.
7. [[tony-oz-how-i-make-a-living-trading-stocks]] — 116 real trades with timestamps, losers included.

Finish with the [Day Trading & Scalping](topics/day-trading-scalping.html) synthesis, which flags how much of this rests on market structure that no longer exists.

### I want to swing trade stocks

1. [[street-smarts-laurence-connors]] — the setup catalogue: failed breakouts, pullbacks, climax reversals.
2. [[alan-farley-the-master-swing-trader]] — the framework the setups sit inside.
3. [[oliver-velez-swing-trading-tactics]] — the simplest fully numeric entry pattern in the library.
4. [[marc-boucher-short-term-trading-course]] — money management taught before entries, with codeable breakout definitions.
5. [[a-practical-guide-to-swing-trading]] — a completely mechanical rule set, useful as a contrast in style.
6. [[gary-smith-how-i-trade-living]] — real dates, real percentages, audited multi-year returns.

### I want to trade forex

1. [[forex-nfa]] — free, short, and the only source with nothing to sell. Read before funding an account.
2. [[all-about-forex-market-in-usa-eng]] — the New York Fed on how spot, forward and swap pricing relate.
3. [[the-bird-watching-lion-country]] — the clearest case that gearing, not entries, decides retail outcomes.
4. [[wiley-currency-strategy-a-practitioner-s-guide-to-currency-trading-hedging-and-forecasting]] — the four-discipline signal grid, written for people who actually hedge currency risk.
5. [[day-trading-the-currency-market]] — the most codeable retail FX book, plus the carry trade explained properly.
6. [[forex-report-when-totrade]] — four years of tick data on when volatility actually occurs.
7. [[chinn]] — what FX dealers themselves believe, from survey data rather than assertion.

### I want to build a mechanical system

1. [[mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies--test-methodology]] — the testing protocol, before you build anything.
2. [[turtlerules]] — a complete, published system to reproduce as a baseline.
3. [[george-pruitt-building-winning-trading-systems-with-tradestation]] — six systems with full source code and 20-year, 17-market backtests.
4. [[richard-l-weissman-mechanical-trading-systems]] — codeable systems with performance tables, and an honest demonstration of a top parameter set becoming the worst the next year.
5. [[perry-kaufman-smarter-trading]] — robustness, price shocks, and why to test components separately.
6. [[trading-as-a-business]] — capitalisation and scaling, with the ANP Pyramid worked in full.
7. [[curtis-faith-way-of-the-turtle]] — robustness statistics, and what trading a system actually feels like.

### I want to trade options

1. [[options-essential-concepts-and-trading-strategies-2nd-edition]] — the CBOE's own text: form a view on direction and volatility, then pick the payoff that matches.
2. [[guy-cohen-the-bible-of-options-strategies]] — 58 strategies presented identically, so they can be compared.
3. [[fontanills-the-options-course-high-profit-and-low-stress-trading-methods-2nd-ed]] — delta-neutral trading with numbered road maps and max risk, reward and breakeven for each structure.
4. [[lawrence-g-mcmillan-profit-with-options]] — percentile-ranked volatility as the basis for deciding whether to buy or sell premium.
5. [[hull-options-futures-and-other-derivative-securities-5th-ed]] — the pricing and Greeks underneath all of it.
6. [[mcgraw-hill-the-options-edge-winning-the-volatility-game-with-options-on-futures]] — the one empirical study here, and it finds no inherent writer's edge.
7. [[george-m-jabbour-the-option-trader-handbook-strategies-and-trade-adjustments]] — what to do after entry, which most options books skip.

### I want to invest rather than trade

1. [[the-intelligent-investor-benjamin-graham]] — margin of safety, Mr. Market, and the defensive/enterprising split.
2. [[damodaran-aswath-investment-fables]] — every popular screen tested against real cross-sections.
3. [[the-little-book]] — the most reproducible system in the category: two ratios and a rank-sum.
4. [[david-dreman-contrarian-investment-strategies-the-next-generation]] — the behavioural case for value, with significance testing.
5. [[investment-valuation-damodaran]] — how to actually value something, and why terminal value dominates the answer.
6. [[r-g-hagstrom-the-warren-buffett-way-2nd-edition]] — a qualitative approach turned into a checklist.
7. [[getting-an-investing-game-plan-creating-it-working-it-winning-it-wiley-2003]] — allocation, which drives more of the outcome than selection.
8. [[harry-d-schultz-bear-market-investing-strategies]] — written for the roughly third of the time the rest of this list struggles.

### I want to fix my discipline

1. [[disciplined-trader]] — the diagnosis: sound analysis, failed execution.
2. [[trading-in-the-zone]] — the probabilistic frame and the 20-trade exercise.
3. [[phantom-of-the-20-pits]] — two rules, stated memorably enough to actually change behaviour.
4. [[kahneman-daniel-investor-psychology]] — the research underneath what the trading books assert, each bias paired with a remedy.
5. [[come-into-my-trading-room-elder-alexander]] — hard caps, so discipline stops depending on willpower.
6. [[brett-steenbarger-psychology-of-trading]] — the self-assessment checklists, and the argument that some "problems" are variance you have labelled.
7. [[taleb-nassim-fooled-by-randomness]] — the corrective for the streak that convinces you it is working.

### I want to size positions properly

1. [[trade-your-way-to-financial-freedom]] — four sizing models demonstrated on identical signals, which isolates their effect.
2. [[balsara-nauzer-j-money-management-strategies-for-futures-traders]] — the complete framework: risk of ruin, Kelly, multi-commodity allocation, pyramiding.
3. [[kellybetting]] — the 1956 original, worth reading to see exactly what it claims and what it does not.
4. [[mathematicsmoneymanagement]] — optimal f, and its candid account of the drawdowns it implies.
5. [[forex-misc-money-management-ryan-jones]] — the strongest critique of fixed-fractional sizing for small accounts.
6. [[jay-kaeppel-the-four-biggest-mistakes-in-futures-trading]] — the most practical account-sizing method for futures, blending three risk measures.
7. [[position-sizing]] — a controlled experiment: smaller positions cut bankruptcy rates roughly tenfold without raising average profit.

### I want to know what actually works

The shortest and least comfortable path in the library.

1. [[mcgraw-hill-encyclopedia-of-trading-strategies]] — moving averages, oscillators and cycles at or below chance out of sample, once costs are included.
2. [[mcgraw-hill-the-options-edge-winning-the-volatility-game-with-options-on-futures]] — indiscriminate option writing has a payout ratio of almost exactly 1.0.
3. [[andrews-scott-understanding-gaps]] — a 72.6% win rate on gap fades, and a profit factor of 1.08.
4. [[griffiths-turnbullb-and-white-re-examining-the-small-cap-myth-problems-in-portfolio-format]] — an anomaly that is real in the data and unavailable in practice.
5. [[using-neural-networks-and-genetic-algorithms-to-predict-stock-market-returns]] — structure exists in the series; neither model beat a "no change" benchmark.
6. [[hedge-fund-risk-factors-and-value-at-risk-of-credit-trading-strategies]] — reported volatility understates true risk by 60-100% for illiquid styles.
7. [[exploring-value-at-risk]] — the standard institutional risk number, and what it does not measure.
8. [[taleb-nassim-fooled-by-randomness]] — why the track records that survived all this still may not mean what they appear to.
9. [[does-trendfollowing-work-on-stocks]] — and then one piece of positive evidence, with the caveat that its publisher sells the strategy.
